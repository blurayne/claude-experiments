Galactic Transit

Where the solar system actually goes: the Sun's 225-million-year orbit of the Milky Way, the planets winding around that track, and half a million real Gaia DR3 stars about them — drawn at true scale.

This is where the solar system actually goes. Not a diagram of the planets, but the path they take through the Milky Way: the Sun on its 225-million-year orbit, the planets winding around that track, half a million real stars from Gaia DR3 around them, and the Galaxy's own shape behind. It was built to make that motion visible, and to be as accurate as a browser allows — where a model stands in for a measurement, the piece says so rather than hoping you will not ask.

The Sun orbits the Milky Way's core at ~230 km/s. The planets, on an ecliptic tilted ~60° to the galactic plane, weave helical paths around its track.

Sources

Everything drawn or claimed traces to these. Data first, then the science, then the images.

  • Gaia DR3 via AT-HYG v3.2 (D. Nash) — the 500,000 real stars and their velocities; Hipparcos for those Gaia saturates on
  • StarHorse (Anders et al.) — the measured 3-D stellar density the disk is checked against
  • NASA/JPL-Caltech/R. Hurt — the face-on Milky Way illustration used as the probability map (reflected on ingest so the arms trail; see the winding tests)
  • Hubble PHAT/PHAST mosaics (heic2508) — Andromeda's photographic map
  • Oort 1927 — differential rotation; Lin & Shu 1964 — density waves; Toomre & Toomre 1972 — tidal bridges and tails; Sellwood & Carlberg — transient, recurring arms
  • VINTERGATAN (Agertz, Renaud et al. 2021) — the cosmological zoom simulation behind the assembly scenario: the contracting, thickened, merger-scrambled young disk and the Gaia-Enceladus convulsion
  • Reid et al. — maser parallaxes, the pattern speeds and the plan view; Han & Qiao — the angular momentum toward the south galactic pole
  • Chemin et al. 2009 and Corbelli et al. 2010 — M31's HI kinematics (the SW limb approaches); van der Marel et al. 2019 — Gaia's direct M31 rotation
  • Block et al. 2006 (Nature 443, 832) — M32's plunge and the off-centre rings; the lay account: "Galactic collision gave Andromeda its arms" (Nature News, 2014)
  • Cox & Loeb 2008 — Milkomeda; Sawala et al. 2025 — ~50% merger odds (the drawn track is their merging-half median); arXiv:2603.22863 (2026) — the ~90% reanalysis whose band the track sits in
  • Berski & Dybczyński 2016 — Gliese 710's pass; Shaviv and Svensmark — the contested cosmic-ray/climate hypothesis the ice ages follow, flagged as such
  • Images: Spitzer sig06-025 (NASA/JPL-Caltech/D. Block) and ssc2006-14a (P. Barmby); NASA/CXC — M31's core; AURA/NOAO/NSF — the LMC; ESO/DSS2 — the SMC; A. Meleg (CC BY-SA) — M33; the Messier Objects guide
