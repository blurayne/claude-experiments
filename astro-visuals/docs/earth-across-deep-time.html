Earth across deep time

A fuller picture than the live Earth panel attempts: named milestones from the first amino acids to the oceans boiling away, with the atmosphere, pressure and UV/radiation environment estimated for each. The panel's own temperature is a simplified continuous model — explicitly not a climate model, right order of magnitude rather than a forecast — while this table gives specific, independently sourced estimates at each point. The two will not always agree to the degree; both are honest about being estimates, not measurements.

The same data as a curve: minimum, maximum and average temperature across the same 23 milestones, on a linear scale from four billion years ago to two billion years from now. Today's minimum and maximum use actual recorded planetary extremes (Antarctica's coldest, Death Valley's hottest) rather than the modelled global-mean range every other row uses, so it is drawn as a separate whisker instead of joining the band — splicing it into the curve would tear a 146-degree spike through an otherwise smooth line for a difference of definition, not of climate. Two points run hotter than the chart's own frame and are marked off scale rather than compressed to fit; their true values are in the table above.
