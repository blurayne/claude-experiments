## Type I fibre (slow-twitch) (`type-i-fibre`)
A slow-twitch, oxidative skeletal muscle fibre built for endurance: a single unbranched, cylindrical cell running centimetres long (~40-60 um across), with hundreds of nuclei flattened at the PERIPHERY just under the sarcolemma, regular cross-striations (A/I bands, Z-disc to Z-disc sarcomeres) alongside lengthwise myofibrils, and dense packing of mitochondria and myoglobin that give it a deep red colour and an essentially inexhaustible aerobic ATP supply, fed by a rich surrounding capillary bed.

## Type II fibre (fast-twitch) (`type-ii-fibre`)
The fast-twitch family of human skeletal muscle fibre, built to contract quickly and forcefully via anaerobic glycolysis rather than to last. The family is not one fibre but two related types, Type IIa and Type IIx, which sit at different points on the same fast design -- so this entry is rendered as a small bundle of two adjacent fibres, one styled IIa (moderate mitochondria, pale rose, richer capillary bed) and one styled IIx (sparse mitochondria, near-white cream, thin capillary), sharing the same cross-striated, peripherally-nucleated architecture but visibly differing in colour, mitochondrial density and capillary supply.

## Type IIa fibre (fast oxidative-glycolytic) (`type-iia-fibre`)
The middle fibre of the three human skeletal muscle types: fast like Type IIx, but with enough mitochondria and myoglobin to also run oxidatively, giving it noticeably better fatigue resistance than its purely glycolytic sibling. A single, unbranched, cylindrical multinucleated cell centimetres long (~30 mm) and ~60 µm across — narrower than Type IIx, wider than Type I — cross-striated by regular transverse A/I banding along its whole length, with many flattened nuclei just under the sarcolemma at the periphery (never one central nucleus — that is cardiac muscle), a moderate density of mitochondria and a moderate surrounding capillary network (more than Type IIx, less than Type I), and a pale rose-pink colour that sits visibly between Type I's deep red and Type IIx's near-white cream. Endurance training shifts some Type IIx fibres toward IIa; disuse pushes the balance back.

## Type IIx fibre (fast glycolytic) (`type-iix-fibre`)
The largest-diameter, fastest, most purely glycolytic fibre in human skeletal muscle: a single, unbranched, cylindrical cell centimetres long and ~70 um across, cross-striated along its whole length, with many flattened nuclei just under the sarcolemma at the periphery (never one central nucleus), sparse mitochondria, a thin capillary supply and abundant glycogen granules, pale pink-cream rather than red. What older histochemistry called human 'Type IIb' is this fibre under a different name -- true MYH4-expressing IIb is a rodent fibre, essentially absent from adult human limb muscle.
