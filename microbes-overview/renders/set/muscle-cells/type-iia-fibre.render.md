# Type IIa fibre (fast oxidative-glycolytic) — render log

**Set:** `muscle-cells` · **Microbe key:** `type-iia-fibre`
**Short description:** The middle fibre of the three human skeletal muscle types: fast like Type IIx, but with enough mitochondria and myoglobin to also run oxidatively, giving it noticeably better fatigue resistance than its purely glycolytic sibling. A single, unbranched, cylindrical multinucleated cell centimetres long (~30 mm) and ~60 µm across — narrower than Type IIx, wider than Type I — cross-striated by regular transverse A/I banding along its whole length, with many flattened nuclei just under the sarcolemma at the periphery (never one central nucleus — that is cardiac muscle), a moderate density of mitochondria and a moderate surrounding capillary network (more than Type IIx, less than Type I), and a pale rose-pink colour that sits visibly between Type I's deep red and Type IIx's near-white cream. Endurance training shifts some Type IIx fibres toward IIa; disuse pushes the balance back.

## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The **Type IIa fibre** is the middle of the three physiologically distinct types of human skeletal muscle fibre (Type I, Type IIa, Type IIx). Like every skeletal muscle fibre it is a **single, unbranched, cylindrical multinucleated cell** — a syncytium formed during development by the fusion of many myoblasts — running the **full length of the muscle or of a long fascicle within it**: centimetres long in an adult limb muscle, far longer than it is wide, so in any single frame it must run off the edges rather than sit as a short self-contained sausage. Its diameter (commonly ~50–65 µm in adult human limb muscle) sits **between its two siblings**: narrower than the large-diameter Type IIx, wider than the slender Type I.

**Cross-striation is the defining visible feature of this cell, and the single most important thing to get right in a render.** Skeletal muscle is striated muscle: regular, narrow, dark and light bands run **transversely — across the fibre, perpendicular to its long axis** — repeating evenly from one end of the fibre to the other, like the rungs of a ladder laid along the tube, or the teeth of a fine-toothed comb dragged down its length. Underneath that transverse banding, and running lengthwise beneath it, are the parallel **myofibrils** — the rod-like contractile bundles that fill the fibre — but the banding itself is a set of narrow rings/bands wrapping across the fibre's width, not a set of stripes running along its length. Each repeating unit is a **sarcomere** (~2–2.5 µm at rest, Z-disc to Z-disc): a darker **A band** (the full length of the myosin thick filaments) flanked by lighter **I bands** (thin actin filaments only, bisected by the dense **Z-disc** line), with the banding of neighbouring myofibrils in register across the fibre's width so the whole cell reads as evenly spaced cross-bands, not a lengthwise grain. **A render whose "striation" consists of stripes running parallel to the fibre's long axis is wrong — that reads as longitudinally-viewed myofibrils or fasciae, not cross-striation, and is the dominant failure mode to check for on every accepted image, measured, not eyeballed** (see the verification method in §5/verdicts).

The second defining, and most commonly bungled, feature is nuclear position. A skeletal fibre carries **many nuclei — dozens to hundreds along its length** — and every one of them sits **flattened just beneath the sarcolemma, at the cell's periphery**, oriented with their long axis parallel to the fibre. A **single central nucleus is the single most reliable "wrong answer" signature**: it means the artist has drawn a cardiomyocyte (which is mononucleate with one central nucleus) or a regenerating/immature fibre, not a mature skeletal fibre. Count the nuclei on every render and confirm each one hugs the rim, none sit mid-cross-section.

What sets Type IIa apart from its two siblings is metabolic, and it sits in the **middle** on every axis, not at either extreme. It expresses **myosin heavy chain IIa (MYH2)**, giving it the fast contraction speed of the Type II family, but unlike Type IIx it retains a **moderate density of mitochondria** — visibly more numerous than Type IIx's sparse, individually-scattered organelles, but still clearly fewer and less densely packed than Type I's abundant rows — and a **moderate myoglobin content**, giving the fibre a **pale rose-pink colour**, distinctly pinker/deeper than Type IIx's pale cream but clearly lighter than Type I's deep red. It runs a **moderate surrounding capillary network** — denser than the thin, sparse supply around a Type IIx fibre, thinner than the dense capillary bed around an oxidative Type I fibre — consistent with a fibre that can sustain oxidative metabolism for longer than IIx but not indefinitely like Type I. It stores glycogen granules for glycolytic backup, though less emphasised in the composition than in a purely glycolytic IIx fibre. Functionally this hybrid oxidative-glycolytic design gives Type IIa **noticeably better fatigue resistance than Type IIx** while remaining faster and more powerful than Type I — the fibre of choice for sustained-but-vigorous efforts (a fast uphill jog, repeated sets, a longer sprint) rather than either an all-day plod or a single maximal explosion. It sits on the same fast motor units as Type IIx and is subject to the same size-principle recruitment order (after Type I, before Type IIx), but tolerates sustained effort better than IIx — which is exactly the profile endurance training tends to shift fibres towards: some Type IIx fibres convert into IIa with sustained training, and disuse tends to push the balance back the other way, IIa toward IIx.

**The fibre-typing point, sharper here than for either sibling.** Older ATPase histochemistry, using a single pH-dependent staining reaction, separates fibres into two groups only — Type I (dark) versus the whole Type II family (pale) — and **cannot distinguish IIa from IIx**; that separation needs either a second ATPase reaction at a different acid pre-incubation pH, or immunohistochemistry/immunofluorescence with an antibody specific to the IIa myosin heavy-chain isoform (or its mRNA). Any single-pH ATPase plate that claims to show "the IIa fibre" specifically should be treated with suspicion — see §2 for how the reference for this subject was chosen precisely because it resolves this distinction, unlike the plates rejected by the Type IIx sibling render.

Sources: [Smerdu et al. 1994, *Am J Physiol* 267(6 Pt 1):C1723-8 — "Type IIx myosin heavy chain transcripts are expressed in Type IIb fibers of human skeletal muscle"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7545970/), [Schiaffino & Reggiani 2011, *Physiol Rev* 91(4):1447-1531 — "Fiber Types in Mammalian Skeletal Muscles"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8262285/) (open review, DOI 10.1152/physrev.00031.2010), [Bloemberg & Quadrilatero 2012, *PLoS ONE* 7(4):e35273 — "Rapid Determination of Myosin Heavy Chain Expression in Rat, Mouse, and Human Skeletal Muscle Using Multicolor Immunofluorescence Analysis"](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0035273) (open access, CC BY), [OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e — 10.5 Types of Muscle Fibers](https://open.oregonstate.education/anatomy2e/chapter/types-muscle-fibers/), [Pette & Staron 2000, *Microsc Res Tech* 50(6):500-9 — "Myosin isoforms, muscle fiber types, and transitions"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10998639/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **NO single central nucleus.** That is the cardiomyocyte signature. This fibre has MANY nuclei, and every one sits flattened at the periphery, against the sarcolemma — never floating mid-cross-section.
- **NO longitudinal-only striping.** The single biggest science risk for this subject: bands must run TRANSVERSELY, across the tube, perpendicular to its long axis, repeating evenly along the whole visible length like rungs on a ladder — not as stripes running parallel to the long axis (that reads as fasciae or lengthwise fibrils, not cross-striation). If a render shows only lengthwise colour bands with no repeating perpendicular banding, it fails regardless of anything else being correct.
- **Not branched, not Y-shaped, no intercalated discs.** Those are cardiac muscle features. This is a plain, unbranched cylinder running out of frame at both ends.
- **Not a short stubby sausage that fits entirely inside the frame, and not a capped cylinder with both rounded ends visible inside the canvas.** A muscle fibre is centimetres long; compose it running diagonally off two opposite edges so its true proportions (far longer than wide) read even though the whole length cannot fit. Confirm against the actual pixels at the frame edges, not just the prompt wording.
- **Not deep red (that is Type I) and not pale cream/near-white (that is Type IIx).** Keep the fibre a pale ROSE-PINK, visibly between the two — a render that reads as either sibling's colour is wrong for this subject.
- **Not a dense wall-to-wall carpet of large mitochondria (Type I), and not scarce, thinly-scattered singles (Type IIx).** Mitochondria here are a MODERATE, visibly-intermediate density — noticeably more numerous/clustered than the sparse IIx profile, noticeably less packed than the dense Type I profile.
- **No smooth, unstriated surface anywhere along the fibre.** Cross-striations (A/I banding) must be continuous and in register the whole visible length; a smooth patch reads as smooth muscle, which is wrong here.
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids, flagella or cilia** — this is an animal cell, not a bacterium or a protist.
- **No motor end plate drawn as a large obvious structure dominating the composition** — a neuromuscular junction may appear small and subordinate at most one end, but the fibre itself is the subject.
- **Never claim a single-pH ATPase stain identifies this fibre specifically.** IIa vs IIx is exactly the distinction a single-reaction ATPase plate cannot make (see §1 fibre-typing point and §2).
- One clearly dominant fibre as the subject of the composition; at most a couple of neighbouring fibres, subordinate and cropped by the frame, so the frame does not turn into an unreadable cross-section thicket.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The Type IIa fibre is the intermediate physiological type among the three human skeletal muscle fibre types, characterised by expression of myosin heavy chain IIa (MYH2). It combines the fast contraction kinetics of the Type II lineage with a hybrid oxidative-glycolytic metabolic profile: a moderate mitochondrial volume density and myoglobin content — intermediate between the sparse, low-myoglobin Type IIx and the dense, myoglobin-rich Type I — together with an intermediate capillary-to-fibre ratio, consistent with a fibre optimised for sustained moderate-to-high-intensity output rather than either maximal peak force or long-duration low-intensity endurance. Its cross-sectional diameter (commonly ~50–65 µm in adult human limb muscle) likewise falls between the two other types. Metabolically it relies on both oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis, giving it markedly greater fatigue resistance than Type IIx while retaining substantially higher force and shortening velocity than Type I. Motor unit recruitment follows the size principle, placing Type IIa units between Type I and Type IIx in recruitment order. Its proportion within the fast-fibre pool is plastic: chronic endurance training drives a well-documented fast-to-fast transition of Type IIx fibres toward the Type IIa phenotype (IIx → IIa, and further toward IIc/I-hybrid states with sufficiently prolonged aerobic stimulus), while detraining or disuse shifts the balance back toward IIx. Critically, Type IIa cannot be distinguished from Type IIx using the classical single-pH-preincubation myosin ATPase histochemistry that separates Type I from the Type II family as a whole; differentiation requires either a second ATPase reaction at an alternate acid pre-incubation pH (the basis of the historical IIa/IIb/IIc nomenclature) or isoform-specific immunohistochemistry/immunofluorescence against the MYH2 gene product, as used for the reference image in §2 of this log.  
🇩🇪 Die Typ-IIa-Faser ist der intermediäre physiologische Typ unter den drei menschlichen Skelettmuskelfasertypen, gekennzeichnet durch die Expression der schweren Myosinkette IIa (MYH2). Sie verbindet die schnelle Kontraktionskinetik der Typ-II-Linie mit einem hybriden, oxidativ-glykolytischen Stoffwechselprofil: eine mäßige mitochondriale Volumendichte und einen mäßigen Myoglobingehalt — intermediär zwischen dem spärlichen, myoglobinarmen Typ IIx und dem dichten, myoglobinreichen Typ I —, zusammen mit einem intermediären Kapillar-zu-Faser-Verhältnis, passend zu einer Faser, die für anhaltende Leistung mittlerer bis hoher Intensität optimiert ist, statt für maximale Spitzenkraft oder lang andauernde Ausdauer geringer Intensität. Metabolisch stützt sie sich sowohl auf oxidative Phosphorylierung als auch auf Glykolyse, was ihr eine deutlich höhere Ermüdungsresistenz als Typ IIx verleiht, während sie gegenüber Typ I eine wesentlich höhere Kraft und Verkürzungsgeschwindigkeit behält.
