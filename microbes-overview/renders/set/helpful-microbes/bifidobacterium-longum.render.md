## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

*Bifidobacterium longum* is a **Gram-positive**, **strictly anaerobic**, **non-motile**, **non-spore-forming** bacterium of the phylum **Actinomycetota** (the high-GC Gram-positives, alongside *Corynebacterium* and *Mycobacterium* — not a lactobacillus, despite the shared lactic-acid habit). Cells are roughly **2–5 µm long and 0.5–1.3 µm wide**, and — as the species epithet *longum* suggests — this species is the slenderer, more elongated member of the genus.

**The branching is the whole point.** The genus name comes from Latin *bifidus*, "cleft into two", and the cells are famously **pleomorphic**: an individual can be a plain slightly-curved rod, but a healthy population shows cells forked into a clear **Y or V**, cells with **swollen club- or spatula-shaped ends** (coryneform, "*bifurcated*"), and short branched chains. Henry Tissier isolated exactly this organism from the stools of breast-fed infants in **1900** and named it *Bacillus bifidus* for the split ends he saw down the microscope; Orla-Jensen erected the genus *Bifidobacterium* in 1924. The branching is not an artefact and not a fungal hypha: it arises from **irregular, non-medial cell-wall growth at the poles and at new division sites**, and it becomes markedly more pronounced under nutrient stress — low N-acetylglucosamine or amino acids, bile salts, alkaline pH. A drawing of *B. longum* as a plain straight rod loses the one feature that names it.

**Envelope.** A **thick, multi-layered peptidoglycan sacculus** (a proper Gram-positive wall, ornithine-containing cross-bridges, stained deep purple by the Gram stain), interwoven with **wall polysaccharides and lipoteichoic acid**, and in many strains sheathed in a loose **exopolysaccharide (EPS) coat** that helps the cell tolerate bile and evade the immune system. Directly inside it lies the **plasma membrane**. There is **no outer membrane** and **no periplasmic space** — the Gram-negative double-envelope architecture is simply absent here. Many strains carry **sortase-dependent pili / Tad pili**: fine protein filaments used to grip mucus and epithelium, and to persist in the gut. They are adhesins, **not motility organelles**.

**Interior.** A prokaryotic cytoplasm: a single **circular chromosome** as an irregular **nucleoid** (the reference strain NCC2705 has ~2.26 Mb at ~60 % GC, sequenced in 2002 and notably rich in carbohydrate-handling genes — over 8 % of its genome), **70S ribosomes** scattered as fine granules, and often a small **plasmid**. No nucleus, no mitochondria, no membrane-bound organelles of any kind.

**Metabolism: the bifid shunt.** *Bifidobacterium* is defined biochemically by a single key enzyme, **fructose-6-phosphate phosphoketolase (F6PPK)**, which is used as the genus's diagnostic test. Instead of glycolysis it runs the **"bifid shunt"**: 2 glucose → **3 acetate + 2 lactate** (+ 5 ATP), and — unlike heterofermentative lactic-acid bacteria — **no CO₂ from hexoses**. Both end products matter for us. They drop the luminal **pH**, which directly suppresses acid-intolerant Enterobacteriaceae and other opportunists; the **acetate** is absorbed by the colonic epithelium and, in mouse work, protects the gut lining against enterohaemorrhagic *E. coli* O157:H7; and both acetate and lactate are **cross-fed** to butyrate producers such as *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* and *Anaerostipes*, so this one microbe seeds the butyrate supply the colonocytes run on.

**Human milk is its speciality.** Human milk contains **human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs)** — the third most abundant solid component of breast milk — which the **infant cannot digest at all**. They are not baby food; they are **bifidobacteria food**. *B. longum* is equipped for them: subsp. *infantis* carries a dedicated ~43 kb HMO cluster with ABC importers that pull whole oligosaccharides into the cell before dismantling them with intracellular fucosidases, sialidases, β-galactosidases and hexosaminidases; subsp. *longum* instead uses the **extracellular lacto-N-biosidase LnbB** to snip off lacto-N-biose I and imports that through the GNB/LNB pathway. Either way the mother feeds the microbe and the microbe feeds the baby. This is why *B. longum* is one of the **very first colonisers** of the newborn gut — acquired at birth and from breast milk and skin contact — and why it can make up a large share, in breast-fed infants often the **majority**, of the entire faecal microbiota in the first months, before falling back to a steady few percent after weaning and through adult life. Antibiotics thin the population out; fermented foods, fibre and HMO-like prebiotics help rebuild it.

**Where it sits in this atlas.** *B. longum* lives directly on and in the mucus layer of the small and large intestine, which puts it face to face with three cells already in this collection: the **enterocyte** whose brush border absorbs the short-chain fatty acids it makes, the **goblet cell** whose mucus it grips and partly feeds on, and the **Paneth cell** whose antimicrobial peptides shape which bacteria are tolerated there at all. It is a commensal and a food-grade probiotic organism, **not a pathogen**; invasive disease is a vanishing rarity confined to severely immunocompromised or preterm patients.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **Not a plain straight rod.** The **Y-shaped / branched, club-ended bifid form must be unmistakable in every style** — this is the single feature the organism is named for. A tidy straight sausage is a FAIL.
- **Do not draw it as a coccus.** No spheres, no round cells, no chains of beads, no clusters of balls.
- **No flagella.** *B. longum* is **non-motile** — no flagellum, no tuft, no whip-like tail, no swimming pose. Only short, straight, adhesive pili are permitted, and only a few.
- **No endospores.** It is **non-sporing** — no oval refractile spore inside the cell, no terminal drumstick head, no free spores. A swollen club end is not a spore and must not be drawn as a bright inner body.
- **No outer membrane and no periplasm.** Gram-positive means **one** thick wall layer over **one** membrane. Do not draw the Gram-negative sandwich (inner membrane + thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane), no LPS, no lipopolysaccharide bristles, no porins.
- **No fungal features** — this is a bacterium, not a mould: no true hyphae, no septate mycelium, no conidiophore or spore brush, no budding daughter cell, no chitin.
- **No eukaryotic organelles** — no nucleus with an envelope, no mitochondria, no ER, no Golgi, no vacuole. The DNA is a bare irregular nucleoid, **not** a tidy neat circle or a floating loop of rope.
- **No mesosome** (a classic fixation artefact), and **no plasmids, ribosomes or DNA drifting outside the cell body** — the space around the cell must be empty background.
- **No thick rigid capsule** drawn as a hard second shell; the EPS coat is a faint diffuse haze at most.
- **No pathogen cues** — no red inflamed tissue, no attacking pose, no spilled toxins, no immune cells fighting it. This microbe is a friend.
- **No branching so extreme it reads as a starfish or a snowflake**: one clear fork (occasionally two) with blunt club tips, not a dozen radiating arms.
- No face, no anthropomorphism, no milk bottles, sugar cubes or props in the scientific plates (those belong only to the kids' coloring page), no text, no letters, no numbers, no scale bar, no border or frame.

### Colour note

*B. longum* makes **no pigment**; a single cell is essentially colourless and translucent (a plate of colonies looks creamy-white, `#F1E9DA`). All colour in these plates is therefore **teaching convention**, held consistent across the four styles: cell body warm translucent cream, the thick wall a dusty ochre-olive band, the plasma membrane a fine teal line, the nucleoid blue-violet fibrils, ribosomes muted teal speckles, the exopolysaccharide a pale mint haze, and pili fine ochre threads. The reference SEM is false-coloured warm beige on charcoal to match.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Bifidobacterium longum is one of the first bacteria to move into a newborn's gut, and it arrives with a job no human cell can do. Breast milk contains human milk oligosaccharides — complex sugars that are, astonishingly, the third most abundant solid in milk and completely indigestible to the infant. They are not food for the baby at all; they are food for this bacterium. Mother feeds the microbe, and the microbe feeds the baby back. B. longum ferments those sugars into acetate and lactate, which lowers the pH of the gut, and an acidic gut is a poor place for Salmonella, pathogenic E. coli and other opportunists to establish themselves. The acetate is absorbed by the cells lining the intestine and used as fuel, and it is passed on to other gut bacteria that turn it into butyrate, the preferred energy source of the colon lining. In breast-fed infants this single species can dominate the entire gut flora; after weaning it settles back to a steady few percent and stays with us for life. It is a genuine commensal rather than a passenger: it lives right against the mucus layer alongside the enterocytes that absorb its acids, the goblet cells whose mucus it grips, and the Paneth cells whose antimicrobial peptides decide who is tolerated there. Antibiotics deplete it — that is part of why a course can upset digestion for weeks — while fermented foods, fibre and prebiotics support its recovery. It is a food-grade organism used in probiotic products, and disease caused by it is vanishingly rare.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Bifidobacterium longum is a Gram-positive, strictly anaerobic, non-motile, non-sporulating member of the phylum Actinomycetota, 2-5 um long, and pleomorphic: cells are irregular rods that characteristically bifurcate into Y- or V-shaped forms with swollen club-shaped poles, the morphology from which the genus takes its name (Latin bifidus, cleft in two) and which becomes more pronounced under nutrient limitation, bile stress or alkaline pH. The envelope is a thick multilayered peptidoglycan sacculus with associated wall polysaccharides and lipoteichoic acid, frequently overlaid by an exopolysaccharide capsule and sortase-dependent or Tad pili that mediate adhesion to mucus and epithelium; there is no outer membrane and no periplasm. The cytoplasm contains a single circular chromosome as an irregular nucleoid (reference strain NCC2705: 2.26 Mb, ~60% GC, with over 8% of coding capacity devoted to carbohydrate metabolism) and 70S ribosomes. Carbon metabolism runs through the fructose-6-phosphate phosphoketolase pathway, the bifid shunt, the genus-defining biochemical marker: two hexoses yield three acetate plus two lactate and five ATP without CO2 release. B. longum subsp. infantis carries a ~43 kb gene cluster that imports intact human milk oligosaccharides through family-1 ABC transporters for intracellular degradation by fucosidases, sialidases, beta-galactosidases and N-acetyl-beta-hexosaminidases, whereas subsp. longum deploys extracellular lacto-N-biosidase (LnbB) and the GNB/LNB import pathway. The resulting acidification suppresses Enterobacteriaceae, the acetate confers epithelial protection against enterohaemorrhagic E. coli O157:H7 in murine models, and acetate and lactate are cross-fed to butyrogenic taxa such as Faecalibacterium prausnitzii and Anaerostipes. B. longum is among the earliest colonisers of the neonatal intestine, can dominate the breast-fed infant faecal microbiota, and persists at lower relative abundance through adulthood. It is a commensal and food-grade probiotic organism; invasive infection is exceptional and confined to severely immunocompromised or preterm hosts.
