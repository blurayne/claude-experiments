## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* is a **unicellular ascomycete yeast** — a full **eukaryote** packed into a single cell of roughly **5–10 µm** across (typically an **ovoid / ellipsoidal** body about 5–10 µm long by 5–7 µm wide, ten times the volume of a bacterium and clearly larger and rounder than a rod). It is the microbe of the bakery, the brewery and the laboratory bench: it does not cause disease in healthy people, it raises bread and ferments beer and wine, and it was the **first eukaryotic genome ever sequenced** (1996, ~12 Mb, ~6 000 genes).

**Growth is by budding, and budding is the visual signature.** A **bud (blastoconidium, daughter cell)** balloons out of the mother's surface, is fed by cytoplasm and one nuclear lobe through a narrow **bud neck**, and is finally pinched off by a **chitin septum**. Cell-surface growth is *polarised into the bud*: in this species the mother stays roughly the same size while the daughter inflates, so a proper plate shows **one clearly smaller daughter attached at a constricted neck**, not two equal halves and not a detached floating sphere. Separation leaves a raised, **chitin-rich circular bud scar** on the mother wall and a single flatter **birth scar** on the daughter. Scars are never re-used, so an old mother carries **several bud scars — typically a handful of raised rings scattered over the surface** (bipolar in diploids, clustered near one pole in haploids); a mother can bud roughly 20–30 times in its lifetime, which is exactly why this organism is also the standard model of cellular ageing. Bud scars are the single best feature for telling this microbe apart at a glance, and they are what a good SEM plate shows.

**Envelope.** The boundary is a **cell wall** about 100–200 nm thick and roughly 10–25 % of the cell's dry mass: an **inner, load-bearing network of β-1,3-glucan cross-linked to β-1,6-glucan and a little chitin**, and an **outer fibrillar layer of highly glycosylated mannoproteins** that gives the surface its slightly furry look in electron micrographs. There is **no peptidoglycan** anywhere — the chemistry is fungal, not bacterial. Just inside sits the **plasma membrane**, whose dominant sterol is **ergosterol** (not cholesterol), studded with the hexose transporters that pull glucose in.

**Interior.** As a eukaryote it must show a membrane-bound **nucleus** (~1.5–2 µm) with a double **nuclear envelope**, nuclear pores and a dense crescent-shaped **nucleolus** occupying part of it; yeast performs a **closed mitosis** — the envelope never breaks down and the spindle runs from a **spindle pole body** embedded in the envelope (there are **no centrioles**). A single large, often dominant **vacuole** (the yeast lysosome) handles storage, degradation and pH/ion balance and is usually the most conspicuous internal void. **Mitochondria** are present as a few tubular/oval profiles (in a well-aerated cell they form a branched tubular reticulum near the cell cortex; under high sugar they shrink and are partly repressed). **Endoplasmic reticulum** exists as a perinuclear sheet continuous with the nuclear envelope plus a cortical sheet hugging the plasma membrane, studded with **80S ribosomes**; free ribosomes fill the cytoplasm. There are also **lipid droplets** and **glycogen/trehalose stores**, and — unlike animal cells — the **Golgi is a scatter of individual unstacked cisternae**, not a neat stack.

**What it does for us.** In glycolysis the cell splits glucose to pyruvate; **pyruvate decarboxylase** then removes CO₂ to give acetaldehyde, and **alcohol dehydrogenase** reduces that to **ethanol**. The **CO₂ is what inflates a bread dough** — the gas is trapped in the gluten network as bubbles and the loaf rises, with the ethanol boiling off in the oven — while in a sealed vessel the ethanol is the point and the CO₂ carbonates the beer or sparkling wine. Notably *S. cerevisiae* ferments **even in the presence of oxygen** when sugar is plentiful (the **Crabtree effect**), which is why a rich dough starts producing gas immediately. The same organism is the workhorse of cell biology — the cell cycle, secretory-vesicle traffic and autophagy were all worked out in it (Nobel Prizes 2001, 2013 and 2016), and it is used industrially to brew, to bake, to make bioethanol and to manufacture recombinant proteins such as insulin and the hepatitis B vaccine antigen.

Sources: [Saccharomyces Genome Database (SGD) — *S. cerevisiae* overview](https://www.yeastgenome.org/), [Feldmann H., *Yeast Cell Architecture and Functions* (Wiley/NCBI)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK6180/), [Klis, Boorsma & De Groot, *Cell wall construction in Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (Yeast 2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16607600/), [Cabib & Arroyo, *How carbohydrates sculpt cells: chemical control of morphogenesis in the yeast cell wall* (Nat Rev Microbiol 2013, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4064749/), [Botstein & Fink, *Yeast: an experimental organism for 21st century biology* (Genetics 2011, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3176092/), [Goffeau et al., *Life with 6000 genes* (Science 1996)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8849441/), [Walker & Stewart, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae in the production of fermented beverages* (Beverages 2016)](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5710/2/4/30).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **No hyphae, no germ tube, no long filaments.** This is the key difference from `candida-albicans` in this atlas: *S. cerevisiae* is drawn as a **budding yeast only**. (Laboratory strains can make pseudohyphal chains under nitrogen starvation, but that is not the archetype and must not appear here.)
- **No mycelium, no branching mould, no conidiophore/spore brush, no fruiting body** — that is the generic `fungus` plate and *Penicillium*, not a single yeast cell.
- **No bacterial features whatsoever:** no peptidoglycan wall, no nucleoid, no plasmids, no flagellum, no pili/fimbriae, no Gram envelope, no 70S ribosomes.
- **No thick polysaccharide capsule** (that belongs to *Cryptococcus*), no slime layer.
- **Do not omit the nucleus** and do not draw it as a bare blob of DNA — the double nuclear envelope plus nucleolus is the whole eukaryotic point.
- **No centrioles and no open mitosis figure** — yeast uses a spindle pole body in an intact nuclear envelope; also no visible chromosomes/spindle in a resting cell.
- **No stacked Golgi apparatus** — yeast Golgi cisternae are dispersed and unstacked; better to omit it entirely.
- **No chloroplasts, no green photosynthetic pigment**, no plant-style single giant central vacuole filling the whole cell.
- **The bud must stay attached at a constricted neck** and be **clearly smaller than the mother**; not a detached floating sphere, not two equal-sized cells, and not a long chain of equal beads (that reads as a pseudohypha or a streptococcal chain).
- **Bud scars belong on the mother's wall only** — raised rings on the outside surface, a handful at most, never a dense honeycomb over the whole cell and never on the fresh daughter (which carries just one flat birth scar).
- **No ascus with four ascospores** inside the cell — sporulation is a separate starvation state, not the vegetative archetype.
- **No sugar crystals, bread, bubbles, beer or props of any kind** in the scientific plates; those belong only to the kids' coloring page.
- No face, no anthropomorphism, no text, no scale bar.

### Colour note

*S. cerevisiae* makes **no pigment**; a single cell is essentially colourless and translucent (a pressed cake of it looks off-white/cream, `#EFE7D6`). All colour in these plates is therefore **teaching convention**, kept consistent across the styles: cell body warm translucent cream-amber, cell wall dusty ochre, plasma membrane teal line, nucleus blue-violet with a darker nucleolus, vacuole pale grey-green, mitochondria red-brown, ER/ribosomes muted teal speckles, bud scars slightly paler ochre rings.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Saccharomyces cerevisiae is the single-celled fungus behind bread, beer and wine, and one of the oldest tools of human technology — we were using it thousands of years before anyone knew microbes existed. Its trick is fermentation: it breaks sugar down to carbon dioxide and ethanol. In a dough the gas is what matters, trapped in the gluten network as thousands of bubbles that lift the loaf while the alcohol evaporates in the oven; in a sealed fermenter the alcohol is the point and the gas puts the fizz into beer and sparkling wine. Baker's, brewer's and nutritional yeast are all the same species, and eating it is entirely normal: it supplies B vitamins and protein and is a routine part of the diet. Unusually for a microbe, it also has a second career in the laboratory. Yeast cells are built like ours — nucleus, mitochondria, membranes, the same basic machinery — but grow in a day and are easy to manipulate, so much of what we know about how human cells divide, ship proteins around and recycle their own parts was first discovered in yeast. Modern strains are also factories: insulin and the hepatitis B vaccine antigen are both produced in engineered S. cerevisiae.  
🇩🇪 Saccharomyces cerevisiae ist der einzellige Pilz hinter Brot, Bier und Wein und eines der ältesten Werkzeuge der Menschheit — wir haben ihn jahrtausendelang genutzt, bevor überhaupt jemand von Mikroben wusste. Sein Kunststück ist die Gärung: Er baut Zucker zu Kohlendioxid und Ethanol ab. Im Teig zählt das Gas, das als tausende Bläschen im Klebernetz gefangen bleibt und den Laib hebt, während der Alkohol im Ofen verdampft; im geschlossenen Gärtank ist der Alkohol das Ziel, und das Gas sorgt für die Kohlensäure in Bier und Sekt. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Saccharomyces cerevisiae is a unicellular ascomycetous yeast, 5-10 um in diameter, that propagates vegetatively by multilateral budding: polarised surface growth into the daughter, cytoplasmic and nuclear transfer through a septin-organised bud neck, and abscission by a chitin primary septum that leaves a persistent chitin-rich bud scar on the mother wall (never re-used, giving a replicative lifespan of roughly 20-30 divisions). The cell wall is a two-layer structure of inner beta-1,3-/beta-1,6-glucan cross-linked with chitin and an outer layer of mannoproteins; the plasma membrane inside it is ergosterol-based. The interior is fully eukaryotic: a nucleus with an intact double envelope that persists through a closed mitosis nucleated by a spindle pole body (no centrioles), a prominent lytic vacuole, a tubular mitochondrial reticulum, perinuclear and cortical ER with 80S ribosomes, dispersed unstacked Golgi cisternae, peroxisomes and lipid droplets. Energy metabolism is facultatively fermentative and Crabtree-positive: at high glucose, respiration is repressed even under aerobic conditions and glycolytic pyruvate is decarboxylated by pyruvate decarboxylase to acetaldehyde and reduced by alcohol dehydrogenase to ethanol, with stoichiometric CO2 release. That CO2 leavens dough; the ethanol is the basis of brewing and winemaking. S. cerevisiae is the reference eukaryotic model organism - the first eukaryote with a completely sequenced genome (1996; ~12 Mb, ~6,000 genes) - and the system in which cell-cycle control, the secretory pathway and autophagy were dissected (Nobel Prizes 2001, 2013, 2016). It is non-pathogenic in immunocompetent hosts, invasive disease being confined to rare opportunistic cases.  
🇩🇪 Saccharomyces cerevisiae ist eine einzellige Hefe aus der Abteilung der Schlauchpilze mit 5-10 um Durchmesser, die sich vegetativ durch multilaterale Knospung vermehrt: polarisiertes Oberflächenwachstum in die Tochterzelle, Übertritt von Cytoplasma und Zellkernanteil durch einen Septin-organisierten Knospenhals und Abschnürung durch ein primäres Chitinseptum, das eine bleibende, chitinreiche Knospennarbe auf der Mutterzellwand hinterlässt (nie wiederverwendet, daher eine replikative Lebensspanne von etwa 20-30 Teilungen). Die Zellwand besteht aus zwei Schichten: innen ein mit Chitin quervernetztes beta-1,3-/beta-1,6-Glucan-Netz, außen eine Lage aus Mannoproteinen; die darunterliegende Plasmamembran ist ergosterolbasiert. Das Zellinnere ist vollständig eukaryotisch: ein Zellkern mit durchgehend intakter Doppelmembran, der eine geschlossene Mitose vom Spindelpolkörper aus durchläuft (keine Zentriolen), eine große lytische Vakuole, ein tubuläres Mitochondrien-Netzwerk, perinukleäres und kortikales ER mit 80S-Ribosomen, verstreute, nicht gestapelte Golgi-Zisternen, Peroxisomen und Lipidtröpfchen. Der Energiestoffwechsel ist fakultativ fermentativ und Crabtree-positiv: Bei hohem Glucoseangebot wird die Atmung auch unter Sauerstoff unterdrückt, glykolytisches Pyruvat wird von der Pyruvat-Decarboxylase zu Acetaldehyd decarboxyliert und von der Alkoholdehydrogenase zu Ethanol reduziert, unter stöchiometrischer Freisetzung von CO2. Dieses CO2 lockert den Teig; das Ethanol ist die Grundlage von Brauerei und Weinbereitung. S. cerevisiae ist der eukaryotische Referenz-Modellorganismus - der erste Eukaryot mit vollständig sequenziertem Genom (1996; ca. 12 Mb, ca. 6.000 Gene) - und das System, an dem Zellzykluskontrolle, Sekretionsweg und Autophagie aufgeklärt wurden (Nobelpreise 2001, 2013, 2016). Bei immunkompetenten Menschen ist sie nicht pathogen; invasive Verläufe bleiben seltene opportunistische Einzelfälle.
