# RNA (ribonucleic acid) (RNA (Ribonukleinsäure)) — render log

**Set:** `genetics` · **Microbe key:** `rna`
**Short description:** The working copy — usually single-stranded, folding back on itself into hairpins and stem-loops rather than pairing with a second strand along its whole length. Ribose stands in for deoxyribose (an extra -OH on the 2' carbon), and uracil stands in for thymine. Three working forms do the job: mRNA carries a gene's transcript from the nucleus to the ribosome, tRNA folds into an unmistakable cloverleaf/L-shape to ferry the matching amino acid, and rRNA folds into the bulk of the ribosome itself.

## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

RNA (*acidum ribonucleicum*, German *Ribonukleinsäure*) is the working copy of the genetic material — chemically close to DNA but different in exactly three ways that this plate exists to show. **First and most important: RNA is SINGLE-STRANDED.** It is not a double helix. A single strand is flexible enough to fold back on itself, so short complementary runs within the SAME strand pair up locally into **hairpins / stem-loops** — a paired double-stranded "stem" capped by an unpaired "loop" where the strand turns around. That local folding is characteristic of RNA and worth showing explicitly, but at every point the backbone is traceably one continuous strand, never two independent strands paired along the molecule's full length the way DNA's are. A render that shows a DNA-style double helix has failed this subject outright, whatever else it gets right.

**Second: the sugar is RIBOSE, not deoxyribose.** Ribose carries an extra hydroxyl group (-OH) on the sugar ring's 2' carbon that deoxyribose lacks — that single extra oxygen atom is the entire reason for the name "ribonucleic acid," and it is mechanistically why RNA is short-lived: the free 2'-OH makes the backbone far more prone to hydrolysis than DNA's, and sterically it also means that wherever RNA does pair up (a hairpin stem, a tRNA arm), the resulting short duplex is forced into a more tightly wound A-form helix geometry, distinct from DNA's B-form.

**Third: the four bases are A, U, G, C — no thymine anywhere.** Uracil (U) takes thymine's place, pairing with adenine much as thymine does, just lacking thymine's extra methyl group.

RNA does its actual work in three distinct working forms, and the strongest composition for this plate shows them together or in sequence. **Messenger RNA (mRNA)** is a long, mostly extended, readable single strand carrying a gene's transcript — read three bases (one **codon**) at a time — from the nucleus, through a **nuclear pore**, to a ribosome. **Transfer RNA (tRNA)** is short (~76–90 nucleotides, ~25 kDa) and folds into the unmistakable **CLOVERLEAF** secondary structure — four base-paired stem-loop arms radiating from a central junction — which then packs further into a compact **L-SHAPED** three-dimensional fold, first resolved by X-ray crystallography of yeast tRNA-Phe in 1974 (Kim et al.; Robertus et al., independently and concurrently). The two tips of the L sit roughly 70–80 Å apart: the **anticodon loop** at one end, a three-base sequence that pairs with a matching codon on the mRNA, and the amino-acid **attachment site** at the other end — the single-stranded 3' tail, ending in the sequence C-C-A in every tRNA, where the matching amino acid is covalently attached. **Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)** is the longest and most extensively self-folded of the three — from ~120 nucleotides (5S) up to ~1,900 (18S) or ~4,700 (28S) — and, together with ribosomal proteins studding its surface, its dense folded mass forms the two unequal subunits of the ribosome itself (this atlas's sibling organelle entry), with rRNA making up roughly 80% of a cell's total RNA by mass.

A single RNA strand's backbone is roughly **1 nm** across, matching this atlas's own scale entry (0.001 µm / 1 nm). A typical mRNA runs to about **500 kDa** (this atlas's scale entry), corresponding to roughly 1,000–2,000 nucleotides, though real mRNAs vary widely in length.

Two viruses this atlas's own copy already names, influenza and SARS-CoV-2, carry their entire genome as RNA rather than DNA — and mRNA vaccines work by delivering exactly this messenger directly, letting a cell's own ribosomes read it without ever touching the nucleus.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **No DNA-style double helix** — two independent, fully paired strands twisted together along the WHOLE molecule. The single unforgivable error for this subject; check explicitly on every render. Local self-pairing (a hairpin stem, a tRNA arm) is correct and expected — a second, separate full-length backbone is not.
- **No thymine.** The four bases are A, U, G, C only — never T.
- **No baked-in base letters or any text** — labels are an SVG layer added afterwards; a sibling agent lost two model escalations to exactly this mistake.
- **No membrane, envelope or capsule** around the molecule — RNA is a bare nucleic-acid strand (and rRNA, part of a bare ribonucleoprotein machine), never inside a lipid bilayer.
- **No smooth, featureless tube/ribbon** with no visible base projections or fold structure — the single-stranded, self-folding character must be visible.
- **No unfolded, straight tRNA** and no double-helix tRNA — it must show the recognisable cloverleaf (2D) or L-shape (3D) fold with a clearly separate anticodon-loop end and amino-acid-attachment end.
- **No three or more strands**, no braided/plait structure — exactly ONE continuous strand, however much it folds.
- **No smooth sphere or geometric solid for rRNA/ribosome material** — it must read as an irregular, knobbly folded mass, consistent with this atlas's ribosome entry.
- **No scale bar, watermark, arrow or caption** baked into the image.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 RNA is DNA's working copy — chemically almost identical, but different in three ways that matter enormously. It carries an extra oxygen on its sugar (ribose instead of deoxyribose), it uses uracil where DNA uses thymine, and, most consequentially, it is single-stranded rather than a double helix. That last difference is what makes it useful as a messenger: a single strand is light enough to be made quickly, shipped out of the nucleus through a nuclear pore, and broken down again once it's served its purpose, all without ever touching or risking the master copy. A strand of messenger RNA does fold back on itself here and there, forming small hairpin loops, but it stays fundamentally one continuous thread — never two strands paired along their length the way DNA's are. RNA doesn't stop at being a messenger, though; it also does the physical work of building proteins. Transfer RNA molecules, each barely 80 letters long, fold into an unmistakable cloverleaf shape that packs down further into a compact L — one end reads a three-letter 'word' on the messenger strand, the other end carries the matching amino acid, ready to be added to a growing protein chain. And the ribosome that does the actual assembly work is itself built mostly of a third kind of RNA, folded and knotted into a dense working machine. This versatility is also why RNA turns up as the genome of choice for several familiar viruses — influenza and SARS-CoV-2 among them — and why mRNA vaccines work at all: they simply hand a cell's own ribosomes a synthetic messenger strand to read, no different in principle from the ones a cell makes for itself every day.  
🇩🇪 RNA ist die Arbeitskopie der DNA — chemisch fast identisch, aber in drei Punkten anders, die enorm viel ausmachen. Sie trägt einen zusätzlichen Sauerstoff an ihrem Zucker (Ribose statt Desoxyribose), sie verwendet Uracil dort, wo DNA Thymin nutzt, und, am folgenreichsten, sie ist einzelsträngig statt einer Doppelhelix. Genau dieser letzte Unterschied macht sie als Botenstoff so brauchbar: Ein einzelner Strang ist leicht genug, um schnell hergestellt, durch eine Kernpore aus dem Zellkern hinausgeschickt und nach getaner Arbeit wieder abgebaut zu werden, ohne je die Originalkopie zu berühren oder zu gefährden. Ein Boten-RNA-Strang faltet sich zwar hier und da zurück und bildet kleine Haarnadelschleifen, bleibt dabei aber im Kern ein einziger durchgehender Faden — niemals zwei Stränge, die über ihre ganze Länge gepaart sind wie bei der DNA. Doch RNA bleibt nicht nur Botenstoff, sie leistet auch die eigentliche Bauarbeit bei der Proteinherstellung. Transfer-RNA-Moleküle, jedes kaum 80 Buchstaben lang, falten sich zu einer unverwechselbaren Kleeblattform, die sich weiter zu einem kompakten L zusammenpackt — ein Ende liest ein Drei-Buchstaben-'Wort' auf dem Botenstrang, das andere Ende trägt die passende Aminosäure, bereit, an die wachsende Proteinkette angehängt zu werden. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 RNA differs from DNA in three defining features: the pentose sugar is ribose (bearing a 2'-hydroxyl absent from deoxyribose), the pyrimidine base uracil replaces thymine, and the molecule is characteristically single-stranded rather than a fully base-paired duplex. The free 2'-OH renders the phosphodiester backbone substantially more susceptible to hydrolysis than DNA's, underlying RNA's comparatively short cellular half-life, and sterically precludes classic B-form base-stacking, so any locally duplexed region (a hairpin stem, a tRNA helical arm) instead adopts A-form helical geometry. Three functional classes predominate in translation. Messenger RNA (mRNA) is transcribed from a DNA template, processed and exported through the nuclear pore complex to the cytoplasm, where it is read by the ribosome in triplet codons. Transfer RNA (tRNA), ~76-90 nucleotides, folds via intramolecular Watson-Crick pairing into the canonical cloverleaf secondary structure (acceptor stem, D-arm, anticodon arm, T-arm), which packs via coaxial stacking of the acceptor/T-arm and D/anticodon-arm helices into the L-shaped tertiary fold first resolved crystallographically for yeast tRNA-Phe (Kim et al. 1974; Robertus et al. 1974; refined to 1.93 A as PDB 1EHZ) -- the anticodon loop and the 3'-terminal CCA amino-acid-acceptor site occupy opposite ends of the L, roughly 70-80 A apart. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA; 5S, 5.8S, 18S and 28S in eukaryotes) constitutes some 80% of cellular RNA by mass and, together with ribosomal proteins, forms the catalytically active core of both ribosomal subunits, with the peptidyl transferase activity itself being rRNA-mediated (a ribozyme function). Several RNA viruses (Orthomyxoviridae/influenza, Coronaviridae/SARS-CoV-2) encode their genome directly as RNA rather than DNA, and synthetic-mRNA vaccine platforms exploit native translational machinery by delivering an exogenous, chemically modified mRNA for direct ribosomal translation, bypassing nuclear entry entirely.  
🇩🇪 RNA unterscheidet sich von DNA durch drei charakteristische Merkmale: Der Pentose-Zucker ist Ribose (mit einer 2'-Hydroxylgruppe, die der Desoxyribose fehlt), die Pyrimidinbase Uracil ersetzt Thymin, und das Molekül ist typischerweise einzelsträngig statt eines vollständig basengepaarten Doppelstrangs. Die freie 2'-OH-Gruppe macht das Phosphodiester-Rückgrat deutlich anfälliger für Hydrolyse als das der DNA, was der vergleichsweise kurzen zellulären Halbwertszeit von RNA zugrunde liegt, und verhindert sterisch die klassische B-Form-Basenstapelung, sodass jede lokal doppelsträngige Region (ein Haarnadel-Stamm, ein tRNA-Helixarm) stattdessen eine A-Form-Helixgeometrie annimmt. Bei der Translation dominieren drei funktionelle Klassen. Boten-RNA (mRNA) wird von einer DNA-Vorlage transkribiert, prozessiert und über den Kernporenkomplex ins Zytoplasma exportiert, wo sie vom Ribosom in Triplett-Codons abgelesen wird. Transfer-RNA (tRNA), etwa 76-90 Nukleotide lang, faltet sich durch intramolekulare Watson-Crick-Basenpaarung zur kanonischen Kleeblatt-Sekundärstruktur (Akzeptorstamm, D-Arm, Anticodon-Arm, T-Arm), die sich durch koaxiales Stapeln der Akzeptor-/T-Arm- und D-/Anticodon-Arm-Helices zur L-förmigen Tertiärstruktur zusammenpackt, die erstmals kristallographisch für Hefe-tRNA-Phe aufgeklärt wurde (Kim et al. 1974; Robertus et al. 1974; verfeinert auf 1,93 Å als PDB 1EHZ) -- die Anticodon-Schleife und die 3'-terminale CCA-Aminosäure-Akzeptorstelle liegen an entgegengesetzten Enden des L, etwa 70-80 Å voneinander entfernt. Ribosomale RNA (rRNA; 5S, 5.8S, 18S und 28S bei Eukaryoten) macht rund 80% der zellulären RNA-Masse aus und bildet zusammen mit ribosomalen Proteinen den katalytisch aktiven Kern beider ribosomaler Untereinheiten, wobei die Peptidyltransferase-Aktivität selbst rRNA-vermittelt ist (eine Ribozym-Funktion). Mehrere RNA-Viren (Orthomyxoviridae/Influenza, Coronaviridae/SARS-CoV-2) kodieren ihr Genom direkt als RNA statt als DNA, und synthetische mRNA-Impfstoffplattformen nutzen die körpereigene Translationsmaschinerie, indem sie eine exogene, chemisch modifizierte mRNA zur direkten ribosomalen Translation liefern und dabei den Zellkern vollständig umgehen.
