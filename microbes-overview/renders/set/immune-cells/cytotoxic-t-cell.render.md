## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The cytotoxic T lymphocyte (T<sub>C</sub>, CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell, also called a CTL or "killer T cell") is a small (~7–10 µm resting, up to ~15 µm once activated) agranulocyte of the adaptive immune system. Like all resting lymphocytes it is dominated by its nucleus: a single, round to slightly indented nucleus fills most of the cell volume, its chromatin heavily condensed into dark-staining **heterochromatin** clumped at the nuclear periphery around a small, often inconspicuous **nucleolus** (the nucleolus enlarges once the cell is activated and ramps up protein synthesis). This leaves only a **thin rim of basophilic cytoplasm** packed with **free ribosomes and polyribosomes** rather than extensive membrane-bound rough ER — resting naive CD8 T cells have modest RER and few Golgi cisternae. A handful of small, ellipsoidal **mitochondria**, a compact **Golgi apparatus**, and a pair of **centrioles** (the microtubule-organizing centre, MTOC) sit near a slight nuclear indentation. The **plasma membrane** carries short **microvilli**, giving the surface a subtly ruffled, villous texture in SEM images, and displays the cell's defining surface complex: the **T-cell receptor (TCR) bound to CD3** together with the **CD8 co-receptor**, which together recognise short peptide antigens presented on **MHC class I** molecules — a display every nucleated body cell carries, which is why a CTL can survey virtually any infected or transformed cell for trouble, unlike the CD4 helper T cell that reads MHC class II on specialised antigen-presenting cells only.

The feature that most distinguishes an activated cytotoxic T cell from other small lymphocytes under the electron microscope is its cargo: dense, roughly spherical **cytotoxic granules** (secretory lysosomes, historically counted at ~20–30 per cell, each several hundred nanometres across) clustered in the cytoplasm, each granule holding a mix of the pore-forming protein **perforin** and a family of serine proteases called **granzymes**. On recognising its target, the CTL polarises its microtubule-organizing centre and Golgi toward the contact point, forming an "immunological synapse," and directs these granules to fuse with the plasma membrane and release their contents onto the target cell in a tightly focused, calcium-dependent burst — perforin helps deliver the granzymes into the target's cytoplasm, where they trigger programmed cell death (apoptosis), killing the infected or cancerous cell while sparing its neighbours. A cortical **actin cytoskeleton** underlies the membrane and remodels dramatically both to form this synapse and, during migration between potential targets, to polarise the cell into an actin-rich leading edge with organelles trailing toward the rear.

Important caveat for the reference image: light microscopy and standard EM **cannot distinguish a CD8 cytotoxic T cell from a CD4 helper T cell or a B lymphocyte** by shape alone — all small resting lymphocytes look essentially identical (large dense nucleus, thin rim of cytoplasm); only an activated CTL's dense cytotoxic granules offer a morphological hint, and even those require good-quality EM to resolve. The CD4/CD8/CD19 identity is fundamentally a surface-marker (flow-cytometry) distinction, not a shape distinction. A textbook/atlas image captioned simply "T lymphocyte" (as used here) is the standard, honest way to depict this cell.

Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf — StatPearls, Histology, Cytotoxic T Cells](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559279/), [NCBI Bookshelf — Immunobiology, T Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27101/), [Kenhub — Lymphocytes: Histology and function](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/lymphocytes), [de Saint Basile, Ménasché & Fischer 2010, Nat Rev Immunol — molecular mechanisms of cytotoxic granule biogenesis and exocytosis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20948548/), [Peters et al. 1991, Cytotoxic T lymphocyte granules are secretory lysosomes (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2022921/), [Wikipedia — Cytotoxic T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytotoxic_T_cell).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — this is a eukaryotic human cell, not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts or large central vacuole** — not a plant cell.
- **Not a biconcave disc and not anucleate** — unlike a red blood cell, the cytotoxic T cell keeps its (very large) nucleus its whole life; do not draw it without a nucleus.
- **Not granular like a neutrophil/eosinophil/basophil** — lymphocytes are agranulocytes with a single round nucleus, not a multi-lobed one; the only "granules" here are the small, dense cytotoxic granules, not the abundant specific granules of a granulocyte.
- **Cytotoxic granules are not lysosomal debris or vacuoles** — draw a modest number (a handful to a dozen) of small, dense, roughly spherical granules clustered toward one side (near the MTOC/Golgi), not scattered randomly or drawn oversized/foamy.
- **Sparse rough ER, not a plasma-cell "cartwheel" nucleus** — a resting/naive CD8 T cell has only modest RER/Golgi (mostly free ribosomes) and a smooth, evenly dense nucleus, unlike the abundant perinuclear RER and clock-face chromatin of a plasma cell.
- **No cilia or flagella for locomotion** — T cells move by amoeboid crawling (actin-driven lamellipodia/uropod), not by beating appendages.
- **Cytoplasm must stay a thin rim** — the defining look of a lymphocyte is "mostly nucleus"; do not draw a large, roomy cytoplasm dwarfing the nucleus.
- A single specimen, not a dense cluster of lymphocytes — individual morphology must stay readable.
- General: no baked-in text/letters/numbers/scale bars/watermarks, no black border/frame/vignette/letterbox, no "sheet lying on a surface" for the watercolor style.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The cytotoxic T cell (CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell, or CTL) is the immune system's precision killer, specialised in eliminating individual body cells that have gone wrong rather than attacking pathogens directly. It only becomes active after a dendritic cell 'primes' it, presenting antigen fragments and often relying on help from a CD4 helper T cell to fully licence the response. Once primed, cytotoxic T cells circulate and survey the surface of virtually every nucleated cell they meet for MHC class I, a molecular display that all body cells use to show a sample of what's inside them. Virus-infected cells and many tumour cells present abnormal fragments this way, marking themselves as targets. On finding one, the T cell forms a tight, focused contact called an immunological synapse and releases its cytotoxic granules — pre-loaded packets of the pore-forming protein perforin and the protease family granzymes — directly onto that single cell, driving it into programmed cell death while leaving healthy neighbours untouched. This targeted killing underlies the body's defence against viral infection and much of its natural surveillance against cancer, and is also the biological principle behind modern CAR-T and checkpoint-inhibitor cancer therapies.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The CD8<sup>+</sup> cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) is an MHC class I-restricted effector cell of adaptive cellular immunity. Naive CD8 T cells require priming by professional antigen-presenting cells (typically dendritic cells) that present peptide-MHC-I via cross-presentation, together with co-stimulatory signals and, for full licensing, help delivered indirectly by CD4<sup>+</sup> T cells (via CD40L-driven DC maturation) or directly by IL-2. Morphologically the resting cell is a small agranulocyte dominated by a heterochromatin-rich nucleus and a thin rim of cytoplasm with modest rough ER and free polyribosomes; activation and differentiation into an effector CTL upregulates biogenesis of dense-core secretory lysosomes ('cytotoxic granules') containing perforin, granzymes (A, B and others) and granulysin. TCR-CD3 engagement of peptide-MHC-I, co-receptor stabilisation by CD8, and integrin (LFA-1/ICAM-1)-mediated adhesion drive formation of an immunological synapse, polarising the microtubule-organising centre and Golgi toward the contact zone and directing calcium-dependent, SNARE-mediated granule exocytosis onto the target. Perforin oligomerises in the target membrane to form pores that facilitate granzyme entry into the cytosol, where granzyme B activates caspase-dependent (and caspase-independent) apoptotic pathways; CTLs can also induce death via Fas ligand (FasL)-Fas signalling. This serial, polarised killing allows a single CTL to eliminate multiple targets sequentially while sparing untargeted bystander cells, and underlies antiviral immunity, tumour immunosurveillance, and the mechanism exploited by checkpoint-inhibitor and CAR-T immunotherapies.  
🇩🇪 Die CD8<sup>+</sup>-zytotoxische T-Zelle (CTL) ist eine MHC-Klasse-I-restringierte Effektorzelle der adaptiven zellulären Immunität. Naive CD8-T-Zellen müssen zunächst von professionellen antigenpräsentierenden Zellen (typischerweise dendritischen Zellen) geprägt werden, die Peptid-MHC-I über Kreuzpräsentation zeigen, zusammen mit kostimulatorischen Signalen und, für die vollständige Lizenzierung, Hilfe, die indirekt über CD4<sup>+</sup>-T-Zellen (über CD40L-vermittelte DC-Reifung) oder direkt über IL-2 vermittelt wird. Morphologisch ist die ruhende Zelle ein kleiner Agranulozyt mit einem heterochromatinreichen Zellkern und einem schmalen Zytoplasmasaum mit mäßigem rauem ER und freien Polyribosomen; Aktivierung und Differenzierung zur Effektor-CTL steigern die Biogenese elektronendichter sekretorischer Lysosomen ('zytotoxische Granula'), die Perforin, Granzyme (A, B und weitere) und Granulysin enthalten. Die Bindung von TCR-CD3 an Peptid-MHC-I, die Stabilisierung durch den CD8-Korezeptor und integrinvermittelte (LFA-1/ICAM-1) Adhäsion führen zur Bildung einer immunologischen Synapse, polarisieren das Mikrotubuli-Organisationszentrum und den Golgi-Apparat zur Kontaktzone hin und steuern die kalziumabhängige, SNARE-vermittelte Exozytose der Granula gezielt auf die Zielzelle. Perforin oligomerisiert in der Zielmembran zu Poren, die den Granzymen den Eintritt ins Zytosol erleichtern, wo Granzym B caspaseabhängige (und caspaseunabhängige) Apoptosewege aktiviert; CTLs können den Zelltod auch über Fas-Ligand (FasL)-Fas-Signalgebung auslösen. Diese serielle, polarisierte Tötung erlaubt es einer einzelnen CTL, mehrere Zielzellen nacheinander zu eliminieren, ohne unbeteiligte Nachbarzellen zu schädigen, und ist die Grundlage der antiviralen Immunität, der Tumorimmunüberwachung sowie des Wirkprinzips von Checkpoint-Inhibitor- und CAR-T-Immuntherapien.
