## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

***Amoeba proteus*** is a **naked lobose amoeba** (Amoebozoa: Tubulinea: Euamoebida, family Amoebidae) of fresh water and wet *Sphagnum*. It is enormous for a single cell: the locomotive form measures roughly **220–760 µm** in length (mean ≈ 425 µm; the usual teaching figure is 250–750 µm), which puts it right at the limit of naked-eye visibility — in a drop of pond water it can be spotted as a pale, slowly shifting speck. It was named after **Proteus**, the shape-shifting sea god, because it has **no fixed shape at all**: the outline changes continuously from second to second.

The cell is bounded by **nothing but a plasma membrane** and a thin glycocalyx — there is no cell wall, no shell and no test. Just inside the membrane lies a narrow band of **clear, glassy, colourless ectoplasm** (hyaloplasm); it is thickest at the advancing tip of a pseudopod, where it forms the **hyaline cap**. Inside that sits the **granular endoplasm**, visibly grainy with mitochondria, lipid droplets and characteristic **truncated bipyramidal or plate-shaped crystals** (~2–4 µm).

Locomotion is **actin-driven sol–gel cytoplasmic streaming**. The cell is usually **polypodial** — several blunt, sub-cylindrical **lobopodia** with rounded tips push out in different directions, normally with one dominant leading pseudopod. The gelated peripheral ectoplasm forms a semi-rigid tube; fluid granular endoplasm streams forward inside it, everts at the hyaline front and gelates again at the periphery, while the trailing end is resorbed. That trailing end is the **uroid**, a wrinkled, mulberry-like (*morulate*) region where membrane is internalised again — the feature that distinguishes *A. proteus* from the finger-shaped uroid of *Polychaos*. Under a microscope you can literally watch the granules flow forward; the cell can travel up to about 25 mm per hour.

There is exactly **one nucleus**: a single, flattened, **discoid** (often biconcave, red-blood-cell-like) vesicular nucleus, 22–62 µm across (mean 38–47 µm), finely granular with scattered nucleolar material. A single conspicuous **contractile vacuole** — a clear round bubble, usually in the rear half of the endoplasm near the uroid — repeatedly fills from small feeder vacuoles and discharges at the cell surface. In strongly hypotonic fresh water, water leaks in osmotically all the time, and this vacuole is the cell's bilge pump.

Feeding is **phagotrophic** and is the reason this organism is taught at all. Pseudopods flow *around* a ciliate, a flagellate, a diatom or an algal cell (circumfluence) until their membranes meet and fuse, enclosing the prey in a **food vacuole** (phagosome). The vacuole acidifies, fuses with lysosomes, digests the prey, and undigested residue is egested near the uroid. A crawling, engulfing cell is exactly what a **macrophage** or a **neutrophil** in our own body does — the mechanisms of amoeboid motility and phagocytosis are homologous, and phagocytosis originated as a *feeding* mechanism in wall-less single-celled eukaryotes long before it was an immune function. Reproduction is asexual (mitosis then binary fission); no sexual cycle is established.

**Medical framing — important.** *Amoeba proteus* is **non-pathogenic and of no medical significance**. It is a pond and classroom organism. It must not be conflated with ***Entamoeba histolytica***, the much smaller (~15–50 µm) gut parasite that causes amoebic dysentery and liver abscess, nor with the rare brain-infecting *Naegleria fowleri*. Its place in this set is as the archetypal **eukaryotic single cell**, not as a disease agent.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** and no rigid envelope of any kind — the cell is *naked*, bounded only by a plasma membrane. This is the single most common mistake.
- **No flagella and no cilia** — it has neither; it moves only by cytoplasmic streaming into lobopodia. No fringe of hairs, no whip, no tail.
- **No shell, test, scales or house** — that would be a testate amoeba (*Arcella*, *Difflugia*), a different thing.
- **No fixed, regular, symmetric or geometric shape** — no circle, no oval, no star with equal arms. The outline must be irregular and asymmetric, and the pseudopodia **blunt and rounded**, never sharp, spiky, needle-like or branching filaments (those are filopodia/axopodia of other amoebae).
- **Not *Entamoeba histolytica*** — do not draw ingested red blood cells, a gut lumen, ulcers or anything pathological. This organism causes no disease.
- **Not more than one nucleus** in the teaching figure (multinucleate specimens exist but confuse the lesson), and the nucleus is a **flattened disc**, not a large round ball.
- **No chloroplasts, no eyespot, no green photosynthetic body** — it is a heterotroph; green only appears *inside a food vacuole* as swallowed algal prey.
- No bacterial features: no nucleoid, no plasmids, no peptidoglycan, no Gram envelope. It is a full eukaryote with a true nucleus.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Amoeba proteus is the organism almost every school microscopy course starts with — and for good reason. At roughly 250 to 750 micrometres it is enormous for a single cell, big enough that a sharp eye can pick it out of pond water as a pale, slowly shifting speck without a microscope. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Amoeba proteus is a large free-living naked lobose amoeba (Amoebozoa: Tubulinea: Euamoebida) of fresh water, commonly 250–750 µm in its locomotive form and therefore at the limit of naked-eye visibility. The moving cell is polypodial: several blunt, subcylindrical lobopodia with clear hyaline caps extend from a body bounded only by a plasma membrane and a thin glycocalyx — no cell wall, no test, no flagella and no cilia. Locomotion is actin-driven sol–gel cytoplasmic streaming: the peripheral gelated ectoplasm forms a semi-rigid tube through which fluid granular endoplasm flows forward, everts at the advancing hyaline front and gelates again at the periphery, while the trailing uroid is resorbed. The cell carries a single flattened, discoid, granular vesicular nucleus, numerous mitochondria, crystals and lipid inclusions, and one prominent contractile vacuole that periodically fills and discharges at the cell surface, expelling the water taken up osmotically in a strongly hypotonic medium. Nutrition is phagotrophic: pseudopods circumfluent around ciliates, flagellates, algae and other protists until the membranes fuse, forming a food vacuole that acidifies and fuses with lysosomes; undigested residue is egested, typically near the uroid. Reproduction is asexual, by mitosis followed by binary fission; no sexual cycle is established. The species is non-pathogenic and of no medical significance — it is not to be confused with the parasitic Entamoeba histolytica. Its enduring importance is as a model of amoeboid motility and phagocytosis, mechanisms homologous to those used by mammalian macrophages and neutrophils.  
🇩🇪 Amoeba proteus ist eine große, frei lebende nackte Lobosa-Amöbe (Amoebozoa: Tubulinea: Euamoebida) des Süßwassers, in der Fortbewegungsform meist 250–750 µm groß und damit an der Grenze der Sichtbarkeit mit bloßem Auge.
