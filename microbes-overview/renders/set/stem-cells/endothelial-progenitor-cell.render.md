## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

An endothelial progenitor cell (EPC) is a rare, bone-marrow-derived circulating cell first described by Asahara et al. (1997) as a CD34+ mononuclear-cell fraction capable of forming vascular structures in vitro and incorporating into neovessels in vivo. Under physiological or ischaemic/wound stimuli (hypoxia, tissue injury, exercise), VEGF and other angiogenic cytokines (SDF-1/CXCR4, G-CSF, erythropoietin) mobilise EPCs out of the bone-marrow niche into peripheral blood; they then home to sites of vascular damage or active angiogenesis, adhere to the exposed subendothelium, and either directly incorporate into the vessel wall or (more commonly, per current understanding) act paracrinely by secreting pro-angiogenic growth factors that support resident endothelial cells. Early EPCs express the progenitor markers CD34, CD133 (prominin-1) and VEGFR-2 (KDR/Flk-1); as they differentiate toward a mature endothelial phenotype they lose CD133 and gain VE-cadherin (CD144), PECAM-1 (CD31) and von Willebrand factor (vWF), and take up acetylated LDL — the classic in-vitro identification triad. Morphologically an EPC/early endothelial-lineage cell is a flattened, adherent cell that is more elongated and motile than a mature cobblestone-shaped endothelial cell: a spindle or fan-shaped body with a broad leading lamellipodium and fine filopodia at the migrating front, a single ovoid nucleus (often somewhat eccentric), a well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus (supporting active protein secretion and surface-receptor turnover), numerous mitochondria (energy for migration), a prominent actin cytoskeleton (cortical actin plus stress fibres that drive crawling and adhesion via focal contacts), and — as the cell matures along the endothelial lineage — Weibel–Palade bodies: elongated, rod-shaped, membrane-bound secretory granules unique to endothelial cells that store von Willebrand factor and P-selectin for regulated release during haemostasis and inflammation.

Sources: [Asahara et al. 1997, Isolation of Putative Progenitor Endothelial Cells for Angiogenesis, Science (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9020076/), [Wikipedia — Endothelial progenitor cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endothelial_progenitor_cell), [Medina et al. 2017, Endothelial Progenitors: A Consensus Statement on Nomenclature (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5442856/), [Wikipedia — Weibel–Palade body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibel%E2%80%93Palade_body), [Medina et al. 2020, Endothelial Progenitor Cells: Characterization, Past, Present, and Future (PMC9318195)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9318195/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts** — not a plant cell.
- **No large central vacuole** — that is a plant-cell feature.
- **Not round/biconcave like a red blood cell** — no haemoglobin colour, no anucleate disc shape; this is a nucleated, adherent, migrating cell.
- **Not a dense-granule/alpha-granule cell like a platelet** — do not draw platelet-style storage granules; the distinguishing endothelial-lineage granule is the elongated rod-shaped Weibel–Palade body, drawn as a FEW discrete rods, not a dense scatter of round granules.
- **No motile cilia or flagella for locomotion** — the cell moves by actin-driven crawling (lamellipodium/filopodia), not by beating appendages; if a primary cilium is shown at all it must be a single short non-motile stub, not multiple beating cilia (and it is commonly omitted/resorbed in an actively migrating cell, so prefer to omit it).
- Single specimen, not a dense confluent monolayer — individual morphology must stay readable.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The endothelial progenitor cell (EPC) is a young, bone-marrow-derived cell that the body dispatches whenever a blood vessel needs repairing or a tissue needs a new blood supply. In response to VEGF and related growth factors — released, for example, after an injury or during exercise — EPCs leave the bone marrow, travel through the bloodstream, and home in on the site of vascular damage or on tissue that is actively building new capillaries (angiogenesis). Once there, they either integrate directly into the vessel wall or, more often, support nearby cells by releasing their own growth signals, before maturing into full endothelial cells that form the smooth, blood-contacting lining of a vessel. Because they mark and support the body's capacity to grow and repair blood vessels, circulating EPC numbers are widely studied as an indicator of vascular health and regenerative potential.  
🇩🇪 Die endotheliale Vorläuferzelle (EPC) ist eine junge, aus dem Knochenmark stammende Zelle, die der Körper immer dann losschickt, wenn ein Blutgefäß repariert werden muss oder ein Gewebe eine neue Blutversorgung braucht. Als Reaktion auf VEGF und verwandte Wachstumsfaktoren – die etwa nach einer Verletzung oder beim Sport freigesetzt werden – verlassen EPCs das Knochenmark, wandern durch die Blutbahn und steuern gezielt die Stelle einer Gefäßschädigung oder ein Gewebe an, das gerade neue Kapillaren bildet (Angiogenese). **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The endothelial progenitor cell (EPC) is a bone-marrow-derived, CD34+/CD133+/VEGFR-2+ mononuclear cell population, first characterised by Asahara et al. (1997), that is mobilised into peripheral blood by angiogenic cytokines (VEGF, SDF-1/CXCR4, G-CSF) in response to ischaemia, tissue injury, or exercise. EPCs home to sites of endothelial denudation or active neovascularisation, where they contribute to vessel repair and angiogenesis predominantly through paracrine secretion of pro-angiogenic factors, with direct incorporation into the vessel wall now considered a comparatively minor contribution. Morphologically, an EPC differentiating along the endothelial lineage is a flattened, motile, spindle/fan-shaped cell with a broad leading lamellipodium and filopodia driven by cortical and stress-fibre actin, a single eccentric nucleus, extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi supporting active receptor and secretory-protein turnover, and, as maturation proceeds, Weibel-Palade bodies — rod-shaped, endothelial-lineage-specific secretory organelles that store von Willebrand factor and P-selectin for regulated exocytosis. Progressive loss of CD133 and acquisition of VE-cadherin (CD144), PECAM-1 (CD31) and von Willebrand factor mark the transition from progenitor to mature endothelial phenotype; circulating EPC number and function are studied as biomarkers of vascular regenerative capacity in cardiovascular disease.  
🇩🇪 Die endotheliale Vorläuferzelle (EPC) ist eine aus dem Knochenmark stammende, CD34+/CD133+/VEGFR-2+ mononukleäre Zellpopulation, die erstmals von Asahara et al. (1997) beschrieben wurde und durch angiogene Zytokine (VEGF, SDF-1/CXCR4, G-CSF) als Reaktion auf Ischämie, Gewebeverletzung oder körperliche Belastung ins periphere Blut mobilisiert wird. EPCs steuern Orte einer Endothelläsion oder aktiver Neovaskularisation an, wo sie überwiegend durch parakrine Sekretion proangiogener Faktoren zur Gefäßreparatur und Angiogenese beitragen, während eine direkte Einbindung in die Gefäßwand heute als vergleichsweise geringer Beitrag gilt. Morphologisch ist eine sich entlang der endothelialen Linie differenzierende EPC eine abgeflachte, bewegliche, spindel- bis fächerförmige Zelle mit einem breiten, durch kortikales und Stressfaser-Aktin angetriebenen Lamellipodium und Filopodien an der Vorderkante, einem einzelnen exzentrischen Zellkern, ausgedehntem rauem endoplasmatischem Retikulum und Golgi-Apparat zur Unterstützung des aktiven Rezeptor- und Sekretionsprotein-Umsatzes sowie, mit fortschreitender Reifung, Weibel-Palade-Körperchen – stäbchenförmigen, endotheltypischen Sekretionsorganellen, die von-Willebrand-Faktor und P-Selektin für die regulierte Exozytose speichern.
