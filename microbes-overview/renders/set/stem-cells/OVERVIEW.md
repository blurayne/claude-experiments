## Embryonic stem cell (ESC) (`embryonic-stem-cell`)
Pluripotent cell from the inner cell mass of the early blastocyst: small, with a very large nucleus, a prominent nucleolus and only a thin rim of cytoplasm; can become any of the body's 200+ cell types.

## Endothelial progenitor cell (EPC) (`endothelial-progenitor-cell`)
Young, bone-marrow-derived endothelial cell mobilised into the bloodstream during wound healing and angiogenesis; migrates to damaged vessel wall and differentiates into a mature vessel-lining endothelial cell. Elongated and motile, with a leading lamellipodium, rich rough ER/Golgi/mitochondria, actin cytoskeleton, and rod-shaped Weibel-Palade bodies as it matures. Responds to VEGF and other vascular growth factors.

## Hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) (`hematopoietic-stem-cell`)
Rare bone-marrow stem cell (~7–10 µm), the mother of all blood cells — a small round agranular cell with a large euchromatic nucleus and a thin cytoplasmic rim, that self-renews and gives rise to every blood lineage.

## Induced pluripotent stem cell (iPS) (`induced-pluripotent-stem-cell`)
A body cell reprogrammed by a few transcription factors (Yamanaka factors: OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC) back into an embryonic-like pluripotent state; small, round, with a huge nucleus, prominent nucleoli and immature mitochondria.

## Neural stem cell (NSC) (`neural-stem-cell`)
Precursor of all neural cell types (neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes); a radial-glia-like cell with a single apical primary cilium and a long basal process, high nucleus-to-cytoplasm ratio and a nestin/GFAP intermediate-filament cytoskeleton. In the adult brain only active in a few niches (e.g. the hippocampus).
