## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Bacteriophage T4 (species *Tequatrovirus T4*, family *Straboviridae*) is a large tailed dsDNA virus that infects **only *Escherichia coli***. It has no lipid envelope — the whole particle is naked protein, unlike HIV or influenza in the viruses set.

**Head (capsid).** A **prolate (elongated) icosahedron**, not a sphere and not a regular (equilateral) icosahedron: the two end-caps are built to T=13(laevo) geometry and the cylindrical mid-section to T=20, giving a capsid **~120 nm long and ~86 nm wide** — visibly longer than it is wide, with flat facets and edges (mainly the major capsid protein gp23, reinforced by gp24 at the vertices, Soc/Hoc on the surface). Inside, the **~172 kbp double-stranded DNA genome** is packed so densely under pressure that it fills the head as a tightly coiled mass, not a loose visible double helix.

**Neck / collar.** A short connector/collar sits at the one special (portal) vertex of the head, joining it to the tail. This is the anchor point the whole tail assembly hangs from.

**Tail.** A **contractile tail** built from two concentric parts: a rigid **inner tail tube** (gp19) surrounded by a **contractile outer sheath** (gp18) roughly **~100 nm long** when extended (published extended-tail figures run ~92–100 nm; contracted it shortens to under half that). The sheath is built of stacked helical rings of gp18 subunits, giving it a subtly ringed/segmented surface texture. On infection the sheath contracts like a spring, driving the tube through the outer membrane so the DNA can be injected — for the default (resting, pre-infection) pose used here, the tail is shown **extended**, not contracted.

**Baseplate.** At the far end of the tail sits a **hexagonal baseplate** (~50–60 nm across), the six-fold symmetric docking hub that both anchors the six long tail fibres and triggers sheath contraction once the phage is properly attached.

**Tail fibres — the checkable count.** **Six long tail fibres** (~130–145 nm, built of gp34/35/36/37) attach to the baseplate and splay outward and down; each has a **kink partway along its length**, like a bent leg, not a straight spike — this is what gives T4 its lunar-lander silhouette and what the catalogue text calls "landing legs." They make first, reversible contact with the lipopolysaccharide on the *E. coli* outer membrane. **Six short tail fibres** (gp12, ~34 nm) are folded up flat against the underside of the baseplate in the resting state; they only swing down and lock on (irreversible attachment) after the long fibres have bound and the baseplate flexes from hexagon to star shape. Both sets are drawn: six long legs radiating from the baseplate, plus six short stubs folded against it.

**Scale.** Whole particle (head + extended tail, along the long axis) is about **200 nm**. Note on the catalogue's "roughly 1/50th the length of the *E. coli* it infects": published dimensions put T4 at ~200 nm and *E. coli* at a typical ~2 µm (up to ~4–5 µm for an elongated/dividing cell), which works out to roughly a **1:10 to 1:20** length ratio, not 1:50 — I could not find a source supporting 1:50 specifically. None of the four renders depict a full host-cell body (single-specimen framing, matching the rest of the atlas), so this ratio is not something the images assert either way; it is flagged here rather than silently accepted or silently corrected in catalogue text I was told not to contradict.

**Colour.** A single phage particle is colourless protein + DNA — real colour is a staining/EM-false-colour convention, not the organism's own colour (see `reference/colors.md`). Convention used across all four styles: capsid head a cool pale blue-grey, DNA a warm gold/amber coiled mass, collar a neutral grey, tail sheath teal-blue with faint ring striations, tail tube a paler contrasting rod visible through/under the sheath, baseplate a darker navy hexagon, long tail fibres a thin tan/bronze, short tail fibres the same tan but stubbier.

Sources: [ViralZone / SIB — Tequatrovirus T4](https://viralzone.expasy.org/6136), [Rossmann lab / PMC3012670 — Structure and assembly of bacteriophage T4 head](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3012670/), [Leiman et al., Cell 2004 — Three-dimensional rearrangement of proteins in the tail of bacteriophage T4 on infection](https://www.cell.com/cell-metabolism/fulltext/S0092-8674(04)00713-5), [ASM EcoSal Plus — Molecular Biology of Bacteriophage T4](https://journals.asm.org/journal/ecosalplus/molecular-biology-of-bacteriophage-t4-part6), [microbewiki (Kenyon College) — T4 Bacteriophage](https://microbewiki.kenyon.edu/index.php/T4_Bacteriophage).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **A lipid envelope or membrane** — T4 is naked protein; no bilayer anywhere on the particle.
- **A spherical or perfectly regular (equilateral) icosahedral head** — it must read as visibly elongated (prolate), longer than it is wide, with flat facets/edges, not a smooth ball or a smooth ellipsoid blob.
- **Any number of long tail fibres other than six** — six splayed legs, each with a visible kink/elbow, not straight spikes and not a starburst that fuses into the outline.
- **A single undifferentiated tail rod** — the tail must show two distinct concentric parts, an outer sheath and an inner tube.
- **A contracted/star-shaped baseplate** — default pose is the resting, pre-infection state: tail extended, baseplate hexagonal, short fibres folded up (not swung down).
- **DNA drawn as a loose visible double helix spilling out** — it is packed too densely to resolve as helix; show it as a dense coiled/tangled mass filling the head.
- **A face, eyes, or any anthropomorphism** — despite the "lunar lander" nickname, this is a protein machine, not a character.
- **Bacterial anatomy on the phage itself** — no cell wall, no flagella, no pili; those belong to the *E. coli* host, not the phage.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 T4 is one of the best-studied viruses on Earth, and it looks like nothing else in this atlas: an elongated, twenty-sided head on a spring-loaded tail, standing on six bent tail fibres like landing legs. It infects only Escherichia coli, never human cells, so it poses no threat to people at all — in fact its harmlessness to us is exactly why it has become a workhorse of molecular biology, from the classic experiments that first proved DNA (not protein) carries genetic information to today's phage-display and phage-therapy research. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Bacteriophage T4 (Tequatrovirus T4, family Straboviridae) is a large, non-enveloped, tailed dsDNA virus obligately lytic for Escherichia coli. The virion comprises a prolate icosahedral capsid (T=13(laevo)/T=20, ~120 x 86 nm) enclosing a ~172 kbp genome, connected via a neck/collar to a contractile tail (inner tube surrounded by a sheath of stacked gp18 rings) terminating in a hexameric baseplate. Six long tail fibres (gp34-37) mediate initial, reversible attachment to lipopolysaccharide on the host outer membrane; this triggers a baseplate conformational change from hexagonal to star-shaped that extends six short tail fibres (gp12) for irreversible binding, contracts the sheath, and drives the tail tube through the envelope to inject the genome. The infection cycle is exclusively lytic, running roughly 20-30 minutes and releasing a burst size of 100-200 progeny virions; T4 never integrates or lysogenizes.  
🇩🇪 Der Bakteriophage T4 (Tequatrovirus T4, Familie Straboviridae) ist ein großes, unbehülltes, doppelsträngiges DNA-Virus mit Schwanz, das obligat lytisch auf Escherichia coli spezialisiert ist. Das Virion besteht aus einem prolaten ikosaedrischen Kapsid (T=13(laevo)/T=20, ca. 120 x 86 nm), das ein rund 172 kb großes Genom umschließt und über einen Hals/Kragen mit einem kontraktilen Schwanz verbunden ist (innere Röhre, umgeben von einer Scheide aus gestapelten gp18-Ringen), der in einer sechszähligen Basalplatte endet. Sechs lange Schwanzfasern (gp34-37) vermitteln die anfängliche, reversible Bindung an Lipopolysaccharid der äußeren Membran des Wirts; dies löst eine Konformationsänderung der Basalplatte von hexagonal zu sternförmig aus, wodurch sechs kurze Schwanzfasern (gp12) für die irreversible Bindung ausklappen, die Scheide kontrahiert und die Schwanzröhre die Hülle durchstößt, um das Genom zu injizieren. Der Infektionszyklus ist ausschließlich lytisch, dauert etwa 20 bis 30 Minuten und setzt eine Burst-Größe von 100 bis 200 Nachkommen-Virionen frei; T4 integriert oder lysogenisiert niemals.
