## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Bacteriophage ΦX174 (species *Sinsheimervirus phiX174*, family *Microviridae*, class *Malgrandaviricetes*) is a non-tailed, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) virus that infects **Escherichia coli** (and related enterobacteria carrying an F pilus is NOT required — ΦX174 uses lipopolysaccharide, unlike the filamentous ssDNA phages that need a pilus). It is the structural and taxonomic outlier of this set: T4 and T7 are both tailed dsDNA phages of class *Caudoviricetes*; ΦX174 belongs to an entirely different lineage (*Microviridae*) with **no tail of any kind** and the smallest capsid and genome of the three.

**Capsid — a T=1 icosahedral shell, no tail.** The mature virion is built from 60 copies each of two structural proteins occupying the same icosahedral lattice: the **major coat protein F** (426 aa, eight-stranded antiparallel β-barrel fold) forms the 20 triangular facets of the shell, and the **major spike protein G** (175 aa) assembles into **12 pentamers — one at each of the icosahedron's 12 five-fold vertices** — that project outward as short, stubby knob-like spikes. There is **no tail, no baseplate, no sheath, no collar/neck** of any kind; the spikes themselves are the whole "business end" of the virion and dock directly onto the host's lipopolysaccharide. Overall particle diameter is about **27 nm** across the faceted shell (spikes add a few nm further out) — by far the smallest phage in this set: roughly **a quarter of T7's ~55–60 nm head** and a small fraction of T4's ~200 nm overall length.

**Minor / internal proteins.** Beneath each spike, on the capsid's inner face, sits the **pilot protein H** (~10–12 copies total across the twelve vertices, not one per vertex at full stoichiometry) — it is thought to plug the channel at the vertex and, upon infection, to extrude through the spike as a transient tube that helps translocate the genome into the host, playing a role loosely analogous to T7's ejectosome core but built completely differently and far smaller. Bound to the genome on the shell's interior is the small, highly basic **DNA-binding protein J** (60 copies, only 37 amino acids), which partially neutralizes the DNA's charge and helps condense it against the inner capsid wall. Two further proteins, the internal scaffolding protein **B** and the external scaffolding protein **D**, are required to assemble the procapsid but are **released and are NOT present in the mature virion** — nothing extra should be drawn inside a finished particle.

**Genome — a closed circle, not a helix.** The genome is **single-stranded, circular DNA of ~5,386 nucleotides** (positive/viral sense) — barely bigger than a single human gene, and only about **an eighth of T7's ~40 kbp** genome. Because it is single-stranded it is chemically **one continuous thread with no complementary partner strand**, closed into a loop with **no free ends** — it must never be drawn as a double helix or as two intertwined strands (that is the double-stranded T4/T7 look, and is scientifically wrong here). Despite its small size the genome is famous for extreme economy: **11 genes packed into overlapping reading frames** (some genes are read in a different frame from within another gene's sequence), a level of genetic compression essentially unmatched among the phages in this set.

**Two firsts.** ΦX174's genome was the **first DNA genome of any organism ever fully sequenced** (Sanger and colleagues, 1977, the paper that also revealed the overlapping-gene phenomenon), and in **2003 it became the first genome of any kind chemically synthesized from scratch** and assembled into an infectious virus (Smith, Hutchison, Venter and colleagues) — an early proof-of-concept for synthetic genomics.

**Scale relative to the set.** ΦX174 (~27 nm) is dramatically smaller than T7 (~55–60 nm head, ~60 nm overall) and T4 (~200 nm overall) — it is the clear odd one out on every axis: smallest, tailless, and single-stranded circular DNA versus the siblings' double-stranded linear DNA.

**Colour.** Same convention as T4 and T7: a single phage particle is colourless protein + DNA, so colour is a **teaching convention**, not the organism's real colour (see `reference/colors.md`). To read as part of the same family while staying visually distinct, the same legend family is reused: capsid shell a cool pale blue-grey (like T4/T7's heads), the twelve spikes a teal-blue (echoing the tail-stub teal of the tailed siblings, repurposed here since ΦX174 has no tail to carry that colour), DNA a warm gold/amber closed loop, the internal pilot protein H a small darker navy shape tucked under each spike (echoing T7's internal-core navy), and the DNA-binding protein J as tiny warm bronze dots studding the interior face of the shell where the genome touches it.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **Any tail, stub, sheath, baseplate or collar** — ΦX174 has none of these; the spikes sit directly on the bare faceted shell, nothing hangs below or behind it.
- **Long fibres, rays or a starburst/sunburst of spines** — the twelve spikes are SHORT, BLUNT, PENTAMERIC TURRETS sitting flush on the shell's corners, not thin radiating spikes; describe them positively (short stubby knobs on the vertices), never as rays, since anything radiating from a hub is one careless word from an unrecoverable starburst.
- **More or fewer than twelve spikes, or an uncountable fused ring of them** — twelve sit at twelve distinct vertices; on any given view several will be on the far side of the shell and simply not visible, and the honest, checkable figure is how many are visible in that specific image, not "twelve" restated from this brief.
- **DNA drawn as a double helix or as two intertwined strands** — this is a single-stranded genome: exactly ONE continuous thread, closed into a loop with no free ends, never two strands wound together (that look belongs to T4/T7's double-stranded DNA and is the single most likely mistake for this subject).
- **DNA outside the capsid, or spilling out of a cutaway** — the genome stays fully enclosed and condensed against the interior wall; only a cutaway view should reveal it, and even then it stays inside the shell boundary.
- **An elongated (prolate) or oversized head** — the capsid is a small, compact, near-spherical icosahedron, not stretched, and is dramatically smaller in impression than T4's or T7's heads.
- **Extra internal shells, a second scaffold layer, or the assembly-only proteins B/D** — those exist only in the transient procapsid during assembly and are gone from the mature virion; draw nothing beyond the single F/G shell, the DNA, and (where a cutaway allows) H and J.
- **A lipid envelope or membrane** — naked protein, like T4 and T7.
- **A face, eyes, or any anthropomorphism** on the science styles (textbook/sem/3d/watercolor) — reserved for the coloring page only.
- **Bacterial anatomy on the phage itself** — no cell wall, no flagella, no pili; those belong to the *E. coli* host, not the virus.
