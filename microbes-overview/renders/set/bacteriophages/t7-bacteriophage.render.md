## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Bacteriophage T7 (species *Escherichia virus T7*, genus *Teseptimavirus*, family *Autotranscriptaviridae*, order *Autographivirales*, class *Caudoviricetes*) is a tailed dsDNA virus that infects **only *Escherichia coli* and close relatives**, exactly like T4. It has no lipid envelope — bare protein + DNA, same as T4. Where T4 is the elaborate "docking-apparatus" phage of this set, T7 is the small, compact one: **the whole particle is only about a third the size of T4's head alone**, and its tail carries no moving parts.

**Head (capsid).** A **regular (non-elongated) icosahedron**, built to a **T=7 laevo** lattice from 415 copies of the major capsid protein gp10, **~55–60 nm in diameter** — round in outline, not stretched like T4's prolate head. The **~39,937 bp double-stranded DNA genome** (~40 kbp, about a quarter the size of T4's ~172 kbp) is spooled inside in roughly six coaxial shells around the central axis, densely packed, not a loose visible helix.

**Connector / internal core.** One icosahedral vertex is occupied by a **connector (portal)** protein through which DNA enters during packaging and later exits during infection. Mounted on its inner face is the **"core"**, a cylindrical internal protein complex (~26 nm long, ~21 nm wide) built from the internal virion proteins gp14/gp15/gp16. Uniquely among the phages in this set, part of this core is **ejected first** and assembles into a transient channel ("ejectosome") that crosses the host's cell envelope so the DNA has a path to follow — T7 has no separate injection machinery, the core *becomes* the injection apparatus for one use.

**Tail — the single most important difference from T4.** A **short, non-contractile tail**, only **~23 nm long** and about 17×8 nm across at rest — a stub, not a tube: think "barely longer than it is wide," not a shaft. Unlike T4's tail there is **no contractile sheath** (no spring-loaded outer sleeve) and **no elaborate hexagonal baseplate docking hub** — T7's tail is structurally simple, just a short conical/cylindrical connector piece between the head and the fibres. (In the instant of infection this stub transiently extends via the core/ejectosome mechanism above, but the resting, pre-infection virion shown here has the short stub, which is the correct default pose exactly as T4's resting pose shows an *extended*, not contracted, tail.)

**Tail fibres — the checkable count.** **Six short tail fibres**, each a trimer of the protein gp17, attached in a ring near the base of the tail stub. Each fibre is **only ~30–32 nm long total** (roughly a fifth to a quarter the length of T4's ~140 nm long fibres) and has a distinct **"L"-shaped kink**: a straight ~16 nm proximal segment nearest the tail, then a kink, then a ~15 nm distal segment ending in a small string of globular beads that contacts the host's lipopolysaccharide. They read as short, stubby, bent legs — not the sweeping long "landing legs" of T4, and there is no second set of short fibres underneath (T4 has two tiers; T7 only has the one set of six).

**Scale.** Whole particle (head + tail stub, along the long axis) is about **60 nm** — roughly **a third of T4's ~200 nm** length, and the head alone is about half T4's head diameter. None of the renders show a full *E. coli* host cell body (single-specimen framing, matching the rest of the atlas).

**Colour.** Same convention as T4 and the rest of the set: a single phage particle is colourless protein + DNA, so colour here is a **teaching convention**, not the organism's real colour (see `reference/colors.md`). To read as one family with T4 while staying visually distinct in *shape*, the same colour legend is reused: capsid head a cool pale blue-grey, DNA a warm gold/amber coiled mass, tail stub a teal-blue (much shorter than T4's ringed sheath, with no visible ring striation since it is not a contractile sheath), tail fibres a thin tan/bronze, and where the internal core is shown in a cutaway, a darker navy cylinder at the head's base — echoing T4's navy baseplate colour for a structure that plays an analogous "engine of injection" role, even though its shape and mechanism are quite different.

Sources: [ViralZone / SIB — Teseptimavirus (T7-like viruses)](https://viralzone.expasy.org/), [Wikipedia — T7 phage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T7_phage), [Wikipedia — Teseptimavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teseptimavirus), [Steven & Trus et al. / PMC4217468 — Capsid expansion mechanism of bacteriophage T7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4217468/), [Molineux lab / PMC2525648 — Evidence for bacteriophage T7 tail extension during DNA injection](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2525648/), [Steven et al. 1988 — Molecular substructure of the gp17 tail-fibre of bacteriophage T7](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/002228368890246X), [EcoliWiki — Phage T7](https://ecoliwiki.org/colipedia/index.php/Phage_T7).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **A long sheathed injection tube like T4's** — the tail is a short stub, barely longer than it is wide; there is no contractile outer sheath and nothing resembling a spring.
- **A hexagonal baseplate or any docking-hub apparatus** — T7 has no baseplate at all; the fibres attach directly near the tail's base.
- **Long "landing-leg" tail fibres** — T7's six fibres are short and stubby (~30 nm), clearly shorter than the tail+head together, not sweeping legs that dominate the silhouette the way T4's do.
- **A second, shorter set of fibres underneath** — T7 has only ONE set of six fibres, unlike T4's two tiers.
- **An elongated (prolate) head** — the head must read as a round, regular icosahedron, not stretched.
- **DNA drawn as a loose visible double helix spilling out** — it is packed too densely to resolve as helix; show it as a dense coiled/spooled mass filling the head.
- **A lipid envelope or membrane** — naked protein, like T4.
- **A face, eyes, or any anthropomorphism** on the science-styles (textbook/sem/3d/watercolor) — reserved for the coloring page only.
- **Bacterial anatomy on the phage itself** — no cell wall, no flagella, no pili; those belong to the *E. coli* host.
- **A starburst of fibres** — the six short fibres must read as individually bent legs around the tail base, never a fused radiating spiky hub.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Bacteriophage T7 (Escherichia virus T7, genus Teseptimavirus, family Autotranscriptaviridae, order Autographivirales, class Caudoviricetes) is a small, non-enveloped, short-tailed dsDNA virus obligately lytic for Escherichia coli. The virion comprises a T=7(laevo) icosahedral capsid (~55-60 nm diameter, 415 copies of gp10) enclosing a 39,937 bp genome spooled around the portal axis, with an internal core complex (gp14/gp15/gp16, ~26 x 21 nm) mounted on the inner face of the unique portal vertex. The tail is a short (~23 nm), rigid, non-contractile structure bearing six short (~30-32 nm), kinked, L-shaped fibres (gp17 homotrimers) that mediate reversible attachment to host lipopolysaccharide; there is no baseplate and no contractile sheath. On infection, part of the internal core is ejected first and assembles a transient channel across the host envelope through which the genome is subsequently transcribed and translocated by host and phage machinery. T7 is exclusively lytic (no lysogeny), with a fast infection cycle (~13 minutes at 37C) driven substantially by its own single-subunit T7 RNA polymerase (gene 1 product), whose promoter specificity and processivity have made it a standard reagent for in-vitro transcription, recombinant protein expression (T7 expression systems) and large-scale mRNA synthesis.  
🇩🇪 Der Bakteriophage T7 (Escherichia-Virus T7, Gattung Teseptimavirus, Familie Autotranscriptaviridae, Ordnung Autographivirales, Klasse Caudoviricetes) ist ein kleines, unbehülltes, kurzschwänziges doppelsträngiges DNA-Virus, das obligat lytisch auf Escherichia coli spezialisiert ist. Das Virion besteht aus einem ikosaedrischen T=7(laevo)-Kapsid (ca. 55-60 nm Durchmesser, 415 Kopien von gp10), das ein 39.937 bp großes, um die Portalachse aufgespultes Genom umschließt, mit einem inneren Kernkomplex (gp14/gp15/gp16, ca. 26 x 21 nm) an der Innenseite des einzigartigen Portal-Vertex. Der Schwanz ist eine kurze (ca. 23 nm), starre, nicht-kontraktile Struktur mit sechs kurzen (ca. 30-32 nm), geknickten, L-förmigen Fasern (gp17-Homotrimere), die die reversible Bindung an das Lipopolysaccharid des Wirts vermitteln; es gibt weder eine Basalplatte noch eine kontraktile Scheide. Bei der Infektion wird zunächst ein Teil des inneren Kernkomplexes ausgestoßen und bildet einen vorübergehenden Kanal durch die Wirtshülle, durch den das Genom anschließend von Wirts- und Phagenmaschinerie transkribiert und eingeschleust wird. T7 ist ausschließlich lytisch (keine Lysogenie) mit einem schnellen Infektionszyklus (ca. 13 Minuten bei 37 °C), der wesentlich von seiner eigenen einzeluntereinheitigen T7-RNS-Polymerase (Genprodukt 1) angetrieben wird, deren Promotorspezifität und Prozessivität sie zu einem Standardreagenz für In-vitro-Transkription, rekombinante Proteinexpression (T7-Expressionssysteme) und die großtechnische mRNA-Synthese gemacht haben.
