## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

**What it is.** A Purkinje fibre — properly a **cardiac conducting myocyte** (*myocytus conducens cardiacus*, Terminologia Histologica) — is **not a nerve cell**. Despite the word "fibre" and despite sharing a name with Jan Evangelista Purkyně's *other* discovery, the cerebellar Purkinje neuron, it is a **striated cardiac muscle cell that has been remodelled into a wire**. It sits at the end of the ventricular conduction chain: sinoatrial node → atrioventricular node → bundle of His → left and right bundle branches → the **subendocardial Purkinje network** that fans out under the endocardium of both ventricles, from the apex upward, before handing the impulse to the working myocardium at Purkinje–muscle junctions.

**Gross morphology.** Purkinje cells are **conspicuously LARGER in diameter than the working ventricular cardiomyocytes around them** — commonly quoted at roughly 2–3× the diameter of an ordinary myocyte (individual cell diameters reported from ~15 µm up to ~40 µm in large mammals, against ~10–20 µm for working myocytes), while being comparatively **short and barrel- or drum-shaped** rather than long and slender. They are joined **end to end into chains and bundles** (the "fibre" of the name is the chain, not a single cell), wrapped in a delicate connective-tissue sheath, and lie **immediately beneath the endocardium** — one or a few rows deep — in the subendocardial layer of the ventricular wall and in free-running false tendons.

**Why it looks pale (the single most diagnostic feature).** The cell has **traded contractile machinery for cable properties**:
- **Sparse, peripherally displaced myofibrils.** What few myofibrils remain are pushed out to a thin ring against the sarcolemma at the cell's rim. The cell is therefore only **faintly and marginally striated** — the cross-striation is present but confined to the periphery, never the dense full-width banding of a working myocyte.
- **A large, glycogen-rich, myofibril-free core.** The bulk of the sarcoplasm is packed with **glycogen**, which dissolves out in routine processing and leaves the interior looking **empty, clear and pale-staining** in H&E — the classic **perinuclear clear zone / clear halo** around the nucleus. Purkinje cells consequently stand out on a slide as a row of **big, pale, washed-out cells** against darker, strongly eosinophilic working muscle.
- **Nuclei:** one or, **frequently, two centrally placed nuclei** (binucleate cells are common), sitting in the middle of that clear zone rather than pushed aside.

**Ultrastructure and electrophysiology.**
- **Few or no T-tubules.** Purkinje cells largely lack the transverse-tubule system (and the associated core dyads) of working myocytes; excitation–contraction coupling instead runs through peripheral junctional sarcoplasmic reticulum. (Species-dependent: rodents show sparse T-tubule profiles; large mammals essentially none.)
- **Abundant gap junctions, dominated by connexin-40 (Cx40)**, alongside Cx43 and Cx45. Cx40 immunoreactivity rises steeply along the conduction axis — roughly ten-fold from AV node to AV bundle, and a further ten-fold from AV bundle to Purkinje fibres — giving very low intercellular electrical resistance. Junctions are numerous and **lateral as well as end-to-end**, so cells couple over broad contact faces; the end-to-end junctional complexes are less regimented than the neat intercalated discs of working muscle, often deeply interdigitated, and carry desmosomes and fasciae adherentes alongside the nexus.
- **Very high Na⁺-channel (Naᵥ1.5) density** gives an action-potential upstroke of ~1000 V/s. Combined with the large cell diameter and the low-resistance Cx40 coupling, this yields the **fastest conduction velocity in the heart, ~2–4 m/s** (measured values around 2.3 m/s are typical) versus ~0.3–1 m/s in working ventricular muscle and ~0.05 m/s through the AV node.
- **Abundant mitochondria**, but proportionally fewer than in the tirelessly contracting working myocyte; the Purkinje cell's job is electrical, not mechanical.
- Purkinje cells retain **latent pacemaker activity** (a slow phase-4 depolarisation) and can take over as a ventricular escape rhythm at ~20–40 bpm if the sinus and AV nodes fail — and, clinically, Purkinje-cell triggered activity is a recognised initiator of ventricular fibrillation.

**Functional payoff (the teaching hook).** Because the network is fast and arborises over the whole endocardial surface of both ventricles almost simultaneously, ventricular activation is a near-synchronous **squeeze from the apex upward** rather than a slow ripple spreading cell by cell from one entry point. The whole ventricular myocardium is depolarised in a few tens of milliseconds. Damage to a bundle branch shows up on the ECG as a widened, notched QRS complex — the ripple returning.

### Do NOT draw

- **NOT a neuron.** This is a muscle cell. **No dendrites, no axon, no myelin sheath, no Nodes of Ranvier, no synapses, no synaptic vesicles, no growth cone, no soma-with-processes silhouette.** The word "fibre" here means a chain of muscle cells, not a nerve fibre.
- **NOT the cerebellar Purkinje NEURON.** Do not draw a flask-shaped cell body with a huge flat espaliered dendritic tree. That is a completely different cell in the brain that merely shares Purkyně's name — drawing it here is the single worst possible failure for this entry.
- **NOT a densely striated working cardiomyocyte.** Do not fill the cell with full-width, edge-to-edge sarcomere banding; the striation must be sparse and confined to the cell's periphery, leaving a pale, clear, myofibril-free core.
- Not slender and thread-like: Purkinje cells are **wider** than the myocytes beside them, not thinner. Never draw them smaller than the working muscle.
- No T-tubule system running deep into the cell (they are largely absent).
- No nucleus squashed flat against the sarcolemma — the nuclei sit **centrally**, in the clear zone.
- No bacterial or plant features: no cell wall, no nucleoid, no plasmids, no chloroplasts, no large central vacuole, no flagellum or cilia.
- No branching, tree-like or star-shaped cell outline (that is the working cardiomyocyte's branching, or a neuron's) — Purkinje cells are blunt, barrel-shaped units stacked end to end in a chain.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The Purkinje fibre is one of biology's neatest bits of repurposing: a cardiac muscle cell rebuilt into a wire. Its ancestors were ordinary contractile cardiomyocytes, but during development these cells shed most of their myofibrils, filled the space with glycogen, grew to two or three times the diameter of their working neighbours, and studded their contact faces with connexin-40 gap junctions — the highest-conductance electrical connections in the heart. The result is a cell that barely contracts and instead conducts at roughly 2 to 4 metres per second, several times faster than working heart muscle. That speed is the whole point. The Purkinje network spreads through the layer just beneath the inner lining of both ventricles, from the apex upward, so the electrical signal reaches the entire pumping chamber almost simultaneously. Both ventricles therefore squeeze as a single coordinated unit rather than as a slow wave creeping from cell to cell, which is what turns the heart into an efficient pump instead of a quivering bag. When part of the network fails — a bundle branch block, for instance — the affected ventricle has to be activated the slow way, through ordinary muscle, and the ECG immediately shows it as a broadened, notched QRS complex. Purkinje cells also keep a faint pacemaker rhythm of their own in reserve, roughly 20 to 40 beats a minute, which can keep a heart going if the normal pacemakers fail; the same excitability is a recognised trigger of dangerous ventricular arrhythmias, which is why the Purkinje system is a target in catheter ablation.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The Purkinje fibre (cardiac conducting myocyte, myocytus conducens cardiacus) is the terminal element of the ventricular conduction system, forming the subendocardial network distal to the bundle of His and its two bundle branches. Morphologically it is a profoundly modified working cardiomyocyte: cell diameter is typically two to three times that of adjacent ventricular myocytes, the cells are short and barrel-shaped and joined end to end into chains invested by a delicate connective-tissue sheath, and one or frequently two nuclei sit centrally. Myofibrils are sparse and displaced to a thin subsarcolemmal ring, so cross-striation is peripheral and faint, while the myofibril-free core is loaded with glycogen; because glycogen is extracted during routine processing, the cells appear conspicuously pale and clear in H&E, with the classic perinuclear clear zone that distinguishes them from the strongly eosinophilic working myocardium. Ultrastructurally the transverse-tubule system and its core dyads are largely or entirely absent (species-dependent, minimal in large mammals), excitation-contraction coupling relying instead on peripheral junctional sarcoplasmic reticulum with L-type Ca2+ channels triggering RyR2-mediated Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release. Electrical coupling is the cell's defining specialisation: gap junctions are numerous, both end-to-end and lateral, and are dominated by the high-conductance isoform connexin-40, whose abundance rises roughly ten-fold from AV node to AV bundle and a further ten-fold from AV bundle to Purkinje fibres, with Cx43 and Cx45 also present. Together with a very high Nav1.5 density giving action-potential upstroke velocities near 1000 V/s, and with the favourable large-diameter cable geometry, this yields conduction velocities of approximately 2-4 m/s, against roughly 0.3-1 m/s in working ventricular myocardium and about 0.05 m/s in the AV node. Functionally, near-simultaneous delivery of the impulse across the endocardial surface of both ventricles converts activation from a slowly propagating myocyte-to-myocyte wavefront into a coordinated apex-to-base contraction, maximising stroke volume; loss of conduction in one bundle branch produces the widened, notched QRS of bundle branch block. Purkinje cells additionally retain diastolic phase-4 depolarisation, providing a ventricular escape rhythm of approximately 20-40 min-1, and their propensity for delayed afterdepolarisations and triggered activity implicates them in the initiation and maintenance of ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation.  
🇩🇪 Die Purkinje-Faser (kardiale Leitungsmyozyte, Myocytus conducens cardiacus) bildet das terminale Element des ventrikulären Reizleitungssystems und damit das subendokardiale Netzwerk distal des His-Bündels und seiner beiden Kammerschenkel. Morphologisch handelt es sich um eine tiefgreifend umgebaute Arbeitsmyokardzelle: Der Zelldurchmesser beträgt typischerweise das Zwei- bis Dreifache benachbarter Ventrikelmyozyten, die Zellen sind kurz und tonnenförmig, End-an-End zu Ketten verbunden und von einer zarten Bindegewebsscheide umhüllt, und ein oder häufig zwei Zellkerne liegen zentral. Die Myofibrillen sind spärlich und an einen dünnen subsarkolemmalen Ring verlagert, sodass die Querstreifung randständig und schwach ausfällt, während der myofibrillenfreie Kern mit Glykogen beladen ist; da Glykogen bei der Routineaufarbeitung herausgelöst wird, erscheinen die Zellen in der HE-Färbung auffallend blass und aufgehellt, mit der klassischen perinukleären Aufhellungszone, die sie vom stark eosinophilen Arbeitsmyokard abgrenzt. Ultrastrukturell fehlen das transversale Tubulussystem und die zugehörigen Dyaden weitgehend oder vollständig (speziesabhängig, bei großen Säugern minimal); die elektromechanische Kopplung erfolgt stattdessen über peripheres junktionales sarkoplasmatisches Retikulum, wobei L-Typ-Ca2+-Kanäle eine RyR2-vermittelte Calcium-induzierte Calciumfreisetzung auslösen. Die elektrische Kopplung ist die eigentliche Spezialisierung der Zelle: Gap Junctions sind zahlreich, sowohl end-an-end als auch lateral, und werden von der hochleitfähigen Isoform Connexin-40 dominiert, deren Menge etwa zehnfach vom AV-Knoten zum AV-Bündel und nochmals zehnfach vom AV-Bündel zu den Purkinje-Fasern ansteigt; Cx43 und Cx45 sind ebenfalls vorhanden. Zusammen mit einer sehr hohen Nav1.5-Dichte, die Aufstrichgeschwindigkeiten des Aktionspotenzials von nahezu 1000 V/s ermöglicht, und der günstigen Kabelgeometrie des großen Durchmessers ergibt dies Leitungsgeschwindigkeiten von etwa 2-4 m/s gegenüber rund 0,3-1 m/s im Arbeitsmyokard und etwa 0,05 m/s im AV-Knoten. Funktionell verwandelt die nahezu gleichzeitige Zustellung des Impulses über die endokardiale Oberfläche beider Kammern die Erregungsausbreitung aus einer langsam von Myozyt zu Myozyt fortschreitenden Wellenfront in eine koordinierte Kontraktion von der Herzspitze zur Basis und maximiert so das Schlagvolumen; ein Leitungsverlust in einem Kammerschenkel erzeugt den verbreiterten, geknoteten QRS-Komplex des Schenkelblocks. Purkinje-Zellen behalten zudem eine diastolische Phase-4-Depolarisation und liefern damit einen ventrikulären Ersatzrhythmus von etwa 20-40 min-1; ihre Neigung zu späten Nachdepolarisationen und getriggerter Aktivität macht sie zu einem wesentlichen Faktor bei Auslösung und Unterhaltung von Kammertachykardie und Kammerflimmern.
