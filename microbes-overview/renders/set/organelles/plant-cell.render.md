## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The plant cell (*cellula plantae*, German *Pflanzenzelle*) closes the organelles set beside the animal cell, and exists in the catalogue **for the contrast**: the same basic organelles, three additions, an entirely different life. The honest classic teaching view is a **cutaway/sectioned cell**, because the subject's whole point — the vacuole's dominance and the thin strip everything else is squeezed into — only shows in section.

**The outline is the first thing to get right, and it is the opposite of the animal cell's.** A rigid **cellulose cell wall** (a primary wall ~0.1–1 µm thick, over a thin middle lamella shared with neighbours) sets a **boxy, rounded-cornered rectangular/polygonal outline** — plant cells cannot crawl or change shape; they stack like bricks. Typical plant cells run **10–100 µm** (a leaf-parenchyma teaching figure: ~40–70 µm, distinctly larger than the ~20 µm generic animal cell). The **plasma membrane** is a separate, single thin line pressed against the wall's inner face — wall and membrane are two different things, always both drawn. **Plasmodesmata** cross the full thickness of the wall as thin cytoplasmic channels (~50 nm in reality; a few subtle threads in the diagram) connecting neighbour to neighbour.

**The interior is dominated by ONE large central vacuole** — often more than 80 % of the volume — bounded by its own single membrane, the **tonoplast**, and filled with optically clear, watery cell sap. Its turgor pressure against the wall is what holds the plant up; wilting is simply this compartment losing water. Nothing floats inside it: the vacuole pushes all the living machinery into a **thin peripheral cytoplasm strip** between tonoplast and membrane.

In that strip, the parts, each following the convention its own atlas subject established:

- **Chloroplasts** — the defining plant organelle: **green** (the one real colour in this subject — chlorophyll), **lens/discus-shaped, 3–10 µm long — clearly larger than the mitochondria**, each with a double envelope and internal thylakoid membranes stacked into **grana — neat coin-stacks** — connected by thin lamellae in paler stroma. Several in view (a real mesophyll cell holds tens to ~100). Like mitochondria, they descend from engulfed bacteria.
- **Nucleus** — one, **pressed to the periphery** by the vacuole (never centred): double envelope with pores, heterochromatin patches at the rim, one membrane-less nucleolus.
- **Mitochondria** — plant cells have **BOTH chloroplasts and mitochondria**: smaller (1–4 µm) beans, double membrane, inner membrane folded as **wide wavy cristae ribbons**. THE distinguishing check of this subject: **coin-stack grana vs wavy cristae curtains, green vs rose, big lens vs small bean.**
- **Rough ER** — dotted cisternae near the nucleus, continuous with the outer nuclear membrane; **Golgi** — a small separate stack (plant cells hold many dispersed dictyosomes; one stands for them); **ribosomes** — thousands of tiny dots; **cytoskeleton** — fine threads.

**Colour:** chloroplast green is real; everything else keeps the set's teaching-convention legend — sand/straw (#D9C79A) wall band, thin teal membrane line, very pale watery-blue vacuole with a slightly deeper tonoplast line, grass-green (#5B8C3E) chloroplasts with darker grana, the set's nucleus blues/amber/purple/terracotta, steel-blue-and-dusty-rose mitochondria, grey-blue dotted ER, dusty sage-green Golgi (greyer and duller than the chloroplasts), warm cream cytosol strip.

**Baked-text danger, stated up front:** like the animal cell, this is one of the most label-contaminated diagrams in the training set — practically every published "plant cell" carries printed callouts. Every prompt therefore states positively that **all surfaces are clean, unlettered biological material**, and verification checks **magnified crops of every region** of every accepted render for lettering.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **No centrioles** — higher plant cells lack them; they belong to the animal cell next door.
- **No lysosomes as drawn bodies** — the vacuole covers the lytic role in the plant teaching diagram.
- **No soft irregular blobby outline** — the wall makes the outline angular; the blob is the animal cell's shape.
- **No crowd of small vacuoles** — ONE dominant central vacuole.
- **No organelles floating inside the vacuole** — its sap is clear; everything lives in the peripheral strip.
- **No green mitochondria and no wavy curtains inside chloroplasts** — coin-stack grana belong to chloroplasts, wavy cristae to mitochondria; swapping them is this subject's defining error.
- **No centred nucleus** — the vacuole presses it to the periphery.
- **No baked-in text, letters, numbers, labels, leader lines, arrows, scale bars or watermarks anywhere** — every textbook version of this diagram carries printed labels, making this the render's dominant failure risk; checked on magnified crops.
- **No metaphase (X-shaped) chromosomes** in the intact nucleus.
- **No bacterial structures** — no flagellum, pili, capsule or nucleoid.
- **No membrane around the nucleolus.**
- **No anthropomorphising** in the scientific plates (faces belong only on the colouring page).

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The plant cell sits in this atlas for one reason: contrast. It carries the full animal-cell inventory — nucleus, ER, Golgi, mitochondria, ribosomes, cytoskeleton — and then three additions that add up to an entirely different way of being alive. The cellulose cell wall trades movement for architecture: no crawling, no swallowing, but the compressive strength that lets a sequoia stand on cells stacked like masonry. The central vacuole, often more than 80% of the volume, is a hydraulic skeleton on the cheap — a bag of watery sap whose pressure against the wall keeps tissue rigid, which is why lettuce wilts when that pressure drops and revives in water. And the chloroplasts are the planet's food supply: solar factories that build sugar from carbon dioxide and water, exhaling the oxygen we breathe. Two details reward a closer look. Plant cells keep both chloroplasts and mitochondria — photosynthesis stores energy in sugar, but burning that sugar back into usable power is still the mitochondrion's job, day and night. And both of those organelles carry their own DNA and descend from free-living bacteria engulfed over a billion years ago — the chloroplast is a domesticated cyanobacterium, still running on its own instruction set. Walled in as they are, plant cells aren't isolated: thousands of fine channels called plasmodesmata pierce the walls and connect each cell's cytoplasm to its neighbours', turning a plant into one continuous, quietly cooperating community.  
🇩🇪 Die Pflanzenzelle steht aus einem einzigen Grund in diesem Atlas: des Kontrasts wegen. Sie trägt die komplette Ausstattung der Tierzelle — Zellkern, ER, Golgi, Mitochondrien, Ribosomen, Zytoskelett — und dazu drei Ergänzungen, die zusammen eine völlig andere Art zu leben ergeben. Die Zellulosewand tauscht Beweglichkeit gegen Architektur: kein Kriechen, kein Verschlucken, dafür die Druckfestigkeit, mit der ein Mammutbaum auf Zellen steht, die wie Mauerwerk geschichtet sind. Die Zentralvakuole, oft über 80 % des Volumens, ist ein hydraulisches Skelett zum Nulltarif — ein Sack voll wässrigem Zellsaft, dessen Druck gegen die Wand das Gewebe straff hält; genau deshalb welkt der Salat, wenn dieser Druck abfällt, und richtet sich im Wasserglas wieder auf. Und die Chloroplasten sind die Nahrungsquelle des Planeten: Solarfabriken, die aus Kohlendioxid und Wasser Zucker aufbauen und dabei den Sauerstoff ausatmen, den wir einatmen. Zwei Details lohnen den zweiten Blick. Pflanzenzellen besitzen Chloroplasten und Mitochondrien — die Photosynthese speichert Energie im Zucker, aber diesen Zucker wieder zu nutzbarer Energie zu verbrennen bleibt Aufgabe der Mitochondrien, bei Tag wie bei Nacht. Und beide Organellen tragen ihre eigene DNS und stammen von freilebenden Bakterien ab, die vor über einer Milliarde Jahren verschluckt wurden — der Chloroplast ist ein domestiziertes Cyanobakterium, das noch immer nach eigener Anleitung arbeitet. Eingemauert sind Pflanzenzellen trotzdem nicht isoliert: Tausende feiner Kanäle, die Plasmodesmen, durchbohren die Wände und verbinden das Zytoplasma jeder Zelle mit dem ihrer Nachbarn — eine Pflanze ist eine einzige, still kooperierende Gemeinschaft.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The generalised plant cell is a composite teaching abstraction: a eukaryotic cell (typically 10–100 µm; the canonical figure is a vacuolated parenchyma cell) enclosed by a cellulose-hemicellulose-pectin primary wall laid over a pectin-rich middle lamella, with the plasma membrane appressed to the wall's inner face and turgor pressure — not a cytoskeletal cortex — as the main determinant of form. Canonical content: one large central vacuole (commonly 50–90% of cell volume) bounded by the tonoplast, functioning in turgor generation, storage, and lytic digestion (the plant counterpart of the lysosomal compartment); chloroplasts (3–10 µm, double-enveloped, with thylakoids stacked into grana linked by stroma lamellae, their own circular genome and 70S ribosomes) performing oxygenic photosynthesis; mitochondria for oxidative phosphorylation; nucleus displaced to the parietal cytoplasm; rough and smooth ER; multiple dispersed Golgi stacks (dictyosomes) which, among standard functions, synthesise wall polysaccharides; free ribosomes; an actin/microtubule cytoskeleton (cortical microtubules orient cellulose deposition); and plasmodesmata — membrane-lined cytoplasmic channels, threaded by a desmotubule of appressed ER, crossing the wall to form the symplast. Higher plant cells lack centrioles; spindle poles organise acentrosomally. Where the simplification breaks: 'the' plant cell exists nowhere — chloroplasts are confined to photosynthetic tissue (root cells and most epidermal cells have none, carrying leucoplasts or amyloplasts instead), meristematic cells are small, wall-thin and hold many small vacuoles rather than one, and woody tissue adds lignified secondary walls the diagram omits; the single drawn Golgi stands for hundreds of motile stacks; and the tidy empty vacuole understates its real load of ions, pigments, and defence metabolites.  
🇩🇪 Die verallgemeinerte Pflanzenzelle ist ein zusammengesetztes Lehrkonstrukt: eine eukaryotische Zelle (typisch 10–100 µm; die kanonische Figur ist eine vakuolisierte Parenchymzelle), umschlossen von einer Primärwand aus Zellulose, Hemizellulosen und Pektin über einer pektinreichen Mittellamelle, mit der Plasmamembran dicht an der Wandinnenseite und dem Turgordruck — nicht einem Zytoskelettkortex — als wichtigstem Formgeber. Kanonischer Inhalt: eine große Zentralvakuole (meist 50–90 % des Zellvolumens), begrenzt vom Tonoplasten, zuständig für Turgor, Speicherung und lytischen Abbau (das pflanzliche Gegenstück zum lysosomalen Kompartiment); Chloroplasten (3–10 µm, doppelmembranig, mit zu Grana gestapelten Thylakoiden, verbunden durch Stromalamellen, mit eigenem ringförmigem Genom aus DNS und 70S-Ribosomen) für die oxygene Photosynthese; Mitochondrien für die oxidative Phosphorylierung; ein in das wandständige Zytoplasma gedrängter Zellkern; raues und glattes ER; zahlreiche verteilte Golgi-Stapel (Dictyosomen), die neben den Standardaufgaben Wandpolysaccharide synthetisieren; freie Ribosomen; ein Zytoskelett aus Aktin und Mikrotubuli (kortikale Mikrotubuli richten die Zelluloseablagerung aus); und Plasmodesmen — membranumkleidete Zytoplasmakanäle mit einem Desmotubulus aus angepresstem ER, die die Wand durchqueren und den Symplasten bilden. Höheren Pflanzenzellen fehlen Zentriolen; die Spindelpole organisieren sich azentrosomal. Wo die Vereinfachung bricht: „Die“ Pflanzenzelle existiert nirgends — Chloroplasten beschränken sich auf photosynthetisches Gewebe (Wurzelzellen und die meisten Epidermiszellen haben keine, sondern Leukoplasten oder Amyloplasten), meristematische Zellen sind klein, dünnwandig und tragen viele kleine Vakuolen statt einer, und verholztes Gewebe legt lignifizierte Sekundärwände an, die das Schema weglässt; der eine gezeichnete Golgi-Stapel steht für Hunderte beweglicher Stapel; und die aufgeräumt leere Vakuole unterschlägt ihre reale Fracht an Ionen, Pigmenten und Abwehrstoffen.
