## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The cell membrane (Latin *plasmalemma* / *membrana plasmatica*, German *Zellmembran* or *Plasmamembran*) is not an organelle with its own genome but the boundary common to **every** cell — bacterial, archaeal and eukaryotic alike. It is a **phospholipid bilayer**, only about **5 nanometres thick** — a human red blood cell, roughly 8 µm across, is already about 1000× wider than its own membrane is thick. Each phospholipid molecule is *amphipathic*: a round, water-loving (**hydrophilic**) **head** trailing two water-fearing (**hydrophobic**) **tails**. Thousands of these molecules self-assemble into **two stacked leaflets, tail-to-tail**: the tails of both leaflets bury themselves against each other in an oily, water-free middle layer, while the heads of both leaflets face outward into the watery compartments on either side — extracellular fluid on the outer face, cytoplasm on the inner face. **This heads-out/tails-in arrangement is the entire reason the structure exists** (the lowest-energy way to hide hydrophobic tails from water), and it is the single detail illustrations most often get backwards.

Embedded in and on this lipid sea sit **proteins**: **integral (transmembrane) proteins** cross the full thickness of the bilayer, exposed on both faces — many act as channels, pumps, carriers or receptors, and a channel protein shows a visible water-filled **pore** running straight through its centre. **Peripheral proteins**, by contrast, attach to only **one** face of the membrane — resting on the surface or bound to an integral protein's exposed part — and never penetrate the hydrophobic core. **Cholesterol** molecules are wedged in among the phospholipid tails, acting as a fluidity buffer: restraining excessive movement at high temperature, preventing rigid packing at low temperature. On the **outer face only**, sugar chains attached to proteins and lipids form the **glycocalyx**, a fuzzy, highly hydrophilic coat that acts as the cell's ID badge — it is what lets NK cells distinguish "self" from "non-self," and exactly the kind of surface feature receptors that viruses such as SARS-CoV-2 dock onto. **This asymmetry — glycocalyx outside, never inside — is what makes the outside the outside**, and it must be respected in every plate.

Crucially, the membrane is **fluid, not rigid**: lipids and most proteins drift laterally within their own leaflet, which is why the 1972 Singer–Nicolson description is called the **fluid mosaic model**. It must never read as a static brick wall, a crystalline lattice, or a solid unbroken shell — the heads–tails–heads sandwich structure has to stay legible, but soft and mosaic-like rather than rigidly gridded.

Historically, high-magnification thin-section **TEM** of a stained membrane shows the bilayer as the classic **"railroad track"**: two dense dark lines (heavy-metal stain bound to the polar heads) separated by a lighter gap (the unstained hydrophobic tails) — this appearance is right at the resolving limit of conventional TEM for a 5 nm structure, and is what originally led to the 1935 Davson–Danielli "sandwich" model before Singer and Nicolson's 1972 refinement. A complementary technique, **freeze-fracture EM**, splits the membrane along its own hydrophobic mid-plane and photographs a fracture *face* rather than a cross-section; transmembrane proteins then stand out as a dense scatter of **intramembrane particles (IMPs)** across that face — a genuinely different but equally valid way of "seeing" the membrane, which is the modality used for this microbe's real-microscopy reference (see §2).

The membrane has **no colour in life** — it is essentially colourless and far thinner than the wavelength of visible light. All colour in these teaching plates (distinct tints for heads, tails, integral/peripheral proteins, cholesterol and the glycocalyx) is a teaching convention chosen purely for legibility, and in particular to make the outer/inner asymmetry (glycocalyx outside only) visually unmistakable.

Sources: [OpenStax Biology 2e — 5.1 Components and Structure](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-1-components-and-structure), [NCBI Bookshelf — "Structure of the Plasma Membrane"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9898/), [OpenIntro Biology — Fluid Mosaic Model / railroad-track TEM history](https://openintrobiology.pressbooks.tru.ca/chapter/unit3-4-1/), this project's own `cells_data.py` catalogue entry (func/deps text, not edited).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **No single layer.** The membrane is always a **bilayer** — two stacked leaflets, tail-to-tail.
- **No tails pointing outward.** Tails must point inward and meet in the middle; heads must face the watery outside and inside. Reversing this is the single most serious, most common error for this subject.
- **No glycocalyx on the inner face.** Sugar chains belong only on the outer face — never on the cytoplasmic side, and never on both.
- **No rigid brick wall or crystalline lattice.** The membrane is fluid — components drift; it must read as a busy but soft mosaic, not an engineered grid.
- **No featureless solid shell.** The heads–tails–heads sandwich structure must stay legible, especially in the textbook/3d cutaways.
- **No bacterial or plant cell wall** (peptidoglycan mesh, cellulose wall, thick capsule) outside the membrane — this is a generic/eukaryotic plasma membrane, not a walled cell.
- **No ribosomes studding the membrane** — that signifies rough ER, a different structure entirely.
- **No integral proteins floating loose on the surface, and no peripheral proteins buried in the tails** — integral proteins cross through; peripheral proteins sit only on one surface.
- **No channel protein without a visible pore** — at least one integral protein must show a clear passage through its centre.
- **No anthropomorphising** in the textbook/sem/3d/watercolor plates (no face, eyes or limbs — that belongs only on the separate coloring-book page), and **no baked-in text, letters, numbers, labels, scale bars, arrows or watermarks**.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The plasma membrane is easy to take for granted precisely because it works so well: a phospholipid bilayer only about five nanometres thick, roughly a thousandth the width of a red blood cell, and yet it reliably keeps the cell's contents in and everything else out. What makes that possible is a simple bit of chemistry — each phospholipid has a water-loving head and water-fearing tails, so thousands of them spontaneously arrange into two back-to-back sheets, tails buried in the middle, heads facing the water on both sides. Nothing about this is rigid. Lipids and most proteins drift sideways within their own layer, constantly, which is why membrane biologists call it the fluid mosaic model rather than a fixed structure — a description that still holds up more than fifty years after it was proposed. Studded through that fluid sheet are the working parts: channels and pumps that move specific ions and molecules across, receptors that pick up signals, and on the outer face only, a coat of sugar chains — the glycocalyx — that functions as a molecular identity card. That asymmetry is not decorative. It is precisely the set of proteins natural killer cells inspect when they scan a cell to decide whether it still looks like 'self,' and precisely the kind of receptor that viruses, including SARS-CoV-2, have evolved to latch onto in order to get in.  
🇩🇪 Die Plasmamembran wird leicht als selbstverständlich hingenommen, gerade weil sie so zuverlässig funktioniert: eine Phospholipid-Doppelschicht von nur etwa fünf Nanometern Dicke, ungefähr ein Tausendstel der Breite eines roten Blutkörperchens — und trotzdem hält sie zuverlässig den Zellinhalt drinnen und alles andere draußen. Möglich macht das eine einfache chemische Tatsache: Jedes Phospholipid hat einen wasserliebenden Kopf und wasserscheue Schwänze, weshalb sich Tausende von ihnen von selbst zu zwei Rücken-an-Rücken-Schichten anordnen, die Schwänze in der Mitte vergraben, die Köpfe beidseitig dem Wasser zugewandt. Nichts davon ist starr. Lipide und die meisten Proteine treiben ständig seitlich innerhalb ihrer eigenen Schicht umher, weshalb Membranbiologen vom Flüssig-Mosaik-Modell sprechen statt von einer festen Struktur — eine Beschreibung, die auch mehr als fünfzig Jahre nach ihrer Formulierung noch trägt. In dieses flüssige Blatt eingelagert sitzen die eigentlichen Arbeitsteile: Kanäle und Pumpen, die gezielt Ionen und Moleküle hindurchschleusen, Rezeptoren, die Signale aufnehmen, und nur auf der Außenseite eine Schicht aus Zuckerketten — die Glykokalyx —, die wie ein molekularer Ausweis funktioniert. Diese Asymmetrie ist keine Verzierung. Genau diese Proteine prüfen natürliche Killerzellen, wenn sie eine Zelle abtasten, um zu entscheiden, ob sie noch als 'körpereigen' gilt, und genau an solche Rezeptoren docken Viren wie SARS-CoV-2 an, um hineinzugelangen.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The plasma membrane is a selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer, ~5 nm thick, common to all cellular life. Amphipathic glycerophospholipids self-assemble via the hydrophobic effect into two apposed leaflets, hydrophobic acyl chains sequestered in a water-excluded core and polar head groups solvated at the two aqueous interfaces (extracellular and cytosolic); this arrangement, together with a laterally mobile complement of integral and peripheral proteins, constitutes the Singer-Nicolson fluid mosaic model (1972), which superseded the earlier Davson-Danielli protein-lipid-protein sandwich model inferred from the trilaminar 'unit membrane' appearance in thin-section TEM. Integral (often polytopic transmembrane) proteins - channels, carriers, pumps, and receptors - span the bilayer via hydrophobic alpha-helical or beta-barrel segments; peripheral proteins associate non-covalently with one leaflet's headgroups or with an integral protein's exposed domain and can be dissociated without disrupting the lipid bilayer itself. Cholesterol intercalates among the acyl chains and modulates membrane fluidity and order in a temperature-dependent, non-monotonic fashion (condensing effect above the phase-transition temperature, fluidising effect below it). The outer leaflet alone carries the glycocalyx - N- and O-linked oligosaccharides on glycoproteins and glycosphingolipids - which mediates cell-cell and cell-matrix recognition, including MHC-independent 'missing-self'/'altered-self' surveillance by NK-cell receptors (e.g. KIR, NKG2D ligands), and serves as the attachment substrate for numerous viral entry receptors (e.g. ACE2 for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein). Leaflet compositional asymmetry (e.g. phosphatidylserine restricted largely to the inner leaflet under ATP-dependent flippase activity, glycolipids restricted to the outer leaflet) is actively maintained and is itself a recognition signal - its collapse (PS externalisation) is a canonical apoptotic 'eat-me' signal.  
🇩🇪 Die Plasmamembran ist eine selektiv permeable Phospholipid-Doppelschicht von etwa 5 nm Dicke, gemeinsam allen zellulären Lebewesen. Amphipathische Glycerophospholipide lagern sich durch den hydrophoben Effekt zu zwei einander zugewandten Schichten zusammen, wobei die hydrophoben Acylketten in einem wasserfreien Kern verborgen sind und die polaren Kopfgruppen an den beiden wässrigen Grenzflächen (extrazellulär und zytosolisch) solvatisiert werden; diese Anordnung bildet zusammen mit einem lateral beweglichen Bestand an integralen und peripheren Proteinen das Flüssig-Mosaik-Modell nach Singer und Nicolson (1972), das das ältere Davson-Danielli-Sandwichmodell (Protein-Lipid-Protein) ablöste, welches aus der trilaminaren 'Unit-Membrane'-Erscheinung im Dünnschnitt-TEM abgeleitet worden war. Integrale (häufig polytop transmembranäre) Proteine - Kanäle, Carrier, Pumpen und Rezeptoren - durchspannen die Doppelschicht über hydrophobe Alpha-Helix- oder Beta-Fass-Segmente; periphere Proteine assoziieren nicht-kovalent mit den Kopfgruppen einer Schicht oder mit der exponierten Domäne eines integralen Proteins und lassen sich ablösen, ohne die Lipiddoppelschicht selbst zu stören. Cholesterin lagert sich zwischen den Acylketten ein und moduliert Membranfluidität und -ordnung temperaturabhängig und nicht-monoton (verdichtender Effekt oberhalb der Phasenübergangstemperatur, verflüssigender Effekt unterhalb davon). Nur die äußere Schicht trägt die Glykokalyx - N- und O-glykosidisch gebundene Oligosaccharide an Glykoproteinen und Glykosphingolipiden -, die Zell-Zell- und Zell-Matrix-Erkennung vermittelt, einschließlich der MHC-unabhängigen 'Missing-self'/'Altered-self'-Überwachung durch NK-Zell-Rezeptoren (z. B. KIR, NKG2D-Liganden), und als Andockstelle für zahlreiche virale Eintrittsrezeptoren dient (z. B. ACE2 für das SARS-CoV-2-Spike-Protein). Die Zusammensetzungsasymmetrie zwischen den Schichten (z. B. Phosphatidylserin größtenteils auf die innere Schicht beschränkt durch ATP-abhängige Flippase-Aktivität, Glykolipide auf die äußere Schicht beschränkt) wird aktiv aufrechterhalten und ist selbst ein Erkennungssignal - ihr Zusammenbruch (PS-Externalisierung) ist ein klassisches apoptotisches 'Friss-mich'-Signal.
