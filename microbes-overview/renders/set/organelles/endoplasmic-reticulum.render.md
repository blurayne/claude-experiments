## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The endoplasmic reticulum (*reticulum endoplasmicum*, German *Endoplasmatisches Retikulum*) is not a sac but a **single, continuous membrane system**: "a netlike labyrinth of branching tubules and flattened sacs" (Alberts) that all interconnect so the ER membrane forms one continuous sheet enclosing **one continuous internal space**, the ER lumen — which alone occupies over 10% of total cell volume, while the ER membrane makes up more than half of the total membrane of an average animal cell. This makes it the largest membrane-bound compartment in a eukaryotic cell, and it begins right at the nuclear envelope: the outer nuclear membrane *is* rough-ER membrane.

**The single most important thing to get right is the CONTRAST between two connected regions of this one network, both present in the same frame.** Near the nucleus, the membrane stacks into flattened, parallel cisternae whose cytosolic face is densely studded with **ribosomes** docked at the Sec61 translocon — this is **rough ER**, where roughly a third of all cellular proteins are threaded into the lumen as they are made (co-translational translocation) and folded/quality-checked by resident chaperones such as BiP before being allowed to move on. Follow the same membrane outward and the ribosomes disappear entirely: it thins into a branching, anastomosing mesh of narrower **tubules** completely free of ribosomes — this is **smooth ER**, home to enzymes (including cytochrome P450 family members) that synthesise phospholipids, cholesterol and steroid hormones, detoxify drugs and other lipophilic compounds, and manage the cell's main intracellular calcium store (as the **sarcoplasmic reticulum** in muscle, triggering and ending contraction). Smooth ER is a comparatively minor component in most cells but is greatly expanded in steroid-producing endocrine cells and, especially, in **hepatocytes**, where it is the machinery that breaks down medicines.

Between the two lies **transitional ER**: a ribosome-free zone containing ER exit sites where COPII-coated **transport vesicles** bud off, carrying correctly folded cargo onward to the **Golgi apparatus** — the sole forward route out of the ER. The Golgi is already its own subject plate in this atlas, so where it appears here at all it is kept small and clearly downstream, not competing for attention with the ER itself.

In thin-section **TEM**, rough ER reads as stacks of 2–8+ closely spaced parallel membrane lines finely stippled with small dark ribosome particles on their outer face, often whorled; smooth ER reads as a bare, rounded tubular network with no such stippling. A real micrograph rarely captures both regions cleanly in one frame — smooth ER is sparse in most cell types and the two regions can sit far apart in the cytosol — so the illustrated styles below carry the rough-to-smooth contrast as an explicit, deliberate teaching device that a single micrograph usually cannot.

The ER is **colourless in life** and TEM is greyscale, so all colour in these plates is teaching convention. The palette used consistently across all four styles here: the same cool grey-blue already used for "rough ER" context on this set's Golgi-apparatus and nucleus plates, a lighter, slightly greener grey-blue for the ribosome-free smooth-ER tubules, dark warm-brown dots for ribosomes, sage green and mint green for the transitional-ER exit zone and its transport vesicles, and muted slate blue — matching the nucleus plate's envelope tone — for the patch of nuclear envelope the rough ER grows out of.

Sources: [Alberts et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell* — "The Endoplasmic Reticulum" (NCBI Bookshelf NBK26841)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26841/), [StatPearls — "Histology, Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum" (NCBI Bookshelf NBK563126)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK563126/), [Dr. Jastrow's Electron Microscopic Atlas — Rough ER / Smooth ER](http://www.drjastrow.de/WAI/EM/EMRER.html), [OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.3 "Eukaryotic Cells"](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells), [Wikipedia — Endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum), [Wikipedia (de) — Endoplasmatisches Retikulum](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmatisches_Retikulum), [Terminologia Histologica Preface (FIPAT/IFAA)](https://ifaa.unifr.ch/Public/EntryPage/PDF/TH%20Preface.pdf).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **Not only one region.** Showing only rough ER or only smooth ER fails the subject — both must appear together, connected, in one frame, with the ribosome-studded-vs-bare contrast unmistakable.
- **No ribosomes on the smooth ER.** They belong only on the cytosolic face of rough-ER cisternae (and, at the frame's edge, the outer nuclear membrane) — smooth-ER tubules must be completely bare.
- **Not a closed bag or single sac.** The ER is one continuous branching network of connected sheets and tubes enclosing one continuous lumen, never a sealed compartment.
- **Not a Golgi stack.** Rough-ER cisternae must read as connected, continuous sheets — stacked, isolated, unconnected discs with gaps between them is a *Golgi stack*, a different organelle (already its own plate in this atlas) and the most likely confusion here.
- **Not free-floating.** A nucleus with no ER attached, or ER with no visible connection to a nuclear envelope, is wrong — the rough ER begins at, and is continuous with, the outer nuclear membrane.
- **No cristae.** The folded inner-membrane shelves of a mitochondrion belong to a different organelle entirely; don't let ER membrane whorls be mistaken for them.
- **No dominant Golgi.** If a Golgi apparatus appears at all, it must stay small and clearly downstream, never competing with the ER for attention.
- **No anthropomorphising** in the scientific plates (no face, eyes or limbs — that belongs only on the colouring page), and **no baked-in text, letters, numbers, labels, scale bars, arrows or watermarks**.

## 3. Audience descriptions (EN + DE)

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet the endoplasmic reticulum — everyone just calls it the ER — the biggest factory floor inside your cells, and it actually has two very different rooms. Right next to the cell's control room (the nucleus), the walls fold into a stack of shelves called the rough ER. It's covered in thousands of tiny helpers called ribosomes, and this is the workshop floor: every protein the cell builds for export gets checked here first, folded up correctly like origami, before it's allowed to leave. Follow the shelves a little further out and the ribosomes disappear — now you're in the smooth ER, the chemistry lab next door. No workbenches full of helpers here; instead this room mixes up fats, cooks up hormones, and keeps a locked supply cabinet of calcium ready for whenever the cell needs it in a hurry. When a protein finally passes its check in the workshop, it doesn't walk out the front door — it gets sealed into a little delivery bubble that buds off and floats away toward the Golgi apparatus, the post office next door, for its next stop. Muscle cells keep an extra-large chemistry lab wrapped all around their moving parts, ready to release calcium the instant they need to flex.  
🇩🇪 Das ist das endoplasmatische Retikulum — kurz ER — die größte Werkshalle in deinen Zellen, und sie hat tatsächlich zwei ganz unterschiedliche Räume. Gleich neben der Kommandozentrale der Zelle (dem Zellkern) falten sich die Wände zu einem Stapel von Regalen, dem rauen ER. Es ist mit Tausenden winzigen Helfern bedeckt, den Ribosomen, und genau hier ist die Werkbank: Jedes Eiweiß, das die Zelle zum Versand baut, wird hier zuerst geprüft und wie Origami richtig zusammengefaltet, bevor es den Raum verlassen darf. **Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The endoplasmic reticulum is easy to underestimate because it doesn't look like one thing — it's a single, continuous membrane that folds itself into two visibly different neighbourhoods doing two very different jobs. Starting right at the nuclear envelope, it stacks into flattened sheets studded with ribosomes: this is rough ER, and it's where roughly a third of all the proteins your cells make get built, checked and folded correctly before they're allowed to move on — a genuine quality-control floor, complete with resident helper proteins that catch and refold anything that comes out wrong. Follow the same membrane outward and the ribosomes vanish; it thins into a branching mesh of tubules called smooth ER, a kind of in-house chemistry lab that builds fats and cholesterol, assembles steroid hormones, and holds the cell's main reserve of calcium, ready to release it in a fraction of a second to trigger things like muscle contraction. In your liver, that same smooth-ER chemistry runs a second, less glamorous job: it's the machinery — enzymes including the well-known cytochrome P450 family — that actually breaks down most of the medicines you take, which is a big part of why drug doses, interactions and dosing schedules matter so much. Whatever gets finished and approved in the rough ER doesn't just drift off; it's packaged into small transport bubbles that pinch off at a dedicated exit zone and head straight for the Golgi apparatus next door.  
🇩🇪 Man unterschätzt das endoplasmatische Retikulum leicht, weil es nicht wie ein einzelnes Ding aussieht — es ist eine einzige, durchgehende Membran, die sich in zwei sichtbar unterschiedliche Bereiche faltet, die zwei ganz verschiedene Aufgaben übernehmen. Direkt an der Kernhülle beginnend, stapelt sie sich zu flachen, mit Ribosomen besetzten Membranblättern: Das ist das raue ER, und hier wird etwa ein Drittel aller Eiweiße gebaut, geprüft und korrekt gefaltet, bevor sie weiterziehen dürfen — eine echte Qualitätskontrolle, komplett mit ansässigen Helferproteinen, die alles auffangen und neu falten, was falsch herauskommt. Folgt man derselben Membran weiter nach außen, verschwinden die Ribosomen; sie wird zu einem verzweigten Netz aus Röhrchen, dem glatten ER, einer Art hauseigenem Chemielabor, das Fette und Cholesterin aufbaut, Steroidhormone zusammensetzt und den größten Calciumvorrat der Zelle bereithält, bereit, ihn in Sekundenbruchteilen freizusetzen, etwa um eine Muskelkontraktion auszulösen. In deiner Leber übernimmt dieselbe Chemie des glatten ER noch eine zweite, weniger glamouröse Aufgabe: Sie ist die Maschinerie — mit Enzymen wie der bekannten Cytochrom-P450-Familie —, die die meisten Medikamente, die du einnimmst, tatsächlich abbaut, weshalb Dosierung, Wechselwirkungen und Einnahmezeitpunkte so wichtig sind. Was im rauen ER fertiggestellt und freigegeben wird, treibt nicht einfach davon; es wird an einer eigenen Austrittszone in kleine Transportbläschen verpackt, die sich abschnüren und direkt zum benachbarten Golgi-Apparat wandern.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The endoplasmic reticulum is a single continuous membrane system enclosing one continuous lumen, occupying over 10% of cell volume and constituting more than half of total cellular membrane. It differentiates into two structurally and functionally distinct, physically continuous domains. Rough ER comprises flattened cisternae whose cytosolic face is studded with membrane-bound ribosomes docked at the Sec61 translocon, mediating co-translational translocation of nascent secretory and membrane proteins into the lumen or membrane; resident chaperones (e.g. BiP/GRP78) and the oxidising luminal environment support folding and disulfide-bond formation, with the ER quality-control system retaining misfolded species for further folding attempts or ER-associated degradation (ERAD). The outer nuclear membrane is itself rough-ER membrane, ribosome-studded and topologically continuous with the perinuclear space. Smooth ER, ribosome-free and tubular, houses enzymes for phospholipid, cholesterol and steroid-hormone synthesis and for phase-I xenobiotic metabolism (cytochrome P450 monooxygenases), particularly expanded in hepatocytes and steroidogenic cells; it is also the principal intracellular Ca2+ store, with SERCA pumps and IP3/ryanodine receptors mediating uptake and release, specialised in striated muscle as the sarcoplasmic reticulum governing excitation-contraction coupling. Between the two domains lies transitional ER, containing ER exit sites where COPII-coated vesicles bud to carry correctly folded cargo to the Golgi apparatus (via the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment), the sole forward route out of the ER for secretory and membrane-protein cargo.  
🇩🇪 Das endoplasmatische Retikulum ist ein einziges, durchgehendes Membransystem mit einem zusammenhängenden Lumen, das über 10% des Zellvolumens einnimmt und mehr als die Hälfte der gesamten zellulären Membranfläche ausmacht. Es differenziert sich in zwei strukturell und funktionell unterschiedliche, physisch aber kontinuierlich verbundene Domänen. Das raue ER besteht aus abgeflachten Zisternen, deren zytosolische Seite mit membrangebundenen, am Sec61-Translokon angedockten Ribosomen besetzt ist, die die kotranslationale Translokation naszierender Sekretions- und Membranproteine ins Lumen bzw. in die Membran vermitteln; ortsständige Chaperone (z. B. BiP/GRP78) und das oxidierende luminale Milieu unterstützen Faltung und Disulfidbrückenbildung, wobei das ER-Qualitätskontrollsystem fehlgefaltete Proteine für weitere Faltungsversuche oder den ER-assoziierten Abbau (ERAD) zurückhält. Die äußere Kernmembran ist selbst raue-ER-Membran, ribosomenbesetzt und topologisch mit dem perinukleären Raum durchgängig. Das glatte ER, ribosomenfrei und tubulär, beherbergt Enzyme für die Synthese von Phospholipiden, Cholesterin und Steroidhormonen sowie für die Phase-I-Biotransformation von Fremdstoffen (Cytochrom-P450-Monooxygenasen); es ist besonders in Hepatozyten und steroidproduzierenden Zellen stark ausgebaut. Zudem ist es der wichtigste intrazelluläre Calciumspeicher, dessen Aufnahme und Freisetzung durch SERCA-Pumpen sowie IP3- und Ryanodinrezeptoren vermittelt wird — in der quergestreiften Muskulatur spezialisiert als sarkoplasmatisches Retikulum, das die Erregungs-Kontraktions-Kopplung steuert. Zwischen beiden Domänen liegt das transitorische ER mit ER-Austrittsstellen, an denen COPII-umhüllte Vesikel abknospen, die korrekt gefaltete Fracht zum Golgi-Apparat transportieren (über das ER-Golgi-Intermediärkompartiment) — der einzige Vorwärtsweg aus dem ER für Sekretions- und Membranproteinfracht.

Let the same painted membrane continue outward, its ink outline narrowing and branching into soft, rounded, interconnected TUBULES (smooth ER) washed in a lighter, slightly greener grey-blue, with the ink stippling (ribosome dots) stopping completely the moment the tubular shape begins — the tubules must be left clean and bare. Near the tubules, paint a small soft sage-green wash where 2-3 small round mint-green vesicle shapes are shown just detaching from the membrane, drifting a little way off (transitional ER / transport vesicles). At the far side of the sheets, paint the membrane flowing without any break into a short stretch of double-outlined nuclear envelope washed in muted slate blue at the edge of the composition, itself lightly ink-stippled with ribosomes, showing the ER's origin at the nucleus. Optionally, small and pushed into a back corner near the drifting vesicles, a faint, low-opacity wash suggesting a Golgi stack (amber-to-indigo) may appear, but it must stay minor and not compete with the ER. CRITICAL: both the ribosome-dotted sheets and the bare tubules must appear together, connected, in the same painting. Never paint ribosome dots on the tubular region. Do NOT paint mitochondrial cristae, a bacterial cell wall/capsule/flagella, or a plant chloroplast. Do NOT paint the ER as a closed sac with no nuclear connection. Single specimen, no duplicates. Absolutely NO text, letters, numbers, labels, scale bars, arrows or watermarks in the image.
