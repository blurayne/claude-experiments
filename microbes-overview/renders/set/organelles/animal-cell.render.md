## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The animal cell (*cellula animalis*, German *Tierzelle*) is this set's **umbrella subject**: not one particular cell type but the floor plan every body cell in the later chapters varies — and the picture where the organelles rendered individually in this atlas finally appear **assembled, in place, in one working whole**. The honest classic teaching view is a **cutaway/sectioned cell** so the interior shows.

**The outline is the first thing to get right.** A typical human cell is about **10–30 µm** across (the atlas's generic figure: ~20 µm) and is bounded **only by its plasma membrane** — a single thin, flexible line, ~5–10 nm thick in reality, drawn as one thin boundary at this scale. There is **no wall of any kind**, which is exactly why the shape is an **irregular, gently lumpy, rounded blob** — never a perfect sphere, never a box. The soft outline (set from inside by the cytoskeleton) is what lets animal cells change shape, crawl, and swallow things; the boxy pressed-together look belongs to the walled plant cell next door in the catalogue.

Inside the cytoplasm, the parts, each following the convention its own atlas subject established:

- **Nucleus** — one, roughly central, the largest organelle (~6 µm): a **double-membrane envelope** (two close parallel lines with a thin gap) interrupted by **nuclear pores**, containing dense **heterochromatin** patches toward the rim, looser euchromatin centrally, and **one prominent, membrane-less nucleolus** as a denser rounded patch.
- **Rough ER** — connected, continuous folded cisternae **studded with ribosome dots**, flowing **directly out of the outer nuclear membrane** (the envelope is not a sealed bag). Distinguishing convention: rough ER = connected cisternae with dots.
- **Smooth ER** — the same membrane system continuing outward as a **dot-free tubular network** (lipid synthesis, detox, Ca²⁺ store).
- **Golgi apparatus** — **one SEPARATE flat stack** of smooth flattened cisternae near the nucleus, with small transport vesicles between ER and Golgi and **vesicles budding from the rims**. Distinguishing convention: Golgi = a separate stack, never merged into the ER's folds.
- **Mitochondria** — **several** in view (standing for the hundreds a real cell holds): bean-shaped, **double membrane** — smooth outer line, inner membrane folded inward as **wide wavy cristae ribbons** like a rippled curtain. Never an empty capsule.
- **Lysosomes** — a few small round bodies with **exactly one membrane line** and a granular, speckled interior; no folds inside (that is what tells them from mitochondria).
- **Ribosomes** — thousands of **tiny dots**, both free through the cytosol and studding the rough ER.
- **Centriole pair** — two short barrels at right angles near the nucleus (the centrosome), the cell's microtubule organiser.
- **Cytoskeleton** — fine filaments threading the cytosol out to the membrane, holding the soft bag in shape.
- **Cytoplasm/cytosol** — the crowded ground substance all of this sits in.

**Colour is teaching convention** (living cells are colourless; TEM is greyscale). The legend keeps the set's established mapping: warm translucent cream/tan cell body; thin steel-blue/teal plasma membrane; nuclear envelope in two blues (outer #6B8CB8, inner #4C6E9E) with amber (#F2B441) pores; dusty-purple (#7B4B8A) heterochromatin, lavender euchromatin, terracotta (#D9622B) nucleolus; cool grey-blue (#9AA7B5) ER (dots only on the rough part); dusty sage-green Golgi stack; steel-blue-skinned mitochondria with warm dusty-rose interiors and pale cristae ribbons; muted mauve lysosomes; tiny dark slate ribosome dots; amber-grey centriole barrels; fine pale cytoskeleton threads.

**Baked-text danger, stated up front:** this is the single most label-contaminated diagram in the training set — practically every published version of "the animal cell" carries printed callouts. Every prompt therefore states positively that **all surfaces are clean, unlettered biological material**, and verification checks **magnified crops of every region** of every accepted render for lettering.

Sources: [Alberts et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell* — "The Compartmentalization of Cells" (NCBI Bookshelf NBK26907)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26907/), [Cooper, *The Cell: A Molecular Approach* — "The Endoplasmic Reticulum" (NBK9889)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9889/) and ["The Golgi Apparatus" (NBK9838)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9838/), [OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.3 "Eukaryotic Cells"](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells), [OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e, 3.2 "The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles"](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles), [Wikipedia — Cell (biology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)), [Wikipedia (de) — Tierzelle](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierzelle), plus this atlas's own organelle subjects (nucleus, mitochondrion, endoplasmic-reticulum, golgi-apparatus, lysosome, ribosome, cell-membrane, cytoskeleton), whose per-structure conventions and palette this assembled plate reuses.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **No rigid cell wall** — the outline is one soft membrane line; a wall belongs to the plant cell and to bacteria.
- **No chloroplasts** — the plant cell's subject.
- **No large central vacuole** — the plant cell's water tank; an animal cell has at most small vesicles.
- **No perfectly spherical or boxy geometric outline** — the cell is irregularly rounded.
- **No baked-in text, letters, numbers, labels, leader lines, arrows, scale bars or watermarks anywhere** — every textbook version of this exact diagram carries printed labels, making this the render's dominant failure risk; all surfaces are clean unlettered biological material, checked on magnified crops.
- **No condensed X-shaped (metaphase) chromosomes** inside the intact nucleus — interphase chromatin is a diffuse mottled mass.
- **No bacterial structures** — no flagellum, pili, capsule or naked nucleoid loop.
- **No membrane around the nucleolus** — it is a density change, not a walled compartment.
- **No Golgi merged into the ER** — the Golgi is a separate stack; rough ER is the connected, dotted system.
- **No anthropomorphising** in the scientific plates (faces belong only on the colouring page).

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The generalised animal cell is a composite teaching abstraction: a eukaryotic cell (typically 10–30 µm) delimited solely by a plasma membrane (a ~5–10 nm phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins; no cell wall), whose shape is maintained by an actin/microtubule/intermediate-filament cytoskeleton rather than by turgor against a wall. Canonical content: one nucleus with a double-membraned, pore-perforated envelope whose outer membrane is continuous with the rough endoplasmic reticulum; rough ER cisternae bearing membrane-bound ribosomes for secretory and membrane protein synthesis; smooth ER for lipid synthesis, xenobiotic metabolism and Ca²⁺ storage; a polarised Golgi apparatus (cis→trans) as a discrete stack receiving ER-derived vesicles and dispatching processed cargo; mitochondria with a smooth outer membrane and a cristae-folded inner membrane hosting oxidative phosphorylation; lysosomes as single-membrane, acidified hydrolase compartments; free cytosolic ribosomes; a centrosome containing one orthogonal centriole pair as principal microtubule-organising centre; and cytosol. Where the simplification breaks: 'the' animal cell exists nowhere — organelle numbers, proportions and placement vary over orders of magnitude between cell types (hepatocytes carry >1,000 mitochondria, mature erythrocytes none and no nucleus either); the tidy cutaway drastically under-crowds the cytoplasm, which in vivo approaches 200–300 mg/ml macromolecule concentration with organelles in near-contact; the Golgi is drawn as one stack though many cells hold dozens interconnected into a ribbon; and endosomes, peroxisomes and the continuous membrane traffic between compartments are omitted from the canonical diagram entirely.  
🇩🇪 Die verallgemeinerte Tierzelle ist ein zusammengesetztes Lehrkonstrukt: eine eukaryotische Zelle (typisch 10–30 µm), allein von einer Plasmamembran begrenzt (eine ~5–10 nm dicke Phospholipid-Doppelschicht mit eingelagerten Proteinen; keine Zellwand), deren Form ein Zytoskelett aus Aktinfilamenten, Mikrotubuli und Intermediärfilamenten hält — nicht Turgor gegen eine Wand. Kanonischer Inhalt: ein Zellkern mit doppelmembraniger, porendurchsetzter Hülle, deren äußere Membran in das raue endoplasmatische Retikulum übergeht; raue ER-Zisternen mit membrangebundenen Ribosomen für Sekretions- und Membranproteine; glattes ER für Lipidsynthese, Fremdstoffabbau und Ca²⁺-Speicherung; ein polarisierter Golgi-Apparat (cis→trans) als eigenständiger Stapel, der ER-Vesikel empfängt und prozessierte Fracht versendet; Mitochondrien mit glatter Außenmembran und cristaegefalteter Innenmembran als Ort der oxidativen Phosphorylierung; Lysosomen als azidifizierte Hydrolase-Kompartimente mit einfacher Membran; freie zytosolische Ribosomen; ein Zentrosom mit einem orthogonalen Zentriolenpaar als wichtigstem Mikrotubuli-Organisationszentrum; dazu Zytosol. Wo die Vereinfachung bricht: „Die“ Tierzelle existiert nirgends — Zahl, Proportion und Lage der Organellen variieren zwischen Zelltypen um Größenordnungen (Hepatozyten tragen über 1.000 Mitochondrien, reife Erythrozyten keines und auch keinen Kern); der aufgeräumte Schnitt unterschätzt die Dichte des Zytoplasmas drastisch, das in vivo Makromolekülkonzentrationen von 200–300 mg/ml erreicht, mit Organellen in Fast-Berührung; der Golgi wird als ein Stapel gezeichnet, obwohl viele Zellen Dutzende zu einem Band verknüpfter Stapel besitzen; und Endosomen, Peroxisomen sowie der ständige Vesikelverkehr zwischen den Kompartimenten fehlen im kanonischen Schema vollständig.
