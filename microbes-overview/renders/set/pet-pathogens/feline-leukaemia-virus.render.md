## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

FeLV (Feline Leukaemia Virus) is an **enveloped *Gammaretrovirus*** (family *Retroviridae*, genus *Gammaretrovirus*) that infects the blood-forming cells of the bone marrow and other rapidly dividing tissues. It spreads through prolonged close contact — mutual grooming, shared food/water bowls and saliva — rather than through fight-bite wounds. It shares the same overall retrovirus building plan as its atlas sibling FIV (envelope → matrix → capsid → 2×RNA + reverse transcriptase + integrase), but belongs to a **different genus with a different genome organisation, different Gag/Pol/Env protein names, and — critically — a different core shape**.

**Size and gross shape.** The virion is **roughly spherical, ~80-100 nm across** (family-level Retroviridae figure; this atlas uses 0.1 µm / 100 nm, already set in `microbe_scale.py`), close to FIV's own ~100-125 nm but built around a fundamentally different internal core geometry.

**The morphology that matters most for this pair of renders.** Per the ICTV Genus: Gammaretrovirus report: *"Virions exhibit a 'C-type' morphology with barely visible surface spikes. They have a centrally located, condensed core. Capsid assembly occurs at the inner surface of the membrane at the same time as budding."* The ICTV Virus Taxonomy Profile: Retroviridae 2021 spells out the genus-level contrast explicitly: the nucleocapsid/nucleoid is *"concentric"* (i.e. centred, roughly spherical) for genera Alpharetrovirus, **Gammaretrovirus** and Deltaretrovirus, but *"rod or truncated cone-shaped"* for genus **Lentivirus** — the genus that contains FIV and HIV. FeLV's core is therefore the textbook "C-type particle": a round, dense, centrally-placed body budding from the plasma membrane, morphologically the OPPOSITE of the FIV sibling render's off-centre cone.

**Three-layer architecture, outside in:**
1. **Lipid envelope** — a host-cell-derived bilayer picked up during budding, studded with **Env glycoprotein knobs**. Each knob pairs a surface subunit (**SU, gp70**) with a transmembrane subunit (**TM, p15E**). gp70 is the main target of neutralising antibodies and defines FeLV's infection subgroups (A/B/C), and is the basis of FeLV vaccines. ICTV describes Gammaretrovirus surface spikes as **"barely visible"** — even shorter/sparser than FIV's already-short spikes — so FeLV's Env knobs should read as small, low studs, never tall stalked clubs and never a dense corona.
2. **Matrix (MA, p15)** — a protein layer lining the inner face of the envelope, encoded (with the rest of Gag) as part of the Pr65Gag precursor.
3. **Capsid / core (CA, p27)** — the single most important, single most checkable feature for distinguishing this render from FIV: a **roughly spherical, centrally-placed, electron-dense body**, never a cone and never a faceted icosahedral shell. p27 is also the standard clinical antigen-test target for diagnosing FeLV infection. Inside the core: **two copies of positive-sense single-stranded RNA**, coated by **nucleocapsid protein (NC, p10)**, plus the enzymes **reverse transcriptase (p80), integrase (p46) and protease (p14)** — all cleaved from the Gag/Pol precursors by the viral protease during/after budding.

**FeLV-vs-FIV as the teaching point.** These two renders sit side by side in the atlas and must be visually distinguishable by silhouette alone: FIV's defining feature is an off-centre CONE; FeLV's defining feature is a centred SPHERE. Both are enveloped, both carry short (not club-shaped) Env spikes, both package 2×(+)ssRNA with RT/integrase — but the core geometry and the Gag/Env protein names (FeLV: p15/p12/p10/p27, gp70/p15E vs. FIV: p15/p24/p13, gp95/gp40) are genus-specific and must not be borrowed from one virus's render to the other's.

Sources (full list with URLs in [`feline-leukaemia-virus.research.json`](feline-leukaemia-virus.research.json)): ICTV Report — Genus: Gammaretrovirus (C-type morphology, centrally-located condensed core, budding-coupled assembly); Krupovic et al., *ICTV Virus Taxonomy Profile: Retroviridae 2021*, J Gen Virol 102:001712, PMC8744268 (family-level virion size/spike length, genus-level "concentric" vs "cone-shaped" core contrast); Ferrero et al., *Feline Leukemia Virus in Cats: A Novel Rapid ELISA Assay for p27 Antigen Detection*, Veterinary Medicine International 2025 (open access, CC BY), PMC12097850 (Gag/Pol/Env gene products: p15/p10/p12/p27, p14/p80/p46, gp70/p15E); Sykes & Hartmann, *Feline Leukemia Virus Infection*, in *Canine and Feline Infectious Diseases* (2014), summarised via PMC7152252 (core composition, p27/gp70/p15E cross-check); CDC Public Health Image Library #5610 (thin-section TEM of FeLV particles, used for morphology corroboration — full licensing detail in §2 below).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **A conical / cone-shaped core** — that is the sibling render FIV (and HIV), both lentiviruses. FeLV is a GAMMAretrovirus with a roughly SPHERICAL, centrally-placed core — the single most checkable, most confusable feature given FIV was rendered immediately before this one.
- **A non-enveloped / naked particle** — FeLV is always enveloped.
- **A faceted, geometric icosahedral capsid** drawn like a soccer-ball/adenovirus shell — the C-type core reads as a smooth, dense, roughly spherical body, not a polyhedron with visible facets.
- **A double-stranded DNA genome inside the virion** — the packaged genome is RNA (two copies, positive-sense); it only becomes DNA after reverse transcription inside the host cell.
- **Long, prominent, mushroom-on-a-stalk spikes or a dense fuzzy corona copied from a coronavirus or HIV render** — ICTV explicitly describes Gammaretrovirus surface spikes as "barely visible"; FeLV's Env knobs must be short and sparse, not tall clubs, not a hairy halo.
- **A bullet silhouette with one flat and one rounded end** — that is rabies (also in this set, a rhabdovirus with a helical core), not FeLV. FeLV is roughly spherical.
- **A cat, a person, or any animal figure** in the four science styles — this is a molecule-scale render of the virion alone.
- **Anything implying human infectivity** — FeLV cannot infect people.

## 3. Audience descriptions (EN + DE)

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 This is FeLV, a germ that only bothers cats. It spreads in everyday cat life — sharing a food bowl, or one cat licking another clean while grooming — so cats who live together can pass it on without meaning to. The good news: lots of cats who meet this germ fight it off completely on their own and are never bothered by it again. The vet has a quick test that checks in minutes, and a jab (a vaccine) that helps keep cats who go outdoors safe in the first place. If your cat goes outside, ask the vet about the jab at the next check-up.  
🇩🇪 Das ist FeLV, ein Keim, der nur Katzen etwas angeht. Er verbreitet sich im ganz normalen Katzenalltag — beim Teilen eines Futternapfs oder wenn eine Katze die andere beim Putzen abschleckt — deshalb können Katzen, die zusammenleben, ihn weitergeben, ohne es zu wollen. Die gute Nachricht: Viele Katzen, die mit diesem Keim in Berührung kommen, werden ganz von allein wieder damit fertig und haben nie wieder etwas davon. Der Tierarzt oder die Tierärztin hat einen schnellen Test, der in Minuten Klarheit schafft, und eine Spritze (eine Impfung), die Freigänger-Katzen von vornherein schützen hilft. Wenn deine Katze nach draußen darf, frag beim nächsten Check-up nach der Impfung.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 A positive FeLV test is unsettling news, and it deserves a straight answer rather than false comfort: FeLV is more serious than its cousin FIV, and cats that go on to develop a persistent infection often do not live as long as an uninfected cat. But the full picture is less frightening than that first sentence suggests. A substantial share of cats exposed to FeLV clear the virus entirely on their own, especially adults with a healthy immune system, and never develop disease from it. For cats that don't clear it, good veterinary care — regular check-ups, prompt treatment of secondary infections, keeping them indoors and away from stress — can still mean years of normal life. A simple blood test at the vet gives a fast, reliable answer either way, and an effective vaccine exists; it's recommended for any cat that spends time outdoors or lives with a cat of unknown status, since grooming, shared bowls and saliva are enough to pass the virus on.  
🇩🇪 Ein positiver FeLV-Test ist eine beunruhigende Nachricht, und die verdient eine ehrliche Antwort statt falschen Trost: FeLV ist ernster als seine Verwandte FIV, und Katzen, die eine dauerhafte Infektion entwickeln, leben damit oft kürzer als eine gesunde Katze. Aber das Gesamtbild ist weniger erschreckend, als dieser erste Satz vermuten lässt. Ein erheblicher Teil der Katzen, die mit FeLV in Kontakt kommen, wird das Virus ganz von allein wieder los — besonders erwachsene Katzen mit einem gesunden Immunsystem — und erkrankt nie daran. Bei Katzen, die es nicht loswerden, kann gute tierärztliche Betreuung — regelmäßige Kontrollen, zügige Behandlung von Folgeinfektionen, ein Leben drinnen ohne unnötigen Stress — trotzdem noch Jahre eines normalen Lebens bedeuten. Ein einfacher Bluttest beim Tierarzt gibt in beiden Fällen schnell und zuverlässig Klarheit, und es gibt eine wirksame Impfung; sie wird für jede Katze empfohlen, die Freigang hat oder mit einer Katze unbekannten Status zusammenlebt, denn gegenseitiges Putzen, gemeinsame Näpfe und Speichel reichen aus, um das Virus weiterzugeben.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 FeLV is an enveloped gammaretrovirus that infects haematopoietic progenitor cells in the bone marrow, along with lymphoid and epithelial tissues. Transmission requires prolonged close contact — mutual grooming, shared food/water bowls and saliva — rather than the fight-bite route typical of FIV; queen-to-kitten transmission also occurs in utero and via milk. Outcome after exposure is graded by the host's immune response: abortive infection (virus eliminated before viraemia, most common in adults with strong immunity), regressive infection (transient viraemia, then immune control with provirus retained latently in bone marrow, no ongoing shedding), and progressive infection (persistent viraemia, sustained antigenaemia, most likely in kittens or immunosuppressed cats), which carries the guarded prognosis and predisposes to anaemia, immunosuppression and FeLV-associated neoplasia (lymphoma, leukaemia). Diagnosis rests on point-of-care ELISA for the p27 capsid antigen, confirmed by PCR when needed. An inactivated or subunit vaccine targeting the gp70 envelope glycoprotein reduces the risk of progressive infection and is recommended for cats with outdoor access or uncertain exposure status.  
🇩🇪 FeLV ist ein behülltes Gammaretrovirus, das hämatopoetische Vorläuferzellen im Knochenmark sowie lymphatisches und epitheliales Gewebe befällt. Die Übertragung erfordert längeren engen Kontakt — gegenseitiges Putzen, gemeinsame Futter- und Wassernäpfe sowie Speichel — statt des bei FIV typischen Bissweges; eine Übertragung von der Kätzin auf die Kitten erfolgt zudem in utero und über die Milch. Der Verlauf nach Exposition richtet sich nach der Immunantwort des Wirts: abortive Infektion (Virus wird vor Auftreten einer Virämie eliminiert, am häufigsten bei erwachsenen Katzen mit starker Immunität), regressive Infektion (vorübergehende Virämie, danach Immunkontrolle bei latent im Knochenmark verbleibendem Provirus, keine fortlaufende Ausscheidung) und progressive Infektion (anhaltende Virämie, dauerhafte Antigenämie, am wahrscheinlichsten bei Kitten oder immunsupprimierten Katzen), die mit der ernsten Prognose einhergeht und für Anämie, Immunsuppression und FeLV-assoziierte Neoplasien (Lymphom, Leukämie) prädisponiert. Die Diagnose stützt sich auf einen Schnelltest (ELISA) auf das p27-Kapsidantigen, bei Bedarf per PCR bestätigt. Eine inaktivierte oder Subunit-Impfung gegen das Hüllglykoprotein gp70 senkt das Risiko einer progressiven Infektion und wird für Katzen mit Freigang oder unklarem Expositionsstatus empfohlen.
