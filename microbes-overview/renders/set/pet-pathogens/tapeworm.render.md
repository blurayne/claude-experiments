## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The subject is an **adult *Taenia ovis***, a **cestode** — a parasitic flatworm (phylum Platyhelminthes, class Cestoda, order Cyclophyllidea, family Taeniidae), an *animal*, and the atlas's counterpoint to the heartworm: where the heartworm is a smooth round nematode, the tapeworm is a **ribbon-flat, segmented tape**. The adult lives in the **small intestine of dogs** and other canids and reaches **up to about a metre** (WCVM: dog taeniid adults "up to 100 cm, or more"). Humans are **not** a host for *T. ovis* — unlike its notorious relatives the beef and pork tapeworms. The whole worm cannot fit into a readable square frame, so every render composes the **scolex prominently** with the strobila trailing diagonally out of frame — honest, and it shows both diagnostic features.

**Three body regions, front to back:**

1. **Scolex** — the holdfast head at the anterior end, a knob roughly a millimetre across. It bears **FOUR cup-shaped muscular suckers (acetabula)** arranged radially around it — four separate hollow cups, like suction cups on a plunger head — and an apical **rostellum crowned with a DOUBLE ring of small recurved hooks** (armed scolex). Taenia hook counts overlap heavily between the dog species (Verster/Allan: hook length is the only partly-diagnostic character), so the renders target *the double-row arrangement*, not a species-exact count. Note a correction made during research: WCVM describes the taeniid rostellum as **non-retractable**; the working brief's "retractable" was dropped in favour of the source. The suckers and hooks are **attachment organs, not feeding parts** — the scolex has no mouth.
2. **Neck (collum)** — short, narrow, **unsegmented**: the proliferative zone directly behind the scolex where every new segment is budded. Segments are therefore *youngest at the front*.
3. **Strobila** — the long chain of flat **segments (proglottids)**, each a self-contained **hermaphroditic reproductive unit**, growing steadily **larger and more mature toward the posterior**: immature (wider than long) → mature (roughly square) → **gravid** (longer than wide, packed with eggs). Ripe gravid segments detach from the rear and are passed. Segment boundaries are **transverse creases running ACROSS the ribbon's width** — rectangles joined end to end — never lengthwise stripes.

**No mouth, no digestive tract — at any life stage.** The worm feeds entirely by **absorption across the tegument**, its living body surface, which is covered in **microtriches** (microvillus-like projections) that multiply the absorptive area; it lies bathed in the dog's pre-digested food. The living worm is **cream-white to ivory and gently opalescent** — the reds and carmines of classroom micrographs are stain, not the animal's colour.

**Life cycle (two hosts).** The dog is the definitive host (adult worm in the small intestine); **sheep and goats** are the intermediate host — swallowed eggs hatch into larvae that encyst in the sheep's heart, diaphragm and skeletal muscle (*Cysticercus ovis*, "sheep measles"). Dogs are infected by **eating raw, cyst-bearing sheep meat**. It is dealt with simply: **a dewormer from the vet (praziquantel)** removes the adult reliably, and **not feeding raw sheep carcass meat or offal** breaks the cycle.

Sources: [WCVM Learn About Parasites — Taenia species](https://wcvm.usask.ca/learnaboutparasites/parasites/taenia-species.php) (dog taeniid size, four suckers + non-retractable armed rostellum, life cycle), [Australian Society for Parasitology, Para-Site — Taenia](https://parasite.org.au/para-site/text/taenia-text.html) (cyclophyllidean body plan: scolex/neck/strobila, no gut, hermaphroditic proglottids, immature→mature→gravid gradient, double circle of rostellar hooks), [CDC DPDx — Taeniasis](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/taeniasis/index.html) (armed-scolex morphology), [Allan et al., J. Helminthol. — quantitative characters of dog Taenia spp.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7229326/) (hook-length overlap → no species-exact hook count claimed), plus the catalogue entry in `cells_data.py` (register calibration; ~1 m adult, sheep intermediate host, humans not a host, dewormer).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **Suckers or hooks as rays/spokes radiating from a hub** — the starburst trap. The suckers are composed positively: four separate cup-shaped depressions like suction cups on a plunger head, seen at a three-quarter angle so each reads as an individual cup.
- **A mouth, jaws, teeth, eyes or any face on the scolex** — the suckers and hooks are attachment organs; the worm has no mouth at all.
- **A digestive tract inside the worm** — no gut tube in any cutaway; the tegument is how it feeds.
- **A cylindrical round-bodied worm** — that is a nematode (the heartworm); this body is a flat ribbon.
- **Lengthwise stripes instead of transverse segment boundaries** — the creases run ACROSS the ribbon (the repeating-pattern axis trap).
- **Earthworm-style annelid rings or a clitellum on a round body.**
- **Blood, wounds, gore, or a sick/distressed dog** — a pet-pathogen subject in a children's atlas: show how it is dealt with, not the harm.
- **Humans as a host** — *T. ovis* does not infect people.

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet the tapeworm — a flat ribbon of a creature that can grow as long as a metre inside a dog's tummy, yet starts with a head no bigger than a pinhead. That tiny head is its best trick: four little suction cups and a crown of mini-hooks hold on tight, like a bath toy stuck to the tub. And here's the strangest part — it has no mouth at all! A dog can only catch it by eating raw sheep meat with the worm's baby stage hidden inside — it never lives in people. And when a dog does have one, the fix is wonderfully boring: a deworming tablet from the vet, and the whole train leaves the station. No raw sheep snacks, and it never comes back.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Taenia ovis is a tapeworm of dogs with a strict two-host itinerary: the adult, a ribbon-flat chain up to about a metre long, lives in the dog's small intestine, while the larval stage encysts in the muscles of sheep — shepherds know it as 'sheep measles'. A dog acquires the adult only by eating raw, cyst-bearing sheep meat, and sheep acquire cysts only from eggs shed in dog droppings onto pasture; each host hands the parasite to the other, and humans are simply not on the route (unlike the related beef and pork tapeworms). The worm itself is a study in specialisation: a pinhead-sized scolex with four suckers and a double crown of hooks anchors it to the gut wall, a narrow neck buds off segment after segment, and each segment matures into a self-contained egg factory that eventually breaks off and is passed. It has no mouth and no gut — it absorbs the dog's pre-digested food across its entire body surface. In the dog the adult usually causes little more than mild digestive upset, and the fix is straightforward: a praziquantel dewormer from the vet clears it reliably, and not feeding raw sheep offal or carcass meat — plus keeping farm dogs wormed — breaks the cycle at both ends. The real economic damage is on the sheep side, where cysts get carcasses downgraded at the abattoir.  
🇩🇪 Taenia ovis ist ein Bandwurm des Hundes mit striktem Zwei-Wirte-Fahrplan: Der erwachsene Wurm, ein bandflaches Gliederband von bis zu einem Meter Länge, lebt im Dünndarm des Hundes, während sich das Larvenstadium als Zysten in der Muskulatur von Schafen einnistet — Schäfer kennen das als „sheep measles“, die Schafsfinnen. Ein Hund bekommt den erwachsenen Wurm ausschließlich durch rohes, zystenhaltiges Schaffleisch, und Schafe bekommen die Zysten nur über Eier, die mit Hundekot auf die Weide gelangen; jeder Wirt reicht den Parasiten an den anderen weiter, und der Mensch kommt in diesem Kreislauf schlicht nicht vor (anders als bei den verwandten Rinder- und Schweinebandwürmern). Der Wurm selbst ist ein Musterbeispiel an Spezialisierung: Ein stecknadelkopfgroßer Skolex mit vier Saugnäpfen und doppeltem Hakenkranz verankert ihn in der Darmwand, ein schmaler Hals knospt Glied um Glied ab, und jedes Glied reift zu einer eigenständigen Eierfabrik heran, die sich schließlich löst und ausgeschieden wird. Mund und Darm fehlen völlig — er nimmt den vorverdauten Nahrungsbrei des Hundes über seine gesamte Körperoberfläche auf. Beim Hund verursacht der erwachsene Wurm meist kaum mehr als milde Verdauungsbeschwerden, und die Abhilfe ist unkompliziert: Eine Wurmkur mit Praziquantel vom Tierarzt entfernt ihn zuverlässig, und wer keine rohen Schlachtabfälle verfüttert und Hofhunde regelmäßig entwurmt, unterbricht den Kreislauf an beiden Enden. Der eigentliche wirtschaftliche Schaden entsteht auf der Schafseite, wo Finnen die Schlachtkörper entwerten.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Taenia ovis (Cyclophyllidea: Taeniidae) is a cestode whose adult inhabits the small intestine of dogs and other canids, reaching roughly 0.5-1 m. The body plan is the classic taeniid triad: an anterior scolex (~1 mm) bearing four radially-arranged muscular acetabula and an armed rostellum with a double circle of recurved hooks (hook counts and lengths overlap broadly among the canine Taenia species, so scolex hardware alone does not identify the species); a short unsegmented neck acting as the germinative zone from which all proglottids are budded; and the strobila, a chain of progressively maturing hermaphroditic proglottids — immature (wider than long), mature (approximately square, with complete male and female genitalia), and gravid (longer than wide, the branched uterus packed with eggs) — with gravid terminal proglottids detaching by apolysis and passing in the faeces. Cestodes lack any mouth or alimentary tract at every life stage: nutrient uptake occurs entirely across the syncytial tegument, whose apical surface is amplified by microtriches into a brush border functionally analogous to (and in competition with) the host's own intestinal mucosa. The life cycle is obligately indirect: eggs ingested by sheep or goats release oncospheres that migrate haematogenously and encyst as cysticerci (Cysticercus ovis) in cardiac, diaphragmatic and skeletal muscle; canids complete the cycle by scavenging or being fed infected raw mutton. Humans are not a host for T. ovis in either role; its significance is veterinary and economic (carcass condemnation, 'sheep measles') rather than zoonotic. Praziquantel is reliably cesticidal against the adult; control combines routine deworming of farm dogs with excluding raw sheep carcass material from their diet. Caveat on the simplification: calling the proglottids 'segments' invites a false analogy with annelid metamerism — strobilation is a budding process from the neck, the chain is a single physiologically continuous individual (youngest 'segments' anterior), and a detached gravid proglottid is an egg-dispersal package, not an independent animal; likewise 'up to a metre' is an upper bound, with adult length varying severalfold with worm burden and host.  
🇩🇪 Taenia ovis (Cyclophyllidea: Taeniidae) ist ein Zestode, dessen Adultstadium den Dünndarm von Hunden und anderen Kaniden besiedelt und etwa 0,5-1 m erreicht. Der Bauplan folgt der klassischen Taeniiden-Trias: vorn ein Skolex (~1 mm) mit vier radiär angeordneten muskulösen Acetabula und einem bewaffneten Rostellum mit doppeltem Kranz rückgebogener Haken (Hakenzahl und -länge überlappen zwischen den kaninen Taenia-Arten erheblich, sodass die Skolex-Ausstattung allein keine Artdiagnose erlaubt); dahinter ein kurzer, ungegliederter Hals als germinative Zone, aus der sämtliche Proglottiden hervorknospen; schließlich die Strobila, eine Kette zunehmend reifender zwittriger Proglottiden — unreif (breiter als lang), reif (etwa quadratisch, mit vollständigen männlichen und weiblichen Geschlechtsorganen) und gravid (länger als breit, der verzweigte Uterus prall mit Eiern gefüllt) —, wobei sich gravide Endglieder durch Apolyse ablösen und mit dem Kot abgehen. Zestoden besitzen in keinem Lebensstadium Mund oder Verdauungstrakt: Die Nährstoffaufnahme erfolgt vollständig über das synzytiale Tegument, dessen apikale Oberfläche durch Mikrotrichen zu einem Bürstensaum vergrößert ist — funktionell ein Gegenstück zur Darmschleimhaut des Wirts, mit der es um Nährstoffe konkurriert. Der Zyklus ist obligat indirekt: Von Schafen oder Ziegen aufgenommene Eier entlassen Onkosphären, die hämatogen wandern und sich als Zystizerken (Cysticercus ovis) in Herz-, Zwerchfell- und Skelettmuskulatur einkapseln; Kaniden schließen den Kreislauf, wenn sie zystenhaltiges rohes Schaffleisch fressen. Der Mensch ist für T. ovis in keiner Rolle Wirt; die Bedeutung ist veterinärmedizinisch und ökonomisch (Schlachtkörper-Verwurf, Schafsfinnen), nicht zoonotisch. Praziquantel wirkt zuverlässig zestizid gegen das Adultstadium; die Bekämpfung kombiniert routinemäßige Entwurmung von Hofhunden mit dem Ausschluss rohen Schlachtmaterials aus deren Futter. Vorbehalt zur Vereinfachung: Die Rede von „Gliedern“ legt eine falsche Analogie zur Metamerie der Ringelwürmer nahe — die Strobilation ist ein Knospungsprozess des Halses, die Kette ist ein einziges, physiologisch zusammenhängendes Individuum (die jüngsten Glieder liegen vorn), und eine abgelöste gravide Proglottide ist ein Eier-Verbreitungspaket, kein eigenständiges Tier; ebenso ist „bis zu einem Meter“ eine Obergrenze, die je nach Wurmlast und Wirt um ein Mehrfaches unterschritten wird.
