# Synthetic minimal cell (JCVI-syn3A) — render log

**Set:** `engineered-life` · **Microbe key:** `synthetic-minimal-cell`
**Short description:** A bacterium whose entire genome was designed on a computer, chemically synthesised, and pared down to the essentials: JCVI-syn3.0 (2016) got by on just 473 genes, roughly half of what its nearest natural relative, *Mycoplasma mycoides*, carries. The later, division-stable version, JCVI-syn3A (2021), adds a handful back. It was not built from scratch — the synthetic genome was transplanted into an emptied but still-living recipient cell of a related *Mycoplasma* species — and even after years of paring down, roughly a third of its genes still have no known function.

## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

**Lineage.** JCVI-syn3A is the current end point of a chain of genome-transplant experiments run at the J. Craig Venter Institute:
- **JCVI-syn1.0** (Gibson et al. 2010, *Science*) — the first synthetic-genome cell: a chemically synthesised copy of the *Mycoplasma mycoides* genome, assembled in yeast, then **transplanted into an emptied but still-living recipient cell of *Mycoplasma capricolum*** (a related species, cell wall and all its original DNA removed). The recipient's own machinery booted up the new genome and the cell "rebooted" as *M. mycoides*. This transplant step — not de-novo cell assembly — is the mechanism behind every later syn-cell, including this one.
- **JCVI-syn3.0** (Hutchison et al. 2016, *Science*) — the syn1.0 genome cut down, gene by gene, to the smallest set that would still support life in the lab: **473 genes, ~531 kb**, roughly half of *M. mycoides*' natural gene count. Booted the same way, by transplant into an emptied *M. capricolum* recipient. Its cells divide irregularly: because several genes involved in orderly binary fission were among those removed, daughter cells emerge as **wildly variable blobs and chains of different sizes**, not two even spheres.
- **JCVI-syn3A** (Pelletier et al. 2021, *Cell*, building on Breuer et al. 2019, *eLife*) — syn3.0 with **a small number of division/cell-shape genes added back** (bringing the count to **493 genes**), fixing exactly that defect: **syn3A divides into regular, roughly equal round daughter cells**, a normal-looking binary fission cycle. This is the whole reason syn3A exists and the reason the catalogue names this version specifically — depicting division must show two even round cells, not the irregular blobs of syn3.0.

**"Not built from scratch."** No synthetic cell in this lineage was assembled from raw chemicals into a living cell. What was synthesised is the *genome* — a strand of DNA — chemically built base by base and assembled from fragments. That DNA was then transplanted into a recipient cell that was already alive, already had a working membrane, ribosomes, and metabolism, and simply had its own native genome removed. The recipient cell's existing machinery reads the new genome and rebuilds the cell around it. This is the load-bearing honesty point for the picture: **do not depict, or let the image imply, a cell built up from non-living parts.** If the transplant/reboot step is shown at all, show a transplant into an already-membrane-bound, already-living recipient cell — never an assembly from scratch.

**Morphology (Mollicutes / class *Mollicutes*, genus *Mycoplasma* lineage).**
- **No cell wall.** *Mycoplasma* and its synthetic derivatives are wall-less bacteria — no peptidoglycan layer, no outer membrane (that is a Gram-negative feature and does not apply here either). The entire boundary of the cell is a **single plasma membrane**, stabilised by cholesterol/sterols scavenged from the growth medium (Mollicutes cannot make their own sterols) rather than by a rigid wall. This is the single most checkable structural fact for this subject, and the classic failure mode is drawing a bacterial cell-wall layer or a double (Gram-negative-style) envelope.
- **Size.** Extremely small and roughly spherical, pleomorphic — on the order of **~400 nm** in diameter, among the smallest self-replicating cells known (rivalled among natural bacteria only by things like *Mycoplasma genitalium*). This is close to or below the diffraction limit of visible light (~200–300 nm), so the cell is barely resolvable by light microscopy at all; only electron microscopy shows its shape clearly.
- **Interior.** A single circular chromosome (the synthetic minimal genome, ~543 kb in syn3A) occupying a nucleoid region with no membrane around it (no nucleus — this is a prokaryote), ribosomes scattered through the cytoplasm (a disproportionately large share of the minimal gene set is devoted to translation — the ribosome cannot be minimised away), and otherwise unstructured cytoplasm. No mitochondria, no other organelles, no flagella, no pili, no capsule.
- **Division.** syn3A divides by binary fission into two **regular, round, roughly equal-sized daughter cells** — this is precisely the corrected behaviour that distinguishes syn3A from syn3.0's irregular blobs, and it is what the catalogue explicitly names.

**"A third of its genes have no known function."** Of syn3.0's 473 genes, roughly 149 (~31%, "roughly a third") could not be assigned a known biological function at the time of publication, yet all were retained because removing them killed the cell — they are necessary for the most reduced life scientists have made, and their role remains unidentified. This does not change materially in syn3A (a handful of genes were added back for division, not resolved for function) and is the memorable, humbling, honest hook for this subject: it is worth showing textually (never picturable directly), and the descriptions below carry it explicitly rather than softening it into "still being studied."

Sources: [Hutchison III et al. 2016, *Science* — "Design and synthesis of a minimal bacterial genome" (DOI 10.1126/science.aad6253)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aad6253), [Gibson et al. 2010, *Science* — "Creation of a Bacterial Cell Controlled by a Chemically Synthesized Genome" (DOI 10.1126/science.1190719)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1190719), [Pelletier et al. 2021, *Cell* — "Genetic requirements for cell division in a genomically minimal cell" (PMC8339304)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8339304/), [Breuer et al. 2019, *eLife* — "Essential metabolism for a minimal cell" (PMC6688871)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688871/), [JCVI — "First Minimal Synthetic Bacterial Cell"](https://www.jcvi.org/research/first-minimal-synthetic-bacterial-cell), [PDB-101 Goodsell Gallery — "JCVI-syn3A Minimal Cell"](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/sci-art/goodsell-gallery/jcvi-syn3a-minimal-cell).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, no peptidoglycan layer, no outer membrane, no Gram-negative-style double envelope** — this is a wall-less Mollicute; the boundary is a single plasma membrane only. This is the single most important "do NOT" for this subject.
- **No flagella, no pili, no fimbriae, no capsule** — none of the usual bacterial surface appendages are present; the surface should read essentially smooth/bare aside from the membrane itself.
- **No nucleus, no nuclear envelope, no mitochondria, no ER, no Golgi, no chloroplasts, no vacuoles** — this is a minimal prokaryote; nothing eukaryotic.
- **The chromosome must not read as a tidy, cleanly-coiled cartoon loop sitting apart from the cytoplasm** — keep it a diffuse tangled/coiled mass filling a nucleoid-like region, not a clean ring floating in empty space, and never with a membrane around it.
- **Do not depict the cell as built/assembled from scratch out of non-living parts** — if origin/construction is depicted at all (most styles should not attempt it), it must show a genome transplanted into an already-membrane-bound, already-living recipient cell, never atoms/parts being assembled into a cell.
- **If division is shown, the daughter cells must be regular and round (the syn3A fix), never syn3.0's irregular variable-sized blobs or chains.**
- **Not a rod, not elongated, not the classic "textbook E. coli" bacterium shape** — roughly spherical/pleomorphic and very small.
- **No text, letters, numbers, gene-count digits, labels, scale bars, or watermarks baked into the image** — the "473/493 genes" and "~1/3 unknown function" facts belong to the label text and descriptions, never rendered as pixels.
- **No syringe, lab-coat scientist, computer/keyboard, or "mad science" imagery** — the honest framing is a designed genome transplanted into a living cell, not a machine or a robot.
- A single specimen per frame (a small cluster of 2 is acceptable only if explicitly depicting division per syn3A's corrected round fission), not a dense clump.
- General: no black border/frame/vignette/letterbox, no "sheet lying on a surface" for the watercolor style.

## 2. Real microscopy reference (own set `reference-microscopy`)

**A freely-licensed electron micrograph of JCVI-Syn3A itself was found on the second pass (2026-09-06). No species substitution was needed — this is the subject, not a stand-in.**

**Source.** Justice I, Kiesel P, Safronova N, von Appen A, Saenz JP (2024), *"A tuneable minimal cell membrane reveals that two lipid species suffice for life"*, **Nature Communications 15:9679**, [doi:10.1038/s41467-024-53975-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53975-y) ([PMC11549477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11549477/figure/Fig4/)) — **CC BY 4.0** (the article's own `<permissions>` block carries `creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`; no third-party credit line on this figure). Modality: **thin-section transmission electron micrograph (TEM)**, not a surface image — the earlier search had only looked for SEM plates and for images of the 2016 syn3.0 papers, which are indeed not openly licensed.

**Which panel, and what was done to it.** Figure 4c is a 3×3 grid of TEM panels (three lipid diets × three morphology classes). The reference uses **row "Normal", column "JCVI-Syn3A FBS"** — the cell grown on the standard fetal-bovine-serum lipid diet, i.e. the unmanipulated condition, deliberately in preference to the visually punchier `D.PC` panel, which shows a larger, rounder, more legible cell but on a synthetic diether-phosphocholine diet. Cleaning was a **deterministic Pillow crop and LANCZOS resize only — `edit_image.py` was not run**, because the crop alone removes everything that needed removing: the burnt-in `1 µm` scale bar and the panel lettering both fall outside it. Crop box `(270, 819)–(470, 1019)` of the 1869×2130 composite, i.e. **200×200 native pixels upscaled 5.4× to 1080²**. That upscale is the honest cost of using this figure: the source is a page-resolution journal composite (checked — Springer's `full` render at 1869 px is the largest available, and the PDF's embedded copy is smaller at 1566×2106), so the plate is soft. Sidecar: [`real-01__TEM.json`](../reference-microscopy/theme/tem/synthetic-minimal-cell.attempts/real-01__TEM.json).

**Verification (viewed directly, then measured).** The crop shows one isolated, electron-dense, roughly ovoid cell against a pale embedding matrix, with two smaller partial cells at the corners. Against the figure's own scale bar (measured: 1 µm = 181 px in the source composite) the cell's long axis is **≈0.48 µm and its short axis ≈0.39 µm** — matching the ~400 nm quoted for syn3A in §1. Visible and consistent with §1: a single thin boundary with no second dark track outside it (no wall, no outer membrane), a uniformly granular ribosome-dense interior with no membrane-bounded compartment, and no flagella, pili or surface appendages. Not resolvable at this magnification and therefore *not* claimed: the chromosome as a distinct nucleoid, and individual ribosomes as countable particles.

Searched and rejected on the first pass (kept for the record):
- **The actual published images** — Hutchison et al. 2016 (*Science*) credits its electron micrographs of JCVI-syn3.0 cell clusters (~15,000× magnification) to Tom Deerinck and Mark Ellisman, National Center for Imaging and Microscopy Research (NCMIR), UC San Diego; these appear in *Science*, in JCVI's own press materials, and in a *Bioengineered* commentary, always as "courtesy of" credits, never under a stated open licence (CC BY / CC0 / public domain). Pelletier et al. 2021 (*Cell*) similarly reports cryo-electron tomography of syn3A but as a Cell Press (Elsevier) article, not openly licensed. A web search for explicit reuse terms from NCMIR/JCVI/Deerinck/Ellisman turned up none — "courtesy of" attribution to specific outlets is not a licence for general reuse.
- **Wikimedia Commons** — `Category:Mycoplasma` and a direct search for "Mycoplasma electron micrograph" returned no usable EM plate of any *Mycoplasma* species; the one indexed "Mycoplasma_genitalium.gif" file (public domain, US DOE) is a circular genome map diagram with baked-in scale text, not a micrograph.
- **PDB-101 Goodsell Gallery "JCVI-syn3A Minimal Cell"** — exists and is a real, carefully-sourced cutaway, but it is a **watercolour-style structural illustration** integrating cryo-ET/genomic/proteomic data (Goodsell, 2022, *Journal of Molecular Biology* companion piece), not a photograph — unusable as "real microscopy," though it independently corroborates the morphology in §1.
- **A CC-BY fallback candidate was checked and rejected on quality, not licence:** Rosenau et al. 2013, *PLOS ONE* (`journal.pone.0068373`), Fig. 4B is a freely-licensed (CC BY) transmission electron micrograph of the actual natural relative, ***Mycoplasma mycoides* subsp. *mycoides* PG1**. It was inspected directly (downloaded, viewed) and rejected: at the magnification shown, the frame is an ultra-close-up of the plasma membrane texture at the packed edge of a **dense cell mass**, with a baked-in scale bar and "CM" arrow labels — it does not show an individual cell's outline, poles, or overall shape, failing the "individual specimen/features clearly readable" bar in `reference/verification.md`, not merely the "prefer isolated" preference.
- **Species-specific risk considered and rejected:** other freely-licensed *Mycoplasma* SEM plates that do show whole-cell shape (e.g. *M. pneumoniae*, open-access *J. Bacteriol.* 2014 review) were not used because *M. pneumoniae* carries a polar **attachment organelle** — a specialised gliding-motility tip structure absent from the *M. mycoides*/*M. capricolum* lineage this minimal cell actually derives from. Using it risks implying a polar structure this subject does not have, which is worse than no reference.

Also checked on the second pass and **not** used, though both are freely licensed and both are genuinely of this lineage:
- **Moger-Reischer et al. 2023, *Nature* 620:122, [Fig. 4c](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06288-x) (CC BY 4.0)** — a real SEM of *minimal* cells, and tempting because SEM shows whole-cell shape. Rejected because the panel is of **JCVI-syn3B after 2,000 generations of experimental evolution**, not ancestral syn3A: it is a small cluster threaded with filaments, which would need more caveating than it teaches.
- **Commons `File:JCVI-syn3A Minimal Cell, 2022.tif`, David S. Goodsell / PDB-101, CC BY 4.0** — the correct subject under a clean licence, but a **painted integrative structural illustration**, not microscopy. It corroborates §1; it is not a photograph and is not presented as one.

**Decision: ship with a real-microscopy row.** `theme/tem/synthetic-minimal-cell.attempts/real-01__TEM` — TEM of JCVI-Syn3A, CC BY 4.0, deterministic crop, 5.4× upscale stated.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 JCVI-syn3A (Pelletier et al. 2021, building on Breuer et al. 2019) is a wall-less Mollicute derivative representing the current minimal viable gene set achievable by top-down genome reduction of a Mycoplasma mycoides-derived synthetic chromosome. Its immediate predecessor, JCVI-syn3.0 (Hutchison et al. 2016), was generated by iterative transposon mutagenesis and gene-by-gene deletion testing from JCVI-syn1.0 (Gibson et al. 2010) — itself a de novo-synthesised, yeast-assembled copy of the M. mycoides genome, booted by transplantation into an enucleated, genome-depleted Mycoplasma capricolum recipient cell. Syn3.0 converged on 473 genes (~531 kb) but exhibited pleomorphic, unevenly partitioned daughter cells on division, attributable to the loss of several genes with roles in cell-shape determination and divisome function; syn3A restored 19 of these (493 genes total, ~543 kb), yielding regular, symmetric binary fission comparable to wild-type Mollicutes. Structurally, the cell retains the class-defining absence of a peptidoglycan cell wall and outer membrane, bounded solely by a sterol-stabilised plasma membrane (Mollicutes cannot synthesise sterols de novo and scavenge them from the medium), a single circular chromosome occupying an unbound nucleoid, and a translation apparatus (ribosomes, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases) that consumes a disproportionate fraction of the minimal proteome — consistent with translation being irreducible below a certain genome size. Cell diameter is approximately 400 nm, near the diffraction limit of visible light. Functional annotation remains incomplete: approximately 149 of the 473 syn3.0 genes (~31%) lack an assigned biological function despite being essential for viability under standard culture conditions, underscoring that minimality has been achieved without commensurate mechanistic understanding.  
🇩🇪 JCVI-syn3A (Pelletier et al. 2021, aufbauend auf Breuer et al. 2019) ist ein wandloses Derivat aus der Klasse Mollicutes und repräsentiert den derzeit erreichten minimalen lebensfähigen Gensatz, der durch schrittweise Genomreduktion eines von Mycoplasma mycoides abgeleiteten synthetischen Chromosoms erzielt wurde. Der unmittelbare Vorläufer, JCVI-syn3.0 (Hutchison et al. 2016), entstand durch iterative Transposon-Mutagenese und Gen-für-Gen-Deletionstests ausgehend von JCVI-syn1.0 (Gibson et al. 2010) — selbst eine de novo synthetisierte, in Hefe assemblierte Kopie des M.-mycoides-Genoms, die durch Transplantation in eine entkernte, genomfreie Empfängerzelle von Mycoplasma capricolum hochgefahren wurde. Syn3.0 konvergierte auf 473 Gene (~531 kb), zeigte bei der Teilung jedoch pleomorphe, ungleich verteilte Tochterzellen, zurückzuführen auf den Verlust mehrerer Gene mit Funktionen in der Zellformbestimmung und im Divisom; syn3A stellte 19 davon wieder her (insgesamt 493 Gene, ~543 kb) und erreichte damit eine regelmäßige, symmetrische binäre Teilung vergleichbar mit Wildtyp-Mollicutes. Strukturell behält die Zelle das klassendefinierende Fehlen einer Peptidoglykan-Zellwand und einer äußeren Membran bei; sie wird ausschließlich von einer durch Sterole stabilisierten Plasmamembran begrenzt (Mollicutes können keine Sterole selbst synthetisieren und nehmen sie aus dem Medium auf), besitzt ein einzelnes ringförmiges Chromosom in einem nicht membranumschlossenen Nukleoid sowie einen Translationsapparat (Ribosomen, Aminoacyl-tRNA-Synthetasen), der einen unverhältnismäßig großen Anteil des minimalen Proteoms beansprucht — im Einklang damit, dass die Translationsmaschinerie unterhalb einer bestimmten Genomgröße nicht weiter reduzierbar ist. Der Zelldurchmesser liegt bei etwa 400 nm, nahe der Beugungsgrenze des sichtbaren Lichts. Die funktionelle Annotation bleibt unvollständig: Etwa 149 der 473 Gene von syn3.0 (~31 %) haben trotz essenzieller Bedeutung für die Lebensfähigkeit unter Standardkulturbedingungen keine zugewiesene biologische Funktion — ein Beleg dafür, dass Minimalität erreicht wurde, ohne dass ein entsprechendes mechanistisches Verständnis vorliegt.
