## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

A CAR-T cell is not a new organism or a new cell type — it is a patient's own **cytotoxic T lymphocyte** (CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell, the same cell documented in this atlas as `cytotoxic-t-cell`), collected from blood, genetically modified in the lab, expanded, and infused back. Its baseline morphology is therefore identical to an ordinary resting/activated cytotoxic T cell: a small (~7-10 µm resting, larger once activated and expanded) round agranulocyte dominated by its nucleus. A single round nucleus fills most of the cell's volume, its chromatin condensed into dark heterochromatin clumped at the periphery around a small nucleolus, leaving only a **thin rim of cytoplasm** packed with free ribosomes/polyribosomes, a handful of small mitochondria, a compact Golgi apparatus and a centriole pair (MTOC). The surface carries short microvilli, giving it a softly ruffled texture in SEM. Because it is manufactured from an already cytotoxic T cell, it also keeps that cell's dense **cytotoxic granules** (perforin + granzymes), clustered in the cytoplasm near the Golgi/MTOC — the killing machinery is unchanged; only the trigger for using it is engineered.

**What makes it a CAR-T cell** is the chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) engineered into its genome (usually via a lentiviral vector — see `lentiviral-vector`) and expressed studding the plasma membrane. A CAR is a single synthetic fusion protein built from four parts, always in this order from outside to inside the cell:
1. **Extracellular recognition domain** — a single-chain variable fragment (scFv), the antigen-binding ends of an antibody spliced together and fused to the receptor. This is what recognizes a chosen surface marker on a target cell directly (e.g. CD19 on certain leukaemia/lymphoma B cells) — no MHC presentation needed, unlike a natural TCR.
2. **Hinge/spacer** — a short flexible stalk connecting the recognition head to the membrane.
3. **Transmembrane domain** — a single alpha-helix anchoring the receptor in the plasma membrane.
4. **Intracellular signalling domains** — cytoplasmic tails, typically CD3ζ (the same activation module a natural TCR-CD3 complex uses) plus a co-stimulatory domain (commonly 4-1BB or CD28), which together fire the T cell's killing/proliferation program once the extracellular head binds its target.

**Scale honesty for the images:** a CAR is a single transmembrane protein, a few nanometres across; a whole T cell is several micrometres. At true relative scale a CAR is far too small to resolve any internal structure — it would be an invisible dot. Every style here therefore draws the CAR as a small but visibly distinct multi-part stub studding the membrane (never as a smooth unbroken TCR-style bump), and the **textbook** and **3d** styles additionally draw ONE representative CAR receptor enlarged, right at the cell's own edge (not a separate floating panel/box), so its four-part layout — outside head, hinge, membrane-spanning rod, inside tails — can be labelled. This is a deliberate, disclosed simplification for teaching, not a claim that the domain boundaries are resolvable at whole-cell scale in any real image.

**No war/monster imagery.** A CAR does not give the cell a weapon it did not have — a cytotoxic T cell could already kill via perforin/granzymes; the CAR only changes what the cell is able to *recognize*. The honest, correct framing is "taught a new shape to look for," not "armed." No renders in this log show combat, a weapon-like receptor, or a distressed target cell.

Sources: [NCI — CAR T Cells: Engineering Patients' Immune Cells to Treat Their Cancers](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/research/car-t-cells), [NCBI Bookshelf — StatPearls, Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-Cell Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK556138/), [June & Sadelain 2018, NEJM — Chimeric Antigen Receptor Therapy (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30354820/), [Sadelain, Brentjens & Rivière 2013, Cancer Discovery — The Basic Principles of Chimeric Antigen Receptor Design (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3667586/), and the atlas's own `cytotoxic-t-cell.render.md` §1 for the shared base-cell morphology and sources.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — this is a eukaryotic human cell, not a prokaryote.
- **Not a biconcave disc, not anucleate** — the cell keeps its large nucleus, unlike a red blood cell.
- **Not granular like a neutrophil/eosinophil/basophil** — a single round nucleus, not multi-lobed; the only "granules" are the small dense cytotoxic granules.
- **The CAR is NOT a natural TCR-CD8 complex** — do not draw the simple receptor-stub shapes used for `cytotoxic-t-cell`'s native TCR-CD8; the CAR must read as a distinct, slightly larger, visibly multi-part stub (an outer head + a thin waist at the membrane), because it is a synthetic fusion protein, not the cell's native receptor. Do not draw it as a smooth antenna, spike or thorn (a starburst-prone shape) — keep it a short, blunt, mushroom/lollipop-like stub.
- **No second cell, no target cell, no contact/attack scene** — keep every render to a single specimen. The CAR's job (recognizing a target) is conveyed by the receptor's structure and the label text, never by staging an attack, a bite, a weapon, blood, or a distressed/frightened second cell. This mirrors how the existing `cytotoxic-t-cell` 3d render polarises its granules "as if aimed at an unseen target" without ever drawing that target.
- **No syringe, IV bag, hospital imagery, or human figure** — this is a cell-scale illustration, not a treatment-process diagram (the lentiviral-vector and manufacturing process belong to the `lentiviral-vector` and `deps` text, not this image).
- **Sparse rough ER, not a plasma-cell "cartwheel" nucleus** — mostly free ribosomes, modest RER/Golgi, smooth dense nuclear chromatin.
- **No cilia or flagella for locomotion.**
- **Cytoplasm must stay a thin rim** — the nucleus dominates; do not draw a large roomy cytoplasm.
- A single specimen, not a dense cluster of lymphocytes.
- General: no baked-in text/letters/numbers/scale bars/watermarks, no black border/frame/vignette/letterbox, no "sheet lying on a surface" for the watercolor style, no separate floating inset/panel/box for the enlarged CAR — it must read as part of the one continuous cell outline.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The CAR-T cell is not a new kind of cell but an existing one, redirected: a cytotoxic T lymphocyte drawn from a patient's own blood and genetically engineered — usually with a lentiviral vector — to carry a chimeric antigen receptor, or CAR. The CAR fuses an antibody-derived recognition piece on the outside of the cell to T-cell-receptor activation machinery on the inside, letting the T cell recognize a chosen marker on a cancer cell's surface directly, without needing that cell to present antigen via MHC the way a natural T cell requires. Once modified, the cells are expanded in the lab into the billions and infused back into the patient. On finding a target, a CAR-T cell kills exactly the way any cytotoxic T cell does, releasing perforin and granzymes onto the bound cell — the mechanism is unchanged, only what triggers it is new. Because the modified cells can continue dividing inside the body afterwards, CAR-T therapy behaves less like a drug dose and more like a living treatment, and has produced durable remissions in some blood cancers such as certain leukemias and lymphomas that had stopped responding to other treatment.  
🇩🇪 Die CAR-T-Zelle ist keine neue Zellart, sondern eine bereits vorhandene, die umgeleitet wurde: ein zytotoxischer T-Lymphozyt, der aus dem Blut einer Patientin oder eines Patienten entnommen und gentechnisch verändert wird — meist mit einem lentiviralen Vektor —, um einen chimären Antigenrezeptor, kurz CAR, zu tragen. Der CAR verbindet ein von einem Antikörper abgeleitetes Erkennungsstück außen an der Zelle mit der Aktivierungsmaschinerie eines T-Zell-Rezeptors innen und ermöglicht es der T-Zelle, ein ausgewähltes Merkmal auf der Oberfläche einer Krebszelle direkt zu erkennen, ohne dass diese Zelle Antigen über MHC präsentieren muss, wie es eine natürliche T-Zelle sonst benötigt. Nach der Veränderung werden die Zellen im Labor auf Milliarden vermehrt und der Patientin oder dem Patienten zurückgegeben. Findet eine CAR-T-Zelle ihr Ziel, tötet sie genau so, wie es jede zytotoxische T-Zelle tut, indem sie Perforin und Granzyme auf die gebundene Zelle abgibt — der Mechanismus bleibt unverändert, nur der Auslöser ist neu. Da sich die veränderten Zellen im Körper weiter teilen können, verhält sich die CAR-T-Therapie eher wie eine lebende Behandlung als wie eine einmalige Medikamentengabe und hat bei manchen Blutkrebsarten, etwa bestimmten Leukämien und Lymphomen, die auf andere Behandlungen nicht mehr ansprachen, zu dauerhaften Remissionen geführt.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 A CAR-T cell is an autologous CD8<sup>+</sup> (or, in some products, CD4<sup>+</sup>/CD8<sup>+</sup> mixed) T lymphocyte engineered ex vivo to express a chimeric antigen receptor: a synthetic type I transmembrane fusion protein comprising an extracellular single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from a monoclonal antibody, a hinge/spacer domain, a transmembrane domain, and one or more intracellular signalling domains — canonically CD3ζ (providing the primary activation signal, ITAM-mediated) fused to a co-stimulatory domain such as CD28 or 4-1BB (CD137) in second-generation constructs, with third-generation designs combining both. Because antigen recognition is mediated by an antibody-derived binder rather than the native alpha-beta TCR, target engagement is MHC-independent, bypassing a common tumour immune-evasion route (MHC class I downregulation). Manufacturing typically uses a self-inactivating lentiviral vector to integrate the CAR transgene into T cells isolated from patient leukapheresis product, followed by ex vivo activation/expansion and lymphodepleting chemotherapy prior to infusion. Upon CAR-antigen engagement, downstream signalling recapitulates canonical TCR-proximal signalling (Lck/ZAP-70 recruitment, calcium flux, NF-κB/NFAT/AP-1 activation), driving cytotoxic granule (perforin/granzyme) exocytosis at an immunological-synapse-like interface, cytokine release, and clonal proliferation, with persistence enabling serial killing and, in some products, long-term memory formation. Approved CD19-directed and BCMA-directed CAR-T products have produced durable remissions in relapsed/refractory B-cell malignancies and multiple myeloma, at the cost of characteristic toxicities (cytokine release syndrome, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome) arising from the scale of T-cell activation involved.  
🇩🇪 Eine CAR-T-Zelle ist ein autologer CD8<sup>+</sup>-T-Lymphozyt (bei manchen Produkten eine gemischte CD4<sup>+</sup>/CD8<sup>+</sup>-Population), der ex vivo so verändert wird, dass er einen chimären Antigenrezeptor exprimiert: ein synthetisches Typ-I-Transmembran-Fusionsprotein aus einem extrazellulären, von einem monoklonalen Antikörper abgeleiteten Einzelketten-Fragment (scFv), einer Hinge/Spacer-Domäne, einer Transmembrandomäne und einer oder mehreren intrazellulären Signaldomänen — klassischerweise CD3ζ (liefert über ITAM-Motive das primäre Aktivierungssignal), bei Konstrukten der zweiten Generation fusioniert mit einer kostimulatorischen Domäne wie CD28 oder 4-1BB (CD137), wobei Designs der dritten Generation beide kombinieren. Da die Antigenerkennung über einen von einem Antikörper abgeleiteten Binder statt über den nativen Alpha-Beta-TCR vermittelt wird, ist die Zielerkennung MHC-unabhängig und umgeht damit einen verbreiteten Immunfluchtmechanismus von Tumoren (Herunterregulation von MHC-Klasse-I). Bei der Herstellung wird meist ein selbstinaktivierender lentiviraler Vektor eingesetzt, um das CAR-Transgen in T-Zellen aus dem Leukapherese-Produkt der Patientin oder des Patienten einzubauen, gefolgt von ex-vivo-Aktivierung/Expansion und einer lymphodepletierenden Chemotherapie vor der Infusion. Nach Bindung des CAR an sein Antigen läuft die nachgeschaltete Signalgebung im Wesentlichen wie bei der nativen TCR-Signalgebung ab (Rekrutierung von Lck/ZAP-70, Kalziumeinstrom, Aktivierung von NF-κB/NFAT/AP-1) und treibt die Exozytose zytotoxischer Granula (Perforin/Granzyme) an einer immunologischen-Synapse-ähnlichen Kontaktstelle, die Zytokinfreisetzung sowie die klonale Vermehrung an; die fortbestehende Aktivität ermöglicht serielles Töten und bei manchen Produkten die Bildung eines langfristigen Gedächtnisses. Zugelassene CD19- und BCMA-gerichtete CAR-T-Produkte haben bei rezidivierten/refraktären B-Zell-Malignomen und beim multiplen Myelom dauerhafte Remissionen erzielt, allerdings um den Preis charakteristischer Toxizitäten (Zytokinfreisetzungssyndrom, mit Immuneffektorzellen assoziiertes Neurotoxizitätssyndrom), die aus dem Ausmaß der T-Zell-Aktivierung resultieren.
