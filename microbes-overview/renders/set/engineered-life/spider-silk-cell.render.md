# Spider-silk cell — render log

**Set:** `engineered-life` · **Microbe key:** `spider-silk-cell`
**Short description:** A cell — a goat mammary cell, a bacterium or a silkworm cell, depending on the route taken — carrying one or more spider genes so that it manufactures spidroin, the fibre-forming protein spiders spin their thread from. The best-known version is a goat that secretes the protein into its milk; engineered bacteria and silkworms that replace or supplement their usual silk protein with spidroin are the other two routes in active use.

---

## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

A "spider-silk cell" is not a new kind of cell and not a spider cell at all — it is an ordinary host cell (most documented: a **goat mammary secretory/epithelial cell**; alternative routes use *E. coli* or silkworm cells) carrying one or more inserted spider genes coding for **spidroin**, the family of large repetitive structural proteins (dragline silk uses MaSp1/MaSp2, "major ampullate spidroin" 1 and 2) that spiders themselves spin into dragline silk from their major ampullate gland. The gene is usually placed under a mammary-specific promoter (as is standard for goat "pharming" constructs used to make other recombinant proteins in milk, e.g. beta-casein-promoter constructs) so the transgene switches on specifically in secretory epithelial cells of the mammary gland during lactation, alongside the animal's normal milk-protein genes. The cell's baseline morphology is therefore an ordinary polarized glandular secretory epithelial cell, not anything spider-like: a basal nucleus, an extensive **rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER)** studded with ribosomes where the spidroin chain (like any secreted protein) is co-translationally threaded into the RER lumen, folded and initially processed, packaged into **transport vesicles** that carry it to the **Golgi apparatus** for further processing and sorting, then concentrated into **secretory vesicles** that migrate to and fuse with the **apical plasma membrane** (the surface facing the gland lumen/duct, where milk collects) and release their cargo by **exocytosis** — the same constitutive/regulated secretory pathway used by any protein-exporting gland cell (e.g. a pancreatic acinar cell secreting digestive enzymes, or an ordinary milk-protein-secreting mammary cell making casein). Mitochondria supply the substantial ATP this biosynthetic/secretory traffic requires.

The most-documented real-world work: **Nexia Biotechnologies** (Quebec) inserted the *Nephila clavipes* (golden orb-weaver) dragline silk genes MaSp1 and MaSp2 into a line of transgenic goats nicknamed "BioSteel" goats (including "Webster" and "Peter"), whose mammary epithelial cells expressed and secreted the recombinant spidroin protein into milk, from which it was separated (e.g. by density-gradient centrifugation, away from milk fat and casein) and wet-spun into filaments. In parallel, Lazaris et al. (2002, *Science*) demonstrated expression of soluble recombinant dragline silk proteins (based on ADF-3/MaSpII and MaSpI) directly in cultured mammalian cells and showed these soluble proteins — chemically identical in kind to what the transgenic goat's mammary cells make — could be wet-spun into fibres with mechanical properties approaching (though not matching) native spider dragline silk. Nexia went bankrupt in 2009 without reaching commercial-scale fibre production; goat- and bacteria-based recombinant-spidroin research has continued elsewhere (e.g. Randy Lewis's lab, Utah State University) and other groups have pursued *E. coli* and silkworm-cell hosts instead of goats, which is why the catalogue text frames the goat mammary cell as "the best-known version," not the only one.

**Scale/what the cell can and cannot do:** the cell manufactures only the raw, soluble spidroin protein monomer — it does **not** spin thread itself. Turning the secreted protein into an actual fibre requires a separate, external spinning process (controlled shear, dropping pH, water removal) modelled on a real spider's spinning duct; that step happens after secretion, outside the cell, and is out of scope for this single-cell image (it belongs to the catalogue's `deps` text, not the cell diagram).

**A note on the "one gene" temptation:** the inserted spidroin gene sits somewhere in the cell's genomic DNA inside the nucleus, but DNA packed in a nucleus does not look visually different gene-to-gene — there is no legitimate way to draw "the spider gene" as a distinguishable glowing spot or marked locus without fabricating a visual feature that doesn't exist at this scale. This limit is stated here explicitly rather than solved by inventing a marker; see "do NOT draw" below.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No spider** — no legs, no arachnid body, no spinnerets, no eyes. This is a mammalian (or, in the alternative routes, bacterial/insect) cell, not an animal.
- **No web, no finished/spun thread or fibre**, inside or around the cell — the cell only makes the raw soluble protein; spinning it into thread is a separate downstream process (see `deps` text) that does not happen inside this cell.
- **No visible "gene" as a discrete glowing/marked object in the nucleus** — DNA in a nucleus is not visually distinguishable locus-by-locus; do not fabricate a highlighted spot, ribbon, or double-helix icon labelled "spider gene." If a nucleus-side note is wanted, it must be conveyed only through label text, never as an invented visual marker.
- **No udder or goat anatomy** — this is a single cell, not an animal; no fur, no hooves, no whole-organ silhouette.
- **No bacterial cell wall, nucleoid, plasmid ring, or flagellum** — unless a render is *explicitly and separately* built to depict the bacterial-route variant (not the primary depiction for this entry), keep this a eukaryotic secretory cell.
- **No spinning-duct anatomy (spider ampullate gland structures, valve, taper, draw-down zone)** — that belongs to the spider's own spinning apparatus, a different subject; do not borrow its geometry to decorate this cell.
- **No milk fat globules or casein micelles** unless a specific render simply and clearly justifies including ordinary milk components as background context — they are not part of the spidroin secretory story and risk implying the protein look different from any other secreted protein.
- General atlas rules: no baked-in text/letters/numbers/scale bars/watermarks, no black border/frame/vignette/letterbox.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 A spider-silk-producing cell is a host cell — most often a goat mammary epithelial cell, alternatively E. coli or a silkworm cell line — stably transformed with one or more spidroin genes (e.g. MaSp1/MaSp2 from Nephila clavipes), typically placed under a tissue-specific promoter so expression is restricted to the appropriate secretory context. The transgene is translated and co-translationally translocated into the rough endoplasmic reticulum like any other secreted protein, folded and initially processed there, transported via COPII vesicles to the Golgi apparatus for further processing and sorting, and concentrated into secretory vesicles that traffic to the apical plasma membrane and release their cargo by exocytosis — the unmodified constitutive/regulated secretory pathway of the host cell, unaltered except for which protein is now among its cargo. The most cited proof-of-concept work (Lazaris et al., 2002, Science) expressed soluble recombinant dragline silk protein constructs (ADF-3/MaSp2- and MaSp1-based) in mammalian cell culture and wet-spun the purified, soluble protein into fibers with tensile properties approaching, but not matching, native spider dragline silk; the same soluble-protein output is what a transgenic goat's mammary cells secrete into milk. Converting the secreted monomer into an ordered, hierarchically structured fiber requires a downstream spinning process — controlled shear, a pH drop, and progressive dehydration — engineered to reproduce the physicochemical gradient of a spider's major ampullate gland and duct; this conformational transition (largely disorder-to-beta-sheet) does not occur inside the host cell and remains only partially replicated by artificial spinning systems.  
🇩🇪 Eine Spinnenseiden-produzierende Zelle ist eine Wirtszelle — meist eine Ziegenmilchdrüsenzelle, alternativ E. coli oder eine Seidenraupen-Zelllinie —, die stabil mit einem oder mehreren Spidroin-Genen (z. B. MaSp1/MaSp2 aus Nephila clavipes) transformiert wurde, meist unter Kontrolle eines gewebespezifischen Promotors, sodass die Expression auf den passenden sekretorischen Kontext beschränkt bleibt. Das Transgen wird wie jedes andere sekretierte Protein translatiert und cotranslational ins raue endoplasmatische Retikulum eingeschleust, dort gefaltet und initial prozessiert, über COPII-Vesikel zum Golgi-Apparat für die weitere Prozessierung und Sortierung transportiert und in sekretorischen Vesikeln konzentriert, die zur apikalen Plasmamembran wandern und ihre Fracht durch Exozytose freisetzen — der unveränderte konstitutive bzw. regulierte Sekretionsweg der Wirtszelle, unverändert bis auf das zusätzliche Protein in seiner Fracht. Die meistzitierte Machbarkeitsstudie (Lazaris et al., 2002, Science) exprimierte lösliche rekombinante Fangfaden-Proteinkonstrukte (auf ADF-3/MaSp2- und MaSp1-Basis) in Säugerzellkultur und versponn das gereinigte, lösliche Protein nass zu Fasern mit Zugfestigkeitswerten, die sich der nativen Spinnenseide annäherten, sie aber nicht erreichten; genau dieses lösliche Proteinprodukt scheiden auch die Milchdrüsenzellen einer transgenen Ziege in die Milch aus. Die Umwandlung des sekretierten Monomers in eine geordnete, hierarchisch strukturierte Faser erfordert einen nachgeschalteten Spinnprozess — kontrollierte Scherkräfte, einen pH-Abfall und fortschreitenden Wasserentzug —, der den physikochemischen Gradienten der großen Ampullendrüse und ihres Spinnkanals bei einer Spinne nachbilden soll; dieser Konformationsübergang (überwiegend von ungeordnet zu Beta-Faltblatt) findet nicht in der Wirtszelle statt und wird von künstlichen Spinnsystemen bislang nur teilweise nachvollzogen.
