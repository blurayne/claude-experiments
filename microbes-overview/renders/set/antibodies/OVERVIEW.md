## Immunoglobulin A (IgA) (`iga`)
Secretory IgA — two Y-shaped IgA monomers joined tail-to-tail at their Fc stems by a J-chain, wrapped in a protective secretory component picked up while crossing the mucosal epithelium; the guardian antibody of saliva, tears, gut and breast milk.

## Immunoglobulin D (IgD) (`igd`)
The "training-wheels" antibody of the naive B cell - a single T/Y-shaped monomer of two heavy (delta) and two light chains, co-expressed with IgM on the surface of every mature naive B cell via a short transmembrane anchor as part of the B-cell receptor, and marked by the longest, most flexible hinge of any human immunoglobulin.

## Immunoglobulin E (IgE) (`ige`)
The rarest serum antibody and the allergy trigger - a single ~190 kDa monomer of two heavy (epsilon) and two light chains with NO hinge region; an extra pair of constant domains (Cε2-Cε4) makes the Fc fold back on itself into an acutely bent conformation, giving the whole molecule a curved, fishhook-like silhouette rather than a flat Y.

## Immunoglobulin G (IgG) (`igg`)
The most abundant antibody in human blood (~75% of serum immunoglobulin) - a single Y-shaped monomer of two heavy and two light chains, disulfide-linked, with two identical antigen-binding Fab arms on a flexible hinge and one Fc stem; four subclasses (IgG1-4) differ mainly in hinge length and disulfide count.

## Immunoglobulin M (IgM) (`igm`)
The largest human antibody and the first responder of the immune system - normally a star/wheel-shaped PENTAMER of five Y-shaped monomers, their Fc stems joined at a central hub by a single J chain, with all ten Fab arms pointing outward; flat in solution, it bends into a staple/umbrella shape once it grips a multivalent antigen surface.
