## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

IgG is not alive and has no membrane, cytoplasm or organelles — it is a single soluble glycoprotein, about 150 kDa and roughly 10–15 nm across its long axis. It is built from **four polypeptide chains**: two identical **heavy chains** (γ, ~50 kDa each) and two identical **light chains** (κ or λ, ~25 kDa each), held together by a mix of **interchain and intrachain disulfide bonds**. Each chain folds into repeating ~110-residue **immunoglobulin domains** (a beta-sandwich stabilized internally by one disulfide bond per domain): each light chain has one variable domain (VL) and one constant domain (CL); each heavy chain has one variable domain (VH) followed by three constant domains (CH1, CH2, CH3). A heavy chain pairs with a light chain (VH+VL, CH1+CL) to form each of the two arms; the two heavy chains pair with each other mainly through CH3 and through the hinge. The overall shape is a distorted **Y** (sometimes drawn as a T, since the angle between the arms is flexible and varies between molecules): two identical **Fab fragments** (fragment antigen-binding — VH-CH1 paired with VL-CL) form the arms, joined via a short flexible **hinge region** (rich in proline and cysteine, holds the interheavy-chain disulfide bonds) to a single **Fc fragment** (fragment crystallizable — the paired CH2-CH3 domains of both heavy chains) forming the stem. At the very tip of each Fab arm, the VH and VL domains together create the **antigen-binding site**, whose specificity is set by six hypervariable loops (three from VH, three from VL) called **complementarity-determining regions (CDRs)** — this is where IgG binds one specific epitope with high affinity. The Fc stem carries a conserved **N-linked glycan** (attached at asparagine 297 in each CH2 domain) that is required for proper Fc-receptor and complement engagement, and it is the Fc stem — not the Fab arms — that is recognized by Fc receptors on phagocytes/NK cells and by the complement protein C1q, recruiting the rest of the immune system to whatever the Fab arms have grabbed. Humans have four IgG subclasses (IgG1–IgG4) that are >90% identical in sequence but differ chiefly in hinge length and the number of interheavy-chain disulfide bonds, which changes their flexibility and effector functions (e.g. IgG3 has the longest hinge and most disulfides; IgG4 is the most flexible and poorly activates complement). IgG is produced by plasma cells (terminally differentiated B cells) after class-switch recombination, and it is the only antibody class that crosses the human placenta (via the neonatal Fc receptor, FcRn), giving passive immunity to a fetus/newborn.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell membrane enclosing the whole thing** — IgG is a free-floating soluble protein, not a cell (contrast with membrane-bound IgD/IgM on a B cell, which is not what is drawn here).
- **No nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, cytoplasm or any organelles** — this is a single protein molecule, not a cell.
- **No random blob / amorphous shape** — the Y topology (two arms + one stem) is exact and must stay recognizable in every style, including cutaways.
- **No anthropomorphic face, eyes or expression.**
- **Do not draw more than two arms** — IgG is a single Y-shaped monomer (unlike pentameric IgM's five joined Y-units or dimeric IgA's two).
- **No J-chain or secretory component** — those belong to IgM/secretory IgA, not IgG.
- **Do not make the hinge rigid/straight** — it is a flexible joint; the two arms should read as independently posable, not welded at a fixed angle.
- **Do not omit or hide the disulfide bonds** — at least the interchain ones at the hinge should be visible as short cross-links, not invented as random extra loops.

## 3. Audience descriptions (EN + DE)

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet Immunoglobulin G, or IgG for short - the body's long-term memory keeper! IgG isn't a living cell; it's a tiny Y-shaped tool your body custom-builds, with two sticky hands at the top of the Y that grab onto one exact kind of germ, and a handle at the bottom that other immune cells can grab onto to come help clean up. The first time a new germ shows up, your body needs a few days to design the perfect IgG for it - but once it has learned the trick, it keeps a copy of the recipe on file for years, sometimes for your whole life, so if that same germ ever comes back, your body can build a fresh batch almost instantly. That's exactly how many vaccines work: they let your body practise building the right IgG ahead of time, before the real germ ever shows up. IgG is also the only antibody special enough to cross from a mom to her baby before birth, so newborns start life with a little borrowed protection already on board.  
🇩🇪 Das ist Immunglobulin G, kurz IgG - der Gedächtnis-Bewahrer des Körpers! IgG ist keine lebende Zelle, sondern ein winziges Y-förmiges Werkzeug, das dein Körper maßgeschneidert baut: Oben am Y sitzen zwei klebrige Hände, die sich an genau eine bestimmte Sorte Keim festhalten, und unten ist ein Griff, an dem sich andere Immunzellen festhalten können, um zu helfen. **Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin G (IgG) is the workhorse of long-term humoral immunity and makes up roughly three-quarters of the antibody circulating in your blood. Structurally it's a single Y-shaped protein - two heavy and two light chains stitched together with disulfide bonds - with two identical arms that grab onto one specific target and a stem that recruits the rest of the immune system once that target has been grabbed. IgG usually doesn't show up in force until several days into a first infection, but once your immune system has built it, memory B cells keep the blueprint on file, which is why a second exposure, or a vaccine, typically triggers a much faster and stronger IgG response the next time around. It's also the only antibody class that crosses the placenta, so a newborn arrives already carrying a starter set of its mother's IgG for the first months of life.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin G (IgG) ist das Arbeitspferd der langfristigen humoralen Immunität und macht etwa drei Viertel der Antikörper in deinem Blut aus. Strukturell ist es ein einzelnes Y-förmiges Protein - zwei schwere und zwei leichte Ketten, durch Disulfidbrücken zusammengehalten - mit zwei identischen Armen, die sich an ein ganz bestimmtes Ziel klammern, und einem Stamm, der den Rest des Immunsystems herbeiruft, sobald dieses Ziel gepackt ist. Bei einer ersten Infektion taucht IgG meist erst nach einigen Tagen in größerer Menge auf, doch sobald das Immunsystem es einmal gebaut hat, bewahren Gedächtnis-B-Zellen den Bauplan auf - weshalb ein zweiter Kontakt, etwa durch eine Impfung, in der Regel eine viel schnellere und stärkere IgG-Antwort auslöst. IgG ist zudem die einzige Antikörperklasse, die die Plazenta überquert, sodass ein Neugeborenes für die ersten Lebensmonate bereits einen Grundvorrat mütterlichen IgGs mitbringt.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin G is the dominant serum immunoglobulin isotype (~75% of total Ig, roughly 8-16 mg/mL in adult serum) and the principal product of the secondary (memory) humoral response following B-cell class-switch recombination to Cγ. It is a ~150 kDa heterotetramer (two heavy and two light chains) with the canonical two-Fab / hinge / Fc architecture: each Fab arm pairs a heavy-chain VH-CH1 with a light-chain VL-CL, its antigen specificity set by somatically hypermutated CDR loops, while the paired CH2-CH3 domains of both heavy chains form the Fc stem that engages Fcγ receptors and complement C1q. The four human subclasses (IgG1-4) share >90% sequence identity but differ in hinge length and interheavy-chain disulfide number, and consequently in effector profile - IgG1 and IgG3 are the most potent activators of classical-pathway complement fixation and Fcγ-receptor-mediated phagocytosis/ADCC, whereas IgG4 is comparatively inert and prone to Fab-arm exchange. IgG's serum half-life of roughly three weeks - the longest of any isotype - is sustained by pH-dependent recycling through the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), the same receptor that mediates active transplacental transport and confers passive immunity to the fetus and neonate.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin G ist der dominante Serum-Immunglobulin-Isotyp (~75 % des Gesamt-Ig, etwa 8-16 mg/mL im Serum Erwachsener) und das Hauptprodukt der sekundären (Gedächtnis-)humoralen Antwort nach Klassenwechsel-Rekombination der B-Zelle zu Cγ. Es ist ein ~150 kDa schweres Heterotetramer (zwei schwere und zwei leichte Ketten) mit der klassischen Architektur aus zwei Fab-Armen, Gelenkregion und Fc-Stamm: Jeder Fab-Arm verbindet ein VH-CH1 der schweren Kette mit einem VL-CL der leichten Kette, wobei die Antigenspezifität durch somatisch hypermutierte CDR-Schleifen festgelegt wird, während die gepaarten CH2-CH3-Domänen beider schwerer Ketten den Fc-Stamm bilden, der Fcγ-Rezeptoren und das Komplementprotein C1q bindet. Die vier humanen Subklassen (IgG1-4) sind zu über 90 % sequenzidentisch, unterscheiden sich aber in Gelenklänge und Anzahl der Disulfidbrücken zwischen den schweren Ketten und damit im Effektorprofil - IgG1 und IgG3 aktivieren den klassischen Komplementweg (C1q-Bindung) sowie Fcγ-Rezeptor-vermittelte Phagozytose/ADCC am stärksten, während IgG4 vergleichsweise inert ist und zum Fab-Arm-Austausch neigt. Die Serumhalbwertszeit von IgG - mit rund drei Wochen die längste aller Isotypen - wird durch pH-abhängiges Recycling über den neonatalen Fc-Rezeptor (FcRn) aufrechterhalten, denselben Rezeptor, der auch den aktiven plazentaren Transport vermittelt und dem Fetus bzw. Neugeborenen passive Immunität verleiht.
