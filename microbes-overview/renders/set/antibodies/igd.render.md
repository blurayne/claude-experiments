## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

IgD is not alive and has no cytoplasm or organelles — it is a glycoprotein built, like every antibody, from **four polypeptide chains**: two identical **heavy chains** (δ, "delta", ~64 kDa each) and two identical **light chains** (κ or λ, ~25 kDa each), covalently linked by disulfide bonds and non-covalent domain pairing. Each light chain folds into one variable domain (VL) and one constant domain (CL); each heavy chain folds into one variable domain (VH) followed by **three constant domains** (Cδ1, Cδ2, Cδ3) — the same domain count as IgG and IgA (unlike four-domain IgM/IgE). A heavy chain pairs with a light chain (VH+VL, Cδ1+CL) to form each of the two identical **Fab arms** (fragment antigen-binding); the two heavy chains pair with each other mainly through Cδ3 to form the shared **Fc stem** (fragment crystallizable, the paired Cδ2-Cδ3 domains). At the tip of each Fab arm the VH and VL domains together form the **antigen-binding site**, its specificity set by six hypervariable **complementarity-determining region (CDR)** loops (three from VH, three from VL). What sets IgD apart structurally is its **hinge region**: at roughly 64 residues it is by far the longest and most flexible hinge of any human antibody class, heavily **O-glycosylated** (a dense fringe of short sugar chains that stiffens it into an extended, protease-exposed rod rather than a compact coil) and held together by only a **single inter-heavy-chain disulfide bond** near its Fc-proximal end — far fewer cross-links than IgG's hinge. Because the two arms are not held close together, solution X-ray scattering of intact IgD (PDB 1ZVO) shows the molecule adopting a predominantly **T-shaped** (sometimes Y- or inverted-Y-shaped) conformation, with the two Fab arms projecting away from each other and from the Fc stem rather than folding back toward it. IgD exists in two forms built from the same rearranged VDJ gene segment by alternative RNA splicing/polyadenylation of a single primary transcript that also encodes IgM: a minor **secreted, soluble form** (well under 1% of serum immunoglobulin, short serum half-life of a few days) and — far more importantly — a **membrane-bound form (mIgD)**. In mIgD, the C-terminal end of each heavy chain is extended past Cδ3 by a short extracellular **membrane-proximal "spacer" segment** (~26 residues), then a hydrophobic **transmembrane anchor** that threads through the plasma membrane, ending in a very short **cytoplasmic tail** of just three residues (Lys-Val-Lys, "KVK" — too short to signal on its own). Mature, naive peripheral B cells co-express mIgD alongside mIgM as their antigen-recognition receptors, both anchored in the same small patch of B-cell surface membrane and both non-covalently paired with the invariant signal-transducing **Igα/Igβ (CD79a/CD79b) heterodimer** to form the complete B-cell receptor (BCR) complex — the transmembrane anchor and cytoplasmic tail shown here belong to IgD itself, not to Igα/Igβ. mIgD appears on the B-cell surface shortly after mIgM, as an immature B cell exits the bone marrow and matures into a naive peripheral B cell, and its co-expression is often read as a marker of that naive, antigen-inexperienced state — cells typically downregulate mIgD once they differentiate into memory B cells or antibody-secreting plasma cells. Its precise signalling role alongside mIgM is still not fully resolved, though it is proposed to help set the threshold for B-cell activation/tolerance and to extend the functional lifespan of naive B cells; secreted IgD, meanwhile, has been reported to bind basophils and mast cells and to activate antimicrobial/pro-inflammatory programs relevant to upper-airway mucosal defence.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell membrane enclosing the whole thing** — only a SMALL local patch of plasma membrane beneath the Fc stem/transmembrane anchor, not a full cell outline; IgD is not itself a cell, it merely sits anchored in one.
- **No nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, cytoplasm or any organelles** — this is a protein anchored at a membrane, not a whole cell.
- **No random blob / amorphous shape** — the T/Y topology (two Fab arms + one Fc stem + a distinct long hinge) is exact and must stay recognizable in every style, including cutaways.
- **No anthropomorphic face, eyes or expression.**
- **Do not draw more than two Fab arms** — IgD is a single monomer (unlike pentameric IgM's five joined units or dimeric IgA's two).
- **No J-chain or secretory component** — those belong to pentameric IgM / secretory IgA, not IgD.
- **Do not draw the hinge as short or rigid** — it must read as visibly LONGER and thinner/more extended than a typical IgG-style hinge, since that unusual length is IgD's defining structural feature.
- **Do not draw Igα/Igβ (CD79a/CD79b) as part of the IgD molecule itself** — they are separate signalling proteins of the BCR complex and are out of scope for this label set.
- **Do not draw IgM alongside IgD** — although both sit on the same B cell in life, this image shows IgD alone.

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet Immunoglobulin D, or IgD for short - think of it as the training-wheels antibody! Almost every antibody floats freely in your blood looking for germs, but IgD is different: it stays bolted to the outside of a brand-new B cell, like a little sensor sticking out of the cell's skin. Right next to it sits its buddy IgM, and together they're the very first pair of "feelers" a fresh B cell grows so it can test the world and figure out what it's supposed to recognise. **Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin D (IgD) is a bit of an oddball among antibodies: instead of circulating freely to hunt down antigens, it's found mainly bolted to the surface of freshly matured, naive B cells - the immune cells that haven't yet met their target antigen. There, sitting right alongside its close relative IgM, IgD forms one half of the B-cell receptor, the sensor a young B cell uses to sample its surroundings before committing to a fight. What makes IgD structurally distinctive is its unusually long, floppy hinge - by far the longest of any human antibody class - which lets its two antigen-grabbing arms swing around more freely than in other antibodies. Only a tiny fraction of IgD is ever released into the bloodstream as a free-floating antibody, and even then its job remains only partly understood, with some evidence pointing to a role in surveillance of the upper airways. Once a B cell finally locks onto its target and matures into an antibody-secreting cell, it typically stops making IgD altogether and switches to producing a different, more specialised antibody class instead.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin D (IgD) ist ein bisschen ein Außenseiter unter den Antikörpern: Statt frei zu zirkulieren und Antigene aufzuspüren, sitzt es hauptsächlich fest verankert auf der Oberfläche frisch gereifter, unerfahrener B-Zellen - jener Immunzellen, die ihrem Zielantigen noch nicht begegnet sind. Dort bildet IgD, direkt neben seinem engen Verwandten IgM, eine Hälfte des B-Zell-Rezeptors, des Sensors, mit dem eine junge B-Zelle ihre Umgebung abtastet, bevor sie sich auf einen Kampf einlässt. Strukturell zeichnet sich IgD durch eine ungewöhnlich lange, biegsame Gelenkregion aus - mit Abstand die längste aller menschlichen Antikörperklassen -, die es seinen beiden antigenbindenden Armen erlaubt, sich freier zu bewegen als bei anderen Antikörpern. Nur ein winziger Bruchteil des IgD wird jemals als frei zirkulierender Antikörper ins Blut abgegeben, und selbst dessen Aufgabe ist nur teilweise verstanden - manche Hinweise deuten auf eine Rolle bei der Überwachung der oberen Atemwege hin. Sobald eine B-Zelle schließlich ihr Ziel findet und zur antikörperproduzierenden Zelle heranreift, stellt sie die IgD-Produktion meist komplett ein und wechselt zu einer anderen, spezialisierteren Antikörperklasse.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin D is a ~180 kDa heterotetramer (two δ heavy chains, two κ or λ light chains) that, unlike the other isotypes, exists predominantly as a membrane-bound receptor (mIgD) rather than a secreted serum antibody; free serum IgD makes up well under 1% of total immunoglobulin. Each δ chain carries three constant domains (Cδ1-Cδ3, matching IgG/IgA rather than the four-domain IgM/IgE) joined to VH by an exceptionally long (~64-residue), heavily O-glycosylated hinge stabilized by only a single interheavy-chain disulfide bond - structurally the most extended and flexible hinge of any human isotype, giving intact IgD a predominantly T-shaped (with Y/inverted-Y contributions) solution conformation rather than the compact Y of IgG. mIgD is generated, together with mIgM, by alternative splicing/polyadenylation of a single VDJ-Cμ-Cδ primary transcript, and is anchored via a C-terminal extracellular spacer, a transmembrane segment, and a minimal three-residue (Lys-Val-Lys) cytoplasmic tail, pairing non-covalently with the Igα/Igβ (CD79a/CD79b) signal-transduction heterodimer to constitute the B-cell receptor on mature, antigen-inexperienced naive B cells; mIgD expression is downregulated upon terminal differentiation to memory B cells or plasma cells. Its precise contribution to BCR signalling beyond that of co-expressed mIgM remains incompletely defined, though it has been implicated in modulating activation thresholds and peripheral B-cell survival, while secreted IgD has been reported to engage basophils/mast cells and activate antimicrobial and pro-inflammatory transcriptional programs relevant to upper-respiratory mucosal immunity.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin D ist ein ~180 kDa schweres Heterotetramer (zwei δ-Schwerketten, zwei κ- oder λ-Leichtketten), das im Gegensatz zu den anderen Isotypen überwiegend als membrangebundener Rezeptor (mIgD) und nicht als sezerniertes Serum-Antikörper vorliegt; freies Serum-IgD macht deutlich unter 1 % des gesamten Immunglobulins aus. Jede δ-Kette trägt drei konstante Domänen (Cδ1-Cδ3, wie bei IgG/IgA statt der vierdomänigen IgM/IgE), die über eine außergewöhnlich lange (~64 Reste), stark O-glykosylierte Gelenkregion mit nur einer einzigen Disulfidbrücke zwischen den schweren Ketten mit VH verbunden ist - strukturell die am weitesten gestreckte und beweglichste Gelenkregion aller menschlichen Isotypen, wodurch intaktes IgD in Lösung überwiegend eine T-förmige (mit Y-/invers-Y-Anteilen) Konformation statt des kompakten Y von IgG einnimmt. mIgD entsteht zusammen mit mIgM durch alternatives Spleißen/Polyadenylierung eines einzigen VDJ-Cμ-Cδ-Primärtranskripts und ist über einen extrazellulären C-terminalen Spacer, ein Transmembransegment und einen minimalen dreiteiligen (Lys-Val-Lys) zytoplasmatischen Schwanz verankert; es paart sich nichtkovalent mit dem signalübertragenden Igα/Igβ-Heterodimer (CD79a/CD79b) zum B-Zell-Rezeptor auf reifen, antigen-unerfahrenen naiven B-Zellen, wobei die mIgD-Expression bei der terminalen Differenzierung zu Gedächtnis-B-Zellen oder Plasmazellen herunterreguliert wird. Sein genauer Beitrag zur BCR-Signalgebung über das koexprimierte mIgM hinaus ist noch nicht vollständig geklärt, wird aber mit der Modulation von Aktivierungsschwellen und dem Überleben peripherer B-Zellen in Verbindung gebracht, während sezerniertes IgD Berichten zufolge Basophile und Mastzellen aktiviert und antimikrobielle sowie proinflammatorische Genprogramme mit Bezug zur mukosalen Immunität der oberen Atemwege in Gang setzt.
