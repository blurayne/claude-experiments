## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

IgM is not alive and has no membrane, cytoplasm or organelles — like all antibodies it is a soluble (or membrane-tethered, on naive B cells) glycoprotein assembly. It is the **largest** of the five human antibody classes: where IgG is a single ~150 kDa Y-shaped monomer, secreted IgM is normally a **pentamer** — five identical Y-shaped monomer subunits, each built the same way as an IgG unit (two identical **heavy chains**, here called **μ heavy chains**, ~70 kDa each, paired with two identical **light chains**, κ or λ, ~25 kDa each) — joined together into one ~970 kDa, roughly 30 nm-wide assembly. Each μ heavy chain carries one variable domain (VH) plus **four** constant domains (Cμ1–Cμ4), one more constant domain than IgG's γ chain; critically, IgM has **no flexible hinge region** — the extra Cμ2 domain occupies the position a hinge would in IgG, so the arm-to-stem junction is comparatively rigid at the monomer level (the pentamer's own flexibility comes from a separate joint further down, at the Cμ2/Cμ3 interface). Each monomer thus presents two **Fab arms** (fragment antigen-binding — VH+Cμ1 paired with VL+CL, each ending in a variable-region antigen-binding site) and one **Fc region** (fragment crystallizable — the paired Cμ2–Cμ4 domains of both heavy chains, called **Fcμ**). The defining feature of the pentamer is how the five monomers join: each heavy chain ends in an 18-residue **tailpiece** bearing a penultimate cysteine, and these five tailpieces plus a single small (~15 kDa) **J chain** (joining chain) pack together into an amyloid-like bundle at the very centre of the molecule, cross-linked by interchain disulfide bonds. This tailpiece/J-chain bundle is the **central pentameric core (Fc hub)**: the five Fc regions converge there, and the ten Fab arms (two per monomer × five monomers) radiate outward from it. Classic negative-stain EM and solution-scattering studies (e.g. PDB 2RCJ, used as the reference below) depict this as a flat, roughly symmetric **five-pointed star or wheel** free in solution. Modern cryo-EM of the isolated Fc/J-chain core (e.g. PDB 6KXS, 7K0C) shows the core itself is actually slightly **asymmetric** — closer to a hexagon with one wedge missing, because the J chain occupies the space of a "sixth" subunit — but the whole-molecule, ten-Fab-arm star/wheel silhouette remains the standard and structurally accurate teaching depiction, which is what is drawn here. Upon binding a multivalent antigen surface (e.g. a bacterial cell wall), the flat star bends: the Fab arms tilt down like the legs of a staple or the ribs of an umbrella, an event which exposes Cμ3 and lets the Fc hub engage complement protein **C1q**, making IgM an extremely efficient complement activator — a single bound pentamer can trigger the classical complement cascade, unlike IgG which typically needs two nearby molecules. IgM is the **first antibody class produced** in a primary immune response (secreted by newly activated plasma cells before class-switching to IgG/IgA/IgE), and it is also the antibody expressed as the **B-cell receptor** on naive/immature B cells, there as a single membrane-bound monomer (not the secreted pentamer) paired with signalling co-receptors. Because of its size, secreted pentameric IgM stays largely confined to the bloodstream and lymph and does not cross the placenta (unlike IgG), so IgM detected in a newborn's blood indicates the baby's own immune system made it — a marker of congenital infection. A J-chain-free **hexameric** form (six monomers, no central J chain, an even more potent complement activator) can also form under some circumstances but is not the default; the pentamer with J chain is the physiological norm and the one illustrated here.

Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf — Janeway's Immunobiology, 9th ed., Ch. 5 "Antigen Recognition by B-cell and T-cell Receptors"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27114/), [Li, Wang et al. 2020, "Structural insights into immunoglobulin M", Science 369:1223-1230 (PDB 6KXS)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaz5425), [Kumar, Marches et al. 2020, "Structure of Secretory IgM Core", PDB 7K0C](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7K0C), [Perkins, Nealis, Sutton & Feinstein 1991, "Solution structure of human IgM", J Mol Biol 221:1345-1366 (PDB 2RCJ)](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2RCJ), [Chen, Zheng et al. 2022, "Cryomicroscopy reveals the structural basis for a flexible hinge motion in the immunoglobulin M pentamer", Nat Commun 13:6314](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34090-2), [Wikipedia — Immunoglobulin M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_M).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell membrane enclosing the whole thing** — this is the free, secreted pentameric IgM, a soluble protein assembly, not a cell (contrast with membrane-bound monomeric IgM on a B-cell receptor, which is not what is drawn here).
- **No nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, cytoplasm or any organelles** — this is a protein complex, not a cell.
- **No random blob / amorphous shape** — the radial pentamer topology (5 Y-units, Fc stems at a central hub, 10 Fab arms pointing outward) is exact and must stay recognizable in every style, including cutaways.
- **No anthropomorphic face, eyes or expression.**
- **Do not draw fewer or more than 5 monomer units / 10 Fab arms** — a tetramer, hexamer-look-alike, or a simple single Y (that would be IgG) are all wrong for this default pentameric form.
- **Do not give the monomers a flexible IgG-style hinge between Fab and Fc** — IgM has no hinge; the extra Cμ2 domain sits where a hinge would be, so each arm-to-stem junction should read as comparatively rigid/straight, not floppy.
- **Do not omit the J chain or the central disulfide-linked hub** — it is the defining connector that holds the five monomers together; never draw the five units joined only by touching Fab arms.
- **Do not draw the bent "staple/umbrella" antigen-bound conformation as the default pose** — the free, unbound resting state is the flat star/wheel; the bent form only happens on binding a multivalent surface and is a secondary/optional detail, not the primary depiction.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin M (IgM) is the first antibody your immune system deploys against a new pathogen, appearing within days of a first exposure, well before more targeted antibodies like IgG show up. Structurally it's the giant of the antibody family: five Y-shaped units bolted together tail-to-tail around a small central hub (held in place by a small connector protein called the J chain), giving it ten antigen-grabbing arms instead of the usual two. That's a deliberate trade-off: IgM's grip on any single target is comparatively weak, but with ten arms working together it doesn't need to be precise to hold on, which is exactly what's needed in the earliest, least fine-tuned phase of an immune response. IgM is also unusually good at switching on the complement system, the immune system's backup demolition crew, and because a fresh pentamer is too large to cross the placenta, finding pathogen-specific IgM in a newborn's blood is a sign the infection happened before birth rather than being passed down from the mother.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin M (IgM) ist der Antikörper, den dein Immunsystem als Erstes gegen einen neuen Erreger einsetzt - er taucht schon wenige Tage nach dem ersten Kontakt auf, lange bevor gezielter wirkende Antikörper wie IgG bereitstehen. Strukturell ist es der Riese der Antikörperfamilie: fünf Y-förmige Einheiten, die an ihren Enden um eine kleine zentrale Nabe herum zusammengeschraubt sind (gehalten von einem kleinen Verbindungsprotein namens J-Kette), was ihm zehn Greifarme statt der üblichen zwei verschafft. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Immunoglobulin M is the phylogenetically oldest antibody isotype and the first class secreted during a primary humoral immune response, produced before activation-induced-cytidine-deaminase-mediated class-switch recombination to IgG, IgA or IgE. Its secreted form assembles as a ~970 kDa pentamer of five identical monomers, each heavy (mu) chain contributing a VH domain plus four constant domains (C(mu)1-C(mu)4) rather than IgG's three-domain-plus-hinge arrangement, so the monomer itself has no flexible hinge. The five monomers are held together by disulfide-bonded C-terminal tailpieces packed with a single ~15 kDa J chain into a central Fc(mu) core, presenting ten comparatively low-affinity, germline-proximal Fab arms whose combined avidity compensates for the limited somatic hypermutation typical of an early primary response. This pentameric geometry also makes IgM the most potent single-molecule activator of the classical complement pathway of any isotype: antigen engagement on a multivalent surface drives a conformational transition from the planar 'star' to a bent 'staple' or dome conformation that exposes a C1q-binding surface on C(mu)3, letting one bound IgM molecule fix complement, whereas IgG typically requires two adjacent bound molecules. A J-chain-independent hexameric assembly can also form and is an even more efficient complement activator; monomeric, membrane-anchored IgM additionally serves, together with Ig(alpha)/Ig(beta) signalling subunits, as the B-cell receptor on naive and immature B cells. Owing to its size (~30 nm, ~970 kDa) secreted pentameric IgM remains largely confined to the vascular and lymphatic compartments and does not cross the placenta, so pathogen-specific IgM in neonatal serum is diagnostic of congenital infection.  
🇩🇪 Immunglobulin M ist der phylogenetisch älteste Antikörper-Isotyp und die erste Klasse, die in einer primären humoralen Immunantwort sezerniert wird, noch bevor die durch aktivierungsinduzierte Cytidin-Desaminase vermittelte Klassenwechsel-Rekombination zu IgG, IgA oder IgE einsetzt. In sezernierter Form bildet es ein ca. 970 kDa schweres Pentamer aus fünf identischen Monomeren; jede schwere (mu-)Kette steuert eine VH-Domäne plus vier konstante Domänen (C(mu)1-C(mu)4) bei statt der drei Domänen plus Gelenkregion von IgG, sodass das Monomer selbst keine flexible Gelenkregion besitzt. Die fünf Monomere werden durch disulfidverbrückte C-terminale Tailpieces zusammengehalten, die zusammen mit einer einzelnen ca. 15 kDa schweren J-Kette einen zentralen Fc(mu)-Kern bilden, von dem zehn vergleichsweise niedrigaffine, keimbahnnahe Fab-Arme ausgehen, deren gemeinsame Avidität die begrenzte somatische Hypermutation einer frühen Primärantwort ausgleicht. Diese pentamere Geometrie macht IgM zudem zum wirksamsten Ein-Molekül-Aktivator des klassischen Komplementwegs unter allen Isotypen: Die Bindung an eine multivalente Antigenoberfläche löst einen Konformationswechsel von der planaren 'Stern'- in eine gebogene 'Klammer'- oder Kuppelform aus, die eine C1q-Bindungsfläche an C(mu)3 freilegt, sodass ein einzelnes gebundenes IgM-Molekül Komplement aktivieren kann, während IgG dafür meist zwei benachbarte gebundene Moleküle benötigt. Es kann sich auch eine J-Ketten-unabhängige, hexamere Form bilden, die Komplement sogar noch wirksamer aktiviert; monomeres, membranverankertes IgM dient zudem zusammen mit den Signaluntereinheiten Ig(alpha)/Ig(beta) als B-Zell-Rezeptor auf naiven und unreifen B-Zellen. Aufgrund seiner Größe (ca. 30 nm, ca. 970 kDa) bleibt sezerniertes pentameres IgM weitgehend auf das Gefäß- und Lymphsystem beschränkt und überquert die Plazenta nicht, weshalb erregerspezifisches IgM im Serum eines Neugeborenen auf eine konnatale Infektion hinweist.
