## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The tenocyte (tendon fibrocyte) is the resident fibroblast-lineage cell of tendon, and it makes up roughly 90–95% of tendon cellularity — the remainder being a small resident population of tendon stem/progenitor cells and vascular/perivascular cells confined to the surrounding paratenon and endotenon septa. Tenocytes are the mature, less proliferative counterpart of the more spindle-shaped, mitotically active tenoblast from which they differentiate; both share the same core job of building and maintaining the collagen-rich extracellular matrix that gives tendon its tensile strength. In longitudinal histological section, the classic and most recognisable appearance is a row of flattened, elongated nuclei lined up end-to-end in parallel "box-car" files squeezed between the tightly packed, parallel-running bundles of type I collagen (>95% of tendon collagen, with smaller amounts of types III and V); the thin sheet of cytoplasm around each nucleus is barely distinguishable by light microscopy from the surrounding eosinophilic collagen, which is exactly why tendon histology so often shows little more than rows of dark, spindle-shaped nuclei. Seen in transverse section or by electron/confocal microscopy, the true three-dimensional shape emerges: a stellate to triangular cell body with long, thin, sheet-like ("winglike") cytoplasmic processes that reach out, wrap around and interdigitate with individual collagen fibrils, and touch the processes of neighbouring tenocytes via gap junctions — this creates an extensive body-wide, three-dimensional intercellular network that lets mechanical strain sensed at one point in the tendon be communicated across many cells. Internally, a healthy, matrix-synthesising tenocyte carries a single flattened-oval nucleus with condensed peripheral chromatin, an unusually well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum organised in extensive folded sheets (reflecting a high secretory throughput of procollagen), a prominent Golgi apparatus for packaging and post-translational modification of procollagen, numerous free ribosomes, several small elongated mitochondria supplying ATP for this energetically demanding secretory work, and a cytoskeleton rich in actin microfilaments and vimentin intermediate filaments running the length of the cell body and out into its processes — this cytoskeletal network is central to mechanotransduction, converting matrix strain into the gene-expression changes that govern collagen and matrix-protein turnover. Functionally, tenocytes are strongly mechanoresponsive: cyclic tensile loading (as in normal use and exercise) upregulates collagen and matrix-protein synthesis and helps maintain tendon stiffness, whereas stress-shielding or prolonged immobilisation lets the matrix atrophy — the biological basis for progressive-loading exercise protocols used in tendon rehabilitation. Because tendon proper is hypovascular (blood supply comes mainly from the surrounding paratenon/epitenon and the myotendinous and osteotendinous junctions, not from vessels running through the collagen-dense core) and largely aneural except at those same margins, tenocyte-mediated repair after injury is characteristically slow, proceeding over many months through overlapping inflammatory, proliferative and remodelling phases, and the resulting scar/repair tissue remains biomechanically inferior to native, undamaged tendon.

Sources: [Kannus 2000 — Structure of the tendon connective tissue (Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1034/j.1600-0838.2000.010006312.x), [Wang 2006 — Mechanobiology of tendon (Journal of Biomechanics, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748947/), [McNeilly, Banes et al. 1996 — Tendon cells in vivo form a three-dimensional network of cell processes linked by gap junctions (Journal of Anatomy, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1167946/), [Wikipedia — Tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendon), [Wikipedia — Tendon cell (Tenocyte)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendon_cell).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids, capsule, or chloroplasts** — this is a human connective-tissue cell, not a bacterium or plant cell.
- **No large central vacuole** — that is a plant-cell feature, not present here.
- **No cilia or flagella** — the tenocyte does not swim or beat; its "processes" are thin, static, sheet-like winglike extensions that hug collagen fibrils, not motile appendages. Do not draw them as a eukaryotic axoneme tail either — that would belong to an entirely different cell type (e.g. sperm).
- **Not round, cuboidal or plump** — a tenocyte in longitudinal view is a slim, elongated, spindle shape roughly 4–5× longer than wide; in cross-section it is stellate/triangular with thin winglike processes, never a round blood-cell-like sphere.
- **Not cross-striated** — cross-striations (banding) belong to skeletal/cardiac muscle cells, not to tenocytes or the collagen fibres around them; collagen bundles are drawn as smooth, wavy, unbanded strands.
- **A single specimen as the clear subject** — a modest number of surrounding parallel collagen fibre bundles for context is fine, but keep them sparse enough that the cell body and its processes remain the clearly readable focal point, not lost in a dense thicket.
- **No dense fascicle of many crowded, indistinguishable nuclei** filling the frame — that reads as an unreadable clump rather than a single labelled specimen (acceptable only in the real micrograph reference, where the row-of-nuclei appearance is the authentic histological look).

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The tenocyte is the resident fibroblast of tendons - a slender, elongated cell that lines up end-to-end in longitudinal rows between the tightly packed, parallel bundles of type I collagen that give tendons their tensile strength. Their job is to continuously synthesise, organise and remodel the collagen-rich extracellular matrix in response to the mechanical load placed on the tendon: more use tends to stimulate matrix production, while immobilisation lets the tissue atrophy. This responsiveness is exactly why gradual, progressive loading is central to rehabilitating tendon injuries. Tendons themselves are relatively poorly supplied with blood vessels, which is a major reason tendon injuries such as Achilles tendinopathy or rotator-cuff tears are notoriously slow to heal compared with more vascular tissues.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Tenocytes (also termed tendon fibrocytes, the mature, less proliferative counterpart of the more spindle-shaped, mitotically active tenoblast) are specialised fibroblast-lineage cells that occupy roughly 90-95% of the cellularity of tendon and constitute its principal matrix-synthesising and mechanotransducing population. In longitudinal section they appear as flattened, elongated nuclei arranged in parallel rows (a 'box-car' pattern) between the collagen fascicles; in transverse section they present a triangular to stellate profile with long, thin, sheet-like cytoplasmic processes that ensheath and interdigitate with individual collagen fibrils and connect to neighbouring tenocytes via gap junctions, forming an extensive three-dimensional intercellular network revealed by confocal microscopy. Ultrastructurally, healthy tenocytes show a well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum and prominent Golgi apparatus reflecting high procollagen (type I collagen, >95% of tendon collagen, with smaller amounts of types III, V and others) secretory throughput, numerous free ribosomes, and a cytoskeleton rich in actin microfilaments and vimentin intermediate filaments that mediates mechanotransduction from matrix strain to gene expression governing matrix turnover. Tenocytes are mechanoresponsive: cyclic tensile loading upregulates collagen and matrix-protein synthesis, whereas stress-shielding/immobilisation leads to matrix catabolism and tissue atrophy, a relationship exploited clinically in progressive-loading tendon rehabilitation protocols. Because tendon proper is hypovascular and largely aneural except at the paratenon/epitenon, tenocyte-mediated repair after injury proceeds slowly through overlapping inflammatory, proliferative and remodelling phases that can take many months, and the resulting scar tissue is biomechanically inferior to native tendon.  
🇩🇪 Tenozyten (auch Sehnenfibrozyten genannt, die reife, weniger proliferative Form des spindelförmigeren, mitotisch aktiveren Tenoblasten) sind spezialisierte Zellen der Fibroblasten-Linie, die etwa 90-95% der Zellularität der Sehne ausmachen und deren wichtigste matrixsynthetisierende und mechanotransduzierende Population darstellen. Im Längsschnitt erscheinen sie als flache, längliche Zellkerne, die in parallelen Reihen (einem 'Waggon-Muster') zwischen den Kollagenfaszikeln angeordnet sind; im Querschnitt zeigen sie ein dreieckiges bis sternförmiges Profil mit langen, dünnen, blattartigen Zellfortsätzen, die einzelne Kollagenfibrillen umhüllen und über Gap Junctions mit Nachbarzellen verbunden sind, wodurch ein ausgedehntes dreidimensionales interzelluläres Netzwerk entsteht, das sich in der Konfokalmikroskopie zeigt. Ultrastrukturell weisen gesunde Tenozyten ein gut entwickeltes raues endoplasmatisches Retikulum und einen ausgeprägten Golgi-Apparat auf, was den hohen sekretorischen Durchsatz von Prokollagen (Typ-I-Kollagen, >95% des Sehnenkollagens, mit geringeren Anteilen der Typen III, V und anderer) widerspiegelt, dazu zahlreiche freie Ribosomen sowie ein an Aktin-Mikrofilamenten und Vimentin-Intermediärfilamenten reiches Zytoskelett, das die Mechanotransduktion von Matrixdehnung zu jener Genexpression vermittelt, die den Matrixumsatz steuert. Tenozyten sind mechanosensitiv: zyklische Zugbelastung steigert die Synthese von Kollagen und Matrixproteinen, während Entlastung/Ruhigstellung zu Matrixabbau und Gewebeatrophie führt - ein Zusammenhang, der klinisch in Rehabilitationsprotokollen mit schrittweiser Belastungssteigerung genutzt wird. Da das eigentliche Sehnengewebe hypovaskulär und, außer im Peritendineum/Epitendineum, weitgehend nervenfrei ist, verläuft die tenozytenvermittelte Reparatur nach einer Verletzung langsam über sich überlappende Entzündungs-, Proliferations- und Umbauphasen, die viele Monate dauern können, wobei das entstehende Narbengewebe dem nativen Sehnengewebe biomechanisch unterlegen bleibt.
