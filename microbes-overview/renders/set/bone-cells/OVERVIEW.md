## Chondrocyte (cartilage cell) (`chondrocyte`)
Round to ovoid cell sitting alone or in small isogenous groups inside a matrix-carved lacuna; rich rough ER and Golgi for producing/maintaining the collagen-II and proteoglycan cartilage matrix around it. Cartilage is avascular, so chondrocytes rely on diffusion and store glycogen/lipid as energy reserve.

## Osteoblast (`osteoblast`)
Cuboidal-to-columnar, matrix-bound bone-forming cell that lines the surface of growing/remodeling bone in a single epithelioid layer; intensely basophilic cytoplasm packed with rough ER and a prominent Golgi zone, secreting type I collagen and other osteoid proteins that it then mineralises with calcium. Once it walls itself inside its own matrix it becomes an osteocyte.

## Osteocyte (`osteocyte`)
Mature, star-shaped bone cell walled inside a lacuna within its own mineralised matrix, connected to dozens of neighbours by long dendritic processes running through canaliculi. Senses mechanical strain on the skeleton and orchestrates remodeling by signalling osteoblasts and osteoclasts (via sclerostin and the RANKL/OPG axis). Terminally differentiated from an osteoblast; can live for decades.

## Tenocyte (tendon cell) (`tenocyte`)
Slim, in-line spindle cell of tendons with thin winglike processes, packed with rough ER and Golgi to mass-produce type I collagen that anchors muscle to bone. Responds to mechanical tension; heals slowly because tendons are poorly vascularised.
