## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The osteocyte is the terminally differentiated, most abundant (>90% of all bone cells) and longest-lived cell type of bone, formed when an active osteoblast becomes progressively surrounded and finally walled into the osteoid it is secreting, as that osteoid mineralises around it. As mineralisation closes in, the former osteoblast dramatically remodels itself: its once-huge biosynthetic machinery (rough ER, Golgi) shrinks by roughly 70%, its cell body shrinks and flattens, and it grows dozens of long, thin, branching cytoplasmic (dendritic) processes — technically filopodia — that retract just ahead of the mineralisation front and so leave behind a network of fine tunnels through the hardening matrix. The final resting cell body sits inside an ellipsoidal cavity called a lacuna (roughly 15–20 µm across), and its 40–100 dendritic processes per cell radiate outward through the narrow canaliculi (≈200–900 nm diameter) that riddle the surrounding bone, giving the cell body, seen in three dimensions, the classic stellate ("spider" or dendritic) silhouette that is the single most recognisable feature of an osteocyte. Neighbouring osteocytes' processes meet tip-to-tip inside shared canaliculi and are joined by gap junctions, and the same processes also reach osteoblasts and bone-lining cells on the free bone surface and the blood supply in nearby vascular (Haversian) canals — together the lacunae and canaliculi form the continuous, fluid-filled lacunocanalicular network (LCN), the "osteocyte syncytium." A thin layer of pericellular fluid fills the gap between the cell membrane/processes and the mineralised lacunar/canalicular wall; this fluid layer is what physically moves (shear flow) when the skeleton is mechanically loaded, and that fluid-shear signal is how the buried osteocyte senses mechanical strain — its primary and defining job. Internally the mature osteocyte retains a single, round-to-oval, centrally placed nucleus and a modest complement of organelles for its now-lower secretory workload — some rough ER, a small Golgi, several mitochondria for ATP supply, and a cytoskeleton of actin and microtubule bundles that runs the length of each dendritic process and helps transmit/amplify strain signals to the cell body. Functionally, once it detects mechanical loading (or its absence, i.e. disuse), the osteocyte acts as the master regulator/orchestrator of bone remodeling: it downregulates secretion of sclerostin (a Wnt-pathway inhibitor) under load, which permits osteoblasts to build more bone, and it tunes the RANKL/OPG ratio it secretes to recruit and activate osteoclasts (more RANKL relative to OPG) when resorption is needed, or restrain them when it is not. It also functions as an endocrine cell, releasing FGF23 to regulate systemic phosphate handling in the kidney. Because it sits inside solid mineralised bone rather than in soft, well-perfused tissue, and depends on diffusion through the thin pericellular fluid of the LCN for nutrients and waste exchange, the osteocyte is metabolically quiescent compared with the osteoblast that made it — but it is remarkably long-lived, routinely surviving for decades within the same skeleton, only being cleared, along with its lacuna, when osteoclasts eventually resorb that patch of bone.

Sources: [StatPearls — Histology, Osteocytes (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK558990/), [Dallas & Bonewald 2010 — The Osteocyte: An Endocrine Cell … and More (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3785641/), [Kitase & Prideaux 2022 — Osteocytes: Their Lacunocanalicular Structure and Mechanoresponses (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9032292/), [Wikipedia — Osteocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteocyte), [Wikipedia — Bone canaliculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_canaliculus), [Wikipedia — Lacuna (histology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacuna_(histology)).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **Not multinucleated** — a single nucleus only. A giant cell with many fused nuclei is an *osteoclast*, a completely different, bone-*resorbing* cell — do not draw that here.
- **Not a plump, cuboidal-to-columnar cell sitting free ON a bone surface with dense rough ER filling the cytoplasm** — that is the earlier *osteoblast* stage, before it walled itself in. The osteocyte is smaller, flatter/stellate, its ER/Golgi are markedly reduced, and it is fully surrounded on all sides by mineralised matrix inside its lacuna.
- **Not round or smooth-surfaced like a red blood cell/lymphocyte, and not a plump epithelioid cell** — the single most important, defining shape is the star-like ("dendritic"/"spider") silhouette: a small central body with many long, thin, branching processes radiating outward through visible canaliculi.
- **The processes must stay INSIDE their canaliculi (tunnels through the matrix), never floating loose in open space** — the surrounding matrix with its canaliculi network must be visible, not empty background.
- **No cilia or flagella used for swimming/locomotion** — the dendritic processes are thin, static, branching sensory/connective extensions, not motile beating appendages, and the cell itself does not move once mineralised in.
- A single osteocyte as the clear subject (its lacuna and immediately connecting canaliculi/neighbour stubs may be shown for context) — not a dense, unreadable thicket where the central cell body is lost.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The osteocyte is the bone's resident sensor cell — a former osteoblast that got progressively entombed in its own mineralising matrix and, rather than dying, transformed into a small, star-shaped cell that spends the rest of its long life buried inside a tiny cavity called a lacuna. Dozens of hair-thin cytoplasmic processes radiate out from each osteocyte through microscopic tunnels (canaliculi), linking it to neighbouring osteocytes, to the osteoblasts still working the bone surface, and to nearby blood vessels — together forming a vast, fluid-filled communication network that runs through every bone in your body. Its job is mechanosensation: it detects the tiny fluid movements created whenever your skeleton is loaded, whether by walking, lifting or resistance exercise, and translates that signal into chemical instructions that tell osteoblasts to add bone and osteoclasts to hold back on removing it. This is a big part of why weight-bearing exercise keeps bones dense and why prolonged bed rest or spaceflight causes rapid bone loss — the osteocyte network simply isn't being told to keep building. Remarkably long-lived, individual osteocytes can remain active in the same spot for decades, making them the longest-serving cells in the skeleton.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The osteocyte is the terminally differentiated, most abundant (>90% of all bone cells) resident cell of mineralised bone, generated when a matrix-secreting osteoblast becomes progressively embedded in the osteoid it produces and undergoes a dramatic phenotypic switch: a ~70% reduction in RER/Golgi volume, cell-body shrinkage and flattening, and outgrowth of 40–100 dendritic processes (filopodia) that retract just ahead of the mineralisation front, leaving behind a network of canaliculi. The resulting stellate cell occupies an ellipsoidal lacuna (~15–20 µm), with processes running through canaliculi (~200–900 nm) to couple, via gap junctions, with neighbouring osteocytes, surface osteoblasts/bone-lining cells and the vasculature, forming the lacunocanalicular network (LCN) — the structural basis of the 'osteocyte syncytium.' A thin pericellular fluid layer fills the LCN; mechanical loading drives interstitial fluid flow through this space, and the resulting shear stress on the cell processes constitutes the primary mechanotransduction stimulus, detected via integrins, primary cilia, connexin hemichannels and the cytoskeleton. Downstream, osteocytes act as the master regulators of bone remodeling: load-dependent downregulation of sclerostin (SOST) relieves inhibition of Wnt/β-catenin signalling in osteoblasts (promoting formation), while the osteocyte-tuned RANKL/OPG ratio governs osteoclastogenesis (resorption); osteocytes are the dominant physiological source of both RANKL and OPG in bone. Osteocytes also function endocrinologically, secreting FGF23 to regulate renal phosphate handling and 1,25(OH)2D synthesis. Nutrient and waste exchange occurs by diffusion/convection through the LCN, given the avascular nature of the surrounding mineralised matrix; despite this metabolically constrained environment, osteocytes are exceptionally long-lived, persisting for years to decades until the enclosing matrix is resorbed by osteoclasts.  
🇩🇪 Die Osteozyte ist die terminal differenzierte, häufigste (>90 % aller Knochenzellen) ortsständige Zelle des mineralisierten Knochens, die entsteht, wenn ein matrixsezernierender Osteoblast zunehmend in das von ihm gebildete Osteoid eingebettet wird und dabei einen dramatischen Phänotypwechsel durchläuft: eine Reduktion des RER/Golgi-Volumens um etwa 70 %, Schrumpfung und Abflachung des Zellkörpers sowie das Auswachsen von 40–100 dendritischen Fortsätzen (Filopodien), die sich unmittelbar vor der Mineralisationsfront zurückziehen und dabei ein Netzwerk von Canaliculi hinterlassen. Die entstehende sternförmige Zelle besetzt eine ellipsoide Lakune (~15–20 µm), deren Fortsätze durch Canaliculi (~200–900 nm) verlaufen und über Gap Junctions benachbarte Osteozyten, oberflächliche Osteoblasten/Knochendeckzellen sowie die Gefäßversorgung koppeln — das lakunokanalikuläre Netzwerk (LCN), die strukturelle Grundlage des sogenannten „Osteozyten-Synzytiums“. Eine dünne perizelluläre Flüssigkeitsschicht füllt das LCN; mechanische Belastung erzeugt einen interstitiellen Flüssigkeitsstrom durch diesen Raum, und die daraus resultierende Scherbeanspruchung der Zellfortsätze stellt den primären mechanotransduktiven Stimulus dar, der über Integrine, primäre Zilien, Connexin-Hemikanäle und das Zytoskelett detektiert wird. Nachgeschaltet fungieren Osteozyten als übergeordnete Regulatoren des Knochenumbaus: Die belastungsabhängige Herunterregulation von Sklerostin (SOST) hebt die Hemmung des Wnt/β-Catenin-Signalwegs in Osteoblasten auf (fördert den Aufbau), während das osteozytär regulierte RANKL/OPG-Verhältnis die Osteoklastogenese (Resorption) steuert; Osteozyten sind im Knochen die dominante physiologische Quelle sowohl von RANKL als auch von OPG. Osteozyten wirken zudem endokrin, indem sie FGF23 sezernieren, das die renale Phosphathomöostase und die Synthese von 1,25(OH)2D reguliert. Nährstoff- und Abfallaustausch erfolgen aufgrund der Gefäßlosigkeit der umgebenden mineralisierten Matrix durch Diffusion/Konvektion über das LCN; trotz dieser metabolisch eingeschränkten Umgebung sind Osteozyten außergewöhnlich langlebig und bestehen über Jahre bis Jahrzehnte, bis die umschließende Matrix von Osteoklasten resorbiert wird.
