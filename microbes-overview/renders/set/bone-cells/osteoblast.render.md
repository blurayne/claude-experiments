## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The osteoblast is the bone-forming cell of the skeleton, differentiated from osteoprogenitor cells of the mesenchymal-stem-cell lineage (via Runx2/Osterix-driven commitment). Osteoblasts are never solitary, free-floating cells: they sit shoulder-to-shoulder in a single continuous, epithelioid (cuboidal-to-columnar) layer directly on the surface of forming or remodeling bone, called the osteogenic/osteoblastic seam. Only about 4–6% of all bone cells are osteoblasts at any time, but they are the most metabolically active bone-cell type. Morphologically the cell is polarised: a round-to-oval, euchromatic nucleus sits basally (toward the marrow/vascular side, away from the bone surface), and the cytoplasm between the nucleus and the bone-facing (apical) pole is intensely basophilic on H&E/Giemsa stains because it is packed with an extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) with dilated, dense cisternae — the machinery for the cell's very high rate of type I procollagen synthesis. Adjacent to the nucleus sits a large, often circular Golgi complex made of multiple stacks (the pale "Golgi zone" visible by light microscopy), which processes and packages procollagen and other matrix proteins — osteocalcin, osteopontin, bone sialoprotein, osteonectin — into secretory vesicles that travel to the apical surface. Mitochondria are numerous, reflecting the high ATP demand of this secretory workload. A subset of the secreted vesicles are matrix vesicles rich in alkaline phosphatase, which nucleate hydroxyapatite crystals and begin mineralisation once the collagenous matrix (osteoid) has been laid down and matured (roughly a 1–2 week lag between osteoid deposition and its mineralisation). Osteoblasts on the bone surface are physically and electrically coupled to each other, and to the osteocytes buried in the matrix they are actively producing, via gap junctions on thin cytoplasmic processes — the first step in the osteoblast eventually becoming an osteocyte itself, walled into a lacuna as the surrounding osteoid mineralises around it. Bone formation by osteoblasts is tightly coupled to, and balanced against, bone resorption by osteoclasts (the RANKL/OPG axis, secreted by osteoblasts/osteocytes, is the master regulator of osteoclast differentiation and activity), and osteoblast activity itself is controlled hormonally by parathyroid hormone (PTH, which paradoxically stimulates new bone formation when given intermittently) and vitamin D (calcitriol), alongside mechanical loading and local growth factors (BMPs, TGF-β, Wnt/β-catenin signalling).

Sources: [StatPearls — Histology, Osteoblasts (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557792/), [Endotext — Anatomy and Ultrastructure of Bone (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279149/), [Wikipedia — Osteoblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoblast), [Wikipedia — Bone remodeling / RANKL-OPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_remodeling).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No large central vacuole** — that is a plant-cell feature.
- **Not multinucleated** — a single round-to-oval nucleus only. A giant cell with many nuclei fused together is an **osteoclast**, a completely different, bone-*resorbing* cell — do not draw that here.
- **Not fully encased on all sides by mineralised bone** — that is the *osteocyte* stage, after the osteoblast has walled itself in. The osteoblast itself sits ON the bone surface: one pole (apical) touches the pale, still-soft osteoid it is secreting, the opposite pole (basal, where the nucleus sits) faces open marrow/connective tissue.
- **Not round like a red blood cell or lymphocyte, not a thin flattened plate** (that is a quiescent bone-lining cell) — the defining shape is cuboidal-to-columnar and polarised, packed into a single continuous row alongside its neighbours.
- **No cilia or flagella used for swimming/locomotion** — osteoblasts are matrix-bound, non-motile secretory cells, not free-swimming.
- A single cell, or a small row of a few neighbouring osteoblasts as normally seen lining a bone surface — not a dense sheet where individual cell borders and organelles are lost.

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet the Osteoblast — your skeleton's bricklayer! This little builder lines up shoulder-to-shoulder with hundreds of its teammates along the surface of your bones, and together they lay down a soft, stretchy building material called osteoid, made mostly of collagen. Then the osteoblast sprinkles in calcium to harden it into real, solid bone — like pouring concrete over a wire frame. It works hand-in-hand with the osteoclast, a demolition crew that clears away old bone so the osteoblast has room to build fresh. When an osteoblast finally bricks itself into the wall it just built, it settles down and becomes an osteocyte, a quiet watch-keeper cell that lives inside the bone forever after. Every time you grow taller or heal a broken bone, thank your osteoblasts!  
🇩🇪 Das ist der Osteoblast — der Maurer deines Skeletts! Dieser kleine Baumeister reiht sich mit Hunderten Kolleginnen Schulter an Schulter auf der Oberfläche deiner Knochen auf, und gemeinsam tragen sie ein weiches, dehnbares Baumaterial namens Osteoid auf, das hauptsächlich aus Kollagen besteht. Dann streut der Osteoblast Calcium hinein, damit daraus richtiger, fester Knochen wird — wie Beton, der über ein Drahtgerüst gegossen wird. Er arbeitet Hand in Hand mit dem Osteoklasten, einem Abrisstrupp, der alten Knochen wegräumt, damit der Osteoblast Platz zum Neubauen hat. Jedes Mal, wenn du wächst oder ein gebrochener Knochen heilt, kannst du deinen Osteoblasten danken!

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The osteoblast is the cell responsible for building new bone. Lining the bone surface in tight, organised sheets, it secretes collagen-rich osteoid and then mineralises it with calcium and phosphate, laying down the material that gives your skeleton its strength. Bone is not a static scaffold — it's constantly being renewed — and osteoblasts work in a carefully balanced partnership with osteoclasts, the cells that break old bone down, so that formation and resorption stay roughly matched. When that balance tips toward too much resorption, as happens with age or falling oestrogen levels, bone density drops and osteoporosis can follow, which is why several osteoporosis treatments are designed to support osteoblast activity or rein in the osteoclasts. Osteoblast activity itself is steered by hormones, chiefly parathyroid hormone and vitamin D, together with the mechanical loading your bones experience day to day — which is part of why weight-bearing exercise helps keep bones strong.  
🇩🇪 Der Osteoblast ist die Zelle, die neuen Knochen aufbaut. In dicht organisierten Schichten an der Knochenoberfläche angeordnet, scheidet er kollagenreiches Osteoid aus und mineralisiert es anschließend mit Calcium und Phosphat — genau das Material, das deinem Skelett seine Festigkeit verleiht. Knochen ist kein starres Gerüst, sondern wird ständig erneuert, und Osteoblasten arbeiten in einem fein austarierten Zusammenspiel mit Osteoklasten, den Zellen, die alten Knochen abbauen, damit Aufbau und Abbau ungefähr im Gleichgewicht bleiben. Kippt dieses Gleichgewicht zugunsten des Abbaus, etwa im Alter oder bei sinkendem Östrogenspiegel, sinkt die Knochendichte, und Osteoporose kann die Folge sein — deshalb zielen manche Osteoporose-Therapien darauf ab, die Osteoblasten zu unterstützen oder die Osteoklasten zu bremsen. Gesteuert wird die Osteoblasten-Aktivität vor allem durch Hormone — Parathormon und Vitamin D — sowie durch die mechanische Belastung, der die Knochen im Alltag ausgesetzt sind, weshalb Bewegung mit Gewichtsbelastung den Knochen stärkt.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The osteoblast is a mononuclear, matrix-bound bone-forming cell derived from Runx2/Osterix-committed mesenchymal osteoprogenitors. Osteoblasts occupy a single continuous epithelioid (cuboidal-to-columnar) layer on bone surfaces (the osteogenic seam) rather than existing as isolated cells. Their basally-located nucleus and intensely basophilic, RER-rich cytoplasm reflect a very high rate of type I procollagen synthesis; a large perinuclear Golgi apparatus packages procollagen and non-collagenous matrix proteins (osteocalcin, osteopontin, bone sialoprotein, osteonectin) for apical secretion as osteoid, a subset of secretory (matrix) vesicles carrying alkaline phosphatase to nucleate hydroxyapatite once the osteoid has matured. Osteoblasts are gap-junction-coupled to neighbouring osteoblasts and to the osteocyte network embedded in matrix they have already deposited, providing the structural continuity for the osteoblast-to-osteocyte transition as cells become progressively entombed in mineralising matrix. Osteoblast-driven bone formation is coupled to osteoclast-mediated resorption chiefly through the RANKL/OPG axis (osteoblasts/osteocytes are the principal source of both RANKL, which drives osteoclastogenesis, and its decoy receptor osteoprotegerin), with additional regulation by PTH (catabolic when chronically elevated, anabolic when given intermittently), vitamin D/calcitriol, Wnt/β-catenin and BMP/TGF-β signalling, and mechanical loading (mechanotransduction via osteocytes).  
🇩🇪 Der Osteoblast ist eine mononukleäre, matrixgebundene knochenbildende Zelle, die aus Runx2/Osterix-determinierten mesenchymalen Osteoprogenitoren hervorgeht. Osteoblasten bilden auf Knochenoberflächen eine durchgehende, epithelioide (kuboidale bis zylindrische) Zellschicht (den osteogenen Saum) und liegen nicht als isolierte Einzelzellen vor. Der basal gelegene Zellkern und das ausgeprägt basophile, RER-reiche Zytoplasma spiegeln eine sehr hohe Syntheserate von Typ-I-Procollagen wider; ein großer perinukleärer Golgi-Apparat verpackt Procollagen und nicht-kollagene Matrixproteine (Osteocalcin, Osteopontin, Knochen-Sialoprotein, Osteonectin) zur apikalen Sekretion als Osteoid, wobei ein Teil der sekretorischen (Matrix-)Vesikel alkalische Phosphatase enthält, die nach Reifung des Osteoids die Hydroxylapatit-Bildung nukleiert. Osteoblasten sind über Gap Junctions mit benachbarten Osteoblasten und mit dem Osteozyten-Netzwerk in der bereits abgelagerten Matrix verbunden, was die strukturelle Grundlage für den Übergang vom Osteoblasten zur Osteozyte bildet, während Zellen zunehmend in mineralisierende Matrix eingeschlossen werden. Die osteoblastenvermittelte Knochenbildung ist mit der osteoklastenvermittelten Resorption vor allem über die RANKL/OPG-Achse gekoppelt (Osteoblasten/Osteozyten sind die Hauptquelle sowohl von RANKL, das die Osteoklastogenese antreibt, als auch seines Decoy-Rezeptors Osteoprotegerin), mit zusätzlicher Regulation durch Parathormon (katabol bei chronischer Erhöhung, anabol bei intermittierender Gabe), Vitamin D/Calcitriol, Wnt/β-Catenin- und BMP/TGF-β-Signalwege sowie mechanische Belastung (Mechanotransduktion über Osteozyten).
