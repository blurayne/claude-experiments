## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The chondrocyte is the sole resident cell type of cartilage, differentiating from mesenchymal/perichondrial precursors (chondroblasts) and occupying a small matrix-carved cavity called a lacuna, either alone or as an **isogenous group** of two to eight cells produced by interstitial mitosis of a single progenitor dividing in place. The cell body is round to oval — never elongated or spindle-shaped, and never flattened into a thin plate — with a round, often slightly eccentric nucleus containing one prominent nucleolus. Because its job is to continuously synthesise and maintain the huge volume of surrounding extracellular matrix (mostly type II/IX/XI collagen fibrils and large aggregating proteoglycans, chiefly aggrecan, that trap water and give cartilage its rubbery, shock-absorbing texture), the chondrocyte shows the ultrastructural hallmarks of a busy secretory cell: extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum, a well-developed Golgi apparatus, and a vimentin-based intermediate-filament cytoskeleton (not the actin-stress-fibre cytoskeleton of a migratory, adherent cell like a fibroblast or MSC, since the chondrocyte is matrix-bound and non-motile). A single non-motile primary cilium projects from the cell into the surrounding pericellular matrix and acts as a mechano-/chemosensory organelle, but is fine/thin enough that it is not a required label for a teaching illustration at this scale. Hyaline, elastic and fibrocartilage are all avascular, aneural and alymphatic, so chondrocyte metabolism runs chiefly on anaerobic glycolysis under low-oxygen conditions; the cells accumulate visible cytoplasmic glycogen granules and lipid droplets as energy reserves, and all nutrient/gas exchange happens purely by diffusion through the matrix (ultimately from the perichondrium, synovial fluid, or subchondral bone — there is no local blood supply to draw on). Chondrocytes are terminally differentiated and largely post-mitotic in adult tissue, which — together with the avascularity above — is exactly why cartilage injuries (a torn meniscus, worn joint cartilage in osteoarthritis) heal so slowly and poorly compared to bone or skin.

Sources: [StatPearls — Histology, Cartilage (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544266/), [Wikipedia — Chondrocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrocyte), [Wikipedia — Cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage), [Wikipedia — Hyaline cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyaline_cartilage).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts, no large central vacuole** — plant-cell features, not present here.
- **Not elongated or spindle-shaped** — that is a fibroblast/MSC/tenocyte body plan. The chondrocyte is round to oval and compact, with no long tapering cytoplasmic processes.
- **No long, star-shaped dendritic canalicular processes** — that is the *osteocyte*, a completely different, bone-embedded cell; the chondrocyte has no comparable process network.
- **No actin stress fibres running the length of the cell** — the cytoskeleton here is a fine vimentin intermediate-filament mesh, not the bundled actin of a crawling/adherent cell.
- **No blood vessels anywhere near the lacuna or matrix** — cartilage is avascular; nutrients arrive purely by diffusion. Drawing capillaries here is the single most common and most misleading error for this cell.
- **No cilia or flagella used for swimming/locomotion** — chondrocytes are matrix-bound and non-motile (the one real primary cilium is a short, thin, single non-motile sensory antenna, not a propulsive organelle, and is optional at this illustration scale).
- A single specimen (or a small isogenous cluster of two to a few cells sharing one lacuna, if depicting a group) — not a dense, undifferentiated sheet where individual cell borders and organelles are lost.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The chondrocyte is the sole resident cell type of cartilage, sitting alone or in small clusters of two to eight cells (an isogenous group, born from one parent cell dividing in place) inside a small cavity called a lacuna. From there it continuously builds and maintains the surrounding matrix — mostly type II collagen fibrils and water-trapping proteoglycans — that gives cartilage its rubbery, shock-absorbing texture in joints, the rib cage, the ear, the nose, the airway and the spine's intervertebral discs. Cartilage has essentially no blood vessels of its own, so chondrocytes depend entirely on nutrients diffusing in from the surrounding tissue and stockpile glycogen and lipid droplets to cope with the resulting low-oxygen, low-turnover lifestyle. That same lack of blood supply is exactly why cartilage injuries — a torn meniscus, worn-down joint cartilage in osteoarthritis — heal so slowly and poorly compared to bone or skin.  
🇩🇪 Der Chondrozyt ist der einzige Zelltyp im Knorpelgewebe und sitzt allein oder in kleinen Gruppen von zwei bis acht Zellen (einer isogenen Gruppe, entstanden aus einer Mutterzelle, die sich an Ort und Stelle geteilt hat) in einem kleinen Hohlraum, der Lakune. Von dort aus baut und erhält er fortwährend die umgebende Matrix — vor allem Kollagen-Typ-II-Fibrillen und wasserbindende Proteoglykane —, die dem Knorpel seine gummiartige, stoßdämpfende Beschaffenheit in Gelenken, Rippenbogen, Ohr, Nase, Atemwegen und den Bandscheiben der Wirbelsäule verleiht. Knorpel besitzt so gut wie keine eigenen Blutgefäße, weshalb Chondrozyten vollständig auf Nährstoffe angewiesen sind, die aus dem umliegenden Gewebe eindiffundieren, und Glykogen sowie Lipidtröpfchen bunkern, um mit dem daraus folgenden sauerstoffarmen, langsamen Stoffwechsel zurechtzukommen. Genau dieser fehlende Blutanschluss ist auch der Grund, warum Knorpelverletzungen — ein gerissener Meniskus, abgenutzter Gelenkknorpel bei Arthrose — so viel langsamer und schlechter heilen als Knochen oder Haut.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Chondrocytes are the exclusive resident cell of cartilage, differentiating from mesenchymal/perichondrial precursors and occupying matrix-carved lacunae either singly or as isogenous groups of two to eight cells produced by interstitial mitosis of a single progenitor. Ultrastructurally they show the secretory phenotype expected of a matrix-synthesising cell: a round to oval, often eccentric nucleus with a prominent nucleolus, extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum and a well-developed Golgi apparatus for the synthesis, glycosylation and export of type II/IX/XI collagen and large aggregating proteoglycans (notably aggrecan), a vimentin intermediate-filament cytoskeleton rather than the actin-stress-fibre-dominated cytoskeleton of migratory mesenchymal cells, and a single non-motile primary cilium that projects into the pericellular matrix and acts as a mechanosensory/chemosensory organelle. Because hyaline, elastic and fibrocartilage are avascular, aneural and alymphatic, chondrocyte metabolism operates chiefly under hypoxic conditions via anaerobic glycolysis, and the cells accumulate cytoplasmic glycogen granules and lipid droplets as energy reserves; nutrient and gas exchange occurs entirely by diffusion through the matrix (from the perichondrium, synovial fluid, or subchondral bone). Matrix immediately around the cell (the pericellular/territorial matrix, rich in type VI collagen) differs biochemically from the bulk interterritorial matrix and is thought to transduce mechanical loading to the cell via the primary cilium and integrins. Chondrocytes are terminally differentiated and largely post-mitotic in adult tissue, which — together with the avascularity described above — underlies cartilage's very limited intrinsic capacity for repair.  
🇩🇪 Chondrozyten sind die ausschließliche ortsständige Zellpopulation des Knorpelgewebes; sie differenzieren aus mesenchymalen bzw. perichondralen Vorläuferzellen und besetzen in die Matrix eingelassene Lakunen entweder einzeln oder als isogene Gruppen von zwei bis acht Zellen, die durch interstitielle Mitose einer einzelnen Vorläuferzelle entstehen. Ultrastrukturell zeigen sie den für eine matrixsynthetisierende Zelle typischen sekretorischen Phänotyp: einen runden bis ovalen, häufig exzentrischen Zellkern mit deutlichem Nukleolus, ausgedehntes raues endoplasmatisches Retikulum und einen gut entwickelten Golgi-Apparat für Synthese, Glykosylierung und Sekretion von Kollagen Typ II/IX/XI sowie großen aggregierenden Proteoglykanen (insbesondere Aggrecan), ein Zytoskelett aus Vimentin-Intermediärfilamenten anstelle der Aktin-Stressfasern migrierender mesenchymaler Zellen, sowie ein einzelnes, unbewegliches Primärzilium, das in die perizelluläre Matrix hineinragt und als mechano-/chemosensorisches Organell fungiert. Da hyaliner, elastischer Knorpel und Faserknorpel gefäß-, nerven- und lymphfrei sind, läuft der Chondrozytenstoffwechsel überwiegend unter hypoxischen Bedingungen über anaerobe Glykolyse ab, wobei die Zellen zytoplasmatische Glykogengranula und Lipidtröpfchen als Energiereserve anlegen; Nährstoff- und Gasaustausch erfolgen ausschließlich durch Diffusion durch die Matrix (aus Perichondrium, Synovialflüssigkeit oder subchondralem Knochen). Die unmittelbar zellnahe Matrix (perizelluläre/territoriale Matrix, reich an Kollagen Typ VI) unterscheidet sich biochemisch von der übrigen interterritorialen Matrix und überträgt vermutlich mechanische Belastung über Primärzilium und Integrine auf die Zelle. Chondrozyten sind im adulten Gewebe terminal differenziert und weitgehend postmitotisch, was zusammen mit der oben beschriebenen Gefäßlosigkeit die sehr begrenzte intrinsische Reparaturfähigkeit des Knorpels erklärt.
