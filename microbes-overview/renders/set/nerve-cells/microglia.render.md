## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Microglia are the resident macrophages of the central nervous system (CNS) and its principal immune cell, but unlike other tissue macrophages they are not derived from adult bone-marrow monocytes: fate-mapping shows they originate from **yolk-sac erythromyeloid progenitors** that colonise the neural tube during early embryogenesis and then self-renew locally for life, largely without replacement from circulating blood cells (Ginhoux et al. 2010). In their normal, homeostatic state they are **ramified/surveillant**: a small, compact soma bearing a small, often bean-shaped or oval nucleus with a thick band of electron-dense heterochromatin against the nuclear envelope (much darker/denser-looking than a neuron's large, pale, euchromatic nucleus), surrounded by only a thin rim of cytoplasm holding scattered mitochondria, short stretches of rough endoplasmic reticulum, a modest Golgi apparatus, and numerous lysosomes (reflecting the cell's phagocytic job). From this small body radiate many thin, highly and repeatedly branched processes (primary, secondary and fine tertiary branches) tipped with small motile ruffled/bulbous terminal specialisations. Two-photon in vivo imaging established that, despite the "resting" name, these processes are in constant motion — extending, retracting and re-extending — continuously surveying/probing their entire surrounding territory over a timescale of hours (Nimmerjahn, Kirchhoff & Helmchen 2005, "resting microglial cells are highly dynamic surveillants"); neighbouring microglia tile the tissue into non-overlapping territories rather than overlapping like astrocyte processes. This exploratory behaviour, and directed process movement toward sites of injury/ATP release, is driven largely by purinergic **P2Y12 receptors** and the fractalkine receptor **CX3CR1**, both concentrated on the fine processes; **Iba1** (ionised calcium-binding adaptor molecule 1, an actin-binding protein) is the classic pan-microglial/macrophage marker used to visualise the whole ramified arbour in histology, as in the reference micrograph below. Functionally, microglia continuously engulf and clear cellular debris, dying cells and protein aggregates via phagocytosis (visible ultrastructurally as intracellular phagosomes/phagolysosomes and, in aged tissue, lipid inclusions); during development and in the adult they also perform **complement-dependent synaptic pruning**, physically engulfing weak or unwanted synapses tagged by C1q/C3 to sculpt neural circuits; and they mount the CNS's first line of defence against infection or injury, at which point they retract their arbour and convert toward an amoeboid, more actively phagocytic morphology — a distinct activated state, not the one depicted here (this reference is the classic resting/surveying ramified form).

Sources: [Wikipedia — Microglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microglia), [Nimmerjahn, Kirchhoff & Helmchen 2005, "Resting Microglial Cells Are Highly Dynamic Surveillants of Brain Parenchyma", Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15831717/), [Ginhoux et al. 2010, "Fate Mapping Analysis Reveals That Adult Microglia Derive from Primitive Macrophages", Science (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2996141/), [Savage, Carrillo & Molofsky 2018, "A Brief History of Microglial Ultrastructure...", Frontiers in Immunology (PMC)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00803/full), [Paolicelli et al. 2011 / synaptic pruning review — "The Shape of μ" (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9276927/), [Kettenmann, Hanisch, Noda & Verkhratsky 2011, "Physiology of Microglia", Physiological Reviews (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3236728/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No myelin sheath wrapped around an axon** — that is the job of oligodendrocytes (CNS) / Schwann cells (PNS), not microglia.
- **Not a broad star with thick, flat, GFAP-filament processes and vessel-wrapping end-feet** — that silhouette belongs to the astrocyte; microglia's processes are thinner, more finely and repeatedly branched, spikier/bushier, and do not flatten into end-feet.
- **No axon or dendrite, no synaptic vesicles fired by the cell itself** — this is not a neuron.
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — a human (eukaryotic, animal) cell, not a prokaryote.
- **Default to the small, ramified/surveying morphology**, not the retracted, blobby amoeboid/activated shape — the amoeboid form is a distinct activation state and would misrepresent the resting, patrolling cell this reference describes.
- **Not spindle-shaped with actin stress fibres** — that is a fibroblast/mesenchymal-stem-cell body plan, not microglia.
- **No coarse cytoplasmic granules** typical of a neutrophil/granulocyte — the lysosomes here are small and modest, not large stainable granules.
- **No large single prominent nucleolus dominating the nucleus** the way a neuron's does — keep the nucleus small, dense and comparatively inconspicuous.
- **Single specimen only** (or, for the SEM/reference-style shots, a modest cluster with clearly separated, individually readable arbours) — never a dense confluent sheet.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Microglia are the brain's resident housekeepers and sentinels — small cells with a compact body and a dense crown of thin, constantly moving processes that sweep through the surrounding tissue hour after hour. That restless probing lets them stay on top of the brain's ordinary maintenance: clearing away cellular debris and stray protein clumps, and quietly trimming back synapses that are no longer being used, a process that helps sculpt efficient neural circuits from childhood through adulthood. What makes microglia unusual among the body's cells is their origin story: rather than being replenished from bone marrow like most immune cells, they arrive very early, from the yolk sac of the developing embryo, take up residence in the brain, and then self-renew in place for an entire lifetime. That makes them, in effect, the brain's permanent local staff, always on-site and quick to notice when something in their neighbourhood needs attention.  
🇩🇪 Die Mikroglia ist die ansässige Hausmeisterin und Wächterin des Gehirns — eine kleine Zelle mit kompaktem Zellkörper und einer dichten Krone aus dünnen, ständig beweglichen Fortsätzen, die Stunde um Stunde das umliegende Gewebe abtasten. Dieses rastlose Sondieren erlaubt es ihr, die normale Instandhaltung des Gehirns zu übernehmen: Sie räumt Zelltrümmer und fehlgefaltete Proteinklumpen weg und stutzt nebenbei still Synapsen zurück, die kaum noch genutzt werden — ein Vorgang, der von der Kindheit bis ins Erwachsenenalter hilft, effiziente neuronale Schaltkreise zu formen. Ungewöhnlich unter den Körperzellen ist ihre Herkunft: Statt wie die meisten Immunzellen ständig aus dem Knochenmark nachgeliefert zu werden, wandert sie schon sehr früh aus dem Dottersack des Embryos ein, siedelt sich im Gehirn an und erneuert sich dort ihr ganzes Leben lang selbst. Damit ist sie quasi das feste, ortsansässige Personal des Gehirns, immer vor Ort und schnell zur Stelle, wenn in ihrem Revier etwas Aufmerksamkeit braucht.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Microglia are the resident tissue macrophages of the central nervous system, distinguished from other body macrophages by their ontogeny: fate-mapping studies show they arise from yolk-sac erythromyeloid progenitors that colonise the neural tube during early embryogenesis and subsequently self-renew locally throughout life, largely independent of monocyte replacement (Ginhoux et al. 2010). In the homeostatic state they adopt a ramified morphology — a small soma with a heterochromatin-dense nucleus and thin cytoplasm containing scattered mitochondria, modest rough ER and Golgi, and numerous lysosomes, from which many thin, repeatedly branching processes radiate. In vivo two-photon imaging demonstrated that these processes are highly motile even at rest, continuously extending and retracting to survey non-overlapping tissue territories on a timescale of hours (Nimmerjahn, Kirchhoff & Helmchen 2005), a behaviour mediated substantially by P2Y12 and CX3CR1 receptors concentrated on the fine processes. Functionally, microglia perform constitutive phagocytic clearance of cellular debris, apoptotic cells and protein aggregates, and execute complement-dependent (C1q/C3-tagged) synaptic pruning during development and in the adult CNS, thereby shaping circuit connectivity. Upon injury or infection they retract their arbour and adopt a more amoeboid, actively phagocytic and cytokine-secreting state, constituting the CNS's first line of innate immune defence.  
🇩🇪 Die Mikroglia ist das ortsansässige Gewebemakrophagen-System des zentralen Nervensystems und unterscheidet sich von anderen Körpermakrophagen durch ihre Entwicklungsgeschichte: Fate-Mapping-Studien zeigen, dass sie aus erythromyeloiden Vorläuferzellen des Dottersacks hervorgeht, die während der frühen Embryogenese das Neuralrohr besiedeln und sich anschließend zeitlebens lokal selbst erneuern, weitgehend unabhängig von einer Auffüllung durch Monozyten (Ginhoux et al. 2010). Im homöostatischen Zustand zeigt sie eine ramifizierte Morphologie — ein kleines Soma mit heterochromatindichtem Zellkern und dünnem Zytoplasma, das vereinzelte Mitochondrien, mäßig ausgeprägtes raues ER und einen Golgi-Apparat sowie zahlreiche Lysosomen enthält, von dem aus viele dünne, sich wiederholt verzweigende Fortsätze ausgehen. In-vivo-Zwei-Photonen-Mikroskopie zeigte, dass diese Fortsätze auch im Ruhezustand hochbeweglich sind und kontinuierlich über Stunden hinweg nicht überlappende Gewebereviere absuchen (Nimmerjahn, Kirchhoff & Helmchen 2005) — ein Verhalten, das wesentlich durch die auf den feinen Fortsätzen konzentrierten Rezeptoren P2Y12 und CX3CR1 vermittelt wird. Funktionell übernimmt die Mikroglia die konstitutive phagozytische Beseitigung von Zelltrümmern, apoptotischen Zellen und Proteinaggregaten und führt komplementabhängiges (C1q/C3-markiertes) synaptisches Pruning während der Entwicklung und im adulten ZNS durch, wodurch sie die Konnektivität neuronaler Schaltkreise mitformt. Bei Verletzung oder Infektion zieht sie ihr Fortsatzgeflecht zurück und nimmt eine eher amöboide, aktiv phagozytierende und zytokinsezernierende Form an — die erste Verteidigungslinie der angeborenen Immunität im ZNS.
