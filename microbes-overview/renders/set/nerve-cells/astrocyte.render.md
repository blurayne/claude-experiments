## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The astrocyte is the most abundant glial cell type in the central nervous system, named for its classic **star-shaped (stellate)** body: a modest soma that sends out many radiating primary processes, which branch further into fine, bushy secondary and tertiary processes. Two broad morphological types are recognised — **protoplasmic astrocytes** in grey matter, with short, highly branched, "bushy" processes that densely ensheath synapses and neuronal somata, and **fibrous astrocytes** in white matter, with longer, straighter, less-branched processes rich in intermediate filaments that run between axon bundles. The soma holds a single, relatively small, round-to-oval, **euchromatic (pale-staining) nucleus** — much less prominent than a neuron's large nucleus with its conspicuous single nucleolus — surrounded by cytoplasm containing a normal complement of mitochondria, rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus for the cell's substantial secretory and metabolic workload, plus **glycogen granules**, the brain's only significant intracellular energy store (used to fuel neurons during high activity via the astrocyte–neuron lactate shuttle). Structurally, the processes owe their shape and mechanical resilience to a dense meshwork of **glial (intermediate) filaments built from GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein)** — the classic immunohistochemical marker used to identify astrocytes in tissue. A defining functional feature is that a subset of the fine terminal processes flare out into flattened **perivascular end-feet**, which wrap around and cover the great majority of the surface of nearby brain capillaries; together with the endothelial tight junctions and pericytes, these end-feet (rich in the water channel AQP4 and the potassium channel Kir4.1) help form and maintain the **blood–brain barrier** and couple local blood flow to neuronal activity (neurovascular coupling). Neighbouring astrocytes are electrically and metabolically coupled to one another (and to oligodendrocytes) through **gap junctions** (chiefly connexin-43 and connexin-30), forming a functional syncytium that lets ions and small metabolites spread through the tissue — the basis of astrocytic **potassium spatial buffering**, which clears excess extracellular K⁺ released by firing neurons. Astrocyte processes also enwrap synapses as the third, "silent" partner of the **tripartite synapse**: they take up synaptically released glutamate via EAAT1/GLAST and EAAT2/GLT-1 transporters and convert it to glutamine via glutamine synthetase (the glutamate–glutamine cycle), recycling it back to neurons for new neurotransmitter synthesis and simultaneously protecting the tissue from glutamate excitotoxicity.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Astrocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrocyte), [Verkhratsky & Nedergaard 2018, "Physiology of Astroglia", Physiological Reviews (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6151493/), [Sofroniew & Vinters 2010, "Astrocytes: biology and pathology", Acta Neuropathologica (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2887670/), [NCBI Bookshelf — Purves et al., Neuroscience, 2nd ed., "Neuroglial Cells"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10869/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No myelin sheath wrapped around a long axon** — that is the job of oligodendrocytes (CNS) / Schwann cells (PNS), not astrocytes.
- **No synaptic vesicles or axon-terminal boutons** — astrocytes are not neurons; their processes ensheath synapses but do not fire action potentials or release vesicular neurotransmitter in the classic pre-synaptic sense.
- **No axon or dendrite with a long single fiber** — an astrocyte's defining shape is the multipolar, star-like radiation of many roughly-equal processes, not a single long axon plus branching dendrites.
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids, or bacterial flagella** — this is a human (eukaryotic, animal) cell, not a prokaryote.
- **Not round/biconcave like a red blood cell, and not a small amoeboid immune cell** — keep the stellate, highly branched silhouette.
- **No dense secretory granules** typical of a gland or blood cell — the glycogen granules are small and numerous, not large dark secretory vesicles.
- **No large single nucleolus dominating the nucleus** the way a neuron's does — keep the nucleus pale/euchromatic and comparatively small.
- A single specimen, not a dense confluent mesh of many overlapping astrocytes — individual stellate morphology must stay readable.

🇬🇧 Meet the Astrocyte — the brain's star-shaped everything-doer! With dozens of little arms reaching out in every direction, this cell touches thousands of neurons at once, like a many-armed helper handing out snacks. It feeds neurons with sugar, tidies up leftover chatter-chemicals so the message stays clear, and wraps its flat little feet around tiny blood-vessel pipes to help build a security fence (the blood-brain barrier) that keeps troublemakers out of the brain. Astrocytes even hold hands with their neighbours through tiny doorways called gap junctions, passing snacks and signals down the line like a bucket brigade. No wonder there are more astrocytes in your brain than almost any other kind of cell!

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The astrocyte is the most abundant glial cell in the brain and spinal cord, and far more than passive scaffolding. Its star-shaped body sends out dozens of fine, branching processes that ensheath synapses, supply neurons with glucose-derived fuel, and mop up neurotransmitters such as glutamate before they can build up to toxic levels. Flattened end-feet on the tips of some processes wrap around nearby brain capillaries, contributing to the blood-brain barrier and adjusting local blood flow to match neuronal activity. Neighbouring astrocytes are linked by gap junctions into a communicating network that spreads potassium and metabolites through tissue, helping keep the chemical environment around neurons stable. Because of this central housekeeping role, astrocyte dysfunction is now linked to conditions ranging from epilepsy to neurodegenerative disease.  
🇩🇪 Der Astrozyt ist die häufigste Gliazelle in Gehirn und Rückenmark und weit mehr als ein passives Stützgerüst. Sein sternförmiger Zellkörper streckt Dutzende feine, verzweigte Fortsätze aus, die Synapsen umhüllen, Neuronen mit Brennstoff aus Glukose versorgen und Botenstoffe wie Glutamat aufsaugen, bevor sie sich zu giftigen Mengen ansammeln können. Abgeflachte Fußfortsätze an den Enden mancher Fortsätze umschließen benachbarte Hirnkapillaren, tragen so zur Blut-Hirn-Schranke bei und passen die lokale Durchblutung an die Aktivität der Neuronen an. Benachbarte Astrozyten sind über Gap Junctions zu einem kommunizierenden Netzwerk verbunden, das Kalium und Stoffwechselprodukte im Gewebe verteilt und so das chemische Umfeld der Neuronen stabil hält. Wegen dieser zentralen Haushaltsfunktion wird eine Fehlfunktion von Astrozyten inzwischen mit Erkrankungen von Epilepsie bis zu neurodegenerativen Krankheiten in Verbindung gebracht.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Astrocytes are stellate macroglial cells of the CNS, subdivided into protoplasmic (grey matter, densely branched) and fibrous (white matter, GFAP-filament-rich) subtypes, that provide metabolic, ionic and structural support to neurons. Their fine processes form the glial component of the tripartite synapse, clearing synaptically released glutamate via the EAAT1/GLAST and EAAT2/GLT-1 transporters and recycling it to neurons as glutamine through the glutamate-glutamine cycle, while cytoplasmic glycogen stores fuel neurons via the astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle. Perivascular end-feet, enriched in AQP4 water channels and Kir4.1 potassium channels, cover the majority of brain capillary surface area and, together with endothelial tight junctions and pericytes, constitute the blood-brain barrier while mediating neurovascular coupling. Connexin-43/connexin-30 gap junctions couple neighbouring astrocytes into a functional syncytium that underlies potassium spatial buffering, and the GFAP-based intermediate filament cytoskeleton provides mechanical resilience and serves as the standard immunohistochemical marker for the lineage.  
🇩🇪 Astrozyten sind sternförmige Makrogliazellen des ZNS, die in protoplasmatische (graue Substanz, dicht verzweigt) und fibröse (weiße Substanz, GFAP-filamentreiche) Subtypen unterteilt werden und Neuronen metabolisch, ionisch und strukturell unterstützen. Ihre feinen Fortsätze bilden die gliale Komponente der tripartiten Synapse, entfernen synaptisch freigesetztes Glutamat über die Transporter EAAT1/GLAST und EAAT2/GLT-1 und recyceln es über den Glutamat-Glutamin-Zyklus als Glutamin an Neuronen zurück, während zytoplasmatische Glykogenspeicher Neuronen über den Astrozyten-Neuronen-Laktat-Shuttle mit Energie versorgen. Perivaskuläre Fußfortsätze, angereichert mit AQP4-Wasserkanälen und Kir4.1-Kaliumkanälen, bedecken den Großteil der Oberfläche von Hirnkapillaren und bilden zusammen mit endothelialen Tight Junctions und Perizyten die Blut-Hirn-Schranke, während sie die neurovaskuläre Kopplung vermitteln. Connexin-43/Connexin-30-Gap-Junctions koppeln benachbarte Astrozyten zu einem funktionellen Synzytium, das der kaliumbezogenen räumlichen Pufferung zugrunde liegt, und das GFAP-basierte Intermediärfilament-Zytoskelett verleiht mechanische Widerstandsfähigkeit und dient als Standard-immunhistochemischer Marker für diese Zelllinie.
