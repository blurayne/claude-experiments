## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The lipoblast (also called an adipoblast) is the morphologically recognizable, lipid-accumulating stage of adipocyte differentiation that sits between the still fibroblast-like preadipocyte and the mature, lipid-filled adipocyte. Where the preadipocyte is a spindle-shaped cell that has not yet started storing fat, the defining, diagnostic feature of the lipoblast is that it has: its cytoplasm is studded with **multiple small, discrete lipid droplets** (a "multilocular" pattern), each wrapped in its own thin coat of lipid-droplet surface proteins (perilipins, chiefly PLIN1), rather than the single dominant droplet of a mature white adipocyte or the complete absence of droplets in a preadipocyte. As these droplets enlarge and jostle the nucleus, the nucleus itself develops a characteristic **scalloped or indented outline** — a hallmark pathologists use to recognise lipoblasts in tissue sections — while remaining euchromatic (transcriptionally active) with a visible nucleolus. The rest of the cytoplasm still carries the organelle complement of an actively biosynthetic cell: moderate rough endoplasmic reticulum and a Golgi apparatus (both engaged in producing lipid-droplet coat proteins and lipogenic enzymes), a moderate, evenly dispersed population of mitochondria (more than the quiescent preadipocyte, but nowhere near the dense packing of a brown adipocyte), and a cytoskeleton of actin filaments and vimentin intermediate filaments that is being actively remodelled as the cell shifts from an elongated, tapering shape toward a more rounded-to-polygonal one under the mechanical pressure of the growing droplets. Lipoblasts are the central players of adipogenesis in the embryo and fetus, where sheets of them differentiate in the developing subcutaneous and visceral fat depots from about mid-gestation onward, driven by the same PPARγ/C/EBPα transcriptional programme that governs postnatal adipogenesis. Clinically, the term is best known from tumour pathology: multivacuolated cells with scalloped, hyperchromatic nuclei that resemble developmental lipoblasts are a defining (though not by themselves diagnostic) feature of **liposarcoma**, the most common soft-tissue sarcoma in adults, where a mature-appearing adipose tumour containing scattered lipoblasts raises suspicion for a well-differentiated or myxoid liposarcoma rather than a benign lipoma.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Lipoblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoblast), [Fukumura et al. 2003 — Adipogenesis and angiogenesis (embryonic fat development), Circ Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12958142/), [Iwayama et al. 2015 — PDGFRβ signalling in embryonic/perivascular adipogenesis, Genes Dev (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4315231/), [Fasano et al. 2015 — Lipoblast: Morphologic Features and Diagnostic Value, J UOEH](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/juoeh/36/2/36_115/_pdf/-char/en), [PathologyOutlines — Liposarcoma general](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/testisliposarcoma.html), [Sirvent et al. — WHO classification, cytogenetics of liposarcoma, referenced in StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538265/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No single giant unilocular droplet filling most of the cell** — that "signet ring" look is the *mature adipocyte*, not the lipoblast.
- **No complete absence of lipid droplets** — an empty, purely spindle-shaped fibroblastic cell is the *preadipocyte* stage, one step earlier.
- **No perfectly round, smooth nucleus** — the scalloped/indented nuclear outline caused by the surrounding droplets is a defining, teachable feature; a plain oval nucleus loses this.
- **No dense, tightly packed clusters of mitochondria** — that is the *brown adipocyte* signature; the lipoblast's mitochondria are moderate and evenly dispersed.
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — this is an animal cell, not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts, no large central vacuole** — plant-cell features, not present here.
- **No cilia or flagella for locomotion.**
- A single specimen, or a small, clearly readable cluster (as in the real reference micrograph) — not a dense confluent sheet where individual cells can no longer be told apart.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The lipoblast is the moment adipocyte development becomes visible: a precursor cell, descended from mesenchymal stem cells, that has just begun laying down fat as a scatter of small, separate droplets rather than the single large droplet of a mature fat cell. As those droplets multiply and grow, they press in on the nucleus from several sides, giving it a distinctive scalloped, dented outline that is one of the clearest signs a cell is actively becoming a fat cell rather than sitting quietly as an undifferentiated precursor. Lipoblasts do their most important work before birth, populating the growing fetus's subcutaneous and internal fat depots, and they resurface later in life in a specific medical context: pathologists look for lipoblast-like cells when examining fatty tumours under the microscope, since their presence can help distinguish a harmless lipoma from a liposarcoma that needs closer follow-up.  
🇩🇪 Der Lipoblast markiert den Moment, in dem die Entwicklung einer Fettzelle erstmals sichtbar wird: eine von mesenchymalen Stammzellen abstammende Vorläuferzelle, die gerade erst begonnen hat, Fett in Form mehrerer kleiner, getrennter Tröpfchen einzulagern, statt in dem einen großen Tropfen einer reifen Fettzelle. Während diese Tröpfchen sich vermehren und wachsen, drücken sie von mehreren Seiten auf den Zellkern und verleihen ihm eine charakteristisch gebuchtete, eingedellte Kontur — eines der deutlichsten Zeichen dafür, dass eine Zelle sich aktiv zur Fettzelle entwickelt und nicht mehr ruhig als undifferenzierte Vorläuferin verharrt. Ihre wichtigste Arbeit leisten Lipoblasten schon vor der Geburt, wenn sie die wachsenden Fettdepots des Fötus unter der Haut und im Körperinneren besiedeln, und sie tauchen später im Leben in einem bestimmten medizinischen Zusammenhang wieder auf: Pathologen achten bei der mikroskopischen Untersuchung fettiger Tumoren gezielt auf lipoblastenartige Zellen, da ihr Vorkommen helfen kann, ein harmloses Lipom von einem Liposarkom zu unterscheiden, das genauer beobachtet werden muss.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The lipoblast is the earliest morphologically identifiable stage of adipocyte differentiation, marking the transition of a mesenchymal-derived, previously fibroblast-like preadipocyte into a lipid-accumulating cell under the PPARγ/C/EBPα transcriptional programme. It is defined by a multilocular pattern of small, PLIN1 (perilipin)-coated lipid droplets dispersed through the cytoplasm — mechanically distinct from both the lipid-free preadipocyte and the unilocular mature adipocyte — accompanied by a characteristic scalloped or indented nuclear contour produced by droplet impingement on the nuclear envelope, moderate rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi activity supporting lipogenic and droplet-coat protein synthesis, and a moderately increased, still-dispersed mitochondrial population as the cytoskeleton (actin/vimentin) remodels toward a rounder cell shape. Lipoblasts are the principal effector cells of embryonic and fetal adipogenesis, populating subcutaneous and visceral fat depots from mid-gestation onward. In postnatal pathology, cells with lipoblast-like cytomorphology (multivacuolated cytoplasm, hyperchromatic scalloped nuclei) are a recognised, though non-specific, diagnostic feature in the histological work-up of adipocytic soft-tissue tumours, most notably in distinguishing well-differentiated liposarcoma and myxoid liposarcoma from benign lipoma.  
🇩🇪 Der Lipoblast ist das früheste morphologisch erkennbare Stadium der Adipozytendifferenzierung und markiert den Übergang einer von Mesenchymzellen abstammenden, zuvor fibroblastenähnlichen Präadipozyte in eine lipidspeichernde Zelle unter dem PPARγ/C/EBPα-Transkriptionsprogramm. Er ist definiert durch ein multilokuläres Muster kleiner, PLIN1(Perilipin)-umhüllter Lipidtropfen im Zytoplasma — morphologisch klar unterscheidbar sowohl von der lipidfreien Präadipozyte als auch von der unilokulären reifen Fettzelle —, begleitet von einer charakteristisch gebuchteten oder eingedellten Kernkontur, die durch den Druck der Tropfen auf die Kernhülle entsteht, mäßiger Aktivität des rauen endoplasmatischen Retikulums und des Golgi-Apparats zur Synthese lipogener und tropfenumhüllender Proteine sowie einer moderat erhöhten, weiterhin verstreuten Mitochondrienpopulation, während sich das Zytoskelett (Aktin/Vimentin) hin zu einer runderen Zellform umbaut. Lipoblasten sind die wichtigsten Effektorzellen der embryonalen und fetalen Adipogenese und besiedeln ab der mittleren Schwangerschaft die subkutanen und viszeralen Fettdepots. In der postnatalen Pathologie gelten Zellen mit lipoblastenartiger Zytomorphologie (multivakuolisiertes Zytoplasma, hyperchromatische, gebuchtete Kerne) als anerkanntes, wenn auch unspezifisches diagnostisches Merkmal bei der histologischen Aufarbeitung adipozytärer Weichteiltumoren, insbesondere zur Abgrenzung des gut differenzierten Liposarkoms und des myxoiden Liposarkoms vom gutartigen Lipom.
