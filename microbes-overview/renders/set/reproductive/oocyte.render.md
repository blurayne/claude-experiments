## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The human oocyte (egg cell, ovum) is the female germ cell and, at roughly 100–120 µm in diameter, the largest single cell in the human body — visible to the naked eye. A secondary oocyte ovulates arrested in metaphase of meiosis II (it only completes meiosis if fertilised) and is spherical, with a very large, fluid cytoplasm called the **ooplasm**. Its **plasma membrane (oolemma)** shows a dense fringe of **microvilli** that interdigitate with the innermost layer of the surrounding investment. Immediately outside the oolemma is a narrow, fluid-filled **perivitelline space**, and outside that a thick, translucent, acellular glycoprotein coat, the **zona pellucida** (largely ZP1/ZP2/ZP3 glycoproteins secreted by the oocyte itself), which mediates species-specific sperm binding and — after fertilisation — hardens to block polyspermy. Outside the zona pellucida sit one or more layers of small follicular (granulosa) cells: the innermost layer touching the zona is the **corona radiata**, and the looser, more numerous cells further out (still attached at ovulation) form the **cumulus oophorus** — together often called the cumulus-oocyte complex. Granulosa-cell cytoplasmic processes reach through channels in the zona pellucida and form gap junctions with the oocyte's own microvilli, allowing nutrients and signalling molecules to pass in from the surrounding follicle cells.

Inside the ooplasm, a large **nucleus**, classically called the **germinal vesicle** in the immature (prophase-I-arrested) oocyte, contains the maternal chromosomes; in the mature, ovulated secondary oocyte the nuclear envelope has broken down and the chromosomes are aligned on a barrel-shaped, largely acentriolar **meiotic spindle** positioned eccentrically just under the cortex, having just extruded the tiny **first polar body** into the perivitelline space (a second polar body follows only after fertilisation). Directly beneath the oolemma lies a specialised, actin-rich cortical layer studded with thousands of small, membrane-bound **cortical granules** — secretory organelles that, upon sperm entry, undergo the cortical reaction (exocytosis) and modify the zona pellucida to block additional sperm (the zona reaction), preventing polyspermy. The bulk of the ooplasm is filled with abundant, evenly dispersed **mitochondria** (the oocyte's mitochondria are the sole source of mitochondrial DNA for the next generation), stores of **lipid droplets** and **glycogen/yolk-like nutrient granules** built up during oocyte growth to support the early embryo before implantation, a modest **Golgi apparatus** and **smooth/rough endoplasmic reticulum** (much of the ER is reorganised into clusters near the cortex), and a fine **cytoskeletal meshwork** of actin filaments and microtubules that positions the spindle and drives cortical-granule exocytosis. Unlike a generic animal cell, the mature oocyte has very little rough ER and few ribosome-studded stacks — it is largely a "storage and waiting" cell, transcriptionally quiescent, living on maternal mRNA and protein made earlier during its long growth phase in the ovary.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, chloroplasts or central plant vacuole** — this is a human animal cell, not a plant cell.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No beating flagellum or cilia for locomotion** — the oocyte itself does not swim; it is moved passively by follicular fluid and tubal cilia/muscle. (Do not confuse with the sperm's flagellum — this is a different cell.)
- **Not a small, plain round cell** — the oocyte is unusually large with a thick, clearly visible zona pellucida shell and a corona/cumulus of smaller follicle cells still attached, not a bare sphere.
- **No dense rough-ER/ribosome stacks like a secretory gland cell** — the mature oocyte is largely quiescent; only a modest, mostly smooth ER/Golgi should be shown, not heavy rough-ER sheets.
- **Only one nucleus/spindle, one tiny first polar body** — do not draw a second polar body (that only appears after fertilisation) or multiple spindles.
- **Cortical granules stay as a thin band just under the membrane** — do not scatter them through the whole cytoplasm or draw them as large organelles.
- A single specimen (with its immediate corona/cumulus escort), not a dense clump of many oocytes — individual structures must stay readable.

Once a month, she leaves home on a big adventure down the fallopian tube, patiently waiting for the one special sperm that will team up with her to start building a brand new person.  
🇩🇪 Das ist die Oozyte — der größte Star der ganzen Zellwelt! Sie ist so riesig, dass man sie fast ohne Mikroskop erkennen könnte, etwa so groß wie ein feines Sandkorn. Jeden Monat wird sie von ihrem Helferteam verwöhnt, den Granulosa- und Theca-Zellen, die sich schützend um sie herum kuscheln und sie mit Nährstoffen füttern, damit sie prall und bereit wird. **Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The oocyte is the largest cell in the human body — around 100–120 micrometres across, just visible to the naked eye. Each menstrual cycle, one oocyte matures inside an ovarian follicle, nourished and hormonally supported by surrounding granulosa and theca cells, and is released at ovulation still arrested partway through its final cell division. It is encased in the zona pellucida, a tough glycoprotein shell that both selects compatible sperm and, after fertilisation, hardens to stop any others getting through — a safeguard against fatal genetic overload. Beneath its membrane sit thousands of cortical granules that trigger this block within seconds of the winning sperm's arrival. Packed with mitochondria, stored nutrients and maternal proteins built up over months of growth, the oocyte is less a busy factory than a very well-provisioned life-support capsule, built to sustain a future embryo through its first days before implantation.  
🇩🇪 Die Oozyte ist die größte Zelle des menschlichen Körpers — etwa 100 bis 120 Mikrometer groß und gerade noch mit bloßem Auge erkennbar. In jedem Menstruationszyklus reift eine Oozyte in einem Eierstockfollikel heran, ernährt und hormonell unterstützt von umgebenden Granulosa- und Theca-Zellen, und wird beim Eisprung freigesetzt, während sie mitten in ihrer letzten Zellteilung verharrt. Sie ist von der Zona pellucida umgeben, einer robusten Glykoprotein-Hülle, die passende Spermien auswählt und sich nach der Befruchtung verhärtet, um weitere Spermien abzuwehren — ein Schutz vor einer fatalen genetischen Überladung. Unter ihrer Membran liegen Tausende Corticalgranula, die diesen Verschluss innerhalb von Sekunden nach der Ankunft des siegreichen Spermiums auslösen. Vollgepackt mit Mitochondrien, gespeicherten Nährstoffen und mütterlichen Proteinen, die über Monate des Wachstums angelegt wurden, ist die Oozyte weniger eine geschäftige Fabrik als eine bestens ausgestattete Lebenserhaltungskapsel, die einen künftigen Embryo durch seine ersten Tage vor der Einnistung trägt.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The human oocyte is a secondary oocyte arrested in metaphase II of meiosis at ovulation, roughly 100–120 µm in diameter, developed within an antral ovarian follicle under paracrine and endocrine support from cumulus granulosa cells (gap-junction-coupled nutrient/signal transfer) and theca cells (androgen substrate for granulosa-cell aromatisation to oestrogen). It is enveloped by the zona pellucida, an oocyte-secreted ZP1/ZP2/ZP3 glycoprotein matrix mediating species-specific sperm binding, and retains an outer investment of corona radiata and cumulus oophorus cells at ovulation. The ooplasm contains an eccentrically positioned, largely acentriolar meiotic spindle (having extruded the first polar body), abundant evenly dispersed mitochondria constituting the exclusive maternal source of mtDNA for the embryo, stored lipid droplets and nutrient granules accumulated during the growth phase, a subcortical layer of cortical granules, and comparatively sparse rough ER/ribosomes reflecting transcriptional quiescence. Sperm fusion triggers a Ca2+-wave-driven cortical reaction — cortical granule exocytosis that remodels the zona pellucida (zona reaction) to establish a definitive block to polyspermy, alongside completion of meiosis II and extrusion of the second polar body.  
🇩🇪 Die menschliche Oozyte ist eine sekundäre Oozyte, die beim Eisprung in der Metaphase II der Meiose arretiert ist, mit einem Durchmesser von etwa 100 bis 120 µm. Sie entwickelt sich in einem antralen Ovarialfollikel unter parakriner und endokriner Unterstützung durch Cumulus-Granulosazellen (über Gap Junctions vermittelter Nährstoff- und Signaltransfer) und Theca-Zellen (Androgen-Substrat für die Aromatisierung zu Östrogen in den Granulosazellen). Sie ist von der Zona pellucida umgeben, einer von der Oozyte selbst sezernierten ZP1/ZP2/ZP3-Glykoproteinmatrix, die die artspezifische Spermienbindung vermittelt, und trägt beim Eisprung noch eine äußere Hülle aus Corona radiata und Cumulus oophorus. Das Ooplasma enthält eine exzentrisch gelegene, weitgehend azentriolare Reifeteilungsspindel (nach Ausstoßung des ersten Polkörpers), zahlreiche gleichmäßig verteilte Mitochondrien, die die alleinige mütterliche Quelle der mtDNA für den Embryo darstellen, gespeicherte Lipidtröpfchen und Nährstoffgranula aus der Wachstumsphase, eine subkortikale Schicht von Corticalgranula sowie vergleichsweise spärliches raues ER und wenige Ribosomen, was die transkriptionelle Ruhe widerspiegelt. Die Verschmelzung mit dem Spermium löst eine calciumwellengetriebene corticale Reaktion aus — die Exozytose der Corticalgranula, die die Zona pellucida umbaut (Zona-Reaktion) und so den endgültigen Polyspermieblock etabliert, begleitet vom Abschluss der Meiose II und der Ausstoßung des zweiten Polkörpers.
