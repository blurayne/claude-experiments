## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The human spermatozoon is the male gamete, the smallest and most extremely polarized cell in the body, produced continuously from puberty onward by spermatogenesis in the seminiferous tubules of the testis. Developing sperm are nursed, fed and structurally supported by Sertoli cells and driven to mature by testosterone secreted by neighbouring Leydig cells; during the final differentiation step (spermiogenesis) a round spermatid sheds almost all of its cytoplasm and remodels itself into the compact, streamlined swimming cell seen at ejaculation. A mature spermatozoon is about 55-60 micrometres long overall and is built from two clearly distinct parts: a flattened, oval, paddle-shaped **head** (roughly 4-5 x 2.5-3.5 micrometres) and a long **tail (flagellum)** that makes up most of the cell's length. The head is almost entirely filled by the **nucleus**, containing the haploid (23-chromosome) paternal genome with its chromatin packed far more densely than in any somatic cell — most histones are replaced by small, highly basic **protamines**, which cross-link and compact the DNA into an almost crystalline state that protects it during the long journey to the egg and keeps the head hydrodynamically compact. Draped over the front two-thirds of the nucleus, like a cap, sits the **acrosome**, a large Golgi-derived secretory vesicle containing hydrolytic enzymes (including acrosin and hyaluronidase); during the acrosome reaction near the egg these enzymes are released to help the sperm penetrate the cumulus cells and the zona pellucida. The head connects to the tail through a short **neck (connecting piece)**, which houses the **proximal centriole** (the distal centriole is not retained as a discrete structure in the mature cell — its remnant organizes the axoneme) and a set of segmented columns that continue into the tail as the outer dense fibers. The tail itself is subdivided into three segments. The **midpiece**, just behind the neck, is a short, slightly thicker segment in which a tightly wound helical **mitochondrial sheath** — dozens of elongated mitochondria coiled end-to-end around the axoneme — supplies the ATP for motility via oxidative phosphorylation. The much longer **principal piece** makes up most of the tail's length; here the axoneme and its surrounding outer dense fibers are wrapped by a **fibrous sheath** of longitudinal columns joined by circumferential ribs, which stiffens the tail and shapes the bending wave. Finally, a short, bare **end piece** at the very tip contains only the axoneme, tapering to a fine thread. Running the full length of the tail, core to all three segments, is the **axoneme**: the canonical eukaryotic "9+2" arrangement of nine outer microtubule doublets surrounding a central pair of single microtubules, cross-linked by nexin links and radial spokes, with **dynein arms** on the outer doublets that generate an ATP-powered sliding motion between adjacent doublets — this sliding, resisted by the radial spokes and the fibrous sheath/outer dense fibers, converts into the characteristic whip-like bending wave that propels the cell forward. A thin, continuous **plasma membrane** envelopes the whole cell, head to tail tip. Of the roughly 200-300 million sperm released in a human ejaculate, only a small fraction survive the journey through the female tract, and typically only one fertilizes the egg, delivering the father's chromosomes and triggering the biochemical block to polyspermy.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No bacterial-style rotary flagellum or multiple tails** — this is a single, true eukaryotic flagellum built around a 9+2 axoneme, not a bacterial helical propeller; exactly one continuous, tapering tail per cell.
- **No cell wall, nucleoid or plasmids** — this is a human (eukaryotic) cell, not a prokaryote.
- **Acrosome must not cover the whole nucleus** — it is a cap over roughly the front two-thirds only, not a full shell.
- **Mitochondria belong only in the tightly coiled midpiece sheath** — do not scatter loose mitochondria through the head or along the rest of the tail.
- **The tail is not a uniform, non-tapering tube** — it thickens slightly at the midpiece (mitochondrial sheath) and tapers further along the principal piece into a very fine, bare end piece.
- **Not round or blob-shaped** — the head is a flattened oval/paddle shape, and the whole cell is extremely elongated (tail roughly ten times the length of the head).
- Single specimen per image; no baked-in text, letters, numbers, labels, scale bars, arrows or watermarks; no border/frame/vignette — the background (or paper, for the watercolor style) fills the frame edge-to-edge.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The mature human spermatozoon is a highly polarized, terminally differentiated haploid cell produced by spermatogenesis in the seminiferous epithelium, where Sertoli cells provide structural and nutritive support and establish the blood-testis barrier, while Leydig-cell-derived testosterone drives germ-cell maturation. It comprises a flattened, paddle-shaped head (~4-5 x 2.5-3.5 micrometres) containing a nucleus with chromatin compacted by protamines rather than histones, overlain by the acrosome, a Golgi-derived vesicle housing hydrolytic enzymes (including acrosin and hyaluronidase) that are released during the acrosome reaction to breach the zona pellucida. The head connects via a neck (connecting piece) containing the proximal centriole and segmented columns to the tail, which is subdivided into a midpiece, where a helical mitochondrial sheath encircles the axoneme and outer dense fibers to generate ATP for motility; a principal piece, where the axoneme and outer dense fibers are further wrapped by a fibrous sheath of longitudinal columns and circumferential ribs; and a short end piece containing only the axoneme. The axoneme itself follows the canonical 9+2 microtubule doublet arrangement, with dynein arms generating the sliding-filament motion that produces flagellar beating and forward propulsion.  
🇩🇪 Das reife menschliche Spermium ist eine stark polarisierte, terminal differenzierte haploide Zelle, die durch Spermatogenese im Keimepithel der Samenkanälchen entsteht, wo Sertoli-Zellen strukturelle und nutritive Unterstützung leisten und die Blut-Hoden-Schranke bilden, während von den Leydig-Zellen gebildetes Testosteron die Reifung der Keimzellen antreibt. Es besteht aus einem abgeflachten, ruderförmigen Kopf (etwa 4-5 x 2,5-3,5 Mikrometer), der einen Zellkern mit durch Protamine statt Histone verdichtetem Chromatin enthält, überlagert vom Akrosom, einem vom Golgi-Apparat abgeleiteten Vesikel mit hydrolytischen Enzymen (unter anderem Akrosin und Hyaluronidase), die bei der Akrosomreaktion freigesetzt werden, um die Zona pellucida zu durchdringen. Der Kopf ist über einen Hals (Verbindungsstück) mit dem proximalen Zentriol und segmentierten Säulen mit dem Schwanz verbunden, der sich in ein Mittelstück gliedert, wo eine spiralig gewundene Mitochondrienscheide das Axonem und die äußeren dichten Fasern umgibt und ATP für die Beweglichkeit liefert; ein Hauptstück, in dem Axonem und äußere dichte Fasern zusätzlich von einer Faserscheide aus Längssäulen und Querrippen umschlossen sind; sowie ein kurzes Endstück, das nur noch das Axonem enthält. Das Axonem selbst folgt der klassischen 9+2-Anordnung von Mikrotubuli-Doppeltubuli, wobei Dynein-Arme die Gleitbewegung erzeugen, die den Geißelschlag und den Vortrieb hervorbringt.
