## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The theca cell is a steroidogenic stromal (connective-tissue-derived) cell that recruits and organizes itself around a growing ovarian follicle once it reaches the secondary (pre-antral) stage, forming a two-layered capsule outside the follicular basal lamina: the **theca interna**, an inner, highly vascularized layer of cuboidal-to-polygonal, epithelioid steroidogenic cells that is the true endocrine (androgen-producing) compartment, and the **theca externa**, an outer layer of flattened, smooth-muscle-like fibroblasts and collagen-rich connective tissue that provides structural support and contractile capacity. Theca interna cells are the working steroidogenic cell of interest for a "theca cell" render: they are round-to-polygonal, contain a round euchromatic nucleus with one or more nucleoli, and — as classic androgen-secreting cells — show the ultrastructural hallmarks of steroidogenesis: abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) forming a dense tubular network throughout the cytoplasm (the site of cholesterol side-chain cleavage and later steroidogenic enzyme steps), numerous mitochondria with characteristic tubulovesicular ("tubular") cristae (rather than the flat shelf-like cristae of most other cells — a steroidogenic-cell hallmark, since the first and last steps of steroid synthesis happen inside the mitochondrion), abundant cytoplasmic lipid droplets storing cholesterol esters (the steroid precursor), a well-developed Golgi apparatus, and only modest rough endoplasmic reticulum/free ribosomes (protein secretion is a minor part of its job compared with steroid secretion). Theca interna cells express LH receptors on the plasma membrane; LH from the anterior pituitary binds these receptors and, via cAMP/PKA signalling, drives cholesterol uptake and upregulates the steroidogenic enzymes (StAR, CYP11A1, CYP17A1) that convert cholesterol to androgens (mainly androstenedione, plus some testosterone). Critically, theca cells lack the enzyme aromatase (CYP19A1), so they cannot make estrogen themselves; instead the androgens diffuse across the shared follicular basal lamina into the adjacent granulosa-cell layer, where FSH-driven aromatase converts them to estrone/estradiol — the "two-cell, two-gonadotropin" model of follicular estrogen synthesis. The theca interna is richly supplied by a capillary network (the follicle itself is avascular until ovulation, so this is the only blood supply serving the growing follicle), which both delivers LDL-cholesterol substrate and picks up the secreted androgens. Theca cells arise from ovarian stromal/mesenchymal precursor cells recruited by paracrine signals from the growing follicle (e.g. Hedgehog/PDGF pathways) and, after ovulation, the theca interna contributes theca-lutein cells to the corpus luteum, continuing androgen/some progesterone production alongside the granulosa-lutein cells.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Theca of follicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theca_of_follicle), [StatPearls — Physiology, Ovarian (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539827/), [StatPearls — Histology, Ovary (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK556077/), [Young & Mcneilly, 2010 — Theca: the forgotten cell of the ovarian follicle (Reproduction)](https://rep.bioscientifica.com/view/journals/rep/140/4/489.xml), [ScienceDirect Topics — Theca Interna Cell](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/theca-interna-cell).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial-style flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts, no large central vacuole** — those are plant-cell features.
- **No flagellum/tail or axoneme** — this is a stromal/epithelioid cell, not a sperm cell; do not draw a tail.
- **No aromatase-driven "estrogen glow" or granulosa-cell layer as the star of the image** — this render is specifically the theca (interna) cell; granulosa cells may appear only as faint, out-of-focus context across the basal lamina, never as the main subject.
- **Mitochondrial cristae must read as tubular/tubulovesicular, not flat parallel shelves** — flat-shelf cristae are the generic-cell convention and would misrepresent this steroidogenic cell's defining ultrastructural feature.
- **Not a large, ciliated or highly polarized columnar cell** — theca interna cells are round-to-polygonal and comparatively modest in size, not tall/columnar like Sertoli cells.
- **No oocyte, zona pellucida or antral cavity as the visual centerpiece** — those belong to the follicle interior, not the theca; at most a hint of the follicle's basal lamina at one edge for orientation.
- A single specimen (or a small cluster suggesting the theca interna layer), not a dense undifferentiated sheet — individual cell morphology must stay readable.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The theca cell forms the outer capsule of a growing ovarian follicle, organized in two layers: an inner theca interna of round, hormone-producing cells and an outer theca externa of tougher connective tissue that holds everything in shape. The theca interna is the follicle's only source of blood supply before ovulation, so it doesn't just wall the follicle in, it feeds it too. Its real specialty, though, is hormones: theca cells carry receptors for LH, the pituitary hormone that switches on androgen production. Those androgens then cross into the neighbouring layer of granulosa cells, which convert them into estrogen — a division of labour known as the two-cell system, since neither cell type can make estrogen from scratch alone. After ovulation, theca cells don't retire; alongside granulosa cells they help build the corpus luteum, keeping the hormone supply going in case pregnancy follows.  
🇩🇪 Die Theca-Zelle bildet die äußere Hülle eines heranwachsenden Eibläschens (Follikels) und ist in zwei Schichten organisiert: eine innere Theca interna aus runden, hormonproduzierenden Zellen und eine äußere Theca externa aus stabilerem Bindegewebe, das dem Ganzen seine Form gibt. Die Theca interna ist vor dem Eisprung die einzige Blutversorgung des Follikels — sie ummauert ihn also nicht nur, sie ernährt ihn auch. Ihre eigentliche Spezialität sind aber Hormone: Theca-Zellen tragen Rezeptoren für LH, das Hypophysenhormon, das die Androgenproduktion anschaltet. Diese Androgene wandern anschließend in die benachbarte Schicht der Granulosazellen, die sie in Östrogen umwandeln — eine Arbeitsteilung, die als Zwei-Zell-System bekannt ist, weil keiner der beiden Zelltypen alleine Östrogen von Grund auf herstellen kann. Nach dem Eisprung gehen Theca-Zellen nicht in Rente: Zusammen mit Granulosazellen bauen sie den Gelbkörper (Corpus luteum) auf und halten die Hormonversorgung aufrecht, falls eine Schwangerschaft folgt.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Theca cells are steroidogenic stromal cells recruited from ovarian mesenchyme that organize into a bilayered capsule — theca interna and theca externa — outside the follicular basal lamina once a follicle reaches the secondary (pre-antral) stage. The theca interna comprises round-to-polygonal epithelioid cells with the classic ultrastructural signature of steroidogenesis: abundant smooth endoplasmic reticulum, mitochondria with tubulovesicular cristae, and numerous cholesterol-ester-laden lipid droplets. Theca interna cells express LH receptors that, via cAMP/PKA signalling, upregulate StAR and the steroidogenic enzymes (CYP11A1, CYP17A1) required to convert cholesterol to androgens (chiefly androstenedione). Lacking aromatase (CYP19A1), theca cells cannot synthesize estrogen autonomously; secreted androgens instead diffuse across the basal lamina into the granulosa cell layer, where FSH-driven aromatase completes estrogen synthesis — the two-cell, two-gonadotropin model of follicular steroidogenesis. The theca interna is densely capillarized, constituting the follicle's sole vascular supply prior to ovulation, and its outer theca externa layer of fibroblasts and smooth-muscle-like cells provides structural and contractile support. Following the LH surge and ovulation, theca interna cells luteinize alongside granulosa cells to form theca-lutein cells within the corpus luteum, continuing androgen and progesterone secretion during the luteal phase.  
🇩🇪 Theca-Zellen sind steroidogene Stromazellen, die aus dem ovariellen Mesenchym rekrutiert werden und sich, sobald ein Follikel das sekundäre (präantrale) Stadium erreicht, außerhalb der follikulären Basallamina zu einer zweischichtigen Hülle organisieren — Theca interna und Theca externa. Die Theca interna besteht aus rundlich-polygonalen, epitheloiden Zellen mit der klassischen ultrastrukturellen Signatur der Steroidogenese: reichlich glattem endoplasmatischem Retikulum, Mitochondrien mit tubulovesikulären Cristae und zahlreichen cholesterinester-haltigen Lipidtröpfchen. Theca-interna-Zellen exprimieren LH-Rezeptoren, die über cAMP/PKA-Signalwege StAR sowie die für die Umwandlung von Cholesterin in Androgene (vor allem Androstendion) nötigen steroidogenen Enzyme (CYP11A1, CYP17A1) hochregulieren. Da ihnen die Aromatase (CYP19A1) fehlt, können Theca-Zellen nicht eigenständig Östrogen synthetisieren; die sezernierten Androgene diffundieren stattdessen über die Basallamina in die Granulosazellschicht, wo die FSH-gesteuerte Aromatase die Östrogensynthese vervollständigt — das Zwei-Zell-Zwei-Gonadotropin-Modell der follikulären Steroidogenese. Die Theca interna ist dicht kapillarisiert und stellt vor dem Eisprung die einzige Gefäßversorgung des Follikels dar; ihre äußere Theca-externa-Schicht aus Fibroblasten und glattmuskelähnlichen Zellen sorgt für strukturelle und kontraktile Stabilität. Nach dem LH-Anstieg und dem Eisprung luteinisieren Theca-interna-Zellen gemeinsam mit Granulosazellen zu Theca-Luteinzellen im Gelbkörper (Corpus luteum) und setzen die Androgen- und Progesteronsekretion während der Lutealphase fort.
