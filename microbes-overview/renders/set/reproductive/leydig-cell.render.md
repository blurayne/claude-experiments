## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Leydig cells (interstitial cells of the testis, named after Franz von Leydig) are the steroidogenic endocrine cells of the testicle. They sit in small clusters ("nests") in the loose interstitial connective tissue between the seminiferous tubules, closely associated with fenestrated capillaries, lymphatic-like interstitial spaces, macrophages and unmyelinated autonomic nerve fibres — never inside the tubules themselves, which is Sertoli/germ-cell territory. Each cell is polygonal to rounded, roughly 15–20 µm across, with a single large, round, eccentrically placed, euchromatic (pale, "vesicular") **nucleus** typically showing one to three prominent **nucleoli** — a sign of a transcriptionally very active cell, not a quiescent one. The cytoplasm is strongly **eosinophilic** (stains pink/orange with H&E) because it is packed with smooth membranes.

Leydig cells are the textbook example of a steroid-hormone-secreting cell, and their organelle inventory reflects that job directly. On stimulation by **luteinizing hormone (LH)** from the anterior pituitary binding the LH receptor (LHCGR) on the plasma membrane, a cAMP/PKA cascade activates the steroidogenic acute regulatory protein (StAR), which shuttles cholesterol across the outer to the inner mitochondrial membrane. There, the cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme (CYP11A1) converts it to pregnenolone. Pregnenolone then passes to the extensive, anastomosing **smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER)** — the single most prominent organelle in the cell and visually dominant — where a further enzyme cascade (3β-HSD, CYP17A1, 17β-HSD) completes the synthesis of **testosterone**, which diffuses out (steroids are not packaged into secretory granules; there is no stored hormone). Because of this shuttling between mitochondria and SER, Leydig cells carry unusually **numerous mitochondria with tubulovesicular (finger-like/tubular) cristae** — a distinctive ultrastructural feature of steroidogenic cells, quite different from the flat lamellar (shelf-like) cristae of a typical cell such as a muscle or liver cell. Numerous membrane-bound **lipid droplets** store cholesterol esters, the raw material for steroid synthesis, and are hydrolysed on demand. A well-developed **Golgi apparatus** sits near the nucleus, a modest amount of **rough ER** and free ribosomes support ordinary protein synthesis (Leydig cells also secrete peptides such as insulin-like factor 3, INSL3), and **peroxisomes** carrying sterol-carrier protein assist in shuttling cholesterol. With age, membrane-bound **lipofuscin (residual-body) pigment granules** accumulate from turned-over lipid droplets. The most famous — and diagnostically distinctive — feature of mature human Leydig cells is the **crystal(loid) of Reinke**: an elongated, rod-shaped, eosinophilic protein crystalloid (up to ~20 µm long, a few µm wide) built from a lattice of fine protein filaments, found in the cytoplasm of a subset of adult (post-pubertal) human Leydig cells. Its exact function is unknown; it appears only after puberty as the cell matures and is essentially unique to the human Leydig cell (a rare finding in a few other species), making it a signature feature for teaching and for pathological identification.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Leydig cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leydig_cell), [StatPearls — Histology, Leydig Cells (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK556007/), [Planinić et al. 2022, "Reinke crystals: Hallmarks of adult Leydig cells in humans", Andrology (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35661438/), [PathologyOutlines — Leydig cell tumor (normal Leydig cell ultrastructure section)](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/testisleydig.html), [SIU School of Medicine Histology — Testis](https://histology.siu.edu/erg/testis.htm).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, chloroplasts or plant vacuole** — this is a human animal cell.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No secretory granules storing hormone** — steroids are not packaged/stored in vesicles like a peptide-hormone cell; testosterone diffuses straight out through the membrane, so do not draw large hormone-filled granules.
- **Mitochondrial cristae must be tubular/tubulovesicular (finger-like), not the flat lamellar (shelf) cristae of a typical cell** — a common and important ultrastructural error to avoid.
- **Rough ER/ribosome stacks stay modest** — the SER, not the RER, is the dominant, visually striking membrane system; do not draw a cell dominated by RER sheets like a plasma cell or a pancreatic acinar cell.
- **Not spindle-shaped and not ciliated/flagellated** — the cell is polygonal/rounded, no locomotor appendages (do not confuse with a spermatozoon's flagellum — this is a completely different cell).
- **No acrosome, sperm tail or condensed sperm-head chromatin** — do not draw germ-cell structures; this is a somatic endocrine cell.
- **Reinke crystals are optional/variable** — at most a small number of rod-shaped crystalloids in one region of the cytoplasm, never filling or replacing the whole cell.
- **Sits in the interstitium, never inside a seminiferous tubule** — a small hint of the surrounding loose connective tissue / adjacent capillary in the background is fine, but no tubule wall or germ cells crowding the frame; keep the Leydig cell(s) the clear single subject.
- A single specimen (a lone cell, or a small tight nest of 2–4 cells as they occur naturally in tissue) — not a dense sheet/monolayer where individual cells are unreadable.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The Leydig cell is the testis's testosterone factory. Scattered in small clusters in the loose connective tissue between the sperm-producing seminiferous tubules, it responds to luteinizing hormone (LH) released by the pituitary gland with pulses of activity roughly every couple of hours, converting cholesterol into testosterone through a chain of enzymatic steps split between its mitochondria and an extensive smooth endoplasmic reticulum — by far its most prominent internal feature. Because steroid hormones are small and fat-soluble, none of it gets packaged into storage granules the way insulin or adrenaline are; it diffuses straight out of the cell into nearby blood vessels as fast as it's made. This testosterone does double duty: locally, it reaches astronomical concentrations inside the tubules to drive sperm maturation, and systemically, at far lower blood levels, it maintains muscle mass, bone density, libido, mood and the deep voice and body hair that mark adult male physiology. Leydig-cell output rises sharply at puberty and gradually declines with age, which is part of why testosterone levels in older men are typically lower — an active area of research in andrology.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Leydig (interstitial) cells are steroidogenic endocrine cells occupying the loose interstitial compartment between seminiferous tubules, closely apposed to fenestrated capillaries and resident macrophages. LH binds the G-protein-coupled LH receptor (LHCGR) on the plasma membrane, activating adenylyl cyclase; the resulting cAMP/PKA signal phosphorylates and upregulates the steroidogenic acute regulatory protein (StAR), which mediates the rate-limiting transfer of cholesterol from the outer to the inner mitochondrial membrane. There, CYP11A1 (cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme) converts cholesterol to pregnenolone, which is exported to the smooth endoplasmic reticulum for sequential processing by 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, CYP17A1 (17α-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase) and 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase to yield testosterone. Because steroids are lipophilic and not vesicle-packaged, secretion is constitutive diffusion rather than regulated exocytosis, and intratesticular testosterone concentrations run roughly 25–100× serum levels, driving paracrine support of Sertoli-cell function and spermatogenesis, while spillover into testicular venous drainage supplies systemic androgen action. Leydig cells also secrete insulin-like factor 3 (INSL3), a marker of differentiated Leydig-cell mass and a regulator of testicular descent in development. Ultrastructurally, they show numerous mitochondria with distinctive tubulovesicular cristae (reflecting their role in steroidogenesis rather than oxidative ATP production), abundant lipid droplets storing cholesteryl esters, and, in mature adult cells, cytoplasmic crystalloids of Reinke — protein crystal aggregates of unresolved function that are a species-specific and maturation-specific histological hallmark of the human Leydig cell.  
🇩🇪 Leydig- (interstitielle) Zellen sind steroidogene endokrine Zellen im lockeren interstitiellen Kompartiment zwischen den Samenkanälchen, eng benachbart zu fenestrierten Kapillaren und ansässigen Makrophagen. LH bindet an den G-Protein-gekoppelten LH-Rezeptor (LHCGR) auf der Plasmamembran und aktiviert die Adenylatzyklase; das resultierende cAMP/PKA-Signal phosphoryliert und induziert das steroidogene akute regulatorische Protein (StAR), das den geschwindigkeitsbestimmenden Transfer von Cholesterin von der äußeren zur inneren Mitochondrienmembran vermittelt. Dort wandelt CYP11A1 (Cholesterin-Seitenkettenspaltungsenzym) Cholesterin in Pregnenolon um, das zum glatten endoplasmatischen Retikulum exportiert und dort durch die 3β-Hydroxysteroid-Dehydrogenase, CYP17A1 (17α-Hydroxylase/17,20-Lyase) und die 17β-Hydroxysteroid-Dehydrogenase schrittweise zu Testosteron umgebaut wird. Da Steroide lipophil sind und nicht in Vesikeln verpackt werden, erfolgt die Sekretion als konstitutive Diffusion statt regulierter Exozytose; die intratestikulären Testosteronkonzentrationen liegen etwa 25- bis 100-mal höher als im Serum und unterstützen parakrin die Sertoli-Zell-Funktion und die Spermatogenese, während der Überschuss über die testikuläre Venendrainage den Körper systemisch mit Androgen versorgt. Leydig-Zellen sezernieren außerdem den insulinähnlichen Faktor 3 (INSL3), einen Marker für die differenzierte Leydig-Zell-Masse und einen Regulator des Hodenabstiegs in der Entwicklung. Ultrastrukturell zeigen sie zahlreiche Mitochondrien mit charakteristischen tubulovesikulären Cristae (Ausdruck ihrer Rolle in der Steroidsynthese statt oxidativer ATP-Produktion), reichlich Lipidtröpfchen mit gespeicherten Cholesterinestern und, in reifen adulten Zellen, zytoplasmatische Reinke-Kristalloide — Proteinkristallaggregate mit ungeklärter Funktion, die ein art- und reifungsspezifisches histologisches Kennzeichen der menschlichen Leydig-Zelle darstellen.
