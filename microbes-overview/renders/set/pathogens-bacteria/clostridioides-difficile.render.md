## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

*Clostridioides difficile* (reclassified out of *Clostridium* in 2016, family *Peptostreptococcaceae*) is a **Gram-positive**, **obligately anaerobic**, **spore-forming bacillus**. The vegetative cell is a straight to slightly curved rod that is **notably long and slender** — Bergey's/ABIS gives **0.5–1.9 µm wide × 3.0–16.9 µm long**, clinical sources quote the everyday **3–6 × ~0.5 µm**, so the honest statement for an illustration is *roughly five to fifteen times longer than it is wide*. Cells are pleomorphic and occur singly, in pairs or in short chains. Motile strains (630, R20291 and relatives) carry **peritrichous flagella** distributed over the whole cell body, built from the glycosylated flagellin FliC, which is itself pro-inflammatory through TLR5.

The **envelope is the first thing an illustration has to get right**, because the intuitive "bacterium" picture is a Gram-negative one and that is wrong here. From inside out the layers are: **plasma membrane → a thick peptidoglycan cell wall** carrying the anionic cell-wall polysaccharides PS-I/PS-II **→ a paracrystalline proteinaceous S-layer**. The S-layer is built from the SlpA precursor cleaved by Cwp84 into an inner HMW subunit (anchored to PS-II via CWB2 motifs) and an exposed outer LMW subunit, and it accounts for roughly 15 % of the cell's mass. There is **no outer membrane and no LPS / O-antigen** anywhere. One honest caveat: cultures older than about 48 h decolourise and stain Gram-variable or even Gram-negative in clinical smears — that is a staining artefact of an ageing thick wall, not a second membrane. A classical capsule was reported in a 1989 ruthenium-red EM study, but it is labile and fixative-dependent, so modern work frames the surface as *S-layer plus cell-wall polysaccharide* rather than a fat capsule; an illustration should not draw a dominant capsule.

The **signature structure is the endospore**, and it is why this organism is drawn differently from every other rod in the atlas. Sporulation (Spo0A phosphorelay → sigma-factor cascade) produces a single oval spore in a **subterminal** position — near one end but with ordinary cell body continuing past it to the pole — and the spore **visibly swells and distends the rod at that point**, producing the classic **club / tennis-racquet** outline that is the organism's diagnostic silhouette. *(Point of source disagreement, flagged: a minority of references describe the spore as terminal in some strains; the majority and the standard teaching description is subterminal, which is what these renders show.)* The spore is layered, from inside out: **core** (dehydrated cytoplasm holding the chromosome and calcium-dipicolinate) bounded by the inner forespore membrane and a thin germ cell wall → **cortex** of modified peptidoglycan → laminated proteinaceous **inner and outer coat** → the outermost bumpy **exosporium** with hair-like BclA projections. Unusually for a sporeformer, that exosporium sits directly on the coat with no interspace gap, and two exosporium morphotypes arise from a single clonal culture.

Ecologically the vegetative cell is **extremely oxygen-sensitive** and its disease niche is the **anaerobic colonic lumen and the mucus layer over the epithelium** — so the illustration's context is the gut, never open air. Only the spore survives oxygen, and it survives it for months on surfaces; that, plus the fact that **spores resist alcohol**, is the whole reason soap-and-water handwashing and chlorine-based sporicidal cleaning matter more here than hand gel. Germination is triggered by taurocholate-family primary bile acids together with glycine, and is inhibited by the secondary bile acids that an intact microbiota produces by 7α-dehydroxylation (*Clostridium scindens* and relatives) — the molecular basis of bile-acid-mediated colonisation resistance, and the reason antibiotics unlock the infection and faecal microbiota transplantation closes it again. Disease itself is toxin-mediated: the PaLoc encodes **TcdA** and **TcdB** (with *tcdR*/*tcdC* regulation), whose glucosyltransferase domains modify Rho/Rac/Cdc42, collapsing the colonocyte cytoskeleton and tight junctions; hypervirulent lineages (RT027/BI/NAP1) add the binary toxin CDT.

**Colour is a teaching convention here, not the organism's colour.** *C. difficile* makes no pigment: colonies are flat, irregular, grey to off-white and non-haemolytic (the barnyard odour from p-cresol and the chartreuse fluorescence at ~360–365 nm are metabolite effects, not a visible pigment), and every colour in a published SEM/TEM of this species is digital false colour. The palette used consistently across all four styles is therefore a didactic legend: **slate-blue S-layer · sage-green peptidoglycan wall · violet plasma membrane · warm ivory-amber cytoplasm · dusty blue-purple nucleoid · teal ribosomes · pale-gold spore core · pale-sage spore cortex · dark umber laminated spore coat · coral-pink flagella.**

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **A Gram-negative double envelope** — no outer membrane, no two-membrane sandwich with a periplasm between them, and **no shaggy LPS / O-antigen fringe**. This organism is Gram-positive: membrane, thick wall, S-layer.
- **A coccus, sphere, diplococcus or grape cluster** — it is a long slender rod.
- **A thick polysaccharide capsule** dominating the outline — the S-layer is a thin paracrystalline protein sheath, not a fat halo, and a true capsule in this species is contested.
- **An endospore drawn dead-centre in the cell**, or **capping the very tip** — it is *subterminal*: near one end, with a clear length of ordinary slim rod continuing beyond it to the pole.
- **A spore free-floating outside or beside the cell** as if it were a separate organism — the illustrated spore is inside its mother cell.
- **A virus-like geometric or icosahedral capsid**, spikes, or any phage-like symmetry.
- **A mesosome** — a chemical-fixation artefact, not a real structure.
- The nucleoid as a **tidy little circular loop** or a cartoon "plasmid ring" — it is an irregular, lobed, condensed DNA mass.
- Any **membrane-bound organelle** (no nucleus, mitochondria, ER or Golgi).
- **Polar tufts only** — the flagella are peritrichous, all over the body.
- An **open-air / soil-with-blue-sky** setting — the clinical context is the oxygen-free gut lumen next to colonic epithelium.
- **Saturated "true colour"** implying a real pigment — there is none (see §1).

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Clostridioides difficile — universally shortened to C. diff — is a spore-forming, strictly anaerobic Gram-positive bacterium of the large intestine. A small fraction of healthy adults carry it without ever noticing; the trouble starts when the community around it changes. Broad-spectrum antibiotics prescribed for something else thin out the gut microbiota and strip away what microbiologists call colonisation resistance, notably by altering bile-acid metabolism, so the compounds that normally keep C. difficile dormant disappear. Freed from competition, it expands and produces two toxins, TcdA and TcdB. These enter the cells lining the colon and attach sugar groups to Rho GTPases, the molecular switches that govern the cell's internal scaffolding. The cytoskeleton collapses, the tight junctions between neighbouring cells come apart, and the result is inflammation, watery diarrhoea and, in severe cases, the yellowish plaques of pseudomembranous colitis. C. difficile is the classic healthcare-associated infection because its spores persist for months on bed rails, toilets and hands, and because they shrug off alcohol rub — which is why soap-and-water handwashing and chlorine-based sporicidal surface cleaning remain the mainstays of control. Treatment is a targeted antibiotic, vancomycin or fidaxomicin; metronidazole is no longer recommended first-line. Recurrence is common, affecting roughly one in five patients after a first episode, and for repeated disease faecal microbiota transplantation or a licensed live biotherapeutic can rebuild the community that kept it in check.  
🇩🇪 Clostridioides difficile — überall nur C. diff genannt — ist ein sporenbildendes, streng anaerobes grampositives Bakterium des Dickdarms. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Clostridioides difficile was transferred out of Clostridium in 2016 on 16S rRNA and phenotypic grounds and now sits in the Peptostreptococcaceae. It is an obligate anaerobe, a Gram-positive rod of roughly 3–16 × 0.5–2 µm, motile by peritrichous flagella, sheathed in a paracrystalline S-layer assembled from the SlpA cleavage products, and forming a subterminal endospore that distends the mother cell. Sporulation runs through the Spo0A phosphorelay and a compartmentalised sigma-factor cascade (SigF, SigE, SigG, SigK), yielding a dehydrated core, cortex peptidoglycan, proteinaceous coat and exosporium. Germination is unusual: the receptor CspC senses taurocholate-family primary bile acids with glycine as co-germinant, while secondary bile acids generated by 7α-dehydroxylating commensals such as Clostridium scindens block germination and outgrowth — the biochemical core of bile-acid-mediated colonisation resistance that antibiotics abolish. Virulence rests on the pathogenicity locus (PaLoc) encoding TcdA and TcdB under the sigma factor TcdR and the putative anti-sigma factor TcdC. Both are large clostridial glucosyltransferases: after receptor binding, endosomal acidification and autoprotease-mediated release of the catalytic domain, they mono-O-glucosylate RhoA, Rac1 and Cdc42, collapsing the actin cytoskeleton, disassembling tight junctions and provoking neutrophil influx and pseudomembrane formation. Hypervirulent lineages such as ribotype 027 (BI/NAP1) additionally carry the binary ADP-ribosylating toxin CDT. Diagnosis follows a two-step algorithm — GDH antigen or NAAT for tcdB, confirmed by toxin EIA — and therapy uses fidaxomicin or vancomycin, with bezlotoxumab and FMT or live biotherapeutics for recurrence.  
🇩🇪 Clostridioides difficile wurde 2016 aufgrund von 16S-rRNA-Phylogenie und Phänotyp aus der Gattung Clostridium ausgegliedert und steht nun in der Familie Peptostreptococcaceae. Es ist ein obligater Anaerobier, ein grampositives Stäbchen von etwa 3–16 × 0,5–2 µm, beweglich durch peritriche Geißeln, umhüllt von einer parakristallinen S-Schicht aus den Spaltprodukten von SlpA, und bildet eine subterminale Endospore, die die Mutterzelle auftreibt. Die Sporulation läuft über das Spo0A-Phosphorelais und eine kompartimentierte Sigmafaktor-Kaskade (SigF, SigE, SigG, SigK) und erzeugt einen dehydrierten Core, ein Cortex-Peptidoglykan, eine Proteinhülle und ein Exosporium. Die Keimung ist ungewöhnlich: Der Rezeptor CspC erkennt primäre Gallensäuren der Taurocholat-Familie, mit Glycin als Co-Germinans, während sekundäre Gallensäuren, die von 7α-dehydroxylierenden Kommensalen wie Clostridium scindens gebildet werden, Keimung und Auswachsen hemmen — der biochemische Kern der gallensäurevermittelten Kolonisationsresistenz, die Antibiotika aufheben. Die Virulenz beruht auf dem Pathogenitätslocus (PaLoc) mit tcdA und tcdB unter Kontrolle des Sigmafaktors TcdR und des mutmaßlichen Anti-Sigmafaktors TcdC. Beide Toxine sind große clostridiale Glucosyltransferasen: Nach Rezeptorbindung, endosomaler Ansäuerung und autoproteolytischer Freisetzung der katalytischen Domäne mono-O-glucosylieren sie RhoA, Rac1 und Cdc42, wodurch das Aktinzytoskelett kollabiert, Tight Junctions zerfallen und Neutrophileneinstrom sowie Pseudomembranbildung ausgelöst werden. Hypervirulente Linien wie Ribotyp 027 (BI/NAP1) tragen zusätzlich das binäre ADP-ribosylierende Toxin CDT. Die Diagnostik folgt einem zweistufigen Algorithmus — GDH-Antigen oder NAAT auf tcdB, bestätigt durch Toxin-EIA —, therapiert wird mit Fidaxomicin oder Vancomycin, bei Rezidiven ergänzt durch Bezlotoxumab sowie FMT oder lebende Biotherapeutika.
