## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

*Borrelia burgdorferi* is a **spirochaete** — the first one in this atlas, and its body plan is unlike anything else in the collection. The cell is extraordinarily long and thin: roughly **10–30 µm from end to end but only 0.2–0.33 µm wide**, i.e. something like fifty to a hundred times longer than it is wide, so it reads as a hair or a thread rather than as a rod. It is not a rigid corkscrew. Freeze-fracture and cryo-electron-tomography work established that the resting shape is a **flat wave** (a "flat-wave" or planar sine-like undulation, wavelength ≈ 2.8 µm, amplitude ≈ 0.8 µm) — loose, irregular, gently serpentine, closer to a wet noodle laid on a table than to a spring.

The defining feature is **where the motor sits**. *B. burgdorferi* carries **7–11 flagella inserted subterminally at each pole** (7–11 per end in most strains), and these flagella never leave the cell. They run **inside the periplasmic space**, the compartment between the **inner (cytoplasmic) membrane + thin peptidoglycan wall** and the loosely attached **outer membrane / outer sheath**. The two polar bundles wrap around the protoplasmic cylinder as a ribbon and overlap near the middle of the cell. Because they are enclosed, they are called **periplasmic flagella** or **endoflagella**. When the motors turn, the flagellar ribbon rotates *inside* the sheath and the whole cell body is driven into a travelling wave — the organism literally corkscrews itself forward. This is why it moves *better* in viscous, gel-like media (skin, connective tissue, joint fluid) where ordinary externally-flagellated bacteria stall. The flagella also *make* the shape: mutants that cannot build them are straight rods.

The envelope has a second oddity. Although the architecture is Gram-negative-like, the outer membrane is **unusually poor in lipopolysaccharide** — *B. burgdorferi* lacks classical LPS and instead studs its surface with abundant **surface lipoproteins**. It **swaps that coat as it changes host**: **OspA** dominates while the spirochaete sits in the midgut of an unfed *Ixodes* tick, and expression shifts towards **OspC** (and later VlsE, whose variable cassettes are shuffled to dodge antibodies) as the tick feeds and the bacteria move into the mammal. That coat switch is a large part of why transmission takes time — the tick usually has to stay attached for many hours before the spirochaetes are ready to cross over — and it is exactly why removing a tick early works so well.

Inside, the cytoplasm is a thin thread containing 70S ribosomes and a genome that is itself unusual for a bacterium: a **linear chromosome** of about 900 kb plus a large collection of **linear and circular plasmids** (around twenty), which carry most of the surface-lipoprotein genes. Metabolically it is minimal — no TCA cycle, no respiratory chain, no iron requirement (it uses manganese instead) — so it must scavenge nutrients from its host.

Sources: [Charon et al., *The Unique Paradigm of Spirochete Motility and Chemotaxis*, Annu Rev Microbiol (PMC3771638)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3771638/), [Radolf et al., *Of ticks, mice and men: understanding the dual-host lifestyle of Lyme disease spirochaetes*, Nat Rev Microbiol (PMC3313462)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3313462/), [StatPearls — *Lyme Disease*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431066/), [NCBI *Medical Microbiology* (Baron), Ch. 36 — *Borrelia*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK8451/), [Wikipedia — *Borrelia burgdorferi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrelia_burgdorferi).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **External whip-like flagella on the outside of the cell** — this is the single most common error. Every flagellum is **inside the periplasmic space**, under the outer sheath. Nothing propulsive sticks out.
- **A rigid, tightly-wound corkscrew spring / stiff helix** — the body is a **loose, irregular flat wave**, not a coiled spring.
- **A short comma or gull-wing curve like *Campylobacter* or *Helicobacter*** — those are curved rods, an entirely different body plan.
- **A plain straight rod**, or any short, fat cell — it must read as extremely long and thin.
- **A capsule** or thick polysaccharide halo — there is none.
- A shaggy classical **LPS / O-antigen fringe** — the outer membrane is unusually **LPS-poor**; the surface carries lipoproteins instead.
- **Mesosome** — fixation artefact, not real.
- The nucleoid as a tidy **circular** DNA loop — the main chromosome is **linear**.
- Any **membrane-bound organelles** (no nucleus, mitochondria, ER or Golgi).
- Ticks, blades of grass, skin or other scene props in the four science styles (they belong on the coloring page, not here).

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Borrelia burgdorferi is the bacterium behind Lyme borreliosis, by far the most common tick-borne infection in Europe and North America - and a genuinely everyday concern in German-speaking countries, where ticks are widespread in gardens, meadows and woodland edges. It is a spirochaete: a thread-like cell up to 30 µm long but only about a quarter of a micrometre thick, which swims by rotating a bundle of flagella that stay sealed inside its own outer sheath. Because the motor is internal, the entire body ripples and drills forward, and it moves best in exactly the viscous tissue - dermis, connective tissue, joint fluid - where most bacteria bog down. It lives in a two-host cycle between small mammals or birds and Ixodes ticks, changing its surface protein coat as it moves from tick gut to mammal skin. That changeover takes time, which is the practical key: a tick usually has to feed for many hours, often well over a day, before it can transmit anything. So the first line of defence is mechanical and completely in your hands - check the skin after time outdoors, and pull an attached tick straight out with fine tweezers as soon as you find it. The classic early sign is erythema migrans, a slowly expanding red ring around the bite; less often the infection spreads to joints, nerves or the heart. Diagnosis in the early stage is clinical, since antibody tests lag behind the rash. Treatment is a two-to-three-week course of oral antibiotics - doxycycline, or amoxicillin for children and in pregnancy - and started early it clears the infection completely in the great majority of cases. There is no human vaccine in routine use, though candidates are in late-stage trials.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Borrelia burgdorferi sensu lato is a microaerophilic, host-associated spirochaete of the family Borreliaceae and the aetiological agent of Lyme borreliosis; in Europe the species complex is dominated by B. afzelii and B. garinii alongside B. burgdorferi sensu stricto, which underlies the broader clinical spectrum seen there. The cell is 10-30 µm long and 0.2-0.33 µm wide, and its resting morphology is a planar flat wave (wavelength approximately 2.8 µm, amplitude approximately 0.8 µm) rather than a rigid helix. Motility derives from 7-11 periplasmic flagella (endoflagella) inserted subterminally at each pole, which wrap the protoplasmic cylinder as a ribbon within the periplasmic space and overlap near mid-cell; rotation of this internal ribbon within the outer sheath propagates a travelling wave along the entire body, giving the organism a rare capacity for translational motility that increases rather than decreases in gel-like media. The flagellar ribbon is also morphogenetic - flaB-deficient mutants are rod-shaped and non-motile. The envelope is diderm but atypical: it lacks classical lipopolysaccharide, presenting instead a dense array of surface lipoproteins whose expression is reciprocally regulated across the enzootic cycle by the Rrp2-RpoN-RpoS pathway, with OspA predominant in the unfed Ixodes midgut and OspC induced during tick feeding and required for establishment in the mammalian host, followed by antigenic variation of VlsE through segmental gene conversion from the vls silent cassettes. The genome is unusual for a bacterium: an approximately 910 kb linear chromosome plus roughly twenty linear and circular plasmids that carry most surface-lipoprotein loci. Metabolism is markedly reduced - no tricarboxylic acid cycle, no electron transport chain, and manganese rather than iron as the catalytic metal - obliging the organism to scavenge nucleotides, amino acids and fatty acids from its hosts. Immune evasion further includes binding of host complement regulators factor H and FHL-1 via CspA and OspE-family proteins. Because transmission requires prolonged attachment while the surface programme is remodelled, prompt tick removal is a genuinely effective interruption of the cycle, and early oral antibiotic therapy (doxycycline, amoxicillin or cefuroxime axetil for 10-21 days depending on manifestation) is curative in the large majority of cases; persistent post-treatment symptoms are not associated with demonstrable ongoing infection.  
🇩🇪 Borrelia burgdorferi sensu lato ist eine mikroaerophile, wirtsassoziierte Spirochäte aus der Familie Borreliaceae und der Erreger der Lyme-Borreliose; in Europa dominieren im Artkomplex B. afzelii und B. garinii neben B. burgdorferi sensu stricto, was das dort breitere klinische Spektrum erklärt. Die Zelle ist 10-30 µm lang und 0,2-0,33 µm breit, und ihre Ruhemorphologie ist eine ebene Flachwelle (Wellenlänge etwa 2,8 µm, Amplitude etwa 0,8 µm) und keine starre Helix. Die Beweglichkeit beruht auf 7-11 periplasmatischen Geißeln (Endoflagellen), die an jedem Pol subterminal inserieren, den protoplasmatischen Zylinder als Band innerhalb des periplasmatischen Raums umwickeln und sich in der Zellmitte überlappen; die Rotation dieses inneren Bandes unter der Außenhülle lässt eine Wanderwelle über den gesamten Zellkörper laufen und verleiht dem Organismus die seltene Fähigkeit, sich in gelartigen Medien besser statt schlechter fortzubewegen. Das Geißelband ist zugleich formgebend - flaB-defiziente Mutanten sind stäbchenförmig und unbeweglich. Die Hülle ist zwar zweischichtig, aber untypisch: Klassisches Lipopolysaccharid fehlt, stattdessen trägt die Oberfläche eine dichte Schicht aus Lipoproteinen, deren Expression im enzootischen Zyklus über den Rrp2-RpoN-RpoS-Signalweg reziprok reguliert wird - OspA überwiegt im ungesogenen Ixodes-Mitteldarm, OspC wird während der Blutmahlzeit induziert und ist für die Etablierung im Säugerwirt erforderlich, gefolgt von der Antigenvariation des VlsE durch segmentale Genkonversion aus den stillen vls-Kassetten. Das Genom ist für ein Bakterium ungewöhnlich: ein etwa 910 kb großes lineares Chromosom sowie rund zwanzig lineare und ringförmige Plasmide, die die meisten Oberflächen-Lipoprotein-Loci tragen. Der Stoffwechsel ist stark reduziert - kein Citratzyklus, keine Atmungskette und Mangan statt Eisen als katalytisches Metall -, sodass Nukleotide, Aminosäuren und Fettsäuren aus dem Wirt bezogen werden müssen. Zur Immunevasion gehört ferner die Bindung der Komplementregulatoren Faktor H und FHL-1 über CspA und Proteine der OspE-Familie. Da die Übertragung ein längeres Saugen erfordert, während das Oberflächenprogramm umgebaut wird, unterbricht eine rasche Zeckenentfernung den Zyklus tatsächlich wirksam, und eine früh begonnene orale Antibiotikatherapie (Doxycyclin, Amoxicillin oder Cefuroximaxetil über 10-21 Tage je nach Manifestation) ist in der großen Mehrzahl der Fälle heilend; anhaltende Beschwerden nach der Therapie sind nicht mit nachweisbar fortbestehender Infektion assoziiert.
