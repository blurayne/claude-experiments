## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

*Listeria monocytogenes* is a short, **Gram-positive**, non-branching, **non-sporing** rod — properly a coccobacillus — of roughly **0.4–0.5 µm across × 1–2 µm long**. For an illustration that means *only about two to four times longer than it is wide*: distinctly stubby, and emphatically **not** the long slender filament of *Clostridioides difficile* or *Bacillus*. The envelope, inside out, is short: **plasma membrane → one thick peptidoglycan cell wall** carrying wall teichoic acids. That is all. There is **no outer membrane, no LPS/O-antigen fringe, and no capsule**, and — the point worth repeating because it is the single most common way this organism is drawn wrongly — **no endospore, at any position, ever**. *Listeria* does not sporulate. A spore in this picture would be a hard fail.

Motility is **temperature-gated, not constitutive**. Grown at or below about **30 °C** the cell puts out a *small number* of **peritrichous flagella** (roughly one to six) and shows a characteristic tumbling motion; at mammalian body temperature the flagellar regulon is switched off, so most cells in a host are non-motile and immunologically quiet with respect to flagellin/TLR5. The switch is well characterised: the repressor **MogR** silences the flagellar genes at 37 °C, while at ≤30 °C the thermosensitive antirepressor **GmaR** binds and neutralises MogR and flagellar synthesis resumes. *(Knowing compromise, flagged: the renders show the intracellular, 37 °C state — where flagella would in fact be repressed — yet still draw two to four flagella, because the structure belongs in a teaching figure of the organism. Read them as "what this cell has", not "what it is expressing at that instant". The label carries the ≤30 °C caveat.)*

**The intracellular life cycle is the organism's defining feature, and the centrepiece of these illustrations.** Entry into ordinary non-phagocytic cells is receptor-mediated by two surface invasins: **internalin A (InlA)**, which grips the host adherens-junction protein **E-cadherin**, and **internalin B (InlB)**, which grips the receptor tyrosine kinase **Met**. Both routes pull the bacterium into a membrane-bound vacuole. Within minutes the bacterium ruptures that vacuole with **listeriolysin O (LLO, *hly*)** — a cholesterol-dependent pore-forming cytolysin tuned to the acidic vacuolar pH so that it does *not* simply perforate the plasma membrane — helped by the phospholipases **PlcA** and **PlcB** from the same LIPI-1 island. It is now loose in the host **cytosol**, which is where every one of these renders is set.

There it expresses **ActA**, a surface protein whose distribution is deliberately **asymmetric and unipolar**, concentrated at the **old pole** left over from the previous division. ActA mimics the host's own WASP/N-WASP nucleation-promoting factors: it recruits and activates the host **Arp2/3 complex**, which nucleates a dense **branched (dendritic) network of new actin filaments** against the bacterial surface, while host **VASP** and **profilin** speed elongation at the growing end. Continuous polymerisation at that one pole ratchets the cell forward at roughly **0.02–1.4 µm/s**, smoothly or in a saltatory stop-start fashion. The structure left behind is the **actin comet tail** — and the whole teaching point of this figure is that **the tail is host cytoskeleton, not a bacterial appendage**. It is a comet-shaped brush of **many short, cross-linked, branching actin filaments**, several micrometres long, that stays behind as the bacterium moves and slowly depolymerises. It is *not* a flagellum, not a rudder, not an exhaust flame, and not a set of motion lines. When a moving bacterium reaches the plasma membrane it drives it outward into a finger-like **double-membrane protrusion** that the neighbouring cell engulfs — so the organism spreads cell to cell without ever re-entering the extracellular space where antibodies could reach it.

Physiologically it is a facultative anaerobe, catalase-positive and oxidase-negative, and — the trait that makes it a *food* pathogen rather than a curiosity — it is **psychrotrophic**: it grows slowly down to about **0–4 °C**, i.e. inside a working refrigerator, and tolerates salt (to roughly 10 % NaCl) and moderate acid. That is why it accumulates in **refrigerated ready-to-eat** foods that get no further cooking: raw-milk soft cheeses, cold-smoked fish, pâté, deli meats, hot dogs and pre-packed salads. On blood agar it gives a narrow zone of **β-haemolysis** from LLO, with a positive CAMP-type reaction. Clinically: mild febrile gastroenteritis in healthy adults, but invasive **bacteraemia and meningitis** in neonates, the elderly and the immunocompromised, and a marked tropism for **placental tissue** in pregnancy (InlA/InlB again) that can cause miscarriage, stillbirth or neonatal sepsis. Refrigeration does not control it; **thorough heating (>70 °C core) reliably kills it**, which is why the advice for high-risk groups is to heat deli meats until steaming and skip unpasteurised soft cheese altogether.

**Colour is a teaching convention here, not the organism's colour.** *L. monocytogenes* makes no pigment: colonies are small and translucent, showing the blue-grey "ground-glass" sheen under Henry oblique transmitted light, and the narrow β-haemolysis on blood agar is lysis of the *medium*, not cell colour. Every colour in a published SEM/TEM of this species is digital false colour. The palette used identically across all four styles is therefore a didactic legend: **sage-green peptidoglycan wall · violet plasma membrane · warm ivory-amber bacterial cytoplasm · dusty blue-purple nucleoid · teal ribosomes · coral-pink flagella · gold/ochre ActA at the tail-forming pole**, and — deliberately from a *different colour family from every bacterial structure, so the viewer reads it as hijacked host material* — a **cool pale blue-white actin comet tail**, against a **muted dusky mauve host-cell cytoplasm**.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **Any endospore, forespore or sporangial swelling** — *Listeria* does **not** sporulate. This is the single most common confusion with *Clostridium*/*Bacillus* and a **hard fail** for this species. No knob, no bulge, no oval internal compartment, no local widening of the cell body.
- **A Gram-negative double envelope** — no outer membrane, no membrane–periplasm–membrane sandwich, no shaggy LPS / O-antigen fringe. Gram-positive: plasma membrane plus **one** thick peptidoglycan wall.
- **A capsule** — this species does not produce one.
- **A coccus, sphere, diplococcus or bead-chain** — it is a short rod / coccobacillus.
- **A long slender filament many times longer than wide** (that is *C. difficile* or *Bacillus*) — *Listeria* is **short**, roughly 2–4× longer than wide.
- **The actin comet tail drawn as a flagellum**, a single smooth solid rudder or blade, a fish tail, a rocket exhaust flame, or as motion lines / speed streaks / a radiating starburst. It must read as a **bushy, irregular meshwork of many short branching filaments**, never a single continuous shape.
- **The actin tail at both poles, at the sides, or wrapped around the cell** — it is strictly **unipolar**, from one old pole only.
- **A dense polar tuft of flagella** — real flagella here are few (≈1–6) and **peritrichous**, scattered over the body.
- **A mesosome** — a chemical-fixation artefact, not a real structure.
- Any **membrane-bound organelle** inside the bacterium (no nucleus, mitochondria, ER or Golgi).
- **Free-floating in open water or air under a blue sky** — the correct context is **inside a host cell's cytoplasm**.
- **Saturated "true colour"** implying a real pigment — there is none (see §1).

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet Listeria — a short, stubby little rod with a very strange trick. Most germs get lazy in the cold, but Listeria keeps right on growing inside the fridge, quietly, on a slice of cold ham or a soft cheese, while everything else is fast asleep. And once it slips inside one of your cells, it does something almost cheeky: it grabs the springy scaffolding poles the cell uses to hold its own shape, glues them together behind itself into a bushy tail, and rides that tail like a rocket right through the cell and into the cell next door. Listeria can shrug off cold and salt, but it cannot survive being properly cooked — steaming hot all the way to the middle, not just warm on the outside. That is why leftovers get reheated until they steam, and why smoked fish and soft cheese made from raw milk go into the sandwich of grown-ups rather than into a lunchbox for a pregnant mum. A hot pan is the one thing this fridge-loving rod has no answer for.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Listeria monocytogenes is a short Gram-positive rod that lives in soil, water and silage and finds its way into food. What makes it unusual among foodborne bacteria is that it is psychrotrophic: it grows at refrigerator temperatures, down to around 0 °C, and it also tolerates salt and moderately acidic conditions. Chilling therefore slows it but does not stop it, and a contaminated ready-to-eat food can accumulate a meaningful dose over its shelf life without ever looking, smelling or tasting wrong. The classic risky products are all eaten cold and stored long: soft cheeses made from raw milk, cold-smoked fish, pâté, deli meats and pre-packed salads. The second unusual thing is where it goes. Rather than staying in the gut lumen, Listeria persuades intestinal cells to swallow it, breaks out of the vacuole they trap it in, and then hijacks the host cell's own actin cytoskeleton. A protein on one end of the bacterium recruits the host machinery that builds actin filaments, and the growing mesh pushes the bacterium forward at up to a micrometre per second — the comet tail seen in fluorescence microscopy. Driven into the neighbouring cell as a finger-like protrusion, it spreads from cell to cell without ever entering the bloodstream where antibodies could reach it. For most healthy adults the result is nothing at all, or a short bout of fever and diarrhoea. The danger is concentrated in pregnancy, in newborns, in the elderly and in people whose cell-mediated immunity is suppressed, where it can cause bloodstream infection and meningitis, or cross the placenta and threaten the pregnancy. Because it is invasive rather than toxin-mediated, listeriosis is treated with antibiotics — ampicillin, usually with gentamicin. Prevention is straightforward and mostly thermal: heat food through until it steams, keep the fridge genuinely cold, respect use-by dates, and during pregnancy skip the raw-milk soft cheeses, cold-smoked fish and cold deli meats rather than gambling on them.  
🇩🇪 Listeria monocytogenes ist ein kurzes grampositives Stäbchen, das in Boden, Wasser und Silage lebt und von dort in Lebensmittel gelangt. Unter den lebensmittelbedingten Erregern ist es ungewöhnlich, weil es psychrotroph ist: Es wächst bei Kühlschranktemperatur, bis hinunter zu etwa 0 °C, und verträgt zudem Salz und mäßig saure Bedingungen. Kühlen bremst es also, stoppt es aber nicht, und ein kontaminiertes verzehrfertiges Lebensmittel kann über seine Haltbarkeitsdauer eine relevante Keimzahl aufbauen, ohne dass es auffällig aussieht, riecht oder schmeckt. Die klassischen Risikoprodukte werden alle kalt gegessen und lange gelagert: Weichkäse aus Rohmilch, kalt geräucherter Fisch, Pastete, Aufschnitt und abgepackte Salate. Das zweite Ungewöhnliche ist der Weg, den der Erreger nimmt. Statt im Darmlumen zu bleiben, bringt Listeria Darmzellen dazu, es aufzunehmen, bricht aus dem Vakuolen-Einschluss aus und kapert dann das Aktin-Zytoskelett der Wirtszelle. Ein Protein an einem Pol des Bakteriums rekrutiert die wirtseigene Maschinerie, die Aktinfilamente baut, und das wachsende Geflecht schiebt das Bakterium mit bis zu einem Mikrometer pro Sekunde vorwärts — der Kometenschweif, den man in der Fluoreszenzmikroskopie sieht. Bei den meisten gesunden Erwachsenen passiert daraufhin gar nichts oder es kommt zu einer kurzen Episode mit Fieber und Durchfall. Gefährlich wird es in der Schwangerschaft, bei Neugeborenen, bei alten Menschen und bei geschwächter zellulärer Immunabwehr: Dann drohen Blutstrominfektion und Meningitis, oder der Erreger überquert die Plazenta und gefährdet die Schwangerschaft. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Listeria monocytogenes is a facultatively anaerobic, non-spore-forming, catalase-positive Gram-positive coccobacillus of roughly 0.4–0.5 × 1–2 µm, motile at ≤30 °C by one to a few peritrichous flagella and effectively non-motile at 37 °C, where the repressor MogR — antagonised by the thermosensitive anti-repressor GmaA/GmaR — silences flagellar gene expression. It is psychrotrophic, halotolerant and acid-tolerant, growing from about 0 °C to 45 °C, which underlies its epidemiology in refrigerated ready-to-eat foods. Virulence is coordinated by the master regulator PrfA and clusters largely in Listeria pathogenicity island 1. Entry into non-phagocytic cells is receptor-mediated: internalin A (InlA) engages E-cadherin on epithelial junctions, InlB engages the hepatocyte growth-factor receptor Met, and both routes are exploited at the intestinal barrier, the blood-brain barrier and the placenta, where the species tropism of InlA–E-cadherin binding explains why murine models underestimate human enteric invasion. Once internalised, the cholesterol-dependent pore-forming cytolysin listeriolysin O (hly), acid-activated and tuned to the phagosomal pH so that it does not perforate the plasma membrane, cooperates with the phospholipases PlcA and PlcB and the metalloprotease Mpl to rupture the vacuole and release the bacterium into the cytosol. There the surface protein ActA, distributed asymmetrically and concentrated at the older pole, mimics host nucleation-promoting factors: its acidic and proline-rich regions bind G-actin, profilin–actin and Ena/VASP and activate the Arp2/3 complex, nucleating a dense branched dendritic network that assembles a unipolar comet tail of host actin filaments and propels the bacterium at approximately 0.02–1.4 µm/s. Impact on the plasma membrane generates listeriopods that are engulfed by the neighbouring cell, yielding a double-membrane secondary vacuole lysed by LLO and PlcB, so that spread proceeds intracellularly and largely shielded from humoral immunity. Control depends on cell-mediated immunity, which is why listeriosis concentrates in pregnancy, neonates, the elderly and T-cell-suppressed patients; therapy is ampicillin, commonly with an aminoglycoside, since cephalosporins are intrinsically inactive. The actin comet tail is host cytoskeleton, not a bacterial appendage, and this system — together with Shigella IcsA and vaccinia A36 — is a foundational model for actin-based motility.  
🇩🇪 Listeria monocytogenes ist ein fakultativ anaerobes, nicht sporenbildendes, katalasepositives grampositives Kokkobazillus von etwa 0,4–0,5 × 1–2 µm, bei ≤30 °C beweglich durch eine bis wenige peritriche Geißeln und bei 37 °C praktisch unbeweglich, da dort der Repressor MogR — antagonisiert durch den thermosensitiven Anti-Repressor GmaA/GmaR — die Flagellengene stilllegt. Der Erreger ist psychrotroph, halotolerant und säuretolerant und wächst von etwa 0 °C bis 45 °C, was seine Epidemiologie in gekühlten verzehrfertigen Lebensmitteln erklärt. Die Virulenz wird vom Masterregulator PrfA koordiniert und liegt größtenteils in der Listeria-Pathogenitätsinsel 1. Der Eintritt in nichtphagozytierende Zellen ist rezeptorvermittelt: Internalin A (InlA) bindet E-Cadherin an epithelialen Zellkontakten, InlB bindet den Hepatozyten-Wachstumsfaktor-Rezeptor Met; beide Wege werden an der Darmbarriere, der Blut-Hirn-Schranke und der Plazenta genutzt, wobei die Speziesspezifität der InlA-E-Cadherin-Bindung erklärt, warum Mausmodelle die enterale Invasion beim Menschen unterschätzen. Nach der Aufnahme sprengt das cholesterinabhängige porenbildende Zytolysin Listeriolysin O (hly) — säureaktiviert und so auf den phagosomalen pH abgestimmt, dass es die Plasmamembran nicht perforiert — gemeinsam mit den Phospholipasen PlcA und PlcB sowie der Metalloprotease Mpl die Vakuole und entlässt das Bakterium ins Zytosol. Dort ahmt das asymmetrisch verteilte, am älteren Pol konzentrierte Oberflächenprotein ActA wirtseigene nukleationsfördernde Faktoren nach: Seine sauren und prolinreichen Regionen binden G-Aktin, Profilin-Aktin und Ena/VASP und aktivieren den Arp2/3-Komplex, wodurch ein dichtes verzweigtes dendritisches Netzwerk nukleiert wird, das einen unipolaren Kometenschweif aus Wirtsaktinfilamenten aufbaut und das Bakterium mit etwa 0,02–1,4 µm/s antreibt. Der Aufprall auf die Plasmamembran erzeugt Listeriopoden, die von der Nachbarzelle aufgenommen werden; die entstehende doppelmembranige Sekundärvakuole wird durch LLO und PlcB lysiert, sodass die Ausbreitung intrazellulär und weitgehend vor der humoralen Immunantwort geschützt verläuft. Die Kontrolle beruht auf zellvermittelter Immunität, weshalb sich die Listeriose auf Schwangerschaft, Neugeborene, Ältere und T-Zell-supprimierte Patienten konzentriert; therapiert wird mit Ampicillin, meist zusammen mit einem Aminoglykosid, da Cephalosporine intrinsisch unwirksam sind. Der Aktin-Kometenschweif ist Wirtszytoskelett, kein bakterielles Anhangsgebilde; dieses System ist — zusammen mit IcsA von Shigella und A36 des Vacciniavirus — ein grundlegendes Modell der aktinbasierten Motilität.
