## 0. Corrections to the brief this subject was commissioned with

The brief handed to this render agent stated the science it wanted respected. Four of its statements were checked against primary sources and needed correcting or sharpening. They are recorded here rather than smoothed over, in the same way the tapeworm subject recorded that the taeniid rostellum is non-retractable.

**1. How ALH 84001's Martian origin was established — corrected.** The brief said "Martian origin is established by trapped gases matching the Viking landers' measurement of the Martian atmosphere." That is how the *SNC clan as a whole* was tied to Mars — via trapped gas in impact glass in **EETA 79001** (Bogard & Johnson 1983) — but it is not how this stone was assigned. ALH 84001 was first classified as a **diogenite** (Score & MacPherson 1985; Mason et al. 1992) and was reassigned to the Martian meteorite clan on the strength of **oxygen-isotope analysis (Clayton 1993, Antarctic Meteorite Newsletter 16(4))**, supported by Fe³⁺-bearing chromite, pyrite in place of troilite, and an Fe/Mn ratio wrong for a diogenite (Berkley & Boynton 1992; Mittlefehldt 1993, 1994). The rock does contain a Martian atmospheric component, but elementally fractionated relative to the modern atmosphere. *The conclusion is unchanged — the Martian provenance is not in dispute — but the evidence for it is isotopic and petrographic, not the Viking gas match.* Source: NASA Martian Meteorite Compendium (Meyer 2012), ALH84001 entry.

**2. The ejection age — corrected from ~17 Ma to ~15 Ma.** The brief said "ejected by an impact ~17 Ma ago", which is the figure Wikipedia carries. The compendium lists every published cosmic-ray exposure measurement: Eugster 15 Ma, Miura 14.5, Murty 16.4, Swindle 15.8, Garrison & Bogard 14.1, Bogard 16 (²¹Ne) and 18 (³⁸Ar), Terribilini 15.8 (⁸¹Kr) and 15.5 (²¹Ne). **Nyquist et al. (2001) average these to 15.0 ± 0.8 Ma**, and that is the value used throughout this subject's text. 17 Ma sits at the top of the spread rather than at the consensus.

**3. The size floor for a free-living cell — sharpened.** The brief (and `cells_data.py`) put the 1999 US National Research Council figure at "roughly 200 nm". The workshop's actual finding is a **range of 200–300 nm**, with Panel 1's formal statement being **250 ± 50 nm**; 200 nm appears only as the extreme lower bound under favourable assumptions (very slow growth, so few ribosomes, in a nutritionally rich medium). Quoting 200 nm is therefore the most generous possible reading of the panel. The argument is unaffected and in fact strengthened: at 20–100 nm the ALH 84001 shapes are below even that most generous bound, by a factor of two to ten. Source: National Research Council (1999), *Size Limits of Very Small Microorganisms: Proceedings of a Workshop*, doi:10.17226/9638.

**4. The sputter-coating artefact — flagged as contested rather than settled.** The brief said "much of the apparent segmentation was an ARTEFACT of the gold/palladium sputter-coating applied for SEM". That is Bradley, Harvey & McSween's proposal (*Nature* 390:454, 1997), and their paper's *primary* claim is different and stronger: most of the elongated forms are **non-biological lamellar growth steps and cleavage lamellae on pyroxene and carbonate crystals**, with the beaded *segmentation* attributed to the conductive heavy-metal coating decorating a linear crystallographic step. McKay, Gibson, Thomas-Keprta & Vali's reply (*Nature* 390:455) conceded that numerous crystallographic features do exist on these surfaces. The coating component specifically, however, was later tested by imaging the same areas before and after Au/Pd coating and was **not** supported by that test. So: the crystallographic origin and the size argument are robust; the coating-artefact sub-claim is one contested contributor. The descriptions here lead with the size floor and the crystal growth steps, and treat the coating question as secondary.

**5. Provenance of the reference image — corrected.** The brief called the famous SEM plate "the famous 1996 SEM 'nanofossil' plate" and implied it came from the *Science* paper. It did not. NASA/JSC frame 96-12609 — the "Mars worm" — was released on **9 August 1996**, a week *before* the paper appeared, and NASA's own caption says so explicitly: *"Although this structure is not part of the research published in the Aug. 16 issue of the journal Science, it is located in a similar carbonate glob in the meteorite."* It is the image that carried the story to the public, not a figure from the paper. This is stated in §2 and in `verdicts.json`.

Two of the brief's other statements were checked and stand as written: **1.93 kg** (the compendium's 1931 g rounded) and **~13,000 years** terrestrial residence (Jull et al. 1995, ¹⁴C recalculated after leaching to separate carbon components; earlier ¹⁴C work had given ~6.5 ka). The crystallisation age of **4.091 ± 0.030 Ga** (Lapen et al. 2010, Lu–Hf mineral isochron) also stands.

## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

**This subject is not an organism.** It is a rock. Everything below describes a mineral specimen, and the single hard visual rule for every image in this entry is that nothing may look alive.

### The carbonate globules ("rosettes") — where the 1996 claim lives

- **100–250 µm** concentric spheroids, ~0.5 vol% of the rock, sitting in interstices inside the igneous rock and along natural fractures, where they are **"pancake-like"** (Romanek et al. 1995) and occur as *"minute thin pancakes found attached to broken surfaces"* (McKay et al. 1996; Scott et al. 1998).
- **Optical zoning, from the middle outwards:** dark orange → light orange → **black → white → black**, the "Oreo cookie" rim.
- **Chemical zoning:** Ca- and Fe-rich (with Mn) core grading to Mg-rich. The black outer zones (~1–5 µm) are **Fe- and S-rich and carry fine single-domain magnetite plus sulfide** (pyrrhotite, tentatively greigite, grains ~100 nm). The white sandwich zone is nearly pure **magnesite, with crystal faces projecting outward**.
- The compendium's own compiler notes, pointedly, that *"the exact nature of the black rims on the carbonate rosettes has not been properly characterized, which is surprising, considering that it is where the claim of evidence for life in this rock is to be found."*

### The 1996 claim and what happened to it

McKay D.S., Gibson E.K. Jr., Thomas-Keprta K.L., Vali H., Romanek C.S., Clemett S.J., Chillier X.D.F., Maechling C.R., Zare R.N. (1996), **"Search for Past Life on Mars: Possible Relic Biogenic Activity in Martian Meteorite ALH84001"**, *Science* **273**, 924–930. The title says *Possible*. The paper put forward a hypothesis, not a finding.

| The 1996 observation | The non-biological account |
|---|---|
| Carbonate globules formed by low-temperature aqueous alteration on Mars | Not disputed — this is real Martian water-rock chemistry, and it is the setting for the other three, not evidence of life on its own |
| Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons concentrated in and around the carbonate | PAHs form abiotically, are ubiquitous in carbonaceous meteorites and in the interstellar medium; and terrestrial contamination by Antarctic meltwater cannot be excluded over ~13,000 years in ice |
| Magnetite and iron-sulfide crystals in the black rims, in shapes resembling those made by magnetotactic bacteria | Golden et al. (2004, *Am. Mineral.* 89:681) measured 3D morphologies of ~1000 magnetite crystals from laboratory thermal decomposition of hydrothermally precipitated Fe-rich carbonate (3FeCO₃ → Fe₃O₄ + CO + 2CO₂), from ALH 84001 itself, and from magnetotactic strain MV-1. The ALH 84001 crystals are reproduced by the **inorganic** route and differ from MV-1's commonest habit |
| Segmented and ovoid shapes 20–100 nm on fracture surfaces, read as "nanofossils" | **Too small** — below the 200–300 nm floor a free-living DNA-based cell needs for a genome, ribosomes and proteome (NRC 1999). And **not shapes of biological origin** — Bradley et al. (1997, *Nature* 390:454) showed most are lamellar growth steps and cleavage lamellae on pyroxene and carbonate; the apparent beading was attributed to the Au/Pd conductive coating decorating a linear crystallographic step (that last point remains contested — see §0.4) |

And, covering the organic matter as a whole: **Steele A. et al. (2022), *Science* 375:172**, used co-located nanoscale imaging, isotopic analysis and spectroscopy to show the refractory organic material sits in mineral assemblages diagnostic of **serpentinisation and carbonation** — abiotic organic synthesis during water–rock interaction on early Mars ~4 Ga ago.

### Scope — do not blur this

**What is refuted is the claim about *this rock*.** Whether Mars ever hosted life is not settled by any of the above; it remains open, and it is exactly the question Curiosity and Perseverance are on the surface to investigate, and that Mars Sample Return is being planned around. Nothing in this entry says or implies "there was no life on Mars".

### Tone — equally load-bearing

No debunking sneer, no "scientists were fooled", no hoax framing. The 1996 measurements were real; the interpretation over-reached, and the paper hedged in its own title. The scrutiny that followed is the reason the field now has explicit criteria for what counts as a biosignature — morphology alone is not diagnostic; a candidate must be assessed against the full abiotic phase space of its mineralogical context — and it directly preceded the founding of the NASA Astrobiology Institute in 1998.

### What each style depicts, and why

This is a rock, so none of the four images is a micrograph of an organism, and the four are **not** four views of one body. They are one **scale ladder**:

- **3d** — the hand specimen as a museum object. *This is what ALH 84001 actually is.* Grey-brown hackly fusion crust, spalled at one end to show the coarse greenish-grey interior.
- **textbook** — a cutaway block of the rock, carrying both scales at once: the igneous matrix, a natural fracture, one zoned carbonate globule with its magnetite-bearing dark rim, and, elsewhere on the same fracture face, the granular texture with one segmented welt in it. *This is where the 1996 claim was located.*
- **watercolor** — the same globule at mineral scale as a 19th-century mineralogical plate, in cross-section in its pyroxene setting, with the fracture running through it.
- **sem** — the segmented shape at nanometre scale. *This is the modality the famous 1996 image actually is.*

In every style the segmented shape is composed as **welded mineral relief** — a row of knobs fused end to end, half sunk, with the surrounding grains banked against its flanks and no shadow beneath it — and never as a body lying on a surface. The prompts avoid the words *tube*, *rod*, *worm*, *body*, *capsule* and *organism* entirely, and describe the shape positively rather than saying what it must not be.

## 3. Audience descriptions (EN + DE)

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 This one is not alive — it is a rock, and it came from Mars. A lump the size of a big potato was blasted off Mars by a crash long ago, drifted through space, and landed on the ice in Antarctica. Scientists picked it up on a snowmobile ride two days after Christmas in 1984. Then, in 1996, a team looking at it through a very powerful microscope found something exciting: teeny sausage-shaped bumps, far too small to see any other way. Could these be fossils of Martian bacteria? They wrote it up carefully and said "maybe" — and then the best part of science happened. Research groups all over the world took the idea apart, piece by piece, for years. They measured the bumps and found them far too small to be a living cell: a real cell needs room inside for its instructions and its tiny machines, and these were about a hundred times too small for that. They found the bumps were shaped by the crystals in the stone, the way frost makes ridges on a window. And they discovered that the other clues in the rock could all be made by plain water trickling through hot stone, no life needed. So the answer was no — not this rock. That is not a disappointment, it is a win! Because of all that checking, we now know much better what a real trace of life would have to look like, and rovers are on Mars right now using exactly that knowledge. Being wrong carefully, in public, is one of the most useful things a scientist can do.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 ALH 84001 is genuine Martian rock: 1.93 kg of orthopyroxenite picked up in the Allan Hills of Antarctica on 27 December 1984, crystallised about 4.09 billion years ago, thrown off Mars by an impact around 15 million years ago, and resident in the Antarctic ice for roughly the last 13,000 years. That provenance is not in dispute; it rests on the rock's oxygen-isotope signature and mineralogy, which place it firmly in the Martian meteorite family. What made it famous is a paper. In August 1996 a NASA team led by David McKay published four observations — carbonate globules, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, magnetite and iron-sulfide crystals like those magnetotactic bacteria make, and segmented shapes 20 to 100 nanometres long on fracture surfaces — and argued that together they were best explained by ancient Martian microbes. The paper's own title said "possible". The measurements were real; the interpretation was the claim, and the claim is what got tested. Over the following twenty-five years every strand was given a non-biological route. The tiny shapes are smaller than a free-living cell can be and match the growth steps that pyroxene and carbonate crystals leave on a broken surface. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons form without life and turn up in meteorites generally. Magnetite of exactly that habit was grown in a laboratory by heating iron-rich carbonate. And in 2022 the organic matter itself was traced to water reacting with rock — serpentinisation and carbonation — on early Mars. The scope of all that matters: what failed is the claim about this stone. Whether Mars ever hosted life is untouched by it, and is exactly what today's rovers are on the surface to find out. The episode also paid for itself. Forced to say precisely what would count as evidence of life, the field wrote the modern rules for biosignatures, and NASA founded its Astrobiology Institute in 1998.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 ALH 84001 is a cataclastic cumulate orthopyroxenite (1931 g) recovered from the Far Western Icefield of the Allan Hills, Antarctica, on 27 December 1984 by the ANSMET field party, originally misclassified as a diogenite and reassigned to the Martian meteorite clan on oxygen-isotope evidence (Clayton 1993) together with Fe3+-bearing chromite, pyrite in place of troilite and a non-diogenitic Fe/Mn ratio (Mittlefehldt 1994). Modal mineralogy is ~97 vol% orthopyroxene (Fs27.3Wo3.3En69.4, grains to 6 mm), ~2% chromite and ~1% augite, olivine, pyrite, apatite and shock-transformed plagioclase (maskelynite). Lu-Hf mineral isochron dating gives igneous crystallisation at 4.091 +/- 0.030 Ga (Lapen et al. 2010); secondary carbonate precipitated at ~3.95 Ga; cosmogenic 3He, 21Ne, 38Ar and 81Kr give an ejection (cosmic-ray exposure) age of 15.0 +/- 0.8 Ma (Nyquist et al. 2001) and 14C a terrestrial residence of ~13 ka (Jull et al. 1995). The carbonate occurs as 100-250 um zoned concretions ('rosettes', ~0.5 vol%) in fractures and interstices, zoned optically dark orange to light orange and terminating in a black-white-black rim sequence in which the ~1-5 um Fe- and S-rich zones host single-domain magnetite and sulfide. McKay et al. (1996, Science 273:924) proposed that four co-located features — the carbonate globules, indigenous polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, magnetite and iron-sulfide crystals of putatively biogenic habit, and 20-100 nm segmented and ovoid morphologies on fracture surfaces — were most parsimoniously explained by relic Martian biota. Each has since been given an abiotic account. (i) The sub-100 nm morphologies fall below the minimum volume required to house a genome, ribosomes and the associated proteome: the 1999 NRC workshop placed the lower limit for a free-living DNA-based cell at 200-300 nm, with 250 +/- 50 nm as the panel's formal statement. (ii) Bradley et al. - `SEM` · Public domain (NASA) · NASA / Johnson Space Center (McKay et al. 1996 SEM plate), via Wikimedia Commons — PASS, and it is the honest reference for this subject because the 1996 claim IS this picture. Downloaded and viewed: NASA/JSC frame 96-12609, the 'Mars worm' scanning electron micrograph of a fracture surface in ALH 84001, greyscale, public domain as a work of NASA, via Wikimedia Commons (File:ALH84001_structures.jpg, 1983x1351). APOD credits it to D. McKay (NASA/JSC), K. Thomas-Keprta (Lockheed-Martin), R. Zare (Stanford), NASA. MODALITY: SEM — a scanning electron micrograph of rock, not a photograph of a specimen and not a light micrograph of an organism. WHAT IT SHOWS: a knobbly granular mineral surface with one elongated segmented ridge running diagonally across it and several bright angular mineral fragments. That ridge is the DISPUTED shape — it is not a confirmed fossil, and this atlas does not present it as one. PROVENANCE CAVEAT worth stating plainly: this specific frame was released by NASA on 9 August 1996 and was NOT one of the figures in the 16 August 1996 Science paper. NASA's own caption says so: 'Although this structure is not part of the research published in the Aug. 16 issue of the journal Science, it is located in a similar carbonate glob in the meteorite.' It is the image that carried the story to the public, not a figure from the paper. PROCESSING: deterministic centre crop of the 1351-pixel-tall square region plus LANCZOS resize to 1080 — a DOWNSCALE of 0.80x, no upscaling. edit_image.py was deliberately not run: the source carries no text, no scale bar, no border and no letterbox, so there was nothing to strip and any edit risked re-illustrating a historically important frame. The crop keeps the segmented ridge running through the centre of the square. Other freely-licensed images were found and are recorded in research.json rather than shipped: the PD NASA hand-specimen photographs S85-39565 and File:ALH84001.jpg (both carry baked-in 'ALH84001,0' lettering and a '1cm.' scale cube), a PD USGS close-up of the fracture surface, and two CC BY-SA Smithsonian display photographs. The hand-specimen frames were used as depiction guides for the 3d style.

## 6. Teaching-use decision

| style | verdict | attempts | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3d | teaching-ready | 4 | The anchor image of the set and the one that states the subject: this is a rock. Matched against the real NASA specimen photographs and the ANSMET field description — grey-brown hackly fusion crust over most of the surface, spalled at one end to expose a coarse greenish-grey blocky interior. Labelled, because the crust / spalled break / interior distinction is exactly what a reader needs. |
| textbook | teaching-ready | 4 | The primary labelled figure. Carries the whole argument in one frame: the igneous matrix, the fracture, the zoned carbonate globule with its magnetite-bearing dark rim, and the segmented shape as a texture of the stone. Band order verified by radial pixel scan on the raw PNG. Compromise: the globule stands slightly proud of the fracture face rather than cut flush — accepted as the documented 'pancake attached to a broken surface' habit rather than corrected, after attempt 5 made it worse. |
| watercolor | teaching-ready | 5 | The globule at mineral scale, as a 19th-century mineralogical plate. Chosen over a painted hand specimen so that the four styles form a scale ladder instead of two duplicate rock portraits. Labelled with black ink on a paper halo. The crack passing through the globule is doing real work here: it is what stops a concentric orange oval from reading as a cell. |
| sem | teaching-ready | 3 | The modality of the claim itself, and deliberately the plainest image in the set: nothing but stone. Not labelled — pointing at the ridge with a leader line and a caption is the job of the textbook figure, where the word CLAIMED can sit next to the matrix and the globule that give it context. On its own the SEM is presented as what it is, a rugged mineral surface with one welded ridge on it. |
