## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The keratinocyte is the predominant cell of the epidermis (roughly 90–95% of epidermal cells) and the structural unit of the skin's outer barrier. Keratinocytes are continuously produced by mitotic stem/progenitor cells in the **stratum basale** (basal layer), which sits on the basement membrane and is anchored to it by **hemidesmosomes**. As daughter cells are pushed upward they undergo a well-defined programme of terminal differentiation — **cornification (keratinization)** — passing through the **stratum spinosum** ("prickle cell" layer), the **stratum granulosum**, and (in thick, hairless skin) the **stratum lucidum**, before ending as flattened, anucleate **corneocytes** in the outermost **stratum corneum**.

A living, mid-differentiation keratinocyte (stratum spinosum → granulosum) is the most teachable single-cell snapshot of this cell type: it is a **polyhedral (many-sided) cell**, not round, tightly interlocked with its neighbours via **desmosomes** (macula adherens) — spot-welds that, when tissue shrinks slightly during fixation, pull the cell membrane into short cytoplasmic "spines," giving the layer its classic "prickle cell" look under the light microscope. Bundles of **keratin intermediate filaments (tonofilaments/tonofibrils)**, built mainly from keratin 1/10 in suprabasal cells, sweep through the cytoplasm and insert into the cytoplasmic plaques of the desmosomes, distributing mechanical stress across the whole epithelial sheet. As the cell differentiates further it begins producing two specialised, non-membrane-bound-to-membrane-bound organelle pairs: **keratohyalin granules** (irregular, electron-dense, histidine/profilaggrin-rich granules that clump the keratin filaments into a dense matrix) and **lamellar bodies** ("Odland bodies," ovoid Golgi-derived organelles ~100–500 nm packed with lipid lamellae — ceramides, cholesterol, phospholipids — that are exocytosed at the granulosum/corneum boundary to build the extracellular lipid barrier). The nucleus is large and euchromatic in the lower layers and is progressively degraded and lost by the time the cell reaches the stratum corneum (a corneocyte has no nucleus and almost no organelles — it is a flattened, keratin-and-lipid-filled "shell" wrapped in a cross-linked cornified envelope of involucrin/loricrin just beneath the plasma membrane). Living keratinocytes also carry **melanosomes** — melanin-filled pigment granules that are not made by the keratinocyte itself but are transferred from neighbouring melanocytes and often form a small supra-nuclear "parasol" cap that shields the nucleus from UV damage. Standard organelles — mitochondria, rough endoplasmic reticulum, and a Golgi apparatus (which packages/glycosylates secreted lipids and helps build lamellar bodies) — are present as in any epithelial cell.

Sources: [Ross & Pawlina, Histology: A Text and Atlas — Integument chapter (summarised via Buffalo Histology Notes)](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/histology/integument-histology-notes/), [StatPearls — Anatomy, Skin (Integument), Epidermis (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470464/), [StatPearls — Histology, Keratohyalin Granules (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537049/), [Wikipedia — Keratinocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratinocyte), [Wikipedia — Stratum spinosum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum_spinosum), [OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e — 5.1 Layers of the Skin](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell; the rigidity comes from keratin filaments and desmosomes, not a plant/fungal/bacterial wall.
- **No nucleus on a fully flattened, scale-like corneocyte** — if the illustration is meant to be a mature stratum-corneum cell it must be anucleate and organelle-free; our reference specimen is a living, nucleated mid-layer (spinosum/granulosum) keratinocyte, so it correctly keeps its nucleus — do not add cornified-shell features (like a fully cross-linked envelope with no interior) onto a nucleated cell.
- **No smooth, round, isolated cell without cell-cell junctions** — the single defining textbook feature of a keratinocyte is its polyhedral shape studded with desmosomal "spines" reaching toward where neighbouring cells would be; a perfectly round, smooth-membraned cell (like a lymphocyte or red blood cell) is wrong.
- **No cilia or flagella** — keratinocytes are not motile ciliated cells; they do not swim or beat appendages.
- **No large central vacuole or chloroplasts** — plant-cell features, not present here.
- **Keratohyalin granules must look irregular and electron-dense/clumped**, not like tidy round vesicles or lysosomes.
- **Melanosome pigment should sit as a small supra-nuclear cap**, not fill or tint the whole cytoplasm — and it is understood to originate from a neighbouring melanocyte, not be synthesised by the keratinocyte itself.
- A single specimen, not a dense confluent sheet — individual morphology (shape, spines, granules) must stay clearly readable.

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Say hello to the Keratinocyte — the brick-layer of your skin! Millions and millions of these cells live shoulder to shoulder, holding hands with tiny spot-welds called desmosomes, which make the skin layer look a bit spiky up close, like a tiny wall of LEGO bricks with clips. Every Keratinocyte starts life down in the basement of the skin, then slowly rides up an escalator toward the surface. On the way up it fills itself with a tough protein called keratin — the same stuff your hair and fingernails are made of — and packs little grease-parcels that it squirts out to waterproof the gaps between cells. **Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The keratinocyte is the workhorse cell of the epidermis, making up roughly 90–95% of the cells in your skin's outer layer. Born from stem cells in the basal layer, each keratinocyte migrates outward over two to four weeks, undergoing a remarkable transformation along the way: it locks arms with its neighbours via desmosomes, fills itself with keratin filaments for mechanical toughness, and manufactures lipid-filled granules that it releases to cement the cell layers together and seal the skin against water loss and outside invaders. By the time it reaches the surface it has flattened, lost its nucleus, and become a tough, dead, keratin-packed cell that eventually flakes off — to be replaced from below in a continuous conveyor belt. Along the way it cooperates closely with melanocytes, which hand it pigment granules for UV protection, and Langerhans cells, the skin's resident immune sentinels.  
🇩🇪 Der Keratinozyt ist die Arbeitszelle der Oberhaut und macht etwa 90–95 % der Zellen in der äußersten Hautschicht aus. Er entsteht aus Stammzellen in der Basalschicht und wandert über zwei bis vier Wochen nach außen, wobei er sich unterwegs bemerkenswert verändert: Er verbindet sich über Desmosomen fest mit seinen Nachbarn, füllt sich mit Keratinfilamenten für mechanische Stabilität und stellt fettgefüllte Bläschen her, die er freisetzt, um die Zellschichten zu verkitten und die Haut gegen Wasserverlust und Eindringlinge von außen abzudichten. Wenn er die Oberfläche erreicht, ist er abgeflacht, hat seinen Zellkern verloren und ist zu einer robusten, toten, keratingefüllten Zelle geworden, die irgendwann abschilfert und von unten nachgeliefert wird — ein ständiges Fließband. Dabei arbeitet er eng mit Melanozyten zusammen, die ihm Pigmentkörnchen zum UV-Schutz übergeben, sowie mit Langerhans-Zellen, den ortsansässigen Immunwächtern der Haut.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Keratinocytes are the dominant cell type of stratified squamous epidermis, arising from mitotically active basal keratinocytes attached to the basement membrane via hemidesmosomes and undergoing a tightly regulated programme of terminal differentiation (cornification) as they transit the stratum spinosum, stratum granulosum and (in glabrous skin) stratum lucidum en route to the stratum corneum. Suprabasal keratinocytes are joined by desmosomes into which bundles of keratin 1/10 intermediate filaments (tonofilaments) insert, distributing mechanical load across the epithelial sheet and producing the classic 'prickle cell' morphology of the stratum spinosum. Differentiation is marked by the appearance of keratohyalin granules (profilaggrin-rich aggregates that condense the keratin cytoskeleton) and lamellar bodies (Golgi-derived organelles secreting ceramides, cholesterol and phospholipids that form the intercellular lipid lamellae of the permeability barrier), culminating in nuclear and organelle degradation, assembly of a cross-linked cornified envelope (involucrin, loricrin, small proline-rich proteins) beneath the plasma membrane, and formation of anucleate corneocytes that are eventually desquamated. Keratinocytes additionally act as a paracrine and phagocytic partner to neighbouring cell types: they receive melanosomes transferred from melanocytes via the melanocyte-keratinocyte melanin unit, providing UV-shielding supra-nuclear pigment caps, and they physically house and support Langerhans cells (epidermal dendritic antigen-presenting cells) within the stratum spinosum. Complete epidermal renewal (basal division to desquamation) takes roughly 28–40 days.  
🇩🇪 Keratinozyten sind der dominierende Zelltyp des verhornenden mehrschichtigen Plattenepithels der Epidermis. Sie entstehen aus mitotisch aktiven Basalzellen, die über Hemidesmosomen an der Basalmembran verankert sind, und durchlaufen ein streng reguliertes Programm der terminalen Differenzierung (Kornifikation), während sie das Stratum spinosum, das Stratum granulosum und (in unbehaarter Haut) das Stratum lucidum durchwandern, bevor sie das Stratum corneum erreichen. Suprabasale Keratinozyten sind über Desmosomen verbunden, in die Bündel von Keratin-1/10-Intermediärfilamenten (Tonofilamente) einstrahlen, wodurch mechanische Belastung über das gesamte Epithel verteilt wird und die charakteristische 'Stachelzellen'-Morphologie des Stratum spinosum entsteht. Die Differenzierung zeigt sich am Auftreten von Keratohyalingranula (profilaggrinreiche Aggregate, die das Keratin-Zytoskelett verdichten) und Lamellenkörperchen (vom Golgi-Apparat abstammende Organellen, die Ceramide, Cholesterin und Phospholipide sezernieren und die interzellulären Lipidlamellen der Permeabilitätsbarriere bilden). Am Ende stehen der Abbau von Zellkern und Organellen, der Aufbau einer quervernetzten kornifizierten Hülle (Involucrin, Loricrin, kleine prolinreiche Proteine) unterhalb der Plasmamembran sowie die Bildung kernloser Korneozyten, die schließlich abschilfern (Desquamation). Keratinozyten stehen zudem in parakriner und phagozytärer Wechselwirkung mit Nachbarzellen: Sie nehmen über die melanozytär-keratinozytäre Melanineinheit Melanosomen von Melanozyten auf, die als UV-schützende, kernständige Pigmentkappen dienen, und beherbergen physisch die Langerhans-Zellen (dendritische antigenpräsentierende Zellen der Epidermis) im Stratum spinosum. Die vollständige Erneuerung der Epidermis von der Basalzellteilung bis zur Abschilferung dauert etwa 28 bis 40 Tage.
