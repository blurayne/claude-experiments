## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The podocyte is the **visceral epithelial cell of the renal glomerulus** — the outermost, urinary-side layer of the blood-filtering capillary tuft inside Bowman's capsule. Each of the roughly **one million glomeruli per human kidney** carries a few hundred podocytes (commonly cited ~500–600 per glomerulus), and together with the capillary wall they filter on the order of **180 litres of primary urine per day**. The cell is famously **octopus-shaped**: a plump **cell body** (~15–30 µm across, with a large often-indented nucleus, a well-developed Golgi apparatus, lysosomes and abundant mitochondria) bulges into the **urinary (Bowman's) space**, and from it a small number of thick, tapering **major (primary) processes** — typically two to six, stiffened internally by microtubules and vimentin intermediate filaments — reach out and run **along and around** the outside of the glomerular capillaries.

Each major process splits into **thousands of fine foot processes (pedicels)**, actin-filled fingers on the order of **~200–300 nm wide**, which grip the capillary like interlaced fingers around a branch. Crucially, the pedicels of one podocyte **interdigitate with the pedicels of NEIGHBOURING podocytes** — never with their own — producing the regular alternating comb pattern seen in every SEM. Between adjacent pedicels remain the narrow **filtration slits, ~30–40 nm wide** (sources give 25–60 nm), each bridged by the **slit diaphragm**: a zipper-like modified adherens junction built from **nephrin** (whose strands from the two sides interlock, leaving pores smaller than albumin), NEPH1, **podocin**, CD2AP, P-cadherin and FAT1. The pedicels stand on the **glomerular basement membrane (GBM)** — a ~300–350 nm thick composite of collagen IV (α3α4α5), laminin-521, agrin and nidogen shared with the endothelium — anchored by α3β1-integrin and dystroglycan complexes.

The full **filtration barrier is three-layered**: (1) the capillary's **fenestrated endothelium** (open pores ~70–100 nm, no diaphragms), (2) the **GBM**, (3) the podocyte **slit diaphragms**. Water and small solutes pass; cells and essentially all albumin stay in the blood — size selectivity is distributed across all three layers, helped by the thick negatively charged **glycocalyx** (podocalyxin) that coats the podocyte and keeps neighbouring pedicels electrostatically apart. The foot processes are contractile and consume conspicuous amounts of ATP counteracting the capillary's outward filtration pressure. Mature podocytes are **terminally differentiated and post-mitotic**: what is lost to high glomerular pressure, diabetes or immune injury is not replaced. Podocyte stress shows first as **foot-process effacement** (the fingers retract and flatten into a smeared sheet — minimal change disease is the classic example), then as **proteinuria**, and cumulative podocyte loss drives focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) and the slow, silent creep of chronic kidney damage; mutations in nephrin (NPHS1) or podocin (NPHS2) cause congenital nephrotic syndromes.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **The starburst.** Nothing may radiate symmetrically outward from the cell body into empty space. Every process follows the **surface of the capillary** — the cell reads as a climber gripping a branch, arms trailing along and around the vessel. (This is also the unrecoverable image-model failure mode; compose the arms as trailing ribbons hugging a diagonal vessel.)
- **Not a neuron.** The arms hug a vessel surface; there are no free-floating dendrites, no axon, no synapses, no cobweb of thin threads in open space.
- **Foot processes are not a fringe of the cell body.** They arise from the **major processes**, as rows of short parallel fingers, and they interdigitate **with the neighbouring podocyte's fingers** (alternating combs) — never a solid continuous sheet, never fingers fusing back into their own arm.
- **No cilia, no brush border, no microvillus carpet** — that is the proximal tubule next door. (Fine bristly microprojections on the cell body, as in the real SEM, are acceptable; a dense uniform lawn is not.)
- **No bacterial features** — no cell wall, capsule, nucleoid, plasmids, flagella. Eukaryotic human cell.
- **The pedicels touch down on the basement membrane**, not on bare endothelium; in cutaway the layer order from blood outward is endothelium → GBM → pedicels. Never draw pedicels inside the vessel lumen.
- **The capillary is a closed tube carrying blood**, not a gutter or open channel; erythrocytes belong inside it only where a cutaway opens it.
- **SEM shows surfaces only** — no interior organelles, no cutaway, no translucency in the sem style.
- **No detached, free-swimming octopus** — the cell is fixed in place, wrapped onto its vessel, inside Bowman's capsule; the picture is a close-up of cell-on-vessel, without cartoon water or sea floor.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The podocyte is the reason your urine contains no protein — and the reason kidney damage, once done, is done. Sitting on the outside of the glomerular capillaries in each of the kidney's roughly one million filter units, this octopus-shaped epithelial cell wraps a few thick arms around the vessel, and each arm splits into thousands of foot processes barely 250 nanometres wide. Those fingers interlock precisely with the fingers of neighbouring podocytes, leaving filtration slits of about 30 to 40 nanometres, each bridged by a molecular zipper called the slit diaphragm, built largely from the protein nephrin. Together with the capillary's perforated lining and the basement membrane between them, this makes a three-layer filter that processes around 180 litres of blood plasma a day — water and small wastes pass through towards the urine, while cells and virtually all albumin stay behind. It is a life under constant load: the fingers are packed with contractile actin and burn conspicuous amounts of energy just holding their grip against the filtration pressure. And podocytes have given up dividing. When high blood pressure or diabetes batters the filter, stressed podocytes first flatten their fingers into a smeared sheet — protein starts leaking into the urine, often the earliest measurable sign — and cells that die simply leave gaps that scar over. That is why kidney disease creeps in so quietly, and why the boring advice genuinely matters here: controlling blood pressure and blood sugar is, quite literally, podocyte protection.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The podocyte is the terminally differentiated visceral epithelial cell of the glomerulus, forming the outermost layer of the filtration barrier on the urinary side of the glomerular basement membrane (GBM). Its cell body, containing a large often-indented nucleus, prominent Golgi and abundant mitochondria, floats in Bowman's space and extends two to six microtubule- and vimentin-supported major processes that adhere to and follow the capillary surface. These arborize into thousands of actin-based foot processes (~200–300 nm wide) anchored to the GBM via α3β1-integrin and dystroglycan complexes. Foot processes of adjacent podocytes interdigitate — a given cell's pedicels never interlock with their own — leaving 30–40 nm filtration slits spanned by the slit diaphragm, a modified adherens junction of nephrin, NEPH1, podocin, CD2AP, P-cadherin and FAT1 whose interlocking nephrin ectodomains define pores below the ~3.6 nm effective radius of albumin. Filtration selectivity is a property of the composite barrier — fenestrated endothelium with its glycocalyx (fenestrae 70–100 nm), the ~300–350 nm GBM (collagen IV α3α4α5, laminin-521, agrin) and the slit diaphragm acting in series — with the relative contributions of size and charge selectivity still debated; the podocyte additionally maintains capillary architecture mechanically and sustains the endothelium via VEGF-A. Being post-mitotic, podocyte number can only fall. Injury manifests first as foot-process effacement with slit-diaphragm loss (classically in minimal change disease) and proteinuria; detachment beyond roughly 20–30% podocyte loss drives irreversible focal segmental glomerulosclerosis. Nephrin (NPHS1) and podocin (NPHS2) mutations cause congenital nephrotic syndromes; podocyte depletion is central to diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy. Caveat on the simplification: the 'finest sieve' shorthand assigns filtration to the slit alone, whereas measured permselectivity requires all three layers, and the popular octopus image hides the defining feature — that the interdigitation is strictly between neighbouring cells.  
🇩🇪 Der Podozyt ist die terminal differenzierte viszerale Epithelzelle des Glomerulus und bildet die äußerste Schicht der Filtrationsbarriere auf der Harnseite der glomerulären Basalmembran (GBM). Diese verzweigen sich in Tausende aktinbasierte Fußfortsätze (~200–300 nm breit), die über α3β1-Integrin- und Dystroglykan-Komplexe an der GBM verankert sind. Die Fußfortsätze benachbarter Podozyten interdigitieren — die Pedizellen einer Zelle verzahnen sich nie mit den eigenen — und lassen 30–40 nm weite Filtrationsschlitze frei, überspannt von der Schlitzmembran, einer modifizierten Adhärenzjunktion aus Nephrin, NEPH1, Podocin, CD2AP, P-Cadherin und FAT1, deren ineinandergreifende Nephrin-Ektodomänen Poren unterhalb des effektiven Albuminradius von ~3,6 nm definieren.
