## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The urothelium (transitional epithelium) lines the urinary tract from the renal minor calyces through the ureters and bladder to the proximal urethra, and is a stratified epithelium of 3–6 cell layers built to withstand large changes in volume and the chemically hostile contents of urine. Its base is a layer of small, cuboidal **basal cells** resting on a basement membrane; these are the mitotically active stem/progenitor population that continuously (if slowly — urothelium normally turns over far more slowly than skin or gut) replenishes the tissue. Above them sit one or more layers of polygonal **intermediate cells**. The luminal surface is covered by large, terminally differentiated **umbrella cells** (also called facet cells or superficial cells) — the subject of this poster — each of which is broad enough to cover and seal several underlying intermediate cells, hence the name. Umbrella cells are frequently **binucleate or polyploid** (a result of endoreplication) and their shape changes with bladder fill: dome-shaped and cuboidal with a thick, deeply folded apical membrane when the bladder is empty, flattening toward squamous as the bladder distends. The single most defining feature of the umbrella cell is its **apical membrane armor**: roughly 90% of the luminal surface is covered by rigid, crystalline **uroplakin plaques** (the "asymmetric unit membrane", AUM) — hexagonal 16 nm arrays of the four major uroplakin proteins (UPIa, UPIb, UPII, UPIIIa) — separated by narrow, more flexible **hinge regions** of ordinary membrane. The plaques themselves stay rigid while the hinges let the whole mosaic fold and unfold, giving the apical surface its characteristic **scalloped, cobblestone-like contour**. Beneath this membrane sits the cell's most striking cytoplasmic feature: a large reserve pool of flattened, disc-shaped **fusiform (discoidal) vesicles**, each carrying two more opposing uroplakin plaques on its membrane. As the bladder fills and the apical surface must expand, fusiform vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane by exocytosis, rapidly adding pre-made, plaque-covered membrane; as the bladder empties again, that extra membrane is retrieved by endocytosis and stored back as fusiform vesicles — the literal cell-biological basis of "reshaping its surface as the bladder fills." A dense **cytokeratin filament** cytoskeleton (cytokeratin 20/CK20 is a diagnostic marker of mature umbrella cells) plus a cortical actin meshwork anchors the plaques and vesicle traffic. Neighbouring umbrella cells are sealed to each other by an unusually extensive **tight-junction (zonula occludens) belt** — among the tightest, highest-resistance junctions in the body — and the exposed apical surface is coated by a thin, protective **glycosaminoglycan (GAG)/mucin layer**. Together, the uroplakin plaques, the tight junctions and the GAG coat constitute the "blood–urine barrier" (urothelial permeability barrier) that keeps concentrated, urea-rich, often acidic urine from diffusing into the underlying tissue and blood, and keeps solutes and toxins in urine away from the body — the basis of "sealing against acidic urine." Standard organelles support this heavy membrane traffic: a large, often lobulated **nucleus**; numerous **mitochondria** supplying ATP for vesicle trafficking; and well-developed **rough endoplasmic reticulum** and **Golgi apparatus** synthesising and glycosylating the uroplakins and the GAG coat.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall, nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — this is a normal human (eukaryotic, animal) cell, not a prokaryote.
- **No dense fringe of tall, finger-like microvilli** — do not give this cell an enterocyte-style "brush border." The apical surface is instead covered by rigid, plate-like uroplakin plaques separated by hinge lines, giving a scalloped/cobblestone/crazy-paving texture, not a villous fringe.
- **Not tall and narrow/columnar** — the umbrella cell is large, broad and dome-shaped-to-flattened (cuboidal when the bladder is empty, thinner and more squamous when it is full), never a slender column like an enterocyte.
- **No motile cilia** — the urothelium is not a ciliated epithelium; do not draw a beating cilium, axoneme or basal body.
- **No large pale mucous droplet/vacuole** — that belongs to a goblet cell, a different cell type; do not draw a big bubble-like secretory vacuole.
- **No more than one or two nuclei** — binucleation is a real, notable feature of umbrella cells but should be drawn as at most a pair of nuclei, never a multinucleated syncytium.
- **If basal/intermediate cells are shown for context**, keep them visibly smaller, more cuboidal/polygonal, and WITHOUT the plaque-armored apical surface — only the true umbrella cell facing the lumen carries the uroplakin plaques and fusiform vesicles.
- A single specimen (the umbrella cell) as the clear subject, not a dense uniform confluent sheet with no individual detail readable.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The urothelial (umbrella/facet/superficial) cell is the terminally differentiated, often binucleate or polyploid cell type capping the luminal surface of the stratified transitional epithelium (urothelium) lining the renal pelvis, ureters, bladder and proximal urethra. Roughly 90% of its apical plasma membrane is occupied by the asymmetric unit membrane (AUM): rigid, hexagonally packed 16 nm plaques composed of the four major uroplakins (UPIa, UPIb, UPII, UPIIIa), separated by narrow, flexible hinge regions that permit the plaque mosaic to fold and unfold. A large cytoplasmic reserve of flattened, discoidal fusiform vesicles — each bearing paired plaque-covered membrane leaflets — undergoes regulated exocytic insertion into the apical membrane as the bladder distends and compensatory endocytic retrieval as it empties, providing the membrane-area plasticity underlying the cell's shape change from cuboidal/domed to squamous. A dense cytokeratin (notably CK20) intermediate-filament network and cortical actin cytoskeleton anchor the plaques and support vesicle trafficking, while an extensive apicolateral zonula occludens (tight-junction) belt confers exceptionally high transepithelial electrical resistance, and a glycosaminoglycan/mucin surface coat adds a further diffusion barrier. Together the uroplakin plaques, tight junctions and GAG layer constitute the blood–urine (permeability) barrier that maintains the steep osmotic and pH gradient between hypertonic, acidic urine and the underlying interstitium/blood. The urothelium is renewed from a basal, mitotically active progenitor/stem-cell layer at a markedly slower turnover rate than most other epithelia, with umbrella cells representing the final differentiated product of this lineage.  
🇩🇪 Die Urothelzelle (Deckzelle, Schirmzelle, Superfizialzelle) ist der terminal differenzierte, häufig binukleäre oder polyploide Zelltyp, der die dem Lumen zugewandte Oberfläche des mehrschichtigen Übergangsepithels (Urothel) bildet, welches Nierenbecken, Harnleiter, Harnblase und proximale Harnröhre auskleidet. Etwa 90 % ihrer apikalen Plasmamembran werden von der asymmetrischen Einheitsmembran (AUM) eingenommen: starren, hexagonal gepackten 16-nm-Plaques aus den vier Haupturoplakinen (UPIa, UPIb, UPII, UPIIIa), getrennt durch schmale, flexible Scharnierregionen, die dem Plaque-Mosaik Faltung und Entfaltung erlauben. Ein großer zytoplasmatischer Vorrat abgeflachter, diskoidaler fusiformer Vesikel — jedes mit gepaarten plaquebedeckten Membranflügeln — wird bei Blasenfüllung reguliert exozytotisch in die Apikalmembran eingebaut und bei Entleerung kompensatorisch endozytotisch zurückgeholt, was der Zelle die Membranflächen-Plastizität für den Formwechsel von kuboidal/kuppelförmig zu squamös verleiht. Ein dichtes Zytokeratin-Intermediärfilamentnetz (insbesondere CK20) und ein kortikales Aktin-Zytoskelett verankern die Plaques und unterstützen den Vesikeltransport, während ein ausgedehnter apikolateraler Zonula-occludens-Gürtel (Tight Junctions) einen außergewöhnlich hohen transepithelialen elektrischen Widerstand vermittelt und eine Glykosaminoglykan-/Muzin-Oberflächenschicht eine zusätzliche Diffusionsbarriere bildet. Uroplakin-Plaques, Tight Junctions und GAG-Schicht bilden zusammen die Blut-Urin-Barriere (Permeabilitätsbarriere), die den steilen osmotischen und pH-Gradienten zwischen hypertonem, saurem Urin und dem darunterliegenden Interstitium/Blut aufrechterhält. Erneuert wird das Urothel aus einer basalen, mitotisch aktiven Progenitor-/Stammzellschicht mit einer im Vergleich zu den meisten anderen Epithelien deutlich langsameren Umsatzrate, wobei die Urothelzelle das terminal differenzierte Endprodukt dieser Zelllinie darstellt.
