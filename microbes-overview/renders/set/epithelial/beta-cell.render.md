## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The beta cell (German *Betazelle*) is the insulin-secreting endocrine cell of the pancreatic islets of Langerhans — the small, richly vascularised cell clusters scattered through the exocrine pancreas. Beta cells are the islet majority (roughly 50–70 % of islet cells in humans) and are unremarkable in outline: rounded-polygonal, about 10 µm across, with a central round nucleus, prominent rough ER and Golgi apparatus, and scattered mitochondria. What makes the cell instantly recognisable under the electron microscope is its cargo: on the order of **10,000 insulin secretory granules** filling the cytoplasm. Each mature granule is a membrane-bounded vesicle ~200–400 nm across containing a **dense core** — insulin condensed with zinc into crystals, which often gives the core an **angular, polygonal profile** in section — separated from the limiting membrane by a **clear pale halo**. (The wide halo is largely an artifact of chemical fixation — high-pressure-frozen granules show almost none — but it is *the* textbook TEM signature and the honest convention for teaching images.) The production line runs through the classic secretory pathway: the rough ER synthesises preproinsulin and folds proinsulin; the Golgi packages it into immature granules, where prohormone convertases cleave proinsulin into insulin plus **C-peptide** and the insulin crystallises with zinc as the granule matures. The cell is its own glucose meter: glucose enters through GLUT transporters (GLUT2 in rodents; mainly GLUT1/GLUT3 in humans) and is phosphorylated by glucokinase, so the cell's ATP level tracks blood sugar; rising ATP closes ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels, the membrane depolarises, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open, and calcium triggers granules docked at the plasma membrane to fuse and release insulin by **exocytosis** — in a fast first phase from the docked pool, then a sustained second phase, and in fine pulses rather than a steady drip. Release is aimed at the islet's **fenestrated capillaries**: thin-walled vessels whose pores let insulin pass straight into the blood, which carries it to muscle, fat and liver with the order "take up and store sugar". In type 1 diabetes autoimmune T cells destroy the beta cells; in type 2 the target tissues grow resistant and the overworked beta cells eventually fail. Sulfonylurea drugs close the same K⁺ channel to force secretion; the co-secreted amylin (IAPP) can aggregate as islet amyloid in type 2 diabetes.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Beta cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_cell), [Rorsman & Ashcroft 2018, "Pancreatic β-cell electrical activity and insulin secretion: of mice and men", *Physiol Rev* 98(1):117–214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29212789/), [Suckale & Solimena 2010, "The insulin secretory granule as a signaling hub", *Trends Endocrinol Metab* 21(10):599–609](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20609596/), [Fava et al. 2012, "Novel standards in the measurement of rat insulin granules", *Diabetologia* 55(4):1013–23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22252472/), [In 't Veld & Marichal 2010, "Microscopic anatomy of the human islet of Langerhans", *Adv Exp Med Biol* 654:1–19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20217491/), Junqueira's Basic Histology (16th ed.), ch. 20.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **This is an animal endocrine cell** — plasma membrane only, real organelles; no bacterial wall/nucleoid/flagella, no plant chloroplasts, no brush border of microvilli (that would be an enterocyte) and no cilia.
- **The granules are the crowd, not a garnish** — a beta cell with four or five vesicles misstates its most countable feature; granules must visibly fill the cytoplasm (dozens in view), each with the dense core + pale halo structure, cores often angular.
- **Granules stay inside the cell** — none floating loose in the surroundings; the only granule allowed at the boundary is the one caught mid-exocytosis at the membrane facing the capillary.
- **The dense core sits inside its own vesicle, not naked in the cytoplasm** — core, halo and membrane belong together.
- **No large apical mucus cup** (goblet cell), **no deep branching intracellular canals** (parietal cell), **no zymogen granule cluster draining into a duct** — the beta cell secretes into the *blood*, not into a duct or gut lumen; the neighbouring vessel is a capillary, not a duct.
- **One cell in context, not a whole islet map** — neighbouring islet cells at most faintly implied; the subject fills the frame.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Beta cells are the insulin-secreting endocrine cells of the pancreatic islets of Langerhans (~50–70 % of human islet cells, ~10 µm, rounded-polygonal). Their diagnostic ultrastructure is the insulin secretory granule: a membrane-bounded vesicle of ~200–400 nm with an electron-dense core — insulin condensed as Zn²⁺-stabilised hexamer crystals, hence the often angular core profile — separated from the limiting membrane by an electron-lucent halo; a cell holds on the order of 10⁴ granules. Biosynthesis follows the regulated secretory pathway: preproinsulin translation at the rough ER, proinsulin folding, Golgi packaging into immature granules, cleavage by prohormone convertases PC1/3 and PC2 plus carboxypeptidase E into insulin and C-peptide (co-released 1:1, clinically the measure of endogenous secretion). Stimulus-secretion coupling is metabolic, not receptor-based: glucose uptake (GLUT2 in rodents, predominantly GLUT1/3 in human beta cells) and glucokinase — the flux-determining glucose sensor — raise ATP/ADP, closing K_ATP channels (Kir6.2/SUR1, the sulfonylurea target); depolarisation opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels and Ca²⁺ triggers SNARE-mediated exocytosis of docked granules toward the fenestrated islet capillaries, biphasically (a rapid first phase from the readily releasable pool, a sustained second phase from granule recruitment) and in ~5-min pulses. Caveats where the textbook picture breaks: the wide halo is largely a chemical-fixation artifact — high-pressure-frozen granules show almost none — so the classic TEM look is partly preparation, not native structure; and the 'blood-sugar sensor' is nothing like a taste receptor — the cell infers glucose from its own metabolic rate, which is why metabolic poisons or K_ATP mutations (congenital hyperinsulinism, neonatal diabetes) derange secretion without any change in glucose. Beta cells co-secrete amylin (IAPP), whose aggregation forms islet amyloid in type 2 diabetes; selective autoimmune destruction (GAD65, IA-2, insulin autoantigens) causes type 1.  
🇩🇪 Betazellen sind die insulinsezernierenden endokrinen Zellen der Langerhans-Inseln (~50–70 % der humanen Inselzellen, ~10 µm, rundlich-polygonal). Ihre diagnostische Ultrastruktur ist das Insulingranulum: ein membranumschlossenes Vesikel von ~200–400 nm mit elektronendichtem Kern — Insulin, kondensiert als Zn²⁺-stabilisierte Hexamerkristalle, daher das oft kantige Kernprofil —, vom umhüllenden Membransaum durch einen elektronenhellen Hof getrennt; eine Zelle enthält größenordnungsmäßig 10⁴ Granula. Die Biosynthese folgt dem regulierten Sekretionsweg: Präproinsulin-Translation am rauen ER, Proinsulinfaltung, Golgi-Verpackung in unreife Granula, Spaltung durch die Prohormonkonvertasen PC1/3 und PC2 sowie Carboxypeptidase E zu Insulin und C-Peptid (1:1 kosezerniert, klinisch das Maß der Eigensekretion). Die Reiz-Sekretions-Kopplung ist metabolisch, nicht rezeptorvermittelt: Glukoseaufnahme (GLUT2 bei Nagern, beim Menschen überwiegend GLUT1/3) und Glukokinase — der flussbestimmende Glukosesensor — erhöhen ATP/ADP, K_ATP-Kanäle (Kir6.2/SUR1, Angriffspunkt der Sulfonylharnstoffe) schließen; die Depolarisation öffnet spannungsgesteuerte Ca²⁺-Kanäle, und Ca²⁺ löst die SNARE-vermittelte Exozytose angedockter Granula in Richtung der fenestrierten Inselkapillaren aus — biphasisch (schnelle erste Phase aus dem unmittelbar freisetzbaren Pool, anhaltende zweite Phase durch Granulanachschub) und in ~5-Minuten-Pulsen. Wo das Lehrbuchbild bricht: Der breite Hof ist großteils ein Artefakt der chemischen Fixierung — hochdruckgefrorene Granula zeigen kaum einen —, das klassische TEM-Bild ist also teils Präparation, nicht native Struktur; und der „Blutzuckersensor“ ist kein Geschmacksrezeptor — die Zelle erschließt die Glukose aus ihrer eigenen Stoffwechselrate, weshalb Stoffwechselgifte oder K_ATP-Mutationen (kongenitaler Hyperinsulinismus, neonataler Diabetes) die Sekretion entgleisen lassen, ohne dass sich die Glukose ändert.
