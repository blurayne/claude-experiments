## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The parietal cell (oxyntic cell; German *Belegzelle*) is the acid-secreting cell of the stomach's oxyntic (fundic) glands, concentrated in the isthmus and neck of the glands of the gastric body and fundus. It is one of the most morphologically distinctive epithelial cells in the body: large (~20–25 µm), plump and pyramidal-to-rounded, bulging outward from the gland wall so that in section it looks wedged between its slimmer neighbours (hence *Beleg-* "attached/appended" cell). On H&E stain it is strongly eosinophilic — bright pink with a central round nucleus, the classic "fried egg" look — because its cytoplasm is packed with an extraordinary density of mitochondria: roughly 30–40 % of the cell volume, among the highest of any human cell, supplying the ATP for acid pumping. The second defining feature is the **intracellular secretory canaliculus**: the apical membrane plunges deep into the cell as a branching system of canals that ramify around the nucleus and open through a narrow apex into the gland lumen. The canalicular membrane is lined with long microvilli, multiplying the secretory surface enormously. In the resting cell most of the H+/K+-ATPase ("the proton pump") is held in a third hallmark compartment, the **tubulovesicles** — a cytoplasmic reservoir of flattened membrane tubules and vesicles clustered near the canaliculi. On stimulation (gastrin via CCK2 receptors, histamine from neighbouring ECL cells via H2 receptors, acetylcholine via M3 receptors) the tubulovesicles fuse with the canalicular membrane, the microvilli lengthen, and the pumps begin exchanging cytoplasmic H+ for luminal K+ — concentrating protons about three-million-fold, from pH ~7.2 inside the cell to pH ~1 in the gland lumen, the steepest ion gradient formed by any mammalian cell. Chloride follows through channels to make hydrochloric acid; carbonic anhydrase supplies the protons and exports bicarbonate basolaterally (the "alkaline tide"). The same cell secretes **intrinsic factor**, the glycoprotein without which vitamin B12 cannot be absorbed in the ileum — which is why autoimmune destruction of parietal cells (whose main autoantigen is the H+/K+-ATPase itself) causes pernicious anaemia, and why proton-pump inhibitors such as omeprazole, which covalently block the pump, are among the most-taken medicines in the world. The cell rests on a thin basal lamina; its basolateral membrane carries the stimulus receptors.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Parietal cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parietal_cell), [Engevik, Kaji & Goldenring 2020, "The Physiology of the Gastric Parietal Cell", *Physiol Rev* 100(2):573–602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31670611/), [Yao & Forte 2003, "Cell biology of acid secretion by the parietal cell", *Annu Rev Physiol* 65:103–31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12500969/), [Forte & Zhu 2010, "Apical recycling of the gastric parietal cell H,K-ATPase", *Annu Rev Physiol* 72:273–96](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20148676/), Junqueira's Basic Histology (16th ed.), ch. 15.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **This is an animal epithelial cell** — it has a plasma membrane only, real organelles, no bacterial envelope structures and no plant organelles.
- **The canaliculus is an internal branching canal system, not a surface feature** — the deep microvilli-lined channels ramify *inside* the cytoplasm and drain through one narrow apical opening; a cell with only an ordinary flat brush border on top would be an enterocyte, not a parietal cell.
- **The nucleus stays central and round** — a basal flattened nucleus with an apical mucus cup would be a goblet/mucous neck cell.
- **Mitochondria are the crowd, not a garnish** — a rendering with three or four scattered mitochondria misstates the cell's most quantifiable feature (~a third of the volume); they must visibly pack the cytoplasm between the canaliculi.
- **No large secretory granule cluster** — dense apical zymogen granules would make it a chief cell (pepsinogen) or Paneth cell; the parietal cell's "product" is an ion flux, stored as membrane (tubulovesicles), not as granules.
- **One cell in context, not a whole gland cross-section** — neighbouring gland cells only faintly implied; the subject fills the frame.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Parietal (oxyntic) cells are large (~20–25 µm) acid-secreting epithelial cells of the oxyntic glands, concentrated in the isthmus and neck of the gastric body and fundus. Their ultrastructure is diagnostic: a deeply invaginated intracellular secretory canaliculus — a branching, microvilli-lined extension of the apical membrane opening into the gland lumen — a central round nucleus, an exceptional mitochondrial density (~30–40 % of cell volume by stereology of stimulated cells), and a cytoplasmic reservoir of H+/K+-ATPase-bearing tubulovesicles. Secretagogues (gastrin via CCK2R, histamine from ECL cells via H2R, acetylcholine via M3R; potentiating through Ca2+- and cAMP-dependent pathways) trigger tubulovesicle fusion with the canalicular membrane and actin-dependent microvillar elongation; the recruited H+/K+-ATPase then exchanges cytoplasmic H+ for luminal K+ electroneutrally, with apical KCNQ1/Kir K+ recycling and Cl− efflux (CFTR-independent, via ClC-2/SLC26A9 candidates) completing net HCl secretion at up to ~160 mM H+ — a ~3-million-fold concentration against the cytosol, the steepest ion gradient in mammalian physiology. Cytosolic pH is defended by carbonic anhydrase II with basolateral Cl−/HCO3− exchange (AE2, the post-prandial "alkaline tide"). Note where the textbook simplification breaks: the cell never contains pH 1 acid — H+ and Cl− cross the canalicular membrane by separate transporters and only the canalicular/gland lumen is acidic, while the cytosol stays near pH 7.2; and "pH 1" describes luminal fluid, not a property of the cell. Human parietal cells additionally secrete intrinsic factor (in contrast to rodents, where chief cells contribute); the H+/K+-ATPase is the principal autoantigen of autoimmune gastritis (→ pernicious anaemia), and proton-pump inhibitors inhibit it covalently after acid-activation in the canaliculus.  
🇩🇪 Beleg- oder Parietalzellen sind große (~20–25 µm) säuresezernierende Epithelzellen der Korpus- und Fundusdrüsen, konzentriert in Isthmus und Hals der Drüsen. Ihre Ultrastruktur ist diagnostisch: ein tief eingestülptes intrazelluläres Sekretkanälchen — eine verzweigte, mit Mikrovilli ausgekleidete Fortsetzung der apikalen Membran mit Öffnung ins Drüsenlumen —, ein zentraler runder Zellkern, eine außergewöhnliche Mitochondriendichte (stereologisch ~30–40 % des Zellvolumens in stimulierten Zellen) sowie ein zytoplasmatisches Reservoir H+/K+-ATPase-tragender Tubulovesikel. Sekretagoga (Gastrin über CCK2R, Histamin aus ECL-Zellen über H2R, Acetylcholin über M3R; potenzierend über Ca2+- und cAMP-abhängige Wege) lösen die Fusion der Tubulovesikel mit der Kanälchenmembran und eine aktinabhängige Verlängerung der Mikrovilli aus; die rekrutierte H+/K+-ATPase tauscht dann elektroneutral zytoplasmatisches H+ gegen luminales K+, ergänzt durch apikales K+-Recycling (KCNQ1/Kir) und Cl−-Ausstrom (Kandidaten ClC-2/SLC26A9), sodass netto HCl mit bis zu ~160 mM H+ sezerniert wird — eine ~3-millionenfache Konzentrierung gegenüber dem Zytosol, der steilste Ionengradient der Säugetierphysiologie. Der zytosolische pH wird durch Carboanhydrase II und basolateralen Cl−/HCO3−-Austausch (AE2, die postprandiale „Alkali-Flut“) stabil gehalten. Drüsenlumen, während das Zytosol bei etwa pH 7,2 bleibt; „pH 1“ beschreibt die luminale Flüssigkeit, nicht eine Eigenschaft der Zelle. Menschliche Belegzellen sezernieren zusätzlich den Intrinsic Factor (anders als bei Nagern, wo Hauptzellen beitragen); die H+/K+-ATPase ist das Hauptautoantigen der Autoimmungastritis (→ perniziöse Anämie), und Protonenpumpenhemmer hemmen sie kovalent nach Säureaktivierung im Kanälchen.
