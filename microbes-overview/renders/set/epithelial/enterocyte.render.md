## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The enterocyte (intestinal absorptive cell) is the dominant cell type of the simple columnar epithelium lining the villi of the small intestine (and, as the related colonocyte, the colon). It is a tall, narrow, column-shaped cell — markedly taller than it is wide — with a clear apical/basal polarity: an absorptive apical surface facing the gut lumen and a secretory/exporting basal surface facing the lamina propria and blood/lymph supply. The single most defining feature is the **brush border**: roughly 1,000–3,000 densely packed, uniform **microvilli** (~1 µm long, ~0.1 µm diameter) covering the apical membrane, each built around a core bundle of actin microfilaments (crosslinked by villin, fimbrin/plastin and espin) whose rootlets insert into a dense actin-spectrin-myosin meshwork just beneath the membrane, the **terminal web**. The terminal web anchors the microvilli and links them mechanically to the **junctional complex** — tight junctions (zonula occludens), adherens junctions (zonula adherens) and desmosomes — that seals the apicolateral space between neighbouring enterocytes, sealing the paracellular route and giving the epithelium its barrier function. The brush border membrane itself carries the terminal digestive enzymes (disaccharidases such as lactase and sucrase-isomaltase, peptidases) and nutrient transporters (SGLT1 for glucose/galactose, GLUT5 for fructose, PepT1 for di/tripeptides), so digestion is completed at this membrane immediately before absorption. A thin fuzzy **glycocalyx** coats the microvillar tips. The oval **nucleus** sits basally, leaving the apical cytoplasm free for the absorptive machinery. Enterocytes are extremely metabolically active: **mitochondria** are numerous and concentrated in the apical/supranuclear cytoplasm to power the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-driven, secondary-active nutrient transport. A well-developed **rough endoplasmic reticulum** and **Golgi apparatus** occupy the supranuclear/basal cytoplasm, synthesising and glycosylating membrane and transport proteins; a comparatively prominent **smooth endoplasmic reticulum** is also present and, uniquely for this absorptive lineage, is where absorbed monoglycerides and free fatty acids are re-esterified into triglycerides for packaging into chylomicrons, which are exported by exocytosis across the basolateral membrane into the lacteals of the lamina propria (the enterocyte is the only epithelial cell type in the body that assembles chylomicrons). Enterocytes differentiate from multipotent intestinal stem cells at the base of the crypts of Lieberkühn, migrate up the crypt-villus axis while progressively building a longer, denser brush border, and are shed from the villus tip after a lifespan of only about 3–5 days — one of the fastest-renewing cell populations in the body.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Enterocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterocyte), [Wikipedia — Microvillus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microvillus), [Wikipedia — Brush border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brush_border), [Crawley, Mooseker & Tyska 2014, Shaping the intestinal brush border (J Cell Biol, via PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4210444/), [Meyers 2023, Physiology, Small Bowel — StatPearls/NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532263/), [Azzouz & Sharma 2023, Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis, Small Intestine — StatPearls/NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459366/).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts or large central vacuole** — not a plant cell.
- **Microvilli are NOT motile cilia** — they are short, stiff, actin-filled projections with no 9+2 axoneme and no beating motion; do not draw them long, wavy, or sparse like cilia, and do not add a basal body/axoneme.
- **Not round or cuboidal** — the defining shape is a TALL, NARROW column, clearly taller than wide, with the nucleus basal (not central or apical) and the brush border strictly apical.
- **No large mucous droplet/vacuole** — that is the neighbouring goblet cell, a different cell type; the enterocyte's apical cytoplasm is packed with mitochondria and fine terminal web, not a big pale mucin bubble.
- **No motile flagellum** — enterocytes do not swim or have a tail.
- A single specimen, not a dense confluent monolayer — individual morphology (apical brush border vs. basal nucleus) must stay clearly readable.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The enterocyte is the primary workhorse of nutrient absorption in the small intestine, a tall, column-shaped epithelial cell whose apical surface is covered by a dense brush border of microvilli — a specialisation that multiplies the intestine's effective absorptive surface area many times over. Digestive enzymes anchored right on the microvillar membrane finish breaking down carbohydrates and proteins at the last possible moment, immediately next to the transporters that shuttle the resulting sugars, amino acids and fats into the cell and onward into the bloodstream and lymphatics. Enterocytes are also unique among the body's epithelial cells in reassembling absorbed dietary fat into chylomicrons for export via the lymphatic lacteals. They live fast: continuously produced by intestinal stem cells in the crypts, they migrate up the villus over a few days, do their absorptive work at the tip, and are then shed — one of the quickest cell-turnover cycles in the human body. Packed tightly together and sealed by tight junctions, the enterocyte layer also forms a selective barrier that keeps the gut's resident microbial community safely separated from the rest of the body while still allowing nutrients through.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Enterocytes are the predominant simple columnar epithelial cell of the small-intestinal villus, exhibiting pronounced apicobasal polarity with an apical brush border of ~1,000–3,000 actin-cored microvilli anchored via a terminal web of spectrin, myosin and villin/fimbrin-crosslinked actin filaments beneath the plasma membrane. The brush border membrane hosts terminal hydrolases (lactase, sucrase-isomaltase, aminopeptidases) and secondary-active nutrient transporters (SGLT1, GLUT5, PepT1) that complete luminal digestion and mediate the final absorptive step, while apicolateral tight and adherens junctions of the junctional complex restrict paracellular permeability and maintain epithelial barrier integrity. Enterocytes are metabolically specialised for triglyceride re-esterification and chylomicron assembly: absorbed 2-monoacylglycerols and free fatty acids are re-esterified in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum, packaged with apolipoprotein B-48 in the rough ER/Golgi, and exported basolaterally by exocytosis into lacteals — a function unique to this epithelial lineage. Enterocytes differentiate from Lgr5+ intestinal stem cells at the crypt base, migrate along the crypt-villus axis over roughly 3–5 days while progressively elaborating the brush border, and undergo anoikis-mediated shedding at the villus tip, making the intestinal epithelium one of the most rapidly self-renewing tissues in mammals.  
🇩🇪 Enterozyten sind der vorherrschende einschichtig-hochprismatische Epithelzelltyp der Dünndarmzotte und zeigen eine ausgeprägte apikobasale Polarität mit einem apikalen Bürstensaum aus etwa 1.000–3.000 aktinbasierten Mikrovilli, die über ein Terminalnetz aus Spektrin, Myosin und durch Villin/Fimbrin quervernetzten Aktinfilamenten unterhalb der Plasmamembran verankert sind. Die Bürstensaummembran trägt terminale Hydrolasen (Laktase, Sucrase-Isomaltase, Aminopeptidasen) und sekundär-aktive Nährstofftransporter (SGLT1, GLUT5, PepT1), die die luminale Verdauung abschließen und den letzten Absorptionsschritt vermitteln, während apikolaterale Tight- und Adherens-Junctions des Schlussleistenkomplexes die parazelluläre Permeabilität einschränken und die epitheliale Barrierefunktion aufrechterhalten. Enterozyten sind metabolisch auf die Reveresterung von Triglyzeriden und den Zusammenbau von Chylomikronen spezialisiert: aufgenommene 2-Monoacylglyzerine und freie Fettsäuren werden im glatten endoplasmatischen Retikulum reverestert, im rauen ER/Golgi mit Apolipoprotein B-48 verpackt und basolateral durch Exozytose in die Lymphgefäße (Lacteale) der Zotte abgegeben — eine Funktion, die einzigartig für diese Epithellinie ist. Enterozyten differenzieren sich aus Lgr5+-Darmstammzellen am Kryptengrund, wandern über etwa 3–5 Tage entlang der Krypten-Zotten-Achse, während sie den Bürstensaum zunehmend ausbilden, und werden an der Zottenspitze durch Anoikis abgestoßen — damit gehört das Darmepithel zu den am schnellsten sich selbst erneuernden Geweben des Menschen.
