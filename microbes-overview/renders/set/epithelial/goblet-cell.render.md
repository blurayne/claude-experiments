## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

The goblet cell is a specialised, polarised mucus-secreting epithelial cell interspersed among the absorptive/ciliated cells of the simple columnar epithelium of the gastrointestinal tract (from stomach to rectum, most numerous in the small and large intestine) and the pseudostratified/columnar epithelium of the respiratory tract (trachea, bronchi), as well as the conjunctiva. It takes its name from its distinctive **wine-glass / goblet shape**: a narrow basal "stem" anchored to the basement membrane holds the nucleus and the protein-synthesis machinery, while a broad, expanded apical "cup" (the **theca**) bulges toward the lumen, packed almost completely with mucin-containing secretory granules. The cell is strongly **basal-to-apical polarised**: the oval, flattened **nucleus** is compressed into the basal pole (often the most basal structure in the cell, squeezed by the granule mass above it), and immediately around/above it sit an extensive **rough endoplasmic reticulum** (synthesising the large mucin core proteins, e.g. MUC2 in the intestine, MUC5AC/MUC5B in the airway) and a large, active, supranuclear **Golgi apparatus** where the mucin polypeptides receive their extremely dense O-linked glycosylation before being packaged into secretory granules — the Golgi in intestinal goblet cells has been described as a single perforated, cup-shaped, spiralling cisterna wrapping around the apical granule mass. Numerous small **mitochondria** are scattered through the basal and perinuclear cytoplasm, meeting the high energy demand of continuous mucin synthesis. The apical theca itself is filled with many large, closely packed, membrane-bound **mucin granules** (mucous/theca granules), which appear pale and washy in routine light microscopy (their content is largely washed out by standard processing) but stain vividly with PAS or Alcian blue; ultrastructurally a thin shell of **cytoskeletal filaments (terminal web / theca filaments)** — actin near the apical membrane, a looser microtubule/intermediate-filament meshwork further down — cups and supports the granule mass and separates it from the apical plasma membrane. A modest fringe of short, sparse **microvilli** covers the apical membrane between/around the point of granule discharge (far less dense than the brush border of a neighbouring enterocyte). Mucin release occurs by regulated, and in response to strong stimuli **compound**, exocytosis at the apical membrane, discharging the granule contents which then hydrate and expand roughly 500-fold into the viscous, gel-forming mucus layer that lubricates and protects the epithelium. Goblet cells arise from the same multipotent intestinal/airway stem cells as their neighbours and, in the gut, are continuously renewed along the crypt-villus axis.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Goblet cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblet_cell), [Kenhub — Goblet cells: Definition, location, function](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/intraepithelial-glands), [Pelaseyed & Hansson 2020 / StatPearls — Histology, Goblet Cells (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553208/), [Structure of the Secretory Compartments in Goblet Cells in the Colon and Small Intestine, 2025 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12346421/), [Perez-Vilar et al. 2005, Mucin Granules are in Close Contact with Tubular Elements of the Endoplasmic Reticulum (J Histochem Cytochem)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1369/jhc.5B6713.2005).

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No cell wall** — this is an animal cell, not a plant cell or bacterium.
- **No nucleoid, plasmids or bacterial flagella** — not a prokaryote.
- **No chloroplasts or large central vacuole** — not a plant cell (the mucin-filled theca is granules, not one giant vacuole).
- **Not a tall uniform column like an enterocyte** — the defining shape is a GOBLET/WINE-GLASS: narrow basal stem, ballooning apical cup. Do not draw it as a plain rectangle or as round like a blood cell.
- **Nucleus must be BASAL, not central or apical** — it is squeezed to the base by the granule mass above it, never floating among the granules.
- **No dense uniform brush border like an enterocyte** — apical microvilli here are short and sparse, not a thick velvety carpet; the apical surface is dominated by bulging granules, not by microvilli.
- **Mucin granules are numerous, membrane-bound, round/oval packets, not one continuous blob** — draw them as many closely packed granules filling the cup, not as a single amorphous mass or a single giant bubble.
- **No motile cilia or flagellum on the goblet cell itself** — neighbouring airway cells are ciliated, but the goblet cell is not; it does not swim or beat.
- A single specimen, not a dense confluent monolayer — the goblet shape and basal-nucleus/apical-granule polarity must stay clearly readable.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The goblet cell is the body's dedicated mucus factory, scattered among the absorptive and ciliated cells that line the gut and the airways. True to its name, it is shaped like a tiny wine glass: a narrow base holding the nucleus and protein-making machinery, topped by a broad cup packed with mucin-filled granules. When triggered, it releases those granules at the surface, and the mucins swell rapidly into a slippery, gel-like layer that coats the epithelium beneath. That layer does double duty — it lubricates tissue that is under constant mechanical stress (food moving through the gut, air moving through the airway) and it acts as a physical and chemical barrier, trapping particles, microbes and irritants before they can reach the cells underneath. In the intestine, goblet cells work in close partnership with enterocytes, the nutrient-absorbing cells beside them: the enterocytes handle uptake while the goblet cells keep the protective coating renewed, so the absorptive surface never runs dry or unguarded. The same principle plays out in the respiratory tract, where mucus produced by goblet cells is swept along by neighbouring ciliated cells and cleared from the airway. It is unglamorous, continuous work, but it is one of the reasons your gut lining and airway surfaces stay intact day after day.

Ihrem Namen entsprechend ist sie geformt wie ein kleines Weinglas: ein schmaler Fuß trägt Zellkern und Proteinfabrik, darüber sitzt ein breiter Becher voller mucinhaltiger Granula. **Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Goblet cells are polarised, unicellular mucous glands interspersed within the simple columnar epithelium of the gastrointestinal tract and the pseudostratified/columnar epithelium of the conjunctiva and respiratory tract. Their characteristic goblet morphology reflects extreme secretory specialisation: a basal nucleus and dense rough endoplasmic reticulum synthesise large, heavily O-glycosylated mucin core proteins (e.g. MUC2 in the intestine, MUC5AC/MUC5B in the airway), which pass through a prominent supranuclear Golgi apparatus — described ultrastructurally as a single perforated, spiralling, cup-shaped cisterna — for further glycosylation and packaging into membrane-bound mucin granules that occupy the apical theca. A cortical cytoskeletal shell (terminal web) supports the granule mass and mediates regulated, and under strong stimulation compound, exocytosis at the apical membrane; released mucins hydrate and expand roughly 500-fold to form the viscoelastic mucus gel. In the intestine, goblet cells are functionally coupled to neighbouring enterocytes, maintaining the protective mucus layer over the absorptive epithelium and contributing to the mucosal barrier that limits direct epithelial contact with luminal microbiota; in the airway, goblet-cell mucus is transported by adjacent ciliated cells as part of mucociliary clearance. Goblet cells differentiate from the same multipotent epithelial stem-cell pool as their neighbours (e.g. Lgr5+ crypt stem cells in the gut) and are continuously renewed.  
🇩🇪 Becherzellen sind polarisierte, einzellige Schleimdrüsen, die in das einschichtige hochprismatische Epithel des Magen-Darm-Trakts sowie in das mehrreihige bzw. prismatische Epithel von Bindehaut und Atemwegen eingestreut sind. Ihre charakteristische Becherform spiegelt eine extreme sekretorische Spezialisierung wider: Ein basaler Zellkern und ein dichtes raues endoplasmatisches Retikulum synthetisieren große, stark O-glykosylierte Mucin-Kernproteine (z. B. MUC2 im Darm, MUC5AC/MUC5B in den Atemwegen), die einen ausgeprägten supranukleären Golgi-Apparat durchlaufen — ultrastrukturell als eine einzelne, perforierte, spiralig gewundene, becherförmige Zisterne beschrieben — zur weiteren Glykosylierung und Verpackung in membranumschlossene Mucingranula, die die apikale Theka füllen. Eine kortikale Zytoskelett-Hülle (Terminalnetz) stützt die Granulamasse und vermittelt regulierte, bei starker Stimulation auch kompound ablaufende Exozytose an der apikalen Membran; die freigesetzten Mucine hydratisieren und expandieren dabei um etwa das 500-Fache zu einem viskoelastischen Schleimgel. Im Darm sind Becherzellen funktionell eng mit den benachbarten Enterozyten gekoppelt, erhalten die schützende Schleimschicht über dem resorbierenden Epithel und tragen zur mukosalen Barriere bei, die den direkten Kontakt des Epithels mit der luminalen Mikrobiota begrenzt; in den Atemwegen wird der von Becherzellen produzierte Schleim von benachbarten Flimmerzellen im Rahmen der mukoziliären Clearance abtransportiert. Becherzellen differenzieren sich aus demselben multipotenten Epithel-Stammzellpool wie ihre Nachbarn (z. B. Lgr5+-Krypten-Stammzellen im Darm) und werden fortlaufend erneuert.
