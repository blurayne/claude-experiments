## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

A reticulocyte is an immature erythrocyte that has just left the bone marrow for peripheral blood. It has already extruded its nucleus (during the orthochromatic-normoblast stage of erythropoiesis), so — like the mature red cell it will become — it is anucleate, but it still retains **residual ribosomal RNA and free ribosomes**, plus scattered, degenerating remnants of mitochondria, Golgi membranes and endoplasmic reticulum left over from its nucleated precursor; these leftovers are actively being cleared by autophagy, not built up. When stained supravitally (new methylene blue or brilliant cresyl blue on a live/unfixed smear), this residual RNA precipitates into a characteristic filamentous-granular meshwork — the **"reticulum"** that gives the cell its name — visible under light microscopy as a fine blue-black net or scattered blue-black granules inside an otherwise haemoglobin-pink/blue-green cell. On a routine Wright-Giemsa smear the same residual RNA gives the cell a fainter diffuse blue-grey tinge over the pink of haemoglobin, a phenomenon called **polychromasia**. Reticulocytes are also slightly larger than mature erythrocytes and are often not yet a perfect biconcave disc — the final membrane remodelling (spectrin-actin cytoskeleton reorganisation and loss of surface area) that produces the mature biconcave shape is still happening during this maturation window. The cytoplasm is nonetheless already dominated by haemoglobin, which is the main determinant of the cell's overall pink-red colour, and its principal job — oxygen transport — is already fully active.

Maturation takes about one to two days, partly in the bone-marrow sinusoids/spleen and partly in general circulation: residual ribosomes, mitochondria and other organelle remnants are cleared by autophagy and by exosome release (and, in part, by "pitting" of inclusions in the spleen), and globin synthesis from residual mRNA gradually stops as ribosomes are lost, at which point the cell is a fully mature erythrocyte. Clinically, the **reticulocyte count/percentage** (normally roughly 0.5-2.5%, with ~1% commonly cited as a healthy average share of circulating red cells) is a key marker of erythropoietic activity — how fast the bone marrow is manufacturing new red cells — rising in blood loss, haemolysis or after erythropoietin treatment, and falling in marrow failure or nutritional-deficiency anaemias.

Sources: [Wikipedia — Reticulocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reticulocyte), [Wikipedia — Erythropoiesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythropoiesis), [NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls — Histology, Reticulocytes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534803/), [NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls — Reticulocyte Count](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555930/), Junqueira's Basic Histology, ch. 12 (Blood) — reticulocyte morphology and supravital staining.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)
- **No nucleus** — the reticulocyte has already ejected it at the orthochromatic-normoblast stage; do not draw it as a nucleated precursor (that is the separate `erythroblast` entry in this set).
- **No dense, abundant organelle population like a secretory cell** — only a few sparse, visibly degenerating remnants of mitochondria/Golgi/ER, because the cell is being cleared of organelles on its way to becoming organelle-free, not building them up.
- **No true perforation/hole through the disc** — like the mature erythrocyte, the biconcave profile is a thinned centre, not a torus with an actual opening; and unlike the mature cell, the shape here is still slightly plumper/less perfectly biconcave (still remodelling).
- **No granules like a platelet or neutrophil** — no alpha-granules, no specific/azurophilic granules; the only "granular" feature is the RNA reticulum itself.
- **No cell wall, flagella or cilia** — this is an anucleate animal-blood cell, not a plant cell, bacterium or motile cell.
- **The residual-RNA reticulum is a supravital-stain artifact, not a permanent structure** — render it as a fine internal filamentous/granular network confined to the interior (SEM should NOT show it on the surface, since electron microscopy does not reveal it; it belongs in textbook/3d/watercolor interior views and in the light-microscopy reference).
- **No crescent/sickle shape** — that is the pathological `sickle-cell` entry elsewhere in this set, a distinct, unrelated abnormality.
- **Single specimen (or a small, non-clumped handful for contrast with mature red cells)** — not a dense stack/rouleaux that hides the individual cell and its internal reticulum.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 The reticulocyte is the red blood cell in its final one-to-two days of finishing school. It leaves the bone marrow already anucleate, its haemoglobin cargo already loaded and doing its job, but it is still quietly clearing out the last construction debris from its former life as a nucleated precursor — scraps of ribosomal RNA, a handful of degenerating mitochondria and ER/Golgi fragments — via autophagy, while its membrane skeleton keeps remodelling toward the final, more efficient biconcave disc. That leftover RNA is what makes it clinically useful: stained with a supravital dye, it precipitates into a visible blue meshwork, letting a lab count exactly how many fresh cells the marrow is releasing. The reticulocyte percentage (normally around 0.5-2.5% of circulating red cells) is one of the simplest, most informative numbers in a blood count — it rises sharply after blood loss or in blood-cell-destroying conditions as the marrow ramps up production, and it falls when the marrow itself is struggling, making it a fast, practical window onto how well that production line is running.  
🇩🇪 Der Retikulozyt ist das rote Blutkörperchen in seinen letzten ein bis zwei Tagen der Ausbildung. Es verlässt das Knochenmark bereits ohne Zellkern, seine Hämoglobin-Fracht ist schon geladen und im Einsatz, doch im Hintergrund räumt es noch die letzten Baustellenreste seines früheren Lebens als kernhaltige Vorstufe weg — Schnipsel ribosomaler RNA, ein paar abgebaute Mitochondrien und ER-/Golgi-Fragmente — mittels Autophagie, während sein Membranskelett sich weiter in Richtung der endgültigen, effizienteren bikonkaven Scheibenform umbaut. Genau diese Rest-RNA macht ihn klinisch wertvoll: Mit einem Vitalfarbstoff angefärbt, fällt sie als sichtbares blaues Netzwerk aus, wodurch ein Labor genau zählen kann, wie viele frische Zellen das Knochenmark gerade freisetzt. Der Retikulozyten-Anteil (normal etwa 0,5-2,5 % der zirkulierenden roten Blutkörperchen) ist eine der einfachsten und zugleich aussagekräftigsten Zahlen im Blutbild — er steigt nach Blutverlust oder bei Erkrankungen, die rote Blutkörperchen zerstören, deutlich an, weil das Knochenmark die Produktion hochfährt, und er sinkt, wenn das Knochenmark selbst Probleme hat. Damit ist er ein schnelles, praktisches Fenster darauf, wie gut diese Produktionslinie gerade läuft.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 The reticulocyte is the immediate post-mitotic, post-enucleation product of the orthochromatic normoblast, released from the bone-marrow erythroblastic island into the peripheral circulation (or, for a fraction of the population, retained transiently in the marrow sinusoids/splenic cords) roughly 1-2 days before it completes maturation into a definitive erythrocyte. It is anucleate but retains residual polyribosomes, mitochondria, and Golgi/ER membrane fragments carried over from the nucleated precursor; these are progressively removed by autophagy (mitophagy/reticulophagy pathways) and, in part, by exosome release and splenic 'pitting'. Supravital staining with new methylene blue or brilliant cresyl blue precipitates the residual ribosomal RNA into the characteristic reticulofilamentous network from which the cell takes its name, the basis of both manual reticulocyte counting and modern flow-cytometric/automated reticulocyte indices (e.g. immature reticulocyte fraction, reticulocyte haemoglobin content). Concurrently, the spectrin-actin membrane skeleton continues to reorganise and surface area is lost, converting the still slightly larger, less perfectly biconcave reticulocyte into the mature erythrocyte's optimised biconcave-disc geometry. Because reticulocyte output tracks erythropoietin-driven marrow activity directly, the reticulocyte count/percentage and its corrected/absolute forms are a standard clinical index of erythropoietic response, elevated in haemolysis, acute blood loss, or exogenous erythropoietin administration, and suppressed in marrow aplasia, nutritional deficiency anaemias, or ineffective erythropoiesis.  
🇩🇪 Der Retikulozyt ist das unmittelbare postmitotische, kernlose Produkt des orthochromatischen Normoblasten, das aus der erythroblastischen Insel des Knochenmarks in die periphere Zirkulation entlassen wird (bzw. bei einem Teil der Population noch vorübergehend in den Marksinusoiden/Milzsträngen verbleibt) und etwa 1-2 Tage vor Abschluss seiner Reifung zum definitiven Erythrozyten steht. Er ist kernlos, trägt jedoch noch residuelle Polyribosomen, Mitochondrien sowie Golgi-/ER-Membranfragmente aus der kernhaltigen Vorstufe mit sich, die schrittweise durch Autophagie (Mitophagie-/Retikulophagie-Wege) sowie teilweise durch Exosomenabgabe und milzbedingtes 'Pitting' entfernt werden. Die Vitalfärbung mit Neumethylenblau oder Brillantkresylblau lässt die restliche ribosomale RNA zu dem charakteristischen retikulofilamentösen Netzwerk ausfallen, das der Zelle ihren Namen gibt und die Grundlage sowohl der manuellen Retikulozytenzählung als auch moderner durchflusszytometrischer/automatisierter Retikulozyten-Indizes (z. B. unreife Retikulozytenfraktion, retikulozytärer Hämoglobingehalt) bildet. Parallel dazu reorganisiert sich das Spektrin-Aktin-Membranskelett weiter und Membranoberfläche geht verloren, wodurch sich der noch etwas größere, weniger perfekt bikonkave Retikulozyt in die optimierte bikonkave Scheibengeometrie des reifen Erythrozyten umwandelt. Da die Retikulozytenproduktion die durch Erythropoietin gesteuerte Markaktivität direkt widerspiegelt, ist der Retikulozyten-Anteil bzw. dessen korrigierte/absolute Form ein klinischer Standardindex der erythropoetischen Reaktion — erhöht bei Hämolyse, akutem Blutverlust oder exogener Erythropoietin-Gabe, supprimiert bei Markaplasie, Mangelanämien oder ineffektiver Erythropoese.
