## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Zika virus is classified in genus **Orthoflavivirus** (family **Flaviviridae**), the same genus as dengue, yellow fever and West Nile virus. It is a small (~40-65 nm, commonly cited as ~50 nm), **enveloped**, roughly icosahedral virion. **The single most important teaching point for this microbe is its surface: it is SMOOTH.** Unlike coronaviruses (club spikes) or influenza (HA/NA projections), flaviviruses carry no protruding surface structures at all.

1. **Genome.** A single molecule of **positive-sense single-stranded RNA (+ssRNA), about 10.7 kb**, with one continuous open reading frame encoding three structural proteins (C, prM/M, E) and seven nonstructural proteins (NS1–NS5). In the finished virion it sits as a loosely tangled coil at the core — not a double helix (that would be DNA) and not segmented.
2. **Capsid.** The **C (capsid) protein** forms a comparatively **disordered, loosely organised shell** around the genome — softer and less crisply faceted than the rigid icosahedral capsids of, say, a bacteriophage or a herpesvirus. It is the innermost protein layer, directly enclosing the RNA.
3. **Envelope.** A **host-derived lipid bilayer**, acquired from the endoplasmic reticulum membrane during assembly, wraps around the capsid. This is what makes Zika an *enveloped* virus, unlike the non-enveloped rotavirus and norovirus already in this atlas.
4. **E (envelope) glycoprotein — the defining surface feature.** **180 copies of the E glycoprotein pack as 90 head-to-tail homodimers that lie FLAT against the lipid envelope**, tiled edge-to-edge in an icosahedral **herringbone raft**. This is what gives the mature virion its smooth, continuous, almost geometric surface texture — think a **golf ball or a woven rattan/basket-weave sphere**: textured with facets and seams, but nothing sticks out. **There are no spikes.** This flat-lying arrangement is the structural signature of the whole flavivirus genus (confirmed for Zika itself by cryo-EM at 3.8 Å resolution, near-identical to dengue).
5. **M (membrane) protein.** A small protein retained under the E layer after the immature prM protein is cleaved by furin during maturation. It sits directly against the envelope, mostly hidden beneath the E tiling — visible only as a thin undercoat in a cutaway.

**Biology worth carrying into the text:** Zika is transmitted mainly by **day-biting *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus* mosquitoes**, and also by sexual contact and vertically (transplacentally) from mother to fetus. Most infections are **mild or entirely asymptomatic** — low fever, a mild rash, conjunctivitis, joint pain. The serious risk is **congenital Zika syndrome**: infection during pregnancy can cross the placenta and disturb fetal brain development, causing microcephaly and other brain abnormalities; there is also a smaller elevated risk of Guillain-Barré syndrome in adults. **There is no licensed vaccine yet**; prevention relies on avoiding mosquito bites (repellent, covering up, nets, eliminating standing water).

Sources: standard flavivirus virology consistent with ICTV *Flaviviridae* taxonomy and CDC/WHO Zika fact sheets for transmission/clinical picture; structural detail (smooth herringbone E-dimer raft, disordered capsid, furin-cleaved M protein) drawn from published high-resolution cryo-EM flavivirus structures (Sirohi et al. 2016, *Science* 352(6284):467-470, "The 3.8 Å resolution cryo-EM structure of Zika virus"; Kostyuchenko et al. 2016, *Nature*, on the closely related, structurally near-identical dengue virus), cross-checked against direct visual inspection of the real TEM reference below.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **ANY protruding spikes of any kind** — no coronavirus-style club spikes, no influenza-style HA/NA projections, no rod-like or knob-like studs. This is the #1 error for this virus: Zika's E glycoproteins lie **flat** against the membrane, they do not project outward.
- **A bald, perfectly blank sphere with zero texture** — the E-protein herringbone tiling should read as a subtle woven/faceted/dimpled surface (golf ball, rattan ball), not literally featureless.
- **A naked capsid with no envelope** — Zika is *always* enveloped, unlike the non-enveloped rotavirus/norovirus already in this atlas.
- **A tegument layer or granular padding between capsid and envelope** — that is a herpesvirus feature (varicella-zoster virus, already in this atlas); Zika has no such layer.
- **A helical, rod-shaped or filamentous capsid**, or a **bacteriophage head-and-tail body** with tail fibres/base plate.
- **A segmented genome, or double-stranded/helical DNA** — the genome is a single molecule of **positive-sense single-stranded RNA**, not DNA, not segmented.
- **Bacterial features**: cell wall, peptidoglycan, flagellum, pilus, capsule, nucleoid, ribosomes, mitochondria or any cell organelle — a virion is not a cell.
- **A mosquito, mosquito bite, pregnant belly, fetus, or any depiction of microcephaly/birth defects** in the four science styles (sem/textbook/3d/watercolor) — that is transmission/clinical context, not virus morphology. Per the brief, the pregnancy risk must not appear at all in the kids' materials, and is handled plainly and without alarm only in the adult/scientific text.
- **A face, eyes, mouth or anthropomorphism** on the virion in the four science styles (reserved for the separate kids' coloring page only), and **no text, letters, numbers, labels, arrows, scale bars or watermarks** baked into the image.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Zika virus (ZIKV) is a mosquito-borne member of genus Orthoflavivirus, family Flaviviridae, first isolated in 1947 from a sentinel rhesus macaque in the Zika Forest, Uganda. The mature virion is a small (~40-65 nm), enveloped, roughly icosahedral particle enclosing a positive-sense single-stranded RNA genome of approximately 10.7 kb with a single open reading frame that is co- and post-translationally processed into three structural proteins (capsid C, precursor membrane prM/mature membrane M, and envelope E) and seven nonstructural proteins (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, NS5) that drive replication and immune evasion. The capsid protein forms a comparatively disordered nucleocapsid rather than a rigid icosahedral shell. The defining structural feature, resolved by cryo-electron microscopy at 3.8 A resolution and essentially identical to dengue virus, is the arrangement of the E glycoprotein: 180 copies pack as 90 head-to-tail homodimers lying flat and parallel to the envelope in an icosahedral herringbone raft, producing a smooth, continuous surface with no projecting spikes - in contrast to the trimeric spikes of alphaviruses or the club-shaped spikes of coronaviruses. The M protein, generated by furin cleavage of prM during trans-Golgi maturation, sits beneath the E raft. Transmission occurs primarily via Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes, with documented secondary routes of sexual and vertical (transplacental) transmission. Most infections are subclinical or produce a mild, self-limited illness (low-grade fever, maculopapular rash, arthralgia, conjunctivitis). Clinical significance centers on two associations established during the 2015-2016 American outbreak: congenital Zika syndrome, in which transplacental infection disrupts neural progenitor cell proliferation and causes microcephaly and other structural brain anomalies, and a modestly elevated risk of Guillain-Barre syndrome following infection in adults. No vaccine is currently licensed; several candidates (inactivated, mRNA, DNA, live-attenuated) remain in clinical development.  
🇩🇪 Das Zika-Virus (ZIKV) ist ein durch Mücken übertragenes Mitglied der Gattung Orthoflavivirus, Familie Flaviviridae, erstmals 1947 aus einem Sentinel-Rhesusaffen im Zika-Wald in Uganda isoliert. Das reife Virion ist ein kleines (~40-65 nm), behülltes, annähernd ikosaedrisches Partikel, das ein Genom aus positivsträngiger Einzelstrang-RNA von etwa 10,7 kb mit einem durchgehenden offenen Leserahmen enthält; dieser wird ko- und posttranslational in drei Strukturproteine (Capsid C, Vorläufer-Membranprotein prM/reifes Membranprotein M und Hüllprotein E) sowie sieben Nichtstrukturproteine (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, NS5) prozessiert, die Replikation und Immunevasion steuern. Das Capsidprotein bildet ein vergleichsweise ungeordnetes Nukleokapsid statt einer starren ikosaedrischen Schale. Das entscheidende Strukturmerkmal, mittels Kryo-Elektronenmikroskopie bei 3,8 Å Auflösung aufgeklärt und praktisch identisch zum Dengue-Virus, ist die Anordnung des E-Glykoproteins: 180 Kopien lagern sich zu 90 Kopf-an-Schwanz-Homodimeren zusammen, die flach und parallel zur Hülle liegen und in einem ikosaedrischen Fischgrätmuster ("Herringbone") verlegt sind - das ergibt eine glatte, durchgehende Oberfläche ohne vorstehende Spikes, im Gegensatz zu den trimeren Spikes der Alphaviren oder den keulenförmigen Spikes der Coronaviren. Das M-Protein, das durch Furin-Spaltung von prM während der Reifung im trans-Golgi-Netzwerk entsteht, liegt unter der E-Schicht. Die Übertragung erfolgt vor allem durch die Mücken Aedes aegypti und Aedes albopictus, dokumentiert sind zudem sexuelle und vertikale (transplazentare) Übertragungswege. Die meisten Infektionen verlaufen subklinisch oder als milde, selbstlimitierende Erkrankung (leichtes Fieber, makulopapulöser Ausschlag, Gelenkschmerzen, Konjunktivitis). Die klinische Bedeutung beruht auf zwei Zusammenhängen, die während des Ausbruchs 2015-2016 in Amerika belegt wurden: dem kongenitalen Zika-Syndrom, bei dem eine transplazentare Infektion die Proliferation neuraler Vorläuferzellen stört und Mikrozephalie sowie weitere strukturelle Hirnanomalien verursacht, sowie einem mäßig erhöhten Risiko für das Guillain-Barre-Syndrom nach Infektion Erwachsener. Aktuell ist keine Impfung zugelassen; mehrere Kandidaten (inaktiviert, mRNA, DNA, lebend-attenuiert) befinden sich in der klinischen Entwicklung.
