## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Norovirus is a **small, naked (non-enveloped) icosahedral RNA virus** of the family **Caliciviridae**, genus *Norovirus*. The virion is **round-to-hexagonal in outline and about 38-40 nm across** (ICTV gives 35-40 nm for caliciviruses generally; the classic negative-stain plates of human norovirus measure 27-38 nm depending on stain penetration) — a particle of roughly **0.5 femtogram**, one of the smallest things in this atlas. There is **no lipid envelope, no membrane and no glycoprotein spikes at all**: the outer boundary is bare protein, and that is the single most important fact for every render.

The capsid is the simplest possible design and the most distinctive-looking. It is built from **180 copies of one single major structural protein, VP1**, arranged with **T = 3 icosahedral symmetry** as **90 dimers**. Each VP1 folds into two parts joined by a short hinge: an N-terminal **shell (S) domain**, an eight-stranded antiparallel β-barrel that tiles into the **contiguous inner shell** enclosing the genome; and a C-terminal **protruding (P) domain** that sticks outward. Two P domains from neighbouring subunits clasp into an **arch-shaped dimer** — the "capsomere" you actually see in profile — whose **P1 subdomain forms the legs** of the arch and whose **hypervariable P2 subdomain forms the outward-facing head**. P2 carries the **histo-blood-group antigen (HBGA) binding site** and the main neutralising epitopes, which is why a person's secretor/blood-group status influences susceptibility and why the virus evolves so fast at that surface.

Because 90 arches rise off an otherwise continuous shell, the space **between** them is sunk into **32 evenly spaced, round, cup-shaped depressions — 12 at the icosahedral five-fold axes and 20 at the three-fold axes**. These calices are what Caliciviridae is named for (Latin *calix*, "cup" or "goblet"), and they are exactly what makes a norovirus particle read as a **knobbly, dimpled ball rather than a smooth sphere** in electron micrographs. They are **hollows**, an optical consequence of the arching P dimers around them — they are **never spikes, rays or stalks**, and drawing them as protrusions inverts the whole morphology.

Inside the shell sits a **single, continuous, positive-sense single-stranded RNA molecule of ~7.5 kb** (ICTV: 7.4-8.3 kb for the family; human norovirus genomes cluster around 7.5-7.7 kb). It carries no cap; instead the small viral protein **VPg is covalently linked to its 5' end**, and the 3' end is **polyadenylated**. Because it is plus-strand RNA, host ribosomes read it directly — the virion carries **no packaged polymerase, no matrix layer and no nucleocapsid protein**. A handful of copies of the **minor structural protein VP2** ride inside, associated with the S-domain face of the shell.

**Contrast with rotavirus**, which is already in this atlas and is the other great cause of viral gastroenteritis: rotavirus is a **triple-layered ~75 nm wheel with 11 segments of double-stranded RNA and a packaged polymerase**; norovirus is a **single-layered ~38 nm shell with one continuous plus-strand RNA**. If both appear side by side they must be unmistakably different objects.

Biology worth carrying into the pictures and the text: norovirus is a **gut virus**, replicating in the **enterocytes of the small intestine** (and, in the murine model, in tuft cells), which is why illness is sudden vomiting plus watery, non-bloody diarrhoea rather than a respiratory syndrome. **Incubation is 6-50 hours and the illness usually lasts 12-48 hours.** Transmission is **faecal-oral** — person to person, contaminated food and water (shellfish, salads), and contaminated surfaces — with billions of particles shed per gram of stool and vomit. The **infectious dose is famously tiny: RKI cites 10-100 virus particles, with as few as 10 sufficient**, which is why outbreaks tear through cruise ships, schools, care homes and hospital wards. Being **non-enveloped it is highly environment-stable and resists alcohol**: CDC states plainly that hand sanitiser does not work well against norovirus and that **hand washing with soap and water for at least 20 seconds** is the effective measure; German practice (RKI) requires disinfectants declared **"begrenzt viruzid PLUS"** or **"viruzid"**, not ordinary limited-virucidal alcohol rubs. There is no licensed vaccine and no specific antiviral; treatment is **rehydration** — small sips, often.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **ANY lipid envelope, membrane bilayer, soap-bubble halo or greasy coat** — norovirus is **naked**. This is the most common and most serious error, and it also contradicts the teaching point that alcohol gel works poorly against it.
- **Coronavirus-style long stalked club spikes, a corona of knobs, HA/NA-style projections, radiating rays or a fuzzy fringe** — the relief is only the arching P-domain dimers of the shell itself. Never call or draw them spikes.
- **A smooth featureless sphere, a plain golf ball, a billiard ball or a bare geometric d20** — the 90 arches and the 32 cup-shaped hollows must read clearly; a smooth ball is a FAIL.
- **A helical, rod-shaped, filamentous or pleomorphic capsid** — the particle is icosahedral.
- **DNA, a double helix or base-pair rungs** — the genome is **single-stranded RNA**.
- **A segmented genome, several separate RNA pieces, or eleven little rods** — that is rotavirus; norovirus has exactly **ONE continuous RNA molecule**.
- **A triple-layered rotavirus-style shell** (core + VP6 middle layer + VP7 outer layer), **VP4 spikes**, or a **wheel / hub-and-spoke silhouette** — norovirus has a **single capsid layer**.
- **A packaged RNA polymerase, a matrix layer, a helical nucleocapsid, or nucleoprotein beads strung along the RNA** — the interior holds the bare RNA plus VPg and a few VP2 copies, nothing else.
- **A nucleus, ribosomes, mitochondria, cytoplasm or any cell organelle**, and **no cell wall, flagellum, pilus or capsule** — a virion is not a cell.
- **A bacteriophage head-and-tail body, tail fibres, base plate or legs.**
- **Anything at rotavirus/influenza scale** — beside other viruses it must read as clearly among the smallest.
- **A face, eyes or anthropomorphism** (except in the separate kids' coloring page), and **no text, letters, numbers, labels, arrows, scale bars or watermarks** baked into the image.
- **No black border, frame, vignette, letterbox bar, mat or drop shadow**, and never the artwork drawn as a sheet/card lying on a surface.

**Kids (GiantMicrobes-style).**  
🇬🇧 Meet norovirus — the tiny troublemaker behind the "stomach bug" that races through schools, nurseries and cruise ships. It is one of the smallest viruses in this collection, a ball only about 38 millionths of a millimetre across, and it has no soft coat at all: just a bare, tough shell of protein wrapped straight around its single strand of RNA. Look closely at that shell and you will see it is not smooth. Ninety pairs of the same protein arch up out of the surface like little bridges, and between them sit thirty-two round hollows, like dents pressed into a golf ball with a thumb. Scientists thought those dents looked like tiny cups, so they named the whole virus family after the Latin word for cup, *calix*. Norovirus goes straight to your small intestine and upsets the lining there, which is why it makes people throw up suddenly and gives them watery diarrhoea. What makes it so good at spreading is that it barely needs any help: swallowing about ten particles can be enough, and one sick person sheds billions of them. Here is the surprising part — the squirty alcohol hand gel by the door hardly bothers it, because gel works by dissolving the greasy coat that norovirus does not have. Plain soap and warm water win instead: the soap lifts the little balls off your skin and the running water carries them down the drain, so scrub properly for a good twenty seconds, especially before eating. And if it does get you, the illness is usually short — a rough day or two and then it is over. The one job that really matters while you wait is drinking: small sips, often, so your body gets back the water it is losing.

**Adults (popular science, health).**  
🇬🇧 Norovirus is the single commonest cause of acute gastroenteritis in adults worldwide, and the reason a nursery, a care home, a hospital ward or a cruise ship can go from normal to chaotic in forty-eight hours. The illness itself is unremarkable — abrupt vomiting, watery diarrhoea, cramps and a low fever starting a day or two after exposure and gone again within one to three days. What is remarkable is the transmission arithmetic. The infectious dose is on the order of ten to a hundred virions; an infected person sheds billions per gram of stool and continues shedding for a week or more after feeling well; and projectile vomiting aerosolises the virus across a room. Because the particle is non-enveloped it also survives for days on door handles, taps and worktops, tolerates freezing and normal cooking temperatures poorly enough to persist in raw oysters and salad, and shrugs off the alcohol hand rub that works so well against enveloped viruses like influenza and SARS-CoV-2 — alcohol has no lipid membrane to dissolve here. The practical consequences follow directly: wash with soap and running water rather than relying on gel, disinfect surfaces with a chlorine-based product rather than a quaternary ammonium wipe, keep symptomatic food handlers away from food for at least 48 hours after symptoms stop, and launder soiled fabrics hot. Treatment is oral rehydration and time; antibiotics are irrelevant and antiemetics are only occasionally needed. Immunity is short-lived and strain-specific, partly because the dominant GII.4 lineage keeps swapping its outer surface, so catching it once buys little protection — and susceptibility is partly genetic, since the virus binds histo-blood-group antigens, and people who lack a functioning FUT2 gene (so-called non-secretors, roughly a fifth of Europeans) are largely resistant to the common strains. Vaccine candidates are in clinical trials but none is licensed for general use.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Norovirus (family Caliciviridae, genus Norovirus, type species Norwalk virus) is a non-enveloped, icosahedral, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus of roughly 27-40 nm; cryo-EM and the 1IHM crystal structure of recombinant Norwalk virus-like particles give an outer diameter close to 38 nm. The capsid has T=3 icosahedral symmetry and is built from 180 copies of the major capsid protein VP1 arranged as 90 dimers occupying three quasi-equivalent positions (A, B, C). Each VP1 folds into two domains connected by a flexible hinge: the shell (S) domain, an eight-stranded beta-sandwich whose 180 copies form the contiguous ~30 nm inner shell that encloses the genome, and the protruding (P) domain, which dimerises and arches away from the shell. The P domain subdivides into P1, the stem that supports the arch, and P2, a hypervariable insertion at the top of P1 that forms the outermost surface and carries both the neutralising epitopes and the binding site for histo-blood-group antigens (HBGAs), the fucosylated carbohydrate attachment factors whose expression depends on a functional FUT2 (secretor) gene. The 90 arching dimers leave 32 large cup-shaped depressions at the icosahedral five-fold and three-fold axes; these calices, first resolved by negative-stain electron microscopy as a characteristic ragged, feathery outline with a Star-of-David appearance, give the family its name (Latin calix, cup). A second, minor structural protein, VP2 (ORF3), is basic, present at one or two copies per virion, binds the interior surface of the S domain and is required for infectious particle assembly and genome stability. The genome is a single positive-sense ssRNA molecule of about 7.5-7.7 kb, polyadenylated at the 3' end and carrying the viral protein VPg covalently attached to the 5' end in place of a cap; VPg recruits eIF4E and the initiation machinery directly. Three open reading frames are used by human noroviruses: ORF1 encodes a polyprotein cleaved by the viral 3C-like protease NS6 into six non-structural proteins including the NS7 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, ORF2 encodes VP1 and ORF3 encodes VP2; an additional overlapping ORF4 encoding VF1, an innate-immune antagonist, is present in murine norovirus. Replication occurs on rearranged cytoplasmic membranes with a negative-sense intermediate and a subgenomic RNA that expresses VP1 and VP2. Human noroviruses long resisted culture; they are now propagated in human intestinal enteroid (organoid) monolayers and in B cells with an HBGA-expressing enteric bacterial cofactor, and tuft cells have been identified as the tropic target of murine norovirus. Classification is by VP1 sequence into ten genogroups (GI-GX), of which GI, GII, GIV, GVIII and GIX infect humans; GII.4 has caused successive global epidemic waves through epochal antigenic drift in the P2 subdomain, together with recombination at the ORF1/ORF2 junction. Contrast with rotavirus, the other classic paediatric gut virus in this atlas: rotavirus is roughly twice the diameter, triple-layered, and carries eleven segments of double-stranded RNA, whereas norovirus has a single capsid layer and one continuous ssRNA molecule.  
🇩🇪 Noroviren (Familie Caliciviridae, Gattung Norovirus, Typspezies Norwalk-Virus) sind unbehüllte, ikosaedrische Einzelstrang-RNA-Viren mit Positivpolarität von etwa 27-40 nm; die Kryo-EM-Daten und die Kristallstruktur 1IHM rekombinanter Norwalk-virusähnlicher Partikel ergeben einen Außendurchmesser von annähernd 38 nm. Das Kapsid besitzt eine ikosaedrische T=3-Symmetrie und besteht aus 180 Kopien des Hauptkapsidproteins VP1, angeordnet als 90 Dimere in drei quasiäquivalenten Positionen (A, B, C). Jedes VP1 faltet sich in zwei über ein flexibles Scharnier verbundene Domänen: die Schalendomäne (shell, S), ein achtsträngiges Beta-Sandwich, dessen 180 Kopien die zusammenhängende, rund 30 nm große innere Schale bilden, die das Genom umschließt, und die hervorstehende Domäne (protruding, P), die dimerisiert und sich bogenförmig von der Schale abhebt. Die P-Domäne gliedert sich in P1, den Stiel, der den Bogen trägt, und P2, eine hypervariable Insertion an der Spitze von P1, die die äußerste Oberfläche bildet und sowohl die neutralisierenden Epitope als auch die Bindungsstelle für Histo-Blutgruppenantigene (HBGA) trägt — jene fukosylierten Kohlenhydrat-Anheftungsfaktoren, deren Ausprägung von einem funktionsfähigen FUT2-Gen (Sekretor-Status) abhängt. Die 90 aufgewölbten Dimere lassen 32 große becherförmige Vertiefungen an den fünf- und dreizähligen ikosaedrischen Achsen frei; diese Kelche, elektronenmikroskopisch im Negativkontrast zuerst als charakteristisch ausgefranster, gefiederter Umriss mit Davidstern-Anmutung sichtbar gemacht, gaben der Familie ihren Namen (lateinisch calix, Becher). Ein zweites, mengenmäßig untergeordnetes Strukturprotein, VP2 (ORF3), ist basisch, liegt in ein bis zwei Kopien pro Virion vor, bindet die Innenseite der S-Domäne und wird für den Zusammenbau infektiöser Partikel und die Genomstabilität benötigt. Das Genom ist ein einzelnes Plusstrang-ssRNA-Molekül von etwa 7,5-7,7 kb, am 3'-Ende polyadenyliert und am 5'-Ende anstelle einer Cap-Struktur kovalent mit dem viralen Protein VPg verknüpft; VPg rekrutiert eIF4E und die Initiationsmaschinerie unmittelbar. Humane Noroviren nutzen drei offene Leserahmen: ORF1 codiert ein Polyprotein, das von der viralen 3C-ähnlichen Protease NS6 in sechs Nichtstrukturproteine gespalten wird, darunter die RNA-abhängige RNA-Polymerase NS7; ORF2 codiert VP1, ORF3 codiert VP2. Beim murinen Norovirus kommt ein überlappendes ORF4 für VF1 hinzu, einen Antagonisten der angeborenen Immunantwort. Die Replikation läuft an umgebauten zytoplasmatischen Membranen über ein Minusstrang-Intermediat und eine subgenomische RNA ab, die VP1 und VP2 exprimiert. Humane Noroviren galten lange als nicht kultivierbar; heute vermehrt man sie in Monolayern humaner intestinaler Enteroide (Organoide) sowie in B-Zellen unter Zugabe HBGA-exprimierender Darmbakterien als Kofaktor, und beim murinen Norovirus wurden Tuft-Zellen als Zielzelltyp identifiziert. Die Einteilung erfolgt anhand der VP1-Sequenz in zehn Genogruppen (GI-GX), von denen GI, GII, GIV, GVIII und GIX den Menschen infizieren; GII.4 hat durch epochale Antigendrift in der P2-Subdomäne zusammen mit Rekombination an der ORF1/ORF2-Grenze aufeinanderfolgende weltweite Epidemiewellen ausgelöst. Im Vergleich zum Rotavirus, dem anderen klassischen kindlichen Darmvirus dieses Atlas: Rotaviren sind rund doppelt so groß, dreischichtig aufgebaut und tragen elf Segmente doppelsträngiger RNA, während Noroviren eine einzige Kapsidschicht und ein einziges durchgehendes ssRNA-Molekül besitzen.
