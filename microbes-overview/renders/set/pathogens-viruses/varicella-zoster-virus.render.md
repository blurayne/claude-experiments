## 1. Scientific reference (the yardstick for verification)

Varicella-zoster virus (VZV, human alphaherpesvirus 3) is classified in family **Orthoherpesviridae**, subfamily **Alphaherpesvirinae**, genus *Varicellovirus*. It is the cause of **chickenpox (varicella)** on primary infection and **shingles (herpes zoster)** on reactivation. Like every herpesvirus, the virion has the same **four concentric layers**, and getting all four right — especially the one layer most illustrations quietly drop — is the entire teaching point of this microbe.

1. **Genome.** A **linear double-stranded DNA molecule of about 125 kbp**, encoding roughly 70 open reading frames — the **smallest genome of the nine human herpesviruses**. It is packaged under high internal pressure, tightly wound, inside the capsid.
2. **Capsid.** A **T=16 icosahedral shell built from 162 capsomeres** — **150 hexons plus 12 pentons** — with one vertex replaced by a dodecameric portal through which the DNA is threaded in (and later ejected out during infection). This capsid is essentially identical in architecture across all herpesviruses.
3. **Tegument.** Between the capsid and the envelope sits the **tegument**: an amorphous-to-fibrillar layer of viral proteins that is **characteristically asymmetric**, so the capsid often sits **eccentrically** (off-centre) within the finished particle rather than dead-centre. This layer delivers preformed effector proteins straight into a newly infected cell before any new viral gene is even transcribed — notably the major transactivator **IE62** (ORF62 product) and the **ORF47/ORF66 protein kinases**. **The tegument is the single most commonly omitted structure in generic virus illustrations, and its omission is the main scientific risk for this render:** it must show up as real, substantial, granular/fibrous volume filling the gap between capsid and envelope — not a thin membrane line, not skipped.
4. **Envelope.** The outermost layer is a **lipid envelope acquired during secondary envelopment** from cytoplasmic membranes, carrying **at least eight glycoproteins** — gB, gC, gE, gH, gI, gK, gL and gM (with gN) — which appear in electron micrographs as **short, blunt, densely packed studs**, never long stalked spikes. **gE is essential, the most abundant, and forms a heterodimer with gI** that governs cell-to-cell spread; gE is also the antigen used in the recombinant shingles (zoster) vaccine. Because the envelope is added around an already irregular tegument-wrapped capsid, the **finished virion (180-200 nm) is often slightly irregular / pleomorphic in outline rather than a perfect sphere** — a subtler point than the "no envelope" errors that matter for the *non*-enveloped viruses already in this atlas (rotavirus, norovirus), but worth carrying into the renders where feasible.

**Biology worth carrying into the text:** primary infection begins in respiratory mucosa, is amplified in tonsillar T lymphocytes that traffic the virus to skin, and produces the classic itchy, crop-by-crop vesicular rash of chickenpox roughly two weeks after exposure. VZV is one of the few genuinely **airborne** human viruses, which is why household attack rates in the unvaccinated are extremely high. Rather than being cleared, the virus then travels up sensory nerve fibres and establishes **lifelong latency in neurons of the dorsal root, cranial-nerve and autonomic ganglia**, transcribing almost nothing and making no new virions. Decades later, as VZV-specific cell-mediated immunity wanes with age or immunosuppression, it can reactivate, travel back down a single nerve, and erupt as **herpes zoster (shingles)** — a painful, strictly one-sided band of blisters, sometimes followed by prolonged postherpetic neuralgia. Two different vaccines address the two halves of this story: a **live attenuated varicella vaccine** in childhood, and a **separate adjuvanted recombinant gE-subunit vaccine** for older adults against shingles. Antivirals (aciclovir, valaciclovir) shorten an attack when started early. Replication is nuclear, and in culture VZV is markedly cell-associated, releasing little free infectious virus — one reason it was harder to grow and characterise than some other herpesviruses.

Sources: research compiled during an earlier (interrupted) session pass consistent with standard virology references — ICTV *Orthoherpesviridae* taxonomy, general herpesvirus virology texts (e.g. Fields Virology) on the four-layer architecture (genome/capsid/tegument/envelope), the T=16/162-capsomere capsid geometry, the ~125 kbp genome and ORF62/47/66 tegument proteins, the eight envelope glycoproteins and gE/gI's role, latency in dorsal root/cranial/autonomic ganglia and reactivation as herpes zoster, airborne transmission, and the two-vaccine (varicella vs. recombinant zoster) picture — cross-checked against direct visual inspection of the real TEM reference below, which itself shows a bright capsid core, a granular tegument shell around it, and an irregular envelope edge, corroborating the architecture described here.

### Do NOT draw (scientifically misleading)

- **A naked capsid with no envelope** — VZV is *always* enveloped, unlike the non-enveloped rotavirus/norovirus already in this atlas.
- **A smooth, perfectly round, featureless envelope with no tegument between capsid and envelope** — the tegument must read as a visible granular/fibrous layer filling real volume, not a thin membrane line or nothing at all.
- **The tegument drawn as a second smooth membrane** — it is amorphous/fibrous protein padding, not a lipid bilayer.
- **A helical, rod-shaped or filamentous capsid**, or a **bacteriophage head-and-tail body** with tail fibres/base plate.
- **Long stalked club-shaped spikes** (that is coronavirus/influenza) — VZV's glycoprotein spikes are short, blunt, densely packed studs, never long projections or rays.
- **A perfectly spherical particle** — the envelope is often slightly irregular / scalloped in outline rather than a precise sphere.
- **A segmented genome or RNA of any kind** — the genome is a single linear *double-stranded DNA* molecule, not RNA, not segmented.
- **Bacterial features**: cell wall, peptidoglycan, flagellum, pilus, capsule, nucleoid, ribosomes, mitochondria or any cell organelle — a virion is not a cell.
- **Blisters, pustules, a rash, or any skin lesion depicted on a person** in the four science styles (sem/textbook/3d/watercolor) — those are clinical signs of the disease, not virus morphology.
- **A face, eyes, mouth or anthropomorphism** on the virion in the four science styles (reserved for the separate kids' coloring page only), and **no text, letters, numbers, labels, arrows, scale bars or watermarks** baked into the image.
- **No black border, frame, vignette, letterbox bar, mat or drop shadow**, and never the artwork drawn as a sheet/card lying on a surface.

**Scientific.**  
🇬🇧 Varicella-zoster virus (VZV; human alphaherpesvirus 3) is classified in the family Orthoherpesviridae, subfamily Alphaherpesvirinae, genus Varicellovirus. The enveloped virion measures roughly 180-200 nm and is pleomorphic, with the four-layer herpesvirus architecture. Its genome is a linear double-stranded DNA molecule of about 125 kbp encoding roughly 70 open reading frames, the smallest genome among the human herpesviruses, packaged under pressure inside a T=16 icosahedral capsid built from 162 capsomeres - 150 hexons and 12 pentons - one vertex of which is replaced by the dodecameric portal through which DNA is inserted and later ejected. Between capsid and envelope lies the tegument, an amorphous to fibrillar proteinaceous compartment that is characteristically asymmetric, so the capsid is frequently eccentric within the particle; it delivers preformed effectors into the newly infected cell, notably the major transactivator IE62 (ORF62 product) and the ORF47 and ORF66 protein kinases. The envelope is derived from cytoplasmic membranes during secondary envelopment and carries at least eight glycoproteins - gB, gC, gE, gH, gI, gK, gL and gM with gN - which appear in electron micrographs as short, blunt, densely packed spikes rather than long projections; gE, essential and the most abundant, forms a heterodimer with gI that governs cell-to-cell spread, and gE is the antigen used in the recombinant zoster subunit vaccine. Primary infection begins in respiratory mucosa, is amplified in tonsillar T lymphocytes that traffic the virus to skin, and produces varicella. VZV then establishes lifelong latency in neurons of dorsal root, cranial nerve and autonomic ganglia with highly restricted transcription; reactivation on decline of VZV-specific cell-mediated immunity yields dermatomal herpes zoster. Replication is nuclear and, in culture, the virus is markedly cell-associated, with little free infectious virus released.
