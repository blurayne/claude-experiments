## description
Scientists recently demonstrated that adult mouse brain tissue can be cryopreserved using vitrification (turning tissue into a glass-like state), stored at cryogenic temperatures, and then thawed with recovery of neural activity — including synaptic plasticity and long-term potentiation, a core cellular mechanism underlying learning.

This video explains what the researchers actually did. 
We also explore the broader implications for cryopreservation, connectomics, brain preservation, and biological computatio and why this work reopens old philosophical and technological questions about memory, identity, and whether information in the brain can, in principle, be preserved through extreme physical states.
