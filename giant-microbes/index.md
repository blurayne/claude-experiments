# <img src="icon.png" alt="" width="512" height="512" style="height:1.6em;width:auto;vertical-align:-.4em"> GIANTmicrobes Catalog

Every plush "microbe" GIANTmicrobes sells (or used to sell), scraped from the brand's two storefronts — [giantmicrobes.com/us](https://www.giantmicrobes.com/us/) (US, Magento) and [riesenmikroben.de](https://www.riesenmikroben.de/) (Germany, a separate Ruby storefront run by a licensee) — merged into one dataset with each item's US and German availability, price, category, product format (plush, keychain, sticker, ornament, mug, earrings, fossil-skull, ...), and, where the site itself doesn't say so anymore, a best-effort *retired* status and approximate date range pulled from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine.

- [**catalog.html**](catalog.html) — the interactive browser. Search across name, species and description; filter by US availability, DE availability, product type and category (all multi-select), and by whether the item has a photo at all — that last one is **on by default**, so the 51 records with no recoverable photo stay out of the way until you ask for them; switch every name/description/price between English and German with one button (🇺🇸/🇩🇪) — US is the default/canonical language since a product exists there first, if it ever reaches the German store at all; group by name, category or product type; light/dark theme; a columns/text-size slider for screen or print layout; click a photo for a full-size lightbox with keyboard/swipe navigation. Every setting persists to `localStorage`, and the whole (gzip-compressed) dataset is base64-embedded in the page, so it works opened straight from disk (`file://`) — no server needed.
- [`catalog_data.json.gz`](catalog_data.json.gz) — the underlying dataset, one record per item, 782 items.
- [`images/`](images/) — one product photo per item (AVIF, background removed so it's actually transparent — the source photos are plain studio shots, not pre-cut like some other toy catalogs), 731 items have one; the remaining 51 (retired US items whose photo survives nowhere the pipeline can reach) show a 🦠 placeholder in the catalog.
- [`image_audit.json`](image_audit.json) / [`image_upgrade_report.json`](image_upgrade_report.json) / [`image_adoption_report.json`](image_adoption_report.json) / [`cutout_repair_report.json`](cutout_repair_report.json) / [`ai_upscale_report.json`](ai_upscale_report.json) — per-image quality measurements, what was re-sourced and why (including the 21 candidates a visual pass threw out), which recovered photo came from where, which cut-outs had to be redone, and the (uniformly negative) AI-upscale results. Photos pulled from the catalog for being the shop's not-found placeholder are moved to a local `images_rejected/` (git-ignored — all 19 are the same image); which items were affected is recorded on the records themselves as `image_placeholder_removed`.
- [`manifest.json`](manifest.json) + `icon*.png` / `favicon*` — web app manifest and icon set, so the catalog can be installed to a phone home screen (it opens straight into `catalog.html`, standalone, teal status bar) and shows its own favicon in a browser tab. All of them are generated from `icon-source.png` by [`scripts/make_icons.py`](scripts/make_icons.py), which is run by hand — the icons are committed, not built in CI.
- [`scripts/`](scripts/) — the pipeline that built this, `uv`-shebang Python throughout. See [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) for the full story of how it was built, including two genuinely tricky parts: reverse-engineering riesenmikroben.de's markup (it looks like a client-rendered app at first glance but is actually server-rendered, with the *entire* catalog — including a literal "Archiv" section for retired items — embedded in one homepage fetch) and an LLM-assisted matching+translation pass for the ~70% of items that don't share a machine-comparable name or species string across the two languages.

## By the numbers

- 782 unique items after merging both catalogs
- 315 were matched across the two catalogs and merged into a single record
- US: 318 in stock, 154 out of stock, 273 retired, 37 never offered in the US (re-derived from the live storefront, not inherited from the merge)
- DE: 108 in stock, 12 out of stock, 232 retired (riesenmikroben.de's own "Archiv" section), 430 never offered in Germany
- 731 items have a product photo; 51 do not
- 24 product types (Plush, Keychain, Petri Dish, XL/Gigantic Plush, Gift/Deluxe Box Set, Earrings, Skull Model, Sticker, Ornament, Necklace, Mug, Putty, Vinyl Figure, Tie, Glassware, Puzzle, Coaster, Soap, Magnet, Phone/Tech Case, Tube, Apparel, Gift Card, Mini Plush) across 64 categories

## Data quality notes

- **Retired-item dates are approximate, not authoritative.** Neither site publishes a release date or a "this was discontinued on X" record for the US line (riesenmikroben.de's Archiv section marks *that* a DE item is retired, but not *when*). US retirement/release windows are derived from Internet Archive Wayback Machine capture timestamps (`first_seen_us`/`last_seen_us`, `date_confidence_us: "approximate"`) — bounded by Wayback's own crawl coverage, not the product's real lifecycle. Treat them as "somewhere in this range," not fact.
- **No item is left holding a raw URL slug as its name any more, but 74 names are constructed rather than sourced.** A research pass recovered 56 real product names (with the source URL kept on the record in `name_source`, plus 56 descriptions and 15 species names). The other 74 fall back to a title-cased slug and are flagged `name_is_derived_from_slug: true` — "Bellie Vinyl" is our label, not necessarily what GIANTmicrobes called it. Proposed names that came back with no source at all (14), or that dropped the format qualifier the slug carries (9 — a listing for the ordinary "Bed Bug" plush is not evidence for `bedbug-petri` or `bedbug-gigantic`), were rejected rather than written in.
- **Image quality is measured, not assumed** (`image_audit.json`). Pixel dimensions turned out to be a bad proxy: many photos were thumbnails enlarged to gallery size, carrying full nominal resolution and no detail. Each image is scored on Laplacian variance (focus) and a `detail_ratio` (RMS difference against its own half-scale round trip, which is near zero for an upscale). Across the 662 images that survived from the first crawl, this work moved the median Laplacian variance from 60.7 to 74.9 and the median `detail_ratio` from 1.61 to 1.79; thumbnail-grade images (under 300px) went 115 → 83 and visibly soft ones 329 → 303. What is left is genuinely stuck: the products are retired, the US shop 404s them, and no sharper copy exists anywhere reachable.
- **The licensee storefronts turned out to be the best source in the catalog.** `giantmicrobes.ca` and `giantmicrobes.com.au` run on Shopify, which hands out the entire catalog at `/products.json` — 428 products, titles, SKUs and *original* image uploads at 2000–4200px, where the US shop serves a 1200px re-render. Matching needs both a fuzzy title match and an exact format match, because "Blood Cell Mug" is 0.76 similar to "Blood Cells Gift Box" and they are different SKUs; 179 pairs matched exactly, 74 of them carried a meaningfully sharper photo (up to 6.5× the detail at the same subject) and were adopted after a side-by-side look. Those records carry `image_source_store`.
- **115 images were shop thumbnails, one of them 60×50.** The recorded URL pointed either at a Magento *rendition* (`…/cache/<hash>/…`, and which rendition depended on the widget the crawler scraped it from — the "recently viewed" strip renders at 75×90) or at a literal `product/thumbnails/` directory. Dropping the cache segment gives back the original upload, and where that failed the product's own page is scraped for media filenames matching the slug. That is a resolution rescue, not a sharpness one: `detail_ratio` is per-pixel and a 60×50 crop scores *higher* than the 1600×1200 photo it was cut from, so against a thumbnail-sized incumbent the comparison has to be made on size.
- **Nothing is adopted on a metric alone.** Every proposed replacement goes onto a contact sheet (`scripts/make_contact_sheet.py`) and is looked at next to the image it would replace. Of 104 mechanically-approved upgrades, 21 were wrong: "GIGANTIC!" logo banners with no product in frame, SEM micrographs of the real pathogen, marketing infographics, lifestyle shots, blister packaging instead of the figure, and four cases of simply the wrong product. They are kept in `image_upgrade_report.json` as `rejected-visual` with the reason.
- **The 51 items still without a photo are the hard tail, and stay empty on purpose.** Image search does find *something* for every one of them — it just finds the wrong thing: a generic crocheted brain, a stock photo of a real petri culture, another product's gift box. Nothing was adopted from that pass. `image_search_candidates.json` keeps what was proposed so the next attempt does not start from zero.
- **`angry-brain-cell` shows its blister card rather than the figure, and that is the only photo there is.** The shop publishes exactly two files for that SKU, `angry-brain-front` and `angry-brain-back`, both of the packaging; every other filename on the media path answers with the not-found placeholder, and no reseller listing carries a loose shot. Its siblings (`happy-brain-cell`, `sad-brain-cell`) do have bare-figure photos, which is why it looks out of place.
- **68 photos were recovered for items that had none**, mostly from Wayback Machine captures of the dead US product pages (a domain-wide CDX regex query surfaces far more than guessing exact paths, and `/uk/`, `/es/`, `/ca/` storefronts often kept an image `/us/` lost). Those records carry `image_source_url`, `image_source_page`, `image_recovered_via` and `image_is_researched: true`.
- **Background removal fails on pale packaging.** rembg/u2net is trained to find *a subject*; a white gift box on a white studio backdrop is not one, so the gift boxes, the Einstein paper puzzle and the Germs Deluxe 10-pack came out erased or half-transparent — and so did keyring clips, earring hooks and hang tags. `scripts/repair_cutouts.py` scores each cut-out against a flood-fill of the original (how much of the subject survived, how much is left semi-transparent), and 37 were redone by flood-filling the backdrop inwards from the frame edge instead, which keeps every white pixel not connected to the border. Those records say `cutout_method: "floodfill"`. Flood-fill is only a fallback: on a lifestyle shot or a dark table it has no backdrop to fill, and rembg stays the better tool there.
- **16 items were shipping the shop's not-found placeholder as their product photo.** giantmicrobes.com's Magento media path answers HTTP 200 for *any* filename, serving a group shot of assorted plush microbes — which looks enough like a real product photo to survive a visual check. Those references are cleared (`image_placeholder_removed: true`) and the files moved to `images_rejected/`.
- **`status_us` was wrong on 246 records, and is now re-derived rather than inherited.** Those records said `not_offered` — never sold in the US — while carrying a US slug and a working US product page; the LLM merge pass had failed to carry the US status across when folding a matched pair into one record, and the field defaulted. `refresh_us_availability.py` re-reads every US product page (JSON-LD `availability`, falling back to the page's own "Out of stock"/add-to-cart wording on the older template that has no JSON-LD, and 404 → retired): 171 turned out to be in stock, 45 out of stock, 30 genuinely retired. `not_offered` now means only what it says — the 37 DE-only records with no US counterpart at all.
- **Beyond that bug, the availability data was accurate.** The same full re-check moved only 4 other records. Each refreshed record carries `status_us_checked` (the date) and `status_us_source` (which signal decided it), so staleness is now visible rather than assumed. `status_de` has *not* been re-checked and is still as of the original crawl.
- **One record's status can't be verified live:** `zombievirus` sits behind a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403). It is left as in-stock on the strength of the site's own listing widgets, and deliberately not overwritten.
- **Only one photo per item** was kept (whichever locale's listing had one, US preferred) — both sites show a full gallery per product on their own detail pages, but downloading and background-removing 10+ images per item for 818 items wasn't worth the trade-off for a catalog viewer.
- **Names and short descriptions for items that only exist in one locale are machine-translated** (flagged `name_us_is_translation` / `name_de_is_translation` on the record), not the retailer's own copy — riesenmikroben.de simply doesn't carry most of the US accessory-format SKUs (keychains, stickers, earrings, ornaments...), and giantmicrobes.com doesn't carry a handful of DE-only items either, so there's no authentic text to show on the other side.
- **US/DE matching is inherently fuzzy.** A deterministic pass matched items sharing an exact normalized scientific/parenthetical species string (69 pairs); an LLM-assisted pass then matched another 238 pairs by understanding that, say, US "Waterbear (Tardigrade)" and DE "Bärtierchen" (species "Hypsibius dujardini") are the same plush. Mismatches are possible, especially among near-duplicate items (colour variants, gigantic/XL/keychain versions of the same character).

## Note on rebuilding

Depends on both sites' live catalogs and the Wayback Machine's current archive state — re-running later will produce diffs from stock/retirement changes over time and possibly better/worse Wayback recovery, not from anything in this repo. Re-run order: `fetch_us_catalog_list.py`, `fetch_us_product_details.py`, `discover_retired_products.py`, `fetch_de_catalog.py`, `match_us_de_availability.py` (then the LLM matching/translation pass described in AGENTS.md, then `apply_llm_matches.py`), `classify_product_type.py`, `download_images.py`, `upgrade_images.py`, `remove_background.py`, `convert_to_avif.py`, `audit_images.py`, `refresh_us_availability.py`, then `generate_unified_catalog.py`.

`upgrade_images.py` re-sources photos at their real resolution and must run before background removal; `audit_images.py` measures the result and is what tells you whether a re-run helped. `verify_candidates.py` filters researched image URLs against the not-found placeholder — run it over anything an agent or a manual search proposes, never trust a bare HTTP 200 from that host. `ai_upscale.py` exists and works, but see AGENTS.md: nothing it produced was good enough to ship.

The photo-recovery side runs on its own: `search_image_candidates.py` (DuckDuckGo + Bing image search for items with no photo) and `fetch_licensee_catalogs.py` (the Canadian and Australian licensee stores are Shopify and hand out their whole catalog at `/products.json`) propose sources, `match_licensee_images.py --probe` pairs those stores' products with ours and measures both photos, `download_candidates.py` stages search hits and drops placeholders, `make_contact_sheet.py` tiles them for a visual pass, and `adopt_images.py` is the only step that writes into `images/` and the catalog. Afterwards run `remove_background.py`, `convert_to_avif.py`, then `repair_cutouts.py` over the same source directories to catch the cut-outs rembg ate.

One warning about that last step: `repair_cutouts.py` re-cuts from whatever source directory it is pointed at, so pointing it at a directory of *alternative* photos (all 179 licensee probes rather than the 74 adopted ones) makes it replace pictures instead of repairing them. It happened here to 65 images before the guard existed. It now refuses unless the source and the shipped image are perceptually the same photograph.
